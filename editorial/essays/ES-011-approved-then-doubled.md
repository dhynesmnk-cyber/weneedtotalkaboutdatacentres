## The common perception

When NSW approves a data centre, the public meets it through the assessment: a size, a list of conditions, a decision. The Eastern Creek Data Centre Expansion was assessed at a "Power consumption 32 megawatts (MW)" [SRC_SSD21342738_ASSESSMENT].

That is no longer its size. A modification decided in August 2026 raised it "from 32 MW to 63 MW", and the Department assessed the change as one that "would result in minimal environmental impacts" [SRC_SSD21342738_MOD2_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_NSWPORTAL_MODS]. The approval the public saw is where a data centre starts, not where it stops [RG-045].

## What the records show

### Five modifications

We read the reports on all 17 modifications on the State's data centre files, and eight add power, generators or diesel [RG-034]. Five, which our records first flagged, follow [RG-045]. All five were lodged under section 4.55(1A) of the planning Act, the route for a change that is "substantially the same development as originally approved" and "would result in minimal environmental impacts" [SRC_SSD21342738_MOD2_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_SSD10330_MOD2_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_SSD9741_MOD1_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_SSD24299707_MOD1_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_SSD10330_MOD4_REPORT].

- **Eastern Creek, Mod 2.** Power from 32 MW to 63 MW; back-up generators from "27 x 2.65 MW generators" to "52 x 1.8 MW generators"; additional fuel storage from "Up to 545,000 litres (L)" to "Up to 782,000 L" [SRC_SSD21342738_MOD2_ASSESSMENT]. Decided by the Acting Team Leader, Industry Assessments [SRC_SSD21342738_MOD2_ASSESSMENT].
- **Roberts Road, Mod 2.** "The installation of 24 additional generators and the relocation of four approved units", and diesel storage from 1,914,000 litres to 2,403,600 litres [SRC_SSD10330_MOD2_ASSESSMENT]. The original consent says diesel storage "does not exceed 2,000 tonnes at any one time" [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS]. The modification would "increase total diesel storage capacity above 2,000 tonnes", which the Department's report says "potentially triggers the requirement for an environment protection licence" [SRC_SSD10330_MOD2_ASSESSMENT]. Decided by the Team Leader, Industry Assessments, in March 2026 [SRC_SSD10330_MOD2_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_NSWPORTAL_MODS].
- **Roberts Road, Mod 4, not yet decided.** The applicant seeks to go "from 72 generators to 96 generators" [SRC_SSD10330_MOD4_REPORT].
- **Lane Cove West, Mod 1.** The "replacement of 80 MV generators with 116 LV generators", and diesel moved into 16 internal tanks holding 1,952 kilolitres [SRC_SSD9741_MOD1_ASSESSMENT]. Decided by the Director, Industry Assessments, in April 2020 [SRC_SSD9741_MOD1_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_NSWPORTAL_MODS].
- **Talavera Road, Mod 1.** "An increase in capacity from 38 megawatts (MW) to 47 MW", with four more diesel generators [SRC_SSD24299707_MOD1_ASSESSMENT]. Decided by the Team Leader, Industry Assessments, in December 2024 [SRC_SSD24299707_MOD1_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_NSWPORTAL_MODS].

### How the public hears

For this kind of modification, the Lane Cove West report says, "there is no requirement for section 4.55(1A) modifications to be publicly exhibited", and that one "was not publicly exhibited" [SRC_SSD9741_MOD1_ASSESSMENT]. At Talavera Road, "The Department did not exhibit the modification application but made the supporting documentation publicly available on the NSW planning portal." [SRC_SSD24299707_MOD1_ASSESSMENT]

What happens instead is a posting and a referral: Eastern Creek's application went on the portal on 30 January 2026 and out to the council, agencies and utilities [SRC_SSD21342738_MOD2_ASSESSMENT].

An officer can decide such a modification under delegation where, among other tests, "there are no public submissions in the nature of objections" [SRC_SSD24299707_MOD1_ASSESSMENT].

### Not every change goes up

Eastern Creek's modification cut generator testing from 198 hours a year to 48 [SRC_SSD21342738_MOD2_ASSESSMENT]. Roberts Road's came with "lesser testing hours than currently approved" [SRC_SSD10330_MOD2_ASSESSMENT]. The modifications made sites bigger and, in places, their testing tighter [RG-045].

### Just under the line

The consents sit close to two regulatory lines. Thirteen of the 18 data centre consents we hold cap diesel storage at 2,000 tonnes, the line Roberts Road's modification crossed [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS] [RG-061]. Lane Cove West's fourth modification took its diesel from 1,651 to 1,966.9 tonnes, "below the storage limit of 2,000 t", decided under delegation [SRC_SSD9741_MOD4_ASSESSMENT].

The other line is time. The State's guidelines say "Current regulations allow data centres to use generators for up to 200 hours a year without being subject to any point source emission limits for oxides of nitrogen" [SRC_NSWGUIDE26]. Fifteen of the 18 consents cap generator operation at exactly 200 hours a year; the three decided since July 2026 set 170, 173 and 187 [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS] [RG-061].

### The summary that disagrees

The planning portal's own description of the Eastern Creek modification says it increases power consumption "to 70.2 megawatts" [SRC_NSWPORTAL_MODS]. The Department's assessment report says 63 MW [SRC_SSD21342738_MOD2_ASSESSMENT]. This record does not tell you which one the consent now carries. The modification instrument would [RG-045].

## What to watch next

The Roberts Road Mod 4 report says "The application will be publicly exhibited by DPHI in accordance with the requirements of the Act" [SRC_SSD10330_MOD4_REPORT]. If it is, it will be the first of these generator increases the public is formally invited to comment on [RG-045].

### For journalists and researchers

Every modification has its own portal page, with a case number ending "-Mod-1", "-Mod-2" and so on, holding its report and instrument [SRC_NSWPORTAL_MODS]. When a story cites a data centre's size, check the latest modification, not the original consent [RG-045].

## Our view

A consent describes a data centre on the day it is approved. It does not describe the data centre that gets built.

Some of these changes may be sensible. But doubling power consumption is not a detail: it means more electricity from a stretched grid, more generators and more diesel. When the public was consulted on 32 megawatts, a change to 63 deserves more than a posting and an officer's decision [SRC_SSD21342738_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_SSD21342738_MOD2_ASSESSMENT].

Two changes would fix most of it. Each project's page should show the current approved size after every modification: power, generators, diesel. And a modification that raises power consumption should be publicly exhibited, as the original application was.

> To the Secretary, NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure: for each data centre consent, will the Department publish on the project's page the current approved power consumption, number of back-up generators and diesel storage after all modifications? Will any modification that increases power consumption be publicly exhibited?
