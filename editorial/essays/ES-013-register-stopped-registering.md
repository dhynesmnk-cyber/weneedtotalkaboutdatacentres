## The common perception

The Commonwealth's Hosting Certification Framework tells government buyers that its top tier "represents the highest level of assurance to Australian Government customers and offers the most secure storage solutions for government held data" [SRC_HCF_REGISTER].

The register that carries that promise names each certified provider and facility, and says where none of them is [SRC_HCF_REGISTER] [RG-101]. Since 3 November 2025 it has also stopped taking new providers [SRC_HCF_REGISTER]. A list meant to tell government where its data is safest cannot tell the public where that is.

## What the records show

### What the list says

Each entry is a provider and a name: "Cloud Carrier – DB1", "DigiCo – BNE2", "Macquarie Technology Group – IC1, IC2, IC3 and IC3e" [SRC_HCF_REGISTER]. There is no address, no state, no status and no capacity [RG-101]. A few names carry a suburb, such as "Telstra – Deakin and St Leonards"; most carry nothing a map could use [SRC_HCF_REGISTER].

We read it from the Internet Archive's copy of 28 September 2026, because the live site refused automated readers [SRC_HCF_REGISTER].

### The pause

At the top of the page sits a notice: "The Department will pause the HCF Certification registration of prospective service providers and HCF Certified providers seeking supplementary assessment effective from 3 November 2025 until HCF reforms have been completed." [SRC_HCF_REGISTER] It adds: "HCF Certified providers will not be impacted." [SRC_HCF_REGISTER]

When we read the copy of 28 September 2026, almost eleven months later, the notice was still there, and it gave no date for the reforms [SRC_HCF_REGISTER].

### Placing them ourselves

Reading the list end to end, we found thirteen certified facilities our own record had never held [RG-101]. Eight we could place from the operators' own websites, though most of those pages give a facility's name and little else: no street address, and often no capacity [RG-103].

The names do not always line up. The register certifies "Springfield Real Estate Sales Pty Ltd – Polaris Data Centre" [SRC_HCF_REGISTER]. The facility's own page says "Polaris is 100% owned by the Springfield City Group" [SRC_OP_POLARIS_INDEX]. How the two relate is not on the record we hold [RG-103].

Three more were placed only when the author downloaded DigiCo's own documents by hand [RG-101]. Two are still unplaced. Cloud Carrier's website refuses our crawler, and nothing we have read places Vocus's TransACT House [RG-101]. DigiCo's own location pages, which might place its other sites, answered our crawler with a refusal [RG-103] [RG-106].

### A certified name, an unfinished building

The register certifies Macquarie's "IC1, IC2, IC3 and IC3e" as strategic enclaves [SRC_HCF_REGISTER]. Macquarie's own page for its newest Macquarie Park building says "IC3 Super West is currently under construction with the entire end-state power secured", and lists it as "IC3 Super West – Coming Soon" [SRC_OP_MACQUARIE_IC3_SUPER_WEST].

Is the register's "IC3" that building, or an older one with a similar name? This record does not tell you, and the author could not say either [RG-104]. The certificate's own scope would.

### A certified site, an unnamed tenant

DigiCo's annual report records "SYD1 granted 'Strategic Certification' status under the Australian Government's Hosting Certification Framework (HCF) by the Department of Home Affairs" [SRC_DGT_AR_2026]. In October 2026 DigiCo announced a 15-year contract for 71 megawatts at SYD1 "with a global technology firm" [SRC_DGT_SYD1_71MW_20261015]. Who that firm is, DigiCo has not said [RG-101].

## Where the data is too thin

- Where Cloud Carrier's DB1 and Vocus's TransACT House are [RG-101].
- The addresses, areas and capacities of the facilities placed from operators' pages [RG-103].
- Where DigiCo's other four Australian sites are [RG-106].
- Which building the register's "IC3" is [RG-104].
- When the reforms will finish, and what they will change. The notice does not say [SRC_HCF_REGISTER].

## What to watch next

The reform notice itself: when it goes, the register's new form will show whether location is part of the reform [SRC_HCF_REGISTER]. DigiCo's and Cloud Carrier's own pages are listed for a person to download, since our crawler cannot [RG-103] [RG-101].

### For journalists and researchers

The Internet Archive keeps dated copies of the register, so changes to it can be tracked over time; we cite the copy of 28 September 2026 by its hash [SRC_HCF_REGISTER]. Match each name to the operator's own page before treating it as a site, because the register's provider is not always the operator [SRC_OP_POLARIS_INDEX] [RG-103].

## Our view

A register of where government data may safely be kept should say where that is.

There may be good reasons not to publish a street address for a facility that holds classified material. There is no comparable reason to withhold a state or a suburb. Location decides which laws apply, which grid and water supply a facility draws on, and which community lives beside it. A public list that names a site without placing it asks the public to take its safety on trust.

A pause can be sensible while rules are rewritten. A pause with no end date, nearly a year on, leaves a frozen list doing the work of a live one.

> To the Department of Home Affairs, Hosting Certification Framework: when will the reforms announced with the pause on new registrations be completed? Will the reformed register state the state and suburb of each certified facility, and the date its certification was granted?
