## The common perception

On 1 May 2026, the data centre industry's peak body opened its evidence to a NSW parliamentary inquiry with a promise: "Data centre operators will pay $10.3 billion in energy infrastructure to 2030, and $1.1 billion of that is excess capacity available for public use." [SRC_INQ_T_0501]

The report behind the industry's figures never states that amount as operators' spending. It gives $3.1 billion spent since 2020 and $7.2 billion forecast by 2030, counted from the operators' own data [SRC_MANDALA_DCEI_2025] [RG-043]. Parliament was told what the industry pays for the grid, and nobody outside the industry has checked it [RG-043].

## What the records show

### Where the number comes from

The figures come from a November 2025 report, prepared by the consultancy Mandala for Data Centres Australia and commissioned by four operators: AirTrunk, Amazon Web Services, CDC and NEXTDC [SRC_MANDALA_DCEI_2025].

Its summary says: "Data centres have invested $3.1 billion in grid infrastructure since 2020, with a further $7.2 billion forecast by 2030." [SRC_MANDALA_DCEI_2025] The source line under its figures reads: "Data from select data centre operators; Expert interviews; Mandala analysis." [SRC_MANDALA_DCEI_2025]

Add the two and you get the figure the inquiry heard [SRC_MANDALA_DCEI_2025] [SRC_INQ_T_0501]. One part is money the report says has already been spent. The other is a forecast. "Will pay" folds both into a promise about the future [SRC_INQ_T_0501].

### Where the report does print it

The report does print "$10.3 billion" once [SRC_MANDALA_DCEI_2025]. The sentence reads: "By comparison, the Australian Government has committed $10.3 billion of funding for vital grid electricity infrastructure to 2030." [SRC_MANDALA_DCEI_2025]

That stopped us. The only time the industry's own report writes the number, it is describing public money [SRC_MANDALA_DCEI_2025]. This record does not tell you how the figure put to the inquiry was worked out. A note from the peak body naming the document that states it would [RG-043].

### Who checks the total

The networks agree on the principle. Ausgrid told the inquiry on 29 May 2026 that "in New South Wales, the customer who caused network costs pays for them" [SRC_INQ_T_0529]. On 22 May, Transgrid said it "wouldn't invest in actually building anything unless we have full commitment from the data centre", with the data centre "funding their connection works" [SRC_INQ_T_0522]. Asked how many data centre requests had ended in a connection, it said: "We haven't actually fully connected one yet." [SRC_INQ_T_0522]

No network put a total on what data centres have paid [RG-043].

### What the State gives

The same opening statement said operators "do not receive subsidies or tax incentives" [SRC_INQ_T_0501]. We have found no tax concession to a data centre, but the records that would show one are not public [RG-007]. The Secretary of the Premier's Department told the inquiry: "The one thing that we don't contemplate is government participation financially." [SRC_INQ_T_0522]

The State gives time and attention instead. The 2025-26 Budget put $17.7 million into an Investment Delivery Authority for projects valued over $1 billion [SRC_NSW_IDA_BUDGET_2025]. The government's own media release announcing it quoted NEXTDC's chief executive: "AI infrastructure has outgrown traditional planning systems." [SRC_NSW_IDA_BUDGET_2025]

The State's data centre guidelines add "a concierge function within DPHI" that "regularly and proactively engages with data centre proponents", and advice that begins "before a site has been selected" [SRC_NSWGUIDE26]. Its staff and cost are not published, and no record we have read offers councils or residents the same [RG-063].

### What the public is paid

What a developer pays the public is written into its approval [RG-025]. In the signed NSW consents we hold, single contributions range from $3,568.10, owed to the State for the DigiCo SYD1 expansion in the City of Sydney, to about $8.6 million, owed to Camden Council for a data centre on Turner Road [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS] [RG-025].

## Where the data is too thin

- Whether the operators' totals match what Transgrid, Ausgrid and Endeavour Energy have recorded, and what the energy regulator has approved [RG-043].
- How much of the $7.2 billion forecast is signed, and how much is planned [RG-043].
- Whether any state has given a data centre a tax concession. An application for Revenue NSW's records is drafted, asking for rules and totals, not any one company [RG-007].
- Whether any of these contributions has been paid [RG-025].

## What to watch next

We will test the operators' totals against the networks' published capital plans and the regulator's decisions on them [RG-043]. Once the application for Revenue NSW's records is lodged, the agency must decide within 20 working days [RG-007].

### For journalists and researchers

The report's two sentences are quoted in full in its source record, and the inquiry's corrected transcripts are on the NSW Parliament's website [SRC_MANDALA_DCEI_2025] [SRC_INQ_T_0501]. Every contribution amount, with the consent it comes from, is listed under the open question on contributions [RG-025].

## Our view

An industry is entitled to count its own spending. A figure put to Parliament should be one its source states.

"Will pay" reads as a promise. On the industry's own report, part of the amount is money spent since 2020, and the rest is a forecast built from the operators' own figures [SRC_MANDALA_DCEI_2025]. That does not make the spending small, or the principle that the customer who causes a cost pays for it wrong.

What is missing is a count by someone with no stake in the answer. The networks hold the invoices, and the regulator approves their spending. Either could publish what data centres have actually paid for. In the records we hold, neither has.

"No subsidies" can be true and still leave out what the public gives. A concierge for developers, and none for their neighbours, is a gift of the State's time.

> To the Chief Executive Officer, Data Centres Australia: in your opening statement to the NSW Legislative Council's inquiry into data centres, you said operators "will pay" a total for energy infrastructure by the end of the decade. Which document states that total? How much of it had already been spent when you said it, and how much is a forecast?
