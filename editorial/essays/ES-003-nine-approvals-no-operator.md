## The common perception

In March 2026 the NSW Government published the data centre projects it had endorsed, each beside a company [SRC_NSW_IDA]. Beside the Mamre Road campus at Kemps Creek, which the planning website lists at "a power capacity of 1 GW", it put "KNBDC SYD4 Pty Ltd" [SRC_NSW_IDA] [SRC_NSWPORTAL_REGISTER].

That is how the public meets these projects: a site, a size and a company name. The name on the form is often not the company that will run the building. In nine of the 22 determined NSW data centre consents whose reports we hold, an established consultancy or developer applied for an operator the Department's report does not name [RG-055].

## What the records show

### Standing in for someone else

We read the Department's assessment report for every determined data centre consent we hold. In nine, the applicant is standing in for someone the report calls "the end user", "the client", "the lessee" or "a global data centre operator", and that someone is never named [RG-055] [SRC_SSD59416728_ASSESSMENT].

At Apollo Place in Lane Cove West, the report says the applicant "has been approached by a data centre operator (the end user)" [SRC_SSD67407231_ASSESSMENT]. AirTrunk's own website calls the same project "Phase 5 of AirTrunk's investment within the precinct" [SRC_OP_AIRTRUNK_SYD2_SYDNEY_NORTH]. The operator's marketing page said what the planning file would not.

The reports are not careless about this. They are consistent. At Microsoft's Kemps Creek campus, the report says only that the end user numbers its buildings "SYD05, 06 & 07" [SRC_SSD10101987_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_MSFT_STAGING]. In 2019, the report on a Lane Cove West data centre described its client as an operator with "two data centres in Melbourne and western Sydney", and went no further [SRC_SSD9741_ASSESSMENT].

Is this not simply how development works, with a builder applying before a tenant signs? The reports say otherwise. At Apollo Place the operator had already approached the applicant when the application was made [SRC_SSD67407231_ASSESSMENT]. At Davis Road, below, the report goes further and relies on the unnamed operator's own commitments [SRC_SSD59416728_ASSESSMENT]. The Department knew there was an operator. It chose not to print the name.

Even the applicant's own name can fall behind. The national company register shows EMKC Cubed Management had been renamed CKTSME Management in June 2025, four months before NSW approved Apollo Place under the old name in October 2025 [SRC_ASIC_COMPANIES_202610] [SRC_NSWPORTAL_REGISTER].

### Who the applicants are

The easy story would be shell companies. The register does not support it. Of the companies holding determined consents, only one had existed for less than three years when its consent was decided [RG-055] [SRC_ASIC_COMPANIES_202610].

The applicants are mostly established firms: developers such as Goodman and Stockland, operators such as NEXTDC, CDC and Equinix, and engineering and design firms such as Arup, Cundall, Lehr Consultants and Greenbox Architecture [RG-055] [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS].

That is the detail that surprised us. The question was never whether the applicant is real. It is whether the applicant is the one who will run the building, and on nine files the report tells you it is not, then stops [RG-055].

### Who the conditions bind

At Glendenning Road, the consent names its applicant as Lehr Consultants International (Australia) Pty Ltd [SRC_SSD73761707_CONSENT]. Who will develop and use the 235 megawatt campus is an open question [RG-037]. The consent's most demanding condition is addressed to the applicant: before the data centre may operate, "the Applicant must demonstrate" that all of its electricity is matched with new, firmed renewable supply [SRC_SSD73761707_CONSENT].

The consent defines the applicant as the named firm "or any person carrying out any development to which this consent applies" [SRC_SSD73761707_CONSENT]. So the obligation will follow whoever builds and runs the campus. That is sound drafting. It also means the company that will carry the State's strongest renewable condition is one the consent does not name [RG-037].

### The name on the land

At Davis Road in Wetherill Park, the applicant is the engineering firm Cundall, which the report says "acts on behalf of a global data centre operator" [SRC_SSD59416728_ASSESSMENT]. NSW approved the project in December 2024 [SRC_NSWPORTAL_REGISTER].

Which operator? The report does not say [SRC_SSD59416728_ASSESSMENT]. It does credit that unnamed operator with a promise: the end users' commitment to "100% renewable energy usage by 2025" [SRC_SSD59416728_ASSESSMENT]. An approval weighed a company's pledge without saying which company made it.

None of the 46 data centre projects on the State's planning register is named for Amazon, Google or Meta [RG-023]. So we read the land title the applicant filed with its application. The registered owner is Amazon Australia Company Pty Ltd [SRC_SSD59416728_TITLE]. The national register shows that company was created in November 2022, less than four weeks before the title's current edition was issued [SRC_ASIC_COMPANIES_202610] [SRC_SSD59416728_TITLE].

One attachment on the public file is titled as a certificate for Amazon Corporate Services Pty Ltd. It is locked [RG-057].

So the file holds a consultancy on the consent, an unnamed operator in the report and an Amazon company on the title. Nothing on the public file joins the three [RG-057]. This record does not tell you who will run Davis Road. The locked attachment and the landowner's consent letter would.

### Four letters

The Mamre Road campus carries three names in three places. Its environmental impact statement names Plan Project Management Pty Ltd as the proponent [SRC_SSD92743706_EIS]. The government's list names KNBDC SYD4 Pty Ltd [SRC_NSW_IDA]. The trade press named AirTrunk as the likely buyer of the land, if the campus was approved [SRC_WMEDIA_ISPT].

The national company register settled it, by a change of name. KNBDC SYD4 Pty Ltd was created in June 2025, and on 16 June 2026 it became AirTrunk SYD4 Pty Ltd [SRC_ASIC_COMPANIES_202610]. Its sister companies changed their names the same week, and so did several KNBDC companies in Melbourne [SRC_ASIC_COMPANIES_202610]. The government had endorsed the project under a name that became AirTrunk's less than three months later [SRC_NSW_IDA] [SRC_ASIC_COMPANIES_202610]. The most informative document in the file was a change of name.

The land title fills in the rest. The registered owner is ISPT, which the register now names IFM Real Estate Fiduciary, and on the title sits a caveat, a formal claim on the land, lodged by KNBDC SYD4 [SRC_SSD92743706_TITLE] [SRC_ASIC_COMPANIES_202610]. The landowners' letters consenting to the application, from IFM, GPT and Gibb Group, would say what was agreed. All three are locked [RG-021].

### Seventeen days

The newest proposals are where fresh companies do appear [RG-055]. The planning report for a campus in Queensland's Western Downs is dated 14 August 2026 and was prepared for WDDP Pty Ltd, describing "approximately 2,160MW of IT load" [SRC_WDDP_TPR]. The register shows WDDP Pty Ltd was created on 28 July 2026, seventeen days earlier [SRC_ASIC_COMPANIES_202610].

Trade reports said the developer was a company called Zerra, owned by an investment firm called AGP [SRC_DCD_ZERRA]. The application mentions neither [RG-050]. The register does. Three Zerra companies sharing the project's initials were created on 10 August 2026, and Zerra WDDP Manager Australia took its current name on 13 August; one of its former names is AGP DC Manager Australia [SRC_ASIC_COMPANIES_202610].

A third new vehicle, Project Garden 1 Pty Ltd, was created in February 2025 for a proposal at Brookhollow Avenue [RG-055]. Mamre Road, Western Downs and Brookhollow Avenue are the newest and largest proposals, and none has yet been decided [RG-055].

### The tenant nobody names

Knowing the operator does not settle who the building is for. DigiCo is public about owning and running its Sydney site, and in October 2026 it announced a 15-year contract for 71 megawatts there with "a global technology firm", which it did not name [SRC_DGT_SYD1_71MW_20261015]. The consent for that site's expansion names a third company again as applicant, HDI SYD1 Property Holdings Limited [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS].

### What it costs the record

Names that do not match make records that do not add up. A Stockland data centre at Macquarie Park sat in our own records twice: once from its consent, and once from a directory website that said it was for Amazon Web Services [SRC_SSD10467_ASSESSMENT] [RG-084]. When we checked, the directory page we had cited does not mention Stockland at all [SRC_CERTSTRAT]. We merged the two records, and the Amazon claim is now an open question, not a fact [RG-102].

### How the pieces fit

Anyone can follow the same trail, and on these files it ran the same way every time [RG-055]:

1. The consent names its applicant at the front, in its first schedule [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS].
2. The Department's assessment report says whom the applicant acts for, if anyone, and on nine files does not name them [RG-055].
3. The land title filed with the application names the registered owner and any caveat on the land [SRC_SSD92743706_TITLE].
4. The national company register shows when each company was created and every name it has held [SRC_ASIC_COMPANIES_202610].
5. The landowner's letter consenting to the application would join these up. On every file we tried, it was locked [RG-021].

## What to watch next

The application for the locked ownership documents goes to the Department of Planning, which must decide within 20 working days of receiving it [RG-021].

The three newest proposals are still undecided. When they are determined, their consents will show whether the vehicle or the operator ends up named [RG-055] [SRC_NSWPORTAL_REGISTER].

### For journalists and researchers

Every company in this essay can be checked in the national company register, which the government publishes free each month [SRC_ASIC_COMPANIES_202610].

Two leads worked here and will work elsewhere. A caveat on a title filed with an application names the party with an interest in the land, which is how KNBDC SYD4 surfaced at Mamre Road [SRC_SSD92743706_TITLE]. And where a report says an applicant acts for an operator, the operator's own website is the next place to look, as it was at Apollo Place [SRC_OP_AIRTRUNK_SYD2_SYDNEY_NORTH].

## Our view

A planning system that asks who is applying, and not who it is for, is answering the wrong question.

The people who live beside a data centre, and the councils that speak for them, are asked to weigh its water, its power, its diesel generators and its traffic. They are entitled to know which company will be answerable for all of that once the consent is granted. On nine files, the State's own reports knew there was an operator, said so, and left the name out.

Commercial sensitivity may sometimes justify a delay. It does not justify silence without a reason. If the Department withholds an operator's name, its report should say that it is withholding one, under what provision, and when the name will be published. The government's own list of endorsed projects should name the company that will run each campus, not the vehicle that filed the form.

None of this needs new law to start. It needs the Department to print in its reports what it already knows.

> To the Secretary, NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure: for each data centre consent where the assessment report says the applicant acts for an operator or end user, will the Department publish that operator's name? If a name is withheld, under what provision is it withheld, and when will it be published?
