## The common perception

The NSW planning website tells the public that the Independent Planning Commission "is generally the consent authority" for a major project where "the local council has lodged an objection" [SRC_NSWPORTAL_DETERMINE_DA]. A council's objection is how a local voice reaches a decision-maker outside the Department [SRC_NSWPORTAL_DETERMINE_DA].

Of the eight data centres the Department has decided after a council objected, one reached the commission [RG-040]. In September 2026, under a delegation made the month before, a departmental director approved one while the council's objection stood [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT].

## What the records show

### Eight objections

We read the Department's assessment report for every data centre it has decided, looking for councils that said no [RG-040]. There were eight [RG-040]:

- Station Road, Seven Hills: the council "formally withdrew its objection to the development on 6 October 2022" [SRC_SSD33781208_ASSESSMENT].
- Eastern Creek Data Centre Expansion: the council's submission "was received outside the exhibition period" [SRC_SSD21342738_ASSESSMENT].
- 51 Huntingwood Drive: the objection was not made within the 28-day exhibition period, and the council withdrew it on 10 August 2023 [SRC_SSD41589232_ASSESSMENT].
- Project Echidna, Eastern Creek: withdrawn on 19 March 2024 [SRC_SSD47320208_ASSESSMENT].
- Marsden Park: withdrawn on 9 September 2025 [SRC_SSD70889211_ASSESSMENT].
- Glendenning Road: the council "later advised in writing to the Department that its objection has been resolved" [SRC_SSD73761707_ASSESSMENT].
- Talavera Road, Macquarie Park: Ryde's objection stood, and the commission decided [SRC_IPC_TALAVERA_SOR].
- Project Apollo, Macquarie Park: Ryde's objection stood, and a departmental director decided [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT].

Six of the eight were in Blacktown, and none of those six objections survived to the decision [RG-040].

### The one that went the distance

The commission's statement on Talavera Road gives the reason it decided: "an objection was received from the City of Ryde Council and a company connected to the Applicant disclosed a reportable political donation." [SRC_IPC_TALAVERA_SOR] It decided the expansion in January 2024 [SRC_IPC_TALAVERA_SOR].

### The one that did not

Two years later, Ryde objected to Project Apollo, also in Macquarie Park [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT]. The Department's report records "one objection from City of Ryde Council (Council) and no submissions from the public", and then: "Council maintained its objection" [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT].

Its conclusion: "Accordingly, the application can be determined by the A/Director, Industry Assessments, under delegation." [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT] The acting director approved Project Apollo on 2 September 2026 [SRC_NSWPORTAL_REGISTER].

### What the delegation says

The older delegations let an officer decide only where "the relevant local council has not made a submission by way of objection" [SRC_SSD41589232_ASSESSMENT]. The delegation of 18 August 2026, as the Apollo report quotes it, covers "relevant resource, energy or data centre projects" where "there are less than 15 unique public submissions by way of objection" and "a reportable political disclosure statement has not been made by the Applicant" [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT].

It does not mention councils [RG-040]. It was made the day after the government published its Data Centre Guidelines [SRC_NSWGUIDE26] [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT].

This record does not tell you how the commission's role was set aside for Apollo. The delegation instrument itself, and the planning policy that names the commission as decision-maker, as both stood in September 2026, would [RG-040]. When we read it, the website's explanation still carried its April date [SRC_NSWPORTAL_DETERMINE_DA].

### Next in line

Ryde has objected to another data centre in Macquarie Park, NEXTDC's S5, in these words: "In short, Council strongly objects to the development as submitted." [SRC_NSWPORTAL_COUNCIL_SUBS] When we last checked, it was still being assessed [RG-040].

## Where the data is too thin

- How the commission's role was removed for these cases. The delegation itself has not been read, and the State's legislation website turns automated readers away [RG-040].
- What councils and residents told the parliamentary inquiry. Its website now turns our crawler away too [RG-015].
- What each council objected to. Four councils' submissions are locked, and the attachments to Ryde's cover letters were not fetched [RG-040].

## What to watch next

The decision on NEXTDC S5 will show whether Apollo was the first of a pattern [RG-040]. One application under the State's right-to-information law asks for the delegation and the file behind it. Once lodged, the Department must decide within 20 working days [RG-040].

### For journalists and researchers

Every assessment report has a section on who the consent authority is and why, and quotes the delegation it relies on [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT]. Reading that section across a council's data centres takes an afternoon and shows whether its objections reached anyone independent [RG-040].

## Our view

On the record we hold, a council's objection to a data centre no longer brings the independent decision the planning website describes. If the State has changed who decides when a council objects, it should say so on the page that tells the public how decisions are made, and say why.

Councils objected eight times. Six objections were withdrawn, resolved or late, and that may reflect good negotiation. But the one objection that stood after the new delegation was decided inside the Department, under rules that do not mention councils, and the public explanation was not updated [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT].

> To the general manager of my local council: did the council object to any data centre application in our area, and was the objection maintained to the decision? If it was, who decided the application, and was the council told why the Independent Planning Commission did not?
