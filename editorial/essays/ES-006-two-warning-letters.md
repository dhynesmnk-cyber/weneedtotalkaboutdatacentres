## The common perception

The company behind a 235 megawatt data centre at Glendenning, in Sydney's west, told the Department, in its sustainability report, that "the use of 100% renewable energy by 2040 will effectively reduce the total emissions profile of the development to zero" [SRC_SSD73761707_ASSESSMENT].

The approval asked for more, and sooner [SRC_SSD73761707_CONSENT]. What the public cannot see is whether any condition is enforced. In the State's data centre files, the record of enforcement is two warning letters, both locked [RG-058].

## What the records show

### A stronger rule

Three consents determined from July 2026 carry the same condition: Project Pluto at Guildford West, Project Apollo at Macquarie Park and Glendenning Road [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS] [RG-061]. Before the data centre may operate, the applicant must show the Planning Secretary that "all operational electricity demand is matched with a portfolio of additional, firmed renewable energy supply at all times", including "both non-IT and IT loads", with power purchase and firming agreements "for NSW-based supply" [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS].

None of the 16 earlier determinations in our archive carries it, including one at Macquarie Park as late as 11 March 2026 [RG-061].

### What the companies offered

Each assessment report records a weaker offer first [RG-061]. At Glendenning, it was renewable energy by 2040 [SRC_SSD73761707_ASSESSMENT]. At Apollo, the applicant said it "has been purchasing 100% renewable electricity for electricity under their operational control", and the Department replied that emissions outcomes "remain materially dependent on the successful securing and performance of renewable energy procurement" [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT]. At Pluto, the environment regulator suggested "purchasing of renewable energy certificates", and the rooftop solar would "save 540 tonnes CO₂-e annually" against a development generating "467,924 tonnes CO₂-e annually" [SRC_SSD69223466_ASSESSMENT].

The Department wrote the stronger condition anyway [RG-061].

### How the rule works

A condition that must be met "prior to the commencement of operation" works like the final inspection on a new house [SRC_SSD73761707_CONSENT]. The builder can finish every room, but nobody moves in until the inspector signs off. The literal sequence is this:

1. The consent lets the applicant build [SRC_SSD73761707_CONSENT].
2. Within six months of construction starting, it must show it has begun the process of entering the power agreements, and update the Planning Secretary every six months [SRC_SSD73761707_CONSENT].
3. Before it operates, it must show the Secretary that all its electricity is matched by firmed renewable supply [SRC_SSD73761707_CONSENT].
4. Until the Secretary is satisfied, it may not operate [SRC_SSD73761707_CONSENT].

Where the comparison breaks: an inspector walks through the house. The record does not show how the Department will test the evidence it is given [RG-061].

### Who sees the checking

Once it operates, the Glendenning consent asks for yearly compliance reports and three-yearly independent audits, and from before construction, for the approved plans and monitoring to be published "on its website" [SRC_SSD73761707_CONSENT]. The applicant is an engineering consultancy. We found no website for it, and the obvious domain belongs to an unrelated recruitment firm [RG-039].

### Two letters

So we searched the State's data centre files for any sign that a company had been told off [SRC_NSWPORTAL_REGISTER] [RG-058]. On CDC's Roberts Road file sits a document titled "CDC Data Centres response warning letter" [SRC_NSWPORTAL_REGISTER]. On the Station Road file, at Seven Hills, sits a warning letter dated 29 May 2024, with a Department email about it [SRC_NSWPORTAL_REGISTER]. We tried to open them. All three documents are locked, as is a signed letter on the Roberts Road file [RG-058].

### The form in the folder

Roberts Road has a second puzzle. In December 2019, CDC's director and chief executive, filing as the applicant, declared a $7,500 donation to the Liberal Party's NSW division, made on 18 June 2018 [SRC_SSD10330_DONATION]. An officer could decide the case only where "a political disclosure statement has not been made" [SRC_SSD10330_ASSESSMENT]. The report said: "No reportable political donations were made by the Applicant in the last two years" [SRC_SSD10330_ASSESSMENT]. An executive director approved it under that delegation in July 2020 [SRC_SSD10330_ASSESSMENT].

This record does not tell you why the form made no difference, or from what date the two years were counted. The Department's file on the delegation would [RG-069]. We allege nothing improper.

## What to watch next

The first test comes before any switch-on, with the six-monthly reports on power agreements, and the condition does not say whether they will be published [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS]. One application under the State's right-to-information law asks for the warning letters, the replies and the delegation file. Once lodged, the Department must decide within 20 working days [RG-058] [RG-069].

### For journalists and researchers

The condition is B19 at Pluto, B22 at Apollo and B20 at Glendenning, and the locked documents are listed by title on each project's page on the planning website [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS] [SRC_NSWPORTAL_REGISTER]. A compliance report, once due, should be on the applicant's own website; whether it is there is a check anyone can make [SRC_SSD73761707_CONSENT].

## Our view

On renewable power, the Department asked for more than it was offered, and the record should say so.

A condition is only as strong as the checking behind it. The public record of enforcement on data centre consents is two titles on a website, with the letters behind them locked. That may mean a clean record. It may mean an unread one. A reader cannot tell which, and that is the problem.

Each warning, direction or penalty on a data centre consent should sit on the project's page, beside the condition it concerns.

> To the Secretary, NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure: how many compliance actions, including warning letters, penalty notices and directions, has the Department taken on data centre consents to date? Will it publish each one on the project's page, with the condition it concerned?
