## What you heard
The company behind a data centre planned for Glendenning, in Sydney's west, told the Department that using "100% renewable energy by 2040" would bring the project's emissions down to zero [SRC_SSD73761707_ASSESSMENT].

Another applicant, at Macquarie Park, told the Department it "has been purchasing 100% renewable electricity" [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT].

## The hunt
Our records carried a finding we were fairly sure of: when NSW approves a data centre, the conditions never ask for more than the company itself offered [RG-061]. We tested it against every signed approval we hold, twenty documents covering nineteen projects [RG-061].

For years it held. None of the sixteen earlier projects was told to run on renewable power around the clock [RG-061]. An approval signed in March 2026 mentions renewables and stops there [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS].

Then, from July 2026, three approvals in a row say something new [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS]. Before it may switch on, each data centre must show that every unit of electricity it uses, for the computers and for everything else, is matched by new renewable supply, bought in NSW and backed up so it holds at all times [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS].

Was that what the companies asked for? The Department's own reports say no [RG-061]. At Glendenning, the offer was renewables by 2040 [SRC_SSD73761707_ASSESSMENT]. At Apollo, the company pointed to renewable power it already buys, and the Department replied that the outcome stayed "materially dependent" on deals not yet secured [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT]. At Pluto, the environment regulator went no further than suggesting renewable energy certificates, and the company's rooftop solar would save 540 tonnes of emissions a year against 467,924 tonnes produced [SRC_SSD69223466_ASSESSMENT].

Each time, the Department wrote the stronger rule anyway [RG-061]. Something changed between March and July 2026 [RG-061]. What it was, the record does not say [RG-061].

## What the record shows
- Three approvals signed between July and September 2026 require all of a data centre's electricity to be matched by new, firmed renewable supply at all times, before it may operate [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS].
- None of the earlier approvals in the archive carries that requirement [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS].
- At Glendenning, the applicant had offered 100% renewable energy by 2040 [SRC_SSD73761707_ASSESSMENT].
- At Pluto, the applicant's rooftop solar would save 540 tonnes a year against annual emissions of 467,924 tonnes [SRC_SSD69223466_ASSESSMENT].
- The approvals require yearly reporting of energy and water use; the efficiency targets remain the companies' own [SRC_NSWPORTAL_CONSENTS].

## Still unknown
- What changed between March and July 2026: a new policy, a new instruction, or a new habit inside the Department [RG-061]. The policy the approvals point to is not in our records.
- Whether the stronger rule is only for the biggest projects. The three are also the largest recent approvals [RG-061].
- Whether any of the three operators has signed the power deals the rule requires [RG-061].
- Whether the same tightening shows up in conditions on air, noise, water or traffic [RG-061].

## Essay hooks
- The promise was renewables some day; the rule became renewables before switching on.
- A quiet change in how NSW approves data centres, which nobody announced.
- Who deserves the credit when a regulator asks for more than a company offered?
