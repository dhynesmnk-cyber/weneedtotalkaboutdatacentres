## What you heard
The NSW planning website tells the public that the Independent Planning Commission "is generally the consent authority" for a major project when "the local council has lodged an objection" [SRC_NSWPORTAL_DETERMINE_DA].

## The hunt
That promise matters. The commission sits outside the planning department and the minister, so a council's objection is how a local voice gets a case before someone independent [SRC_NSWPORTAL_DETERMINE_DA].

We read the department's report on every data centre it has decided, looking for councils that said no [RG-040]. There were eight [RG-040]. Five objections were withdrawn or settled before the decision, and one more arrived after the deadline and did not count [SRC_SSD33781208_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_SSD41589232_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_SSD47320208_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_SSD70889211_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_SSD73761707_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_SSD21342738_ASSESSMENT]. One went the distance: Ryde's objection to an expansion at Talavera Road, which the commission decided [SRC_IPC_TALAVERA_SOR].

The last went differently. Ryde objected to Project Apollo in Macquarie Park and kept objecting after the developer answered [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT]. Its letter said it was "not supportive of the development in its current form" [SRC_NSWPORTAL_COUNCIL_SUBS]. In September 2026 an acting director in the department approved it [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_NSWPORTAL_REGISTER].

How? On 18 August 2026 the minister handed data centre decisions to a departmental director wherever fewer than 15 members of the public object and no political donation has been declared [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT]. The conditions, as the report quotes them, say nothing about councils [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT]. The older ones did: an officer could decide only if the council had not objected [SRC_SSD41589232_ASSESSMENT]. The day before, the government had released its new data centre rules [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT].

The website's explanation, last updated in April, has not changed [SRC_NSWPORTAL_DETERMINE_DA]. And the other place councils and residents speak, the parliamentary inquiry, now turns automated readers away altogether [RG-015].

## What the record shows
- The planning website says the commission is generally the consent authority where the local council has lodged an objection [SRC_NSWPORTAL_DETERMINE_DA].
- Of the eight local council objections to data centres the department has decided, five were withdrawn or settled, one arrived too late, and one went to the commission [SRC_SSD33781208_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_SSD21342738_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_IPC_TALAVERA_SOR].
- The delegation of 18 August 2026 for data centre projects requires fewer than 15 public objections and no political disclosure statement [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT].
- Project Apollo was approved under that delegation while the City of Ryde maintained its objection [SRC_SSD74069708_ASSESSMENT].

## Still unknown
- How the commission's role was removed for these cases. The delegation itself has not been read, and the state's legislation website turns automated readers away; an application to the department is drafted [RG-040].
- Whether Ryde's objection to NEXTDC's next building will end the same way. Ryde "strongly objects" to it, and it has not been decided [SRC_NSWPORTAL_COUNCIL_SUBS] [RG-040].
