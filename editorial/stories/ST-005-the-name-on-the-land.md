## What you heard
The Department's report on the Davis Road Data Centre at Wetherill Park says the applicant, the engineering firm Cundall, "acts on behalf of a global data centre operator" [SRC_SSD59416728_ASSESSMENT].

## The hunt
Which operator? The report never says [SRC_SSD59416728_ASSESSMENT]. NSW approved the project in December 2024 all the same [SRC_NSWPORTAL_REGISTER].

None of the 46 data centre projects on the state's planning register is named for Amazon, Google or Meta [RG-023]. On this one, a single attachment on the public file is titled as a certificate for Amazon Corporate Services Pty Ltd. It is locked [RG-057].

So we read the land title the applicant filed with its application. The registered owner of the site is Amazon Australia Company Pty Ltd [SRC_SSD59416728_TITLE].

The national company register shows that company was created in November 2022, less than four weeks before the title's current edition was issued [SRC_ASIC_COMPANIES_202610] [SRC_SSD59416728_TITLE].

The Department's report adds one more detail about its unnamed operator. It notes the end users' commitments to "100% renewable energy usage by 2025" [SRC_SSD59416728_ASSESSMENT].

A consultancy's name on the consent. An operator the report won't name. An Amazon company on the land. Nothing we could read on the public file puts the three together [RG-057].

## What the record shows
- The applicant, Cundall, acts for "a global data centre operator" the report does not name [SRC_SSD59416728_ASSESSMENT].
- The land is registered to Amazon Australia Company Pty Ltd [SRC_SSD59416728_TITLE].
- That company was registered in November 2022 [SRC_ASIC_COMPANIES_202610].
- The project was approved in December 2024 [SRC_NSWPORTAL_REGISTER].

## Still unknown
- Whether an Amazon business will run the data centre, or only owns the land [RG-057].
- What the locked certificate and the landowner's consent letter say. Our drafted request for government information asks for both [RG-057].

## Essay hooks
- A data centre the planning file never names, on land the title does.
- Why the biggest cloud companies so rarely appear on planning applications in their own names.
- What the public loses when the only document naming a company is the one it cannot open.
