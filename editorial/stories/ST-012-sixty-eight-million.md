## What you heard
In October 2025, NVIDIA's chief executive told an audience in Washington: "We work with Palantir to accelerate everything Palantir does ... for our government, for national security, and for enterprises around the world." [SRC_SHERWOOD_GTC_QUOTE] [SRC_SINGJU_GTC_TRANSCRIPT]

In Australia, the reporting was about Defence, which "has now spent more than $26 million on Palantir contracts since 2013" [SRC_CRIKEY_DEF_FEB26].

## The hunt
How much has Australia paid Palantir altogether? The Commonwealth publishes every contract it signs, but its contracts website turns automated readers away, and so does the government's open-data portal [SRC_AUSTENDER_OCDS_202610]. The data is free to republish, though, and an international transparency group keeps a copy [SRC_AUSTENDER_OCDS_202610]. We read that copy and checked it against five contracts we had already read on the government's own site. Every figure matched [SRC_AUSTENDER_OCDS_202610].

The ledger starts in May 2013, with a software licence for Defence [SRC_AUSTENDER_OCDS_202610]. Since then, five agencies have signed 29 contracts with Palantir, worth $68.3 million [SRC_AUSTENDER_OCDS_202610]. Defence's share, about $30 million, bears out the reporting [SRC_AUSTENDER_OCDS_202610] [SRC_CRIKEY_DEF_FEB26].

The surprise was the second-biggest customer: AUSTRAC, the agency that watches money moving through Australia's banks [SRC_AUSTENDER_OCDS_202610]. It has spent $28.1 million since 2016, including the largest single contract, a "Data Analytics capability" worth $12.1 million [SRC_AUSTENDER_OCDS_202610].

Nineteen of the contracts were limited tenders, where the government invites one supplier, or a few, instead of the open market [SRC_AUSTENDER_OCDS_202610]. Eleven were signed with the American parent company rather than its Australian arm, including five of the six bought by the Australian Signals Directorate, the signals intelligence agency [SRC_AUSTENDER_OCDS_202610].

The states were harder. Victoria, South Australia, the ACT and Western Australia all turned us away, as New South Wales had before [RG-086].

## What the record shows
- From May 2013 to April 2026, Commonwealth agencies published 29 contracts with Palantir worth $68,258,634.93 at their latest values, not counting one cancelled [SRC_AUSTENDER_OCDS_202610].
- Defence holds 11 of them ($29,984,631.37), AUSTRAC six ($28,117,548.41), the Australian Criminal Intelligence Commission five ($6,068,875.14) and the Australian Signals Directorate six ($4,037,580.01) [SRC_AUSTENDER_OCDS_202610].
- 19 were limited tenders, worth $26,124,625.09 [SRC_AUSTENDER_OCDS_202610].
- 11 name the US parent, Palantir Technologies Inc., rather than its Australian company [SRC_AUSTENDER_OCDS_202610].
