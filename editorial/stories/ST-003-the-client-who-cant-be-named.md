## What you heard
When NSW approves a data centre, the Department's report tells the public who applied. Read enough of them and one line keeps coming back: the applicant "has been approached by a data centre operator (the end user)" [SRC_SSD67407231_ASSESSMENT].

## The hunt
We read the Department's report for every data centre consent NSW has determined whose report we hold. In nine of them, the company on the application is standing in for someone else: "the end user", "the client", "the lessee", "a global data centre operator" [RG-055] [SRC_SSD59416728_ASSESSMENT].

The someone else is never named. At Microsoft's Kemps Creek campus, the report says only that the end user has numbered the buildings "SYD05, 06 & 07" [SRC_SSD10101987_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_MSFT_STAGING]. In 2019, the Department's report on a Lane Cove West data centre described its client as an operator with "two data centres in Melbourne and western Sydney", and nothing more [SRC_SSD9741_ASSESSMENT].

Sometimes the company tells on itself. A property firm, EMKC Cubed Management, applied to build at Apollo Place in Lane Cove West for an unnamed "end user" [SRC_SSD67407231_ASSESSMENT]. AirTrunk's own website calls the same project "Phase 5 of AirTrunk's investment within the precinct" [SRC_OP_AIRTRUNK_SYD2_SYDNEY_NORTH].

Even the name on that consent was out of date. The national company register shows EMKC Cubed Management had already become CKTSME Management, four months before NSW approved the project in its old name [SRC_ASIC_COMPANIES_202610] [SRC_NSWPORTAL_REGISTER].

Then we went looking for the letters in which landowners consent to each application. They would say who owns the land, and often who the applicant really works for. Every one we tried was locked [RG-021] [RG-035] [RG-037] [RG-057] [RG-059] [RG-064].

## What the record shows
- In nine of the determined NSW data centre consents whose reports we hold, the Department says the applicant acts for, or was approached by, an operator it does not name [SRC_SSD9741_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_SSD10101987_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_SSD59416728_ASSESSMENT] [SRC_SSD67407231_ASSESSMENT] [RG-055].
- AirTrunk's website calls Apollo Place "Phase 5 of AirTrunk's investment within the precinct" [SRC_OP_AIRTRUNK_SYD2_SYDNEY_NORTH].
- EMKC Cubed Management has been registered as CKTSME Management since June 2025, and the Apollo Place consent was determined in October 2025 [SRC_ASIC_COMPANIES_202610] [SRC_NSWPORTAL_REGISTER].

## Still unknown
- Who EMKC Cubed Management works for at 51 Huntingwood Drive, where the report says only that the operator also runs a nearby data centre on Healey Circuit [RG-064].
- Who owns the companies that hold these consents. A paid company search would show it, and we have ranked which to buy [RG-081].
- What the locked letters say. One application for government information could ask for all of them [RG-064].

## Essay hooks
- Planning law asks who is applying, not who it is for.
- When a company tells the public more on its own website than the planning file does.
- What a neighbour is meant to object to when they cannot know who will run the building.
