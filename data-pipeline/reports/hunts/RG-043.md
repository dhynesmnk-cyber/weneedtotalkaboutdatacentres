# RG-043: The industry's $10.3 billion

Question: Test Data Centres Australia's $10.3bn energy infrastructure payment and $1.1bn excess capacity figures against network business capital plans and AER prudence assessments.
Status: in_progress, as at 2026-10-22. Batch 3 of docs/RESEARCH-PLAN.md (story line M3).

## Log
- 2026-10-22: the claim, from the 1 May 2026 transcript (SRC_INQ_T_0501): "Data centre operators will pay $10.3 billion in energy infrastructure to 2030, and $1.1 billion of that is excess capacity available for public use."
- 2026-10-22: the source traced. Mandala's "Data Centres as Enabling Infrastructure" (November 2025), archived on 2026-10-14 for RG-100, was "commissioned for Data Centres Australia by AirTrunk, Amazon Web Services, CDC, and NEXTDC". It gives $3.1bn invested in grid infrastructure 2020-2025 and $7.2bn forecast to 2030, "This includes $1.1 billion of excess capacity", and $500m of excess before. Its source line: "Data from select data centre operators; Expert interviews; Mandala analysis."
- 2026-10-22: $3.1bn plus $7.2bn is $10.3bn. The report states "$10.3 billion" once, for something else: "the Australian Government has committed $10.3 billion of funding for vital grid electricity infrastructure to 2030."
- 2026-10-22: the networks' sworn evidence. Ausgrid, 29 May: "in New South Wales, the customer who caused network costs pays for them." Endeavour Energy: bank guarantees for the life of the asset. Transgrid, 22 May: it does not invest "until we have that full commitment", and had no data centre commitment yet (metric 221). None gave a total.

## Refused or restricted

## Leads (not facts)
- A search summary noticed the $3.1bn plus $7.2bn arithmetic before the report was re-read; the report itself now establishes it.

## Outcome
The $10.3bn is the operators' own past and forecast spending, as they reported it to a consultant they paid. The principle (causer pays) is confirmed by the networks; the quantum is untested against capital plans.
