# RG-040: What councils say, and what happens to it

Question: Collect every council submission on data centre SSD applications in Blacktown, Penrith, Fairfield, Ryde and Hills Shire, and code the objection grounds.
Status: in_progress, as at 2026-10-30. Batch 5 of docs/RESEARCH-PLAN.md (story line C1).

## Log
- 2026-10-22: from the archived project nodes, 29 data centre projects (modifications aside) are in the five councils. The attachment slugs named a main council submission for 22; 7 have none in their list (several are still at the SEARs stage). Each was resolved through the portal's JSON and fetched one at a time with the project's user agent and a 3-second pause (script in the session's scratchpad; same calls as `scrapers/fetch_ssd_documents.py`). One download timed out and was retried.
- 2026-10-22: 18 read, 4 restricted (Honeman Close SSD-58601963, Macquarie Park SSD-10467, Mamre Road SSD-92743706, NEXTDC S4 SSD-63741210; `data/raw/nsw_planning/council_submissions/restricted.json`). Two of the 18 are scans (Marsden Park, Roberts Road), read on 2026-10-30 by Tesseract 5.3.4 OCR. Personal contact details redacted from the text.
- 2026-10-22: positions as the letters state them. Blacktown: "we object" on Eastern Creek expansion, Huntingwood, Echidna, Marsden Park and Glendenning; Station Road's later letter withdraws its objection; Atlas is a pre-lodgement request for information. Ryde: "Council strongly objects" to NEXTDC S5; "not supportive of the development in its current form" on Project Apollo; comments only on Khartoum Road, Julius Avenue and Road 1, whose detail is in attachments not fetched. Penrith and Fairfield raise issues without a headline objection in the letters read. The Hills (Brookhollow) raises engineering issues.
- 2026-10-22: KEYWORD SCREEN, not a coding: of the 18 texts, stormwater or flooding comes up in 10, building height, bulk or visual impact in 10, trees in 10, parking in 9, water supply in 7, energy in 6, traffic in 6, contributions in 5, and urban heat, jobs, noise and diesel generators in 4 each. Cover letters raise nothing because their substance is in attachments. A human coding is still needed.
- 2026-10-30: what became of the objections. Every assessment report archived for a determined NSW data centre (22) was read for its consent-authority section. The local council objected in eight cases:
  - Eastern Creek expansion (SSD-21342738): received outside the exhibition period.
  - Station Road (SSD-33781208): withdrawn 6 October 2022.
  - Huntingwood (SSD-41589232): out of time, then withdrawn 10 August 2023.
  - Echidna (SSD-47320208): withdrawn 19 March 2024.
  - Marsden Park (SSD-70889211): withdrawn 9 September 2025.
  - Glendenning (SSD-73761707): "resolved".
  - Talavera Road (SSD-24299707): stood; the Independent Planning Commission decided.
  - Project Apollo (SSD-74069708): "Council maintained its objection"; an acting Director approved it under delegation.
  Project Pluto's objection came from a neighbouring council, which the delegation does not count.
- 2026-10-30: the delegations, as the reports quote them. 9 March 2020 and 9 March 2022: a departmental officer could decide only where the council had not objected. 18 August 2026, "for relevant resource, energy or data centre projects": fewer than 15 public objections and no political disclosure statement; nothing about the council. The NSW Data Centre Policy Framework came out the day before. The planning portal's own page (last updated 9 April 2026, `data/raw/nsw_planning/portal_rules/`, sha256 `4bbe3982`) still says the Commission "is generally the consent authority" where "the local council has lodged an objection".
- 2026-10-30: corrected community_events row 15 and regulatory_events row 13: Glendenning's objection was resolved before the decision, not overridden.
