# RG-061: does the "no ratchet" finding hold?

Question: for each determined NSW data centre consent, compare the conditions against what the
proponent's own EIS committed to, and identify any case where a condition is **more** demanding
than the EIS commitment.
Status: **answered for the renewable-energy obligation, 2026-11-15 — the "no ratchet" finding is
falsified.** Still open for every other performance measure (see Limits).
**Author accepted 2026-11-15; loaded** by `scripts/curate_rg061_rg099.py` →
`data/packs/rg061_rg099_decisions.json`. The gap stays `in_progress` with the answer, the
corrections and the limits in its notes, and four source quotes attached (the consents bulk
harvest and the three assessment reports).

## Answer in one paragraph

Three consents obligate the applicant to match **all** operational electricity demand — IT and
non-IT — to "a portfolio of **additional**, firmed renewable energy supply **at all times**",
contracted through a NSW-based PPA plus a firming agreement inside the NEM, demonstrated to the
Planning Secretary's satisfaction **before operation may commence**. All three were determined
between 23 July and 14 September 2026. None of the 16 earlier determinations in the archive carries
that obligation. In each of the three, the assessment report records the applicant offering
something materially weaker — a 2040 aspiration, an existing certificate-backed purchase, or
658 MWh/yr of rooftop solar against 467,924 t CO₂-e/yr — and the Department then recommending the
at-all-times condition anyway. The obligation is therefore **Department-imposed, not
proponent-offered**, and it appeared in mid-2026.

## The condition, verbatim

Glendenning `SSD-73761707`, condition **B20** (instrument p9, `8a756325`):

> B20. Prior to the commencement of operation of the development, the Applicant must demonstrate,
> to the satisfaction of the Planning Secretary, that it has, with regard to applicable government
> policy:
> (a) offset all greenhouse gas generation associated with the supply of electricity to the
> development (including both non-IT and IT loads) by demonstrating that all operational
> electricity demand is matched with a portfolio of additional, firmed renewable energy supply at
> all times;
> (b) entered into a Power Purchase Agreement(s) and firming agreement for NSW-based supply within
> the National Electricity Market to meet the requirements of (a); and
> (c) put in place a framework and management principles for accommodating any shortfalls in the
> availability of renewable energy that may occur from time to time, including through demand
> flexibility and firming.

Apollo **B22** (`2176ca5b`) and Pluto **B19** (`a93fca48`) are word-for-word the same except that
they read "in accordance with applicable NSW government policy" where Glendenning reads "with
regard to applicable government policy". Apollo **B23** / Pluto **B20** / Glendenning **B21** add
written evidence within six months of commencing construction that the PPA and firming process has
started, and a written update every six months until finalised.

Glendenning **B22** (`8a756325`) is the audit spine: a Sustainability Management Plan, approved
before operation, forming part of the OEMP, with "direct and continuous measurement" of IT
electricity, total electricity and total water; annual PUE, WUE and CO₂-e reporting; alignment with
"the greenhouse gas mitigation hierarchy, as referenced in the NSW Guide for Large Emitters
(EPA, 2025)"; prioritising "recycled or non-potable water for non-potable functions (including
cooling), where available"; a review of compliance with B20; and revision of mitigation measures
**every three years**. B23 bars operation until the plan is approved.

## Origin: what was offered, and what was imposed

All three assessment reports are archived under `data/raw/nsw_planning/documents/`. Page numbers
below are the `[[page N]]` markers in the extracted text; the printed folio is given where the
report shows one.

**Glendenning — `SSD-73761707_assessment.txt` (`801090e5`, 79 pages).**
p55 records the applicant's ESD Report: up to 455 kW of solar; **220,622 t CO₂-e/yr** at full
operation, a 25.3% reduction against a 3-star NABERS benchmark; PUE target **1.17**; ~**49 ML** of
potable water a year. And the offer itself:

> The ESD report additionally noted that further measures to reduce emissions, including the use of
> **100% renewable energy by 2040** will effectively reduce the total emissions profile of the
> development to zero.

p56 is the Department's response:

> To further minimise GHG emissions to the greatest practicable extent, the Department recommends
> conditions requiring that, prior to the commencement of operation of the development, the
> Applicant offset all GHG emissions associated with operational electricity demand for the data
> centre with firmed renewable energy supply at all times including the entering into of Power
> Purchase Agreement(s) (PPA) and firming agreements for NSW based supply, supported by ongoing
> emissions reporting and periodic auditing.

An aspiration for 2040 became an obligation before first operation, with additionality, firming,
NSW geography and six-monthly evidence attached.

**Apollo — `SSD-74069708_assessment.txt` (`e0395d4f`, 93 pages).**
p33, the EPA's advice: operational emissions "estimated to exceed 25,000 tonnes CO₂-e per annum",
and the EPA "recommended consideration of energy efficiency and renewable energy measures, with
any commitments to be secured through conditions".
p59 (printed folio 52), the applicant's answer and the Department's finding:

> In response, the Applicant advised that it **has been purchasing 100% renewable electricity** for
> electricity under their operational control.
> […] However, the Department considers that emissions outcomes remain **materially dependent on
> the successful securing and performance of renewable energy procurement**. In the absence of such
> arrangements, the development would be subject to higher location-based emissions for an extended
> period.

The same paragraph then recommends the at-all-times firmed PPA condition. An existing
certificate-backed purchase — annual, unbundled, not additional — was held insufficient.

**Pluto — `SSD-69223466_assessment.txt` (`de1de0c4`, 71 pages).**
p27, the EPA's advice, which went no further than certificates:

> recommended that energy efficiency practices and purchasing of **renewable energy certificates**
> be undertaken to reduce Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions.

p50 (printed folio 43), the applicant's ESD Report: high-efficiency water-cooled chillers, variable
speed cooling towers, LED lighting and a rooftop **500 kW** solar PV system; **467,924 t CO₂-e/yr**,
a 19.4% reduction against an identical-IT-load reference building; the PV "will offset 658 MWh/year
and save 540 tonnes CO₂-e annually"; PUE target **1.4**.
p52 (printed folio 45) imposes the same at-all-times firmed NSW PPA condition as the other two.

Arithmetic on the report's own two figures, offered here as a derivation and not as a quoted fact:
540 ÷ 467,924 ≈ **0.12%**. The renewable abatement the applicant offered covers about a thousandth
of its stated annual emissions; the condition covers all of them.

## What this does and does not settle

- **Falsified:** "conditions never exceed what the proponent offered". Three consents do, on the
  single largest operational impact a data centre has, and the assessment reports show the
  Department writing the requirement after reading a weaker offer.
- **Also established:** the numeric targets stay proponent-set. PUE 1.17 (Glendenning) and 1.4
  (Pluto) are the applicants' own design targets; the conditions require *reporting* against them,
  not achieving a Department-set number. The ratchet is on the **energy obligation** — timing,
  additionality, firming, geography, auditing — not on the efficiency numbers.
- **Limits.** The archive holds no EIS for any determined project (all 8 archived EISs belong to
  undetermined projects), so the comparison for the other 16 projects runs through the assessment
  report's summary of the ESD Report, not the EIS itself. Nothing here tests air quality, noise,
  water or traffic conditions for a ratchet; RG-061 stays `in_progress` for those. The three
  firmed consents are also the three largest recent approvals, so "the Department ratchets" and
  "the Department ratchets on the biggest projects" cannot yet be separated.
- **Coverage hole, 2026-11-16.** Lanceley Place, Artarmon (`SSD-66777221`) was **approved on
  2025-04-11** and its instrument is not in the archive; its portal record carries 145 attachments
  and none of the document kinds the fetcher archives. It is a twentieth determined project the
  scan cannot speak to. Augusta Street (`SSD-10469`) is withdrawn, so it is out of scope.
- **Not read:** the NSW Guide for Large Emitters (EPA, 2025) that Glendenning B22(c)(iv) cites, and
  any policy instrument that would explain the mid-2026 change. Neither is in the archive. The
  timing is observed, its cause is not established.
