Report Context

1.1

Background and Proposed Development

MCG Consult has been engaged to prepare a Transport Impact Assessment (TIA) for the proposed Western
Downs Digital Park at Lot 125, 1933 Dalby-Kogan Road, Kogan.
The proposal includes:
•

an ultimate capacity of 1,440 MW, delivered in 4 x 360 MW stages.

•

a workers’ village accommodating 1,500 workers, together with associated retail, recreational/social,
medical and other day-to-day support facilities.

1.2

Scope of Study and Assessed Scenario

This TIA has been prepared as a master plan development assessment of the proposed data centre precinct.
The purpose is to assess its transport impacts and identify the key access, road network, heavy vehicle and
construction traffic measures required to support the project.
The assessment considers:
•

the existing transport environment, including surrounding road hierarchy, traffic volumes, heavy
vehicle access, flooding and crash history

•

the master plan development, including Stage 1a to Stage 2a data centre operations, Stage 2b
construction activity, the Workers’ Village and supporting power infrastructure

•

the proposed site access strategy, proposed intersection treatments and sight distance requirements

•

combined operational and construction traffic generation, parking and transport hub requirements for
the assessed development case

•

construction traffic generation, heavy vehicle access, OSOM logistics and cumulative construction
traffic considerations

•

recommended next-stage investigations and approvals required to support detailed design and
delivery.

The traffic impact assessment considerations are set out below:
•

MW) are open and operational while Stage 2b construction is ongoing.

•

4.3

POTABLE AND NON-POTABLE WATER DEMAND
There is no reticulated (town) water network in the vicinity of the site, the nearest
reticulated water infrastructure being approximately 15 km away. The development will
therefore be serviced by an independent on-site water supply. This is a well-established
servicing arrangement for developments of this nature in the region, and a number of
practical and proven supply options are available to meet the development’s water
requirements for both the construction and operational phases.
Water demand arises from three sources: domestic potable demand (drinking, showering,
hand basins and kitchen facilities); domestic non-potable demand (toilet and urinal
flushing, washdown, general cleaning and landscape irrigation); and mechanical demand
associated with the one-time commissioning (flushing and fill) of the cooling systems.
Demand is highest during construction, when the workers’ village accommodates a
construction workforce of approximately 1,500 people, and reduces substantially to a
modest permanent demand once construction is complete and the village is removed.
Project water demand is primarily associated with personnel onsite. There is no anticipated
continuous water demand for equipment cooling; however, water will be required for the
initial filling and commissioning of the closed-loop cooling systems as completed data halls
are progressively commissioned. These requirements are considered separately in
Section 3.5.
Other potential demands, including site irrigation, have not been included as potable water
demands on the basis that treated recycled water is expected to be available from the
wastewater treatment process.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION

PROJECT DESCRIPTION

The Project will consist of two stages (Stage 1 and Stage 2), each comprising two data halls
(Figure 2-2). Five (5) 2.2MW diesel generators will be installed in each data hall, totaling 20
generators with a combined generation capacity of 44 MW. The diesel generators are proposed
to provide backup power for network rooms and lift safety services only. Diesel will be stored
on-site to facilitate operation of each backup generator for up to 24 hours.
The precinct masterplan build out also involves the development of an associated workers’
village to the southwest of the data halls. This introduces air sensitive receptors within the
project boundary.

2.2.1

AIR EMISSIONS DURING CONSTRUCTION

Dust and particulate matter are the primary pollutants emitted during the construction phase
for both Stages 1 and 2 of the Project. Construction activities that have the potential to
generate dust include the demolition of existing buildings, bulk earthworks, construction of site
infrastructure, on-site concrete batching and transport of raw materials to site.
Minor exhaust emissions from temporary diesel-powered generators used to support
construction activities and the workers' village may also occur during the construction phase.
These emissions are expected to be temporary and localised.
In addition, odour emissions may occur if on-site feedlot effluent ponds are decommissioned,
particularly where residual wastewater, sludge or organic material is disturbed.

2.2.2

AIR EMISSIONS DURING OPERATIONS

Operation of the diesel generators is the key operational activity likely to result in air
emissions. Key air pollutants emitted from diesel generators include carbon monoxide (CO),
nitrogen oxides (NOx), particulate matter (PM10 and PM2.5), sulphur dioxide (SO2) and total
volatile organic compounds (TVOCs). Of these, nitrogen dioxide (NO2) and PM2.5 are typically
the most significant relative to air quality criteria.
Generators would operate on a limited basis for testing. Testing will be conducted on a
quarterly basis, requiring a total of 50 operational hours per year for testing across all four
data halls.
In the event of a worst-case emergency operating scenario involving a catastrophic failure of
the 330 kV substation, generators adjacent to all four buildings would be required to operate
concurrently. Under this scenario, the total installed emergency generation capacity would
comprise 44 MW. The duration of such an event is unknown and will depend on how quickly
normal power can be restored to the site.
While the frequency or likelihood of this scenario is not known, it is possible that such an
occurrence may happen. However, it is understood that the electricity supply to the site is
expected to be highly reliable, supported by high-voltage grid connections and a dedicated
330 kV substation. Three ancillary BESS will be installed to provide short-term backup power
in the event of a grid outage. In the unlikely event of grid outage, the ancillary BESS will
provide the initial instantaneous back-up power supply, with the emergency generators only
powering the network rooms & lift safety services.
