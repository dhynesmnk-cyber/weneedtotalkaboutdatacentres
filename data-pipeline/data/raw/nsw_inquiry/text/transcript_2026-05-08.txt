BRONWYN CUMBO
:
Chair and members of the Committee, thank you for the opportunity to appear
today and for initiating this important inquiry into data centres. I think we can all agree that data centres are
necessary infrastructure. They enable so many digital and AI servi
ces that our modern society has become reliantupon, and they will enable many future innovations that we cannot yet imagine. To me, this is not about whether
we should or should not have data centres; this is about how we have data centres. How can we sha
pe what is aglobal industry and a globally networked infrastructure in a way that aligns with and supports the things that
matter to the Australian people and our environments?

Sydney's data centre industry, as it looks today, has not been shaped by intentional design; it has been
shaped by default. We have all been caught out by the number of data centre applications, the pace at which the
industry has entered the market and clustered across our industrial lands, and their increasing size and resource
intensity. This inquiry provides a valuable opportunity to pause and consider what the industry could look like if
it was designed around the values and priorities of the Australian

people
things like having access to affordablehousing; having water and energy security; living in a thriving, climate
-
resilient community; keeping ourenvironments healthy and biodiverse; and maintaining a healthy economy and diverse employment opportunities.
Here, we have the opportunity to interrogate how the industry is intersecting with these core priorities and how it
can be designed to support us to meet them and minimise any cost to them.

I put forward six recommendations in my submission that reflect some of my thinking on this issue. It is
based on research I've been carrying out for the last year and a half through the University of Technology Sydney.
These six recommendations call for more strategic, inclusive and long
-
term approaches to how we sight, assessand govern this industry. They centre on three core issues: Firstly, integrating data centres thoughtfully into our
urban and regional fabric, so they align with and actively support

other key priorities; secondly, updating how weassess data centres, so they're recognised as the transformative infrastructure they are and so the cumulative
impacts of clusters on communities and environments are properly captured; and, thirdly, bringing local
communities meaningfully into the process, because right now the people living alongside these facilities have
very little influence, but they're the ones bearing the costs. I welcome the opportunity to provide evidence.
