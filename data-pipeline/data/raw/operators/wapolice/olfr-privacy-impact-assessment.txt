Title Overt Live Facial Recognition (OLFR) 
Lifecycle Stage Trial proposal 
Vendor NEC 
Overview OLFR is a real -time facial recognition technology that compares 
live camera feeds against alert lists to identify individuals of interest 
to police . It supports proactive policing, enhances operational 
efficiency, and improves public safety. The proposed trial draws on 
UK policing models, principally  from the Metropolitan Police 
Service. 
Purpose To assess the feasibility and effectiveness of OLFR in identifying 
high-risk individuals and support strategic policing objectives in 
Western Australia. 
Objective • Improve identification and location of individuals wanted for 
arrest or subject to legal restrictions 
• Enhance public safety and operational efficiency 
• Reduce reliance on manual and reactive methods 
Is this replacing or 
improving an existing 
system or process? 
OLFR will improve  and supplement  existing policing and 
intelligence methods. OLFR offers a scalable and timely alternative 
to traditional approaches. 
Agency Benefits Potential Agency benefits include: 
• Real-time alerts and rapid identification 
• Improved enforcement and prevention capabilities 
• Enhanced event security and offender management 
• Efficient resource use and cross-district reach 
Community Benefits Potential Community benefits include: 
• Increased public safety and confidence 
• Faster location of vulnerable individuals 
• Transparent and respectful engagement protocols 
• Reduced risk of harm from high-risk individuals

Consultation How individuals have been informed, consulted and consent 
provided: 
For the OLFR trial, consultation occurred  primarily through 
stakeholder engagement and governance processes. The UK 
model emphasises transparency measures such as public signage 
during deployments and engagement with community groups. WA 
Police Force has adopted similar practices, which include impact 
assessments and stakeholder briefings prior to deployment. 
 
Public availability of scope and objectives: 
The scope and objectives of the OLFR project are documented 
internally and are expected to be made publicly available through 
WA Police Force transparency measures, such as recording and 
reporting of deployment locations, public notifications and signage, 
once the trial progresses beyond initial planning. 
 
Formal processes for appeal or withdrawal: 
As OLFR operates in public spaces for law enforcement purposes, 
individuals cannot opt out of being captured by CCTV if they decide 
to walk through the OLFR zone . Governance frameworks include 
complaint and review mechanisms for individuals who believe they 
have been adversely affected, consistent with W A Police Force 
accountability processes.  
Authorised Users Authorised users include  trained WA Police Force personnel 
responsible for OLFR operations: 
• OLFR operators monitor live feeds 
• Engagement officers adjudicating alerts 
• Supervisor oversight of deployments 
• Technical staff managing system configuration and data 
governance 
Training Requirements All authorised users undergo training before participating in OLFR 
deployments. Training focus is on cover: 
• System functionality and alert adjudication 
• Privacy and security obligations under the Privacy and 
Responsible Information Sharing Act 2024 (PRIS Act  2024) 
and WA Police Force policies 
• Ethical use of AI and bias mitigation 
• Operational protocols for lawful and proportionate engagement

Security and Purpose 
 
Information Security All information collected and processed through OLFR is handled 
in strict accordance with WA Police Force information security 
policies, privacy frameworks, and applicable legislation, including 
the PRIS Act 2024 . Appropriate safeguards, retention protocols, 
and access controls are applied to ensure lawful, proportionate, 
and secure management of personal and sensitive data. 
 
Additional parameters adopted by WA Police Force includes: 
• Biometric data encrypted and deleted immediately if not a 
match (non-matched biometrics not retained) 
• Encrypted alert lists 
• Data stored in Australia 
• Agency’s existing Security Framework 
Permitted Purpose The information collected and processed through OLFR is used 
exclusively for lawful policing and public safety objectives.  
 
OLFR’s purpose is to assist law enforcement functions by 
identifying and locating individuals wanted for arrest, subject to 
court orders, or posing risks to community safety. OLFR also 
enhances community policing by enabling proactive deployments 
in crime hotspots and public events to prevent harm and maintain 
public confidence.

If the project or activity does not contain an AI function or automated decision -making

Assessment are not required to be completed. 
Software Facial Recognition Software . Current engagement with NEC is 
ongoing. If endorsed the technology will be the NEC’s NeoFace Watch 
technology using the NeoFace M40 algorithm. This algorithm is used 
by the Metropolitan Police in an operational policing environment and 
has been assessed  and benchmarked by National Institute of 
Standards and Technology (NIST) and UK National Physical 
Laboratory (NPL). 
Description OLFR detects faces in live CCTV feeds, extracting biometric features. 
These are compared against a n alert list that triggers alerts based on 
similarity scores.  
Decision-making  Generated alerts are reviewed by trained officers before engagement. 
There are no automated decisions or enforcement. Resultant alerts are 
treated as lines of enquiries.  
Performance and 
Fairness 
The OLFR system is monitored through continuous performance 
evaluation and fairness checks to ensure accuracy and compliance 
with ethical standards.  
 
Algorithm calibration includes monitoring date to ID any demographic 
basis.  
 
Post-deployment reviews will assess detection accuracy, false positive 
rates, and operational outcomes, with adjustments made as required.  
 
Independent audits and oversight by governance committees will 
provide additional scrutiny to maintain fairness and transparency.  
(Incorporates UK model methodology) 
Funding  This is a trial/proof of concept. The WA Police Force OLFR project 
requires support, and funding is not yet determined. This will be subject 
of a submission at the appropriate time.

RISK ASSESSMENTS 
 
Information Privacy Principles (IPP) Assessment  
 
See Appendix 1: IPP Risk Assessment Register – OLFR 
Outcome Summary A Privacy Risk Assessment was conducted on th e OLFR project 
across the eleven Information Privacy Principles (IPP) in the PRIS Act 
2024 (Appendix 1). 
 
The highest risk identified was in relation to IPP 4 (Information 
Security), namely the  retention of biometric data beyond legal limits, 
inadequate deletion protocols , unauthorised access  and l ack of 
encryption or audit trails.  
 
The risk assessment determined that all identified risks had adequate 
controls, and after the implementation of  those controls, there was  a 
low residual risk for all categories.  
 
For IPP 6 (Access & Correction), the WA Police Force believes the 
exemption provided in S. 27 of the PRIS Act 2024 is applicable.   
  
For IPP 10 (Automated Decision Making), please refer to the AI Risk 
Assessment (Appendix 2).   
 
The risk assessment also considered that OLFR is in a trial stage that 
will assess the feasibility and effectiveness of the project in supporting 
strategic policing objectives in Western Australia.  
Consequences • Non-compliance and breach of Privacy legislation  
• Legal challenges 
• Loss of public trust and confidence resulting in reputational damage 
• Misuse of personal data 
• Unlawful engagement due to false alerts  
• Perceived surveillance by communities 
• Discriminatory outcomes  
Mitigation Actions  • AI and IPP impact assessments 
• Industry, government and community stakeholder engagement and 
consultation 
• Communication strategy, public awareness  (i.e. signage), Officer 
briefings and industry briefings 
• Recording and reporting of deployment locations 
• System only recognises and matches with individuals on watch list 
• Ongoing deployment reviews and post-deployment evaluations 
• Human adjudication 
• Biometric data encrypted and deleted immediately if not a match, 
with non-matched biometrics not retained 
• Encrypted watchlist 
• WA Police Force existing Security Framework and protocols 
• Governance Committee review and oversight 
• Data stored within Australia

Artificial Intelligence (AI) Assessment 
 
See Appendix 2: AI Risk Assessment Register – OLFR 
Outcome Summary An AI Risk Assessment was conducted on OLFR across the five Ethics 
Principles from the WA Government Artificial Intelligence Assurance 
Framework (see Appendix 2).   
 
The highest risk identified was in  the Transparency category, namely 
due to  insufficient public awareness, limited stakeholder  and 
community engagement, and the absence of feedback mechanisms to 
capture effectiveness.  
 
The risk assessment determined that all categories had excellent 
controls, and after the implementation of the proposed controls, there 
was a low residual risk for all categories except Transparency which 
has a moderate residual risk. 
 
The risk assessment also considered that OLFR is in a trial stage that 
will assess the feasibility and effectiveness of the project in supporting 
strategic policing objectives in Western Australia. 
Consequences • Loss of public trust and confidence resulting in reputational damage 
and negative community 
• Community resistance 
• Poor management of industry and government consequences 
• Legal and policy non-compliance 
• Deployment beyond approved scope 
• Police operations compromised 
• Improper deployment 
• Failure to comply with policing standards and processes 
• System downtime or inaccurate alerts 
Mitigation Actions  • Communication strategy, public awareness (i.e signage), officer 
briefings and industry briefings 
• Industry, government and community stakeholder engagement 
• Governance Committee review and oversight 
• Proposed pre-deployment impact assessments 
• Ongoing deployment review and post-deployment evaluations 
• Human adjudication and verification with supervisor oversight 
• Agency's existing discipline processes 
• Comprehensive training covering ethical use, privacy and data 
protection 
• Superintendent level determination of suitably trained staff, 
including supervisors and action plans 
• Strict retention and deletion policies in line with WA Police Force 
data ownership and legislation 
• Biometric data encrypted and deleted immediately if not a match. 
Non matched biometrics not retained 
• WA Police Force Security Framework and protocols
