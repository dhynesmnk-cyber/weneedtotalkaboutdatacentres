NEXTDC to Join OpenAI in Australia as an Infrastructure Partner
TL;DR : NEXTDC Limited (ASX: NXT) (“NEXTDC” or “the Company”) refers to its announcement of earlier today and confirms that it has agreed a Memorandum of Understanding (“MoU”) with OpenAI, enabling Australia to become a regional infrastructure partner under the Open AI for Countries program.
This partnership will initially focus on the planned S7 hyperscale campus in Sydney, designed to support high-density AI workloads, large-scale GPU deployments, and secure data processing for enterprise, government, research, and critical industries.
National Infrastructure Partnership
Through the MoU, OpenAI and NEXTDC will collaborate on the planning, development and operation of a
next-generation hyperscale AI campus and large-scale GPU supercluster at NEXTDC’s S7 site in Eastern
Creek, Sydney.
This campus will provide secure, sovereign AI infrastructure , supporting sensitive and mission-critical workloads across enterprise, financial services, Government, defence, education, healthcare, research and the broader technology ecosystem. OpenAI is already working with leading enterprises across Australia, including Commonwealth Bank, Canva, Atlassian, Virgin Australia, Coles, UNSW and LaTrobe University. It recognises Australia's strengths in data centre infrastructure, and status as a Five Eyes nation.
S7 Hyperscale Campus
The first phase will focus on NEXTDC’s S7 hyperscale campus , making it one of the most advanced sovereign AI campuses in the Asia-Pacific region. It is engineered as a sovereign AI facility with security, resilience and operational standards aligned to Australia’s SOCI framework.
The S7 site will incorporate next-generation engineering features including closed-loop high-density liquid
cooling for ultra-high-density GPU clusters. These systems will not require ongoing potable drinking water for cooling operations. These engineering choices position S7 at the forefront of sustainable, sovereign digital infrastructure in the Asia-Pacific region and ensure the facility is ready for the next generation of liquid-cooled, high-density AI architectures.
Subject to securing necessary approvals, the first phase of the S7 project is expected to be delivered in the
second half of calendar 2027.
