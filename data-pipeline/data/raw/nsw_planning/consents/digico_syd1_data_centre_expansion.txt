LIMITS OF CONSENT 
Lapsing 
A5. This consent lapses five years after the date from which it operates, unless the development has physically 
commenced on the land to which the consent applies before that date. 
Back-up Generator System 
A6. The Applicant must ensure, at full operation of the development,  the back-up generators installed on site do not 
exceed a total installed generating capacity of 140.4 megawatts.   
A7. The Applicant must ensure: 
(a) operation of the back -up generators (including testing , warm-up and cool -down) does not exceed 200 hours 
per year;  
(b) no more than two (2) generators are tested at any one time; and 
(c) the diesel fuel storage capacity of the site does not exceed 2,000 tonnes at any one time. 
Note:  For the purposes of condition A7, calculation of how many hours the back-up generators are tested, as a collective, should 
be based on the hours (in real time) that testing is in fact undertaken at the site per year. For example, five generators being 
tested concurrently over the course of an hour would count as one hour towards the threshold stipulated by condition A7 
rather than five hours.  
A8. This development consent does not permit the use of the back-up generators:  
(a) for the purpose of generating electricity to be distributed by the National Electricity Market; or  
(b) to support load curtailment at the site.

Ecologically 
Sustainable 
Development  
Appendix 9 – 
Ecologically 
Sustainable 
Development Report 
Design: 
In accordance with the Proposal’s sustainability principles, the development has been designed to pursue the following 
energy and water initiatives: 
• 80%+ increase in power efficiency relative to the IT energy consumption; 
• PUE target of 1.27 annual average uplifting from current 2.15 for existing facility; 
• 30%+ reduction in water consumption relative to the IT energy consumption; and 
• Improved WUE to a target 1.9 annual average from the current 2.74 for the existing facility. 
Electrification: 
 
 
100% of the development’s energy demand is to be met using electricity. Doing so will ensure the development is future-
proof as the electrical grid greens, embedding resilience. Using electricity over fossil-fuels for operations on site also increases 
overall efficiency of the development as wasted energy through the conversion of energy is minimized. This also algins with 
the Government’s goal of net zero emissions by 2050. 
Net Zero: 
In addition to the energy efficiency and on-site renewable energy initiatives being pursued for the development, a plan for 
pursuit of Net Zero emissions in operations is being considered by the Proponent where cost effective. The plan for 
consideration includes initiatives such as: 
• Procurement of non -fossil fuel-based diesel fuel such as bio -diesel for some or all of the fuel stored on -site for 
emergency backup power. This would lead to a reduction in emissions from the diesel fuel in the order of 75%; and 
• Procurement of renewable electricity for data hall infrastructure power. Consideration would be to phase in stepped 
percentages of renewable electricity supply over several years with the outlook of using 100% renewable electricity 
for data hall infrastructure by 2040, considering market conditions. 
On-site Solar PV: 
On-site PV is being pursued for the design maximised to the available roof space, coordinating with the roof area allocated 
to other plant. The project will incorporate a solar photovoltaic (PV) system with a minimum rated capacity of 100 kWe and 
aspirational target of 125 kWe subject to the availability of roof space to be finalised during detailed design. At the minimum 
100kWe capacity on-site solar PV pursued, generated energy is estimated to exceed 125,000 kWh/yr that will be 100% utilised 
by the development.  
Lighting: 
The proposed development will use robust, long-life LED lighting throughout the building for all new luminaires, to reduce 
power demand and energy consumption. Occupant responsive controls will be utilised to ensure lighting is only engaged 
when required by occupants and dimmed or switched off when unoccupied to further reduce energy consumption. The long 
life of LEDs also reduces the need for replacement, which limits waste and its associated impact on the environment.  
Energy Metering: 
Energy meters at the building level, and sub-meters on major plant elements, will be provided to enable ongoing tracking of 
energy consumption and identification of targeted energy management strategies to reduce consumption. 
Backup Power - Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS) System:  
Utilisation of static UPS (battery type) for new backup power in lieu of rotary flywheel style backup. Battery UPS provides a 
significant reduction and associated energy consumption of standby losses in comparison to rotary units. 
Passive Cooling and Heating Design: 
 
 
• Continuous Roof and External Wall Insulation: Passive design elements in the building envelope reduce the 
cooling and heating requirement, however this doesn’t have any significant effect on the cooling and heating 
demand of the data halls where internal heat gains from equipment dominate. In these in stances, excessive 
insulation can even be counterproductive.  
The development will maximise the use of continuous insulation where appropriate to avoid the reduction of the 
thermal performance of the insulation through thermal bridging, whereby heat is gained through structural 
members. 
• High-performance Glazing Systems: Although glazed elements within the development are minor, where utilised 
consideration will be given to high -performance glazing systems. Low whole system U -Values will reduce the 
amount of heat conduction through the window, including the glass and the frames, while low SHGC will reduce 
unwanted solar heat gain.  
• Plant Screening from Solar Exposure: Rooftop plant and mechanical equipment will primarily be screened from 
solar exposure within the designed structure and architectural form, limiting heat gain on equipment and ductwork. 
Mechanical Services: 
• Elevated Data Hall Conditions and Direct to Chip Cooling: Significant improvements to mechanical system energy 
are supported through relaxed data hall air temperature and humidity control setpoints, in combination with direct 
to chip cooling fluid loops for improved heat exchange and utilisation of free cooling. Direct to chip cooling allows 
for the elevation of data hall conditions, reducing overall mechanical plant loads and energy consumption.  
• Chiller Plant: The new chiller plant which produces cooling chilled water will be of very high efficiency, gained 
through current technology and by operating at elevated chilled water temperatures. Providing direct to chip 
cooling and elevated hall temperatures allows th e chilled water temperature to reset upwards, optimising 
compressor efficiency and reducing subsequent energy consumption.  
• Waterside Free Cooling: Cooling will be provided direct from the cooling towers bypassing the chiller plant to 
further improve efficiency as ambient conditions allow. The combination of the elevated hall conditions and direct 
to chip cooling increases the annual operating time. The use of waterside free cooling further reduces energy 
consumption. 
Water Minimisation: 
• Mechanical Plant Water Consumption: The elevated hall conditions and direct chip cooling reduces the load 
density handled by the mechanical plant relative to IT load density, reducing the relative loads handled by the 
cooling towers. Combined with modern water efficient cooling tower technol ogy, this leads to reductions in 
associated water consumption for the design. 
• Rainwater Capture and Reuse: Rainwater capture and reuse is being considered within the design to provide an 
offset for operational water consumption. Due to the multi-storey design and high load density across the floors, 
water consumption will be high, relative to the potential offset from available rainwater, notwithstanding whether 
all water which falls on the site is able to be captured.  
 
 
• Low-Irrigation Demand Plant Selection: Plants with low or zero ongoing irrigation demands are being considered 
for the development to reduce watering requirements. The potential for plants that require low or nil ongoing 
irrigation will contribute significantly towards water usage reduction. 
• Strategic Water Metering: Building-level and sub -metering will be provided to monitor and record the water 
consumption of the overall development, as well as irrigation systems, water fixtures and water use for the 
mechanical systems. This will enable ongoing tracking for identific ation of targeted strategies to minimise 
operational water consumption. 
Waste Minimisation:  
• Construction and Demolition Waste Reduction: Construction and demolition waste will be reduced by diverting 
waste from landfill. The Proposal will identify the potential waste streams and their expected quantities, collection 
and diversion methods for each waste stream, roles and responsibilities, an d the waste haulers to be engaged. 
Targets will be set for waste during this phase with ongoing monitoring throughout construction to ensure Proposal 
is on track.  
Operational waste management: Provision of facilities for the separate collection of identified operational waste streams 
will be made to aid waste recycling of appropriate streams. Sufficient space for the required bins will be provided including 
a storage area that is easily accessed by facility operators and council waste collection.

Unexpected finds protocol – If unexpected finds of historical or archaeological nature are discovered during any work, 
work within 5 m of the find must cease and the following steps taken:

encountered during the activity, the following procedure will be followed immediately upon discovery:  
• all work in the immediate vicinity will cease and the find will be immediately reported to the work supervisor who 
will advise the Environment Manager or other nominated senior staff member; 
• the Environment Manager or other nominated senior staff member will promptly notify the police (as required for 
all human remains discoveries); 
• the Environment Manager or other nominated senior staff member will contact Heritage NSW for advice on 
identification of the human remains; 
• if it is determined that the human remains are Aboriginal ancestral remains, the Local Aboriginal Land Council will 
be contacted, and consultative arrangements will be made to discuss ongoing care of the remains; and 
if it is determined that the human remains are not Aboriginal ancestral remains, the coroner will be contacted by the police 
and further investigation will be conducted to determine if the remains represent a historical grave or if police involvement 
is required.

INCIDENT REPORT REQUIREMENTS 
5. If requested by the Planning Secretary, w ithin 30 days of the date on which the incident occurred or as otherwise 
agreed to by the Planning Secretary, the Applicant must provide the Planning Secretary and any relevant public 
authorities (as determined by the Planning Secretary) with a detailed report on the incident addressing all requirements 
below, and such further reports as may be requested. 
6. The Incident Report must include: 
(a) a summary of the incident; 
(b) outcomes of an incident investigation, including identification of the cause of the incident; 
(c) details of the corrective and preventative actions that have been, or will be, implemented to address the incident 
and prevent recurrence; and 
(d) details of any communication with other stakeholders regarding the incident.
