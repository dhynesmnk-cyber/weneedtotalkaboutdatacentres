Executive summary 
Cundall has been commissioned to prepare an Air Quality Impact Assessment for the proposed datacentre located 
at 3 Davis Road, Wetherill Park, NSW. The proposed development comprises two three storey datacentre (DC)  
buildings, a two storey Tape Storage building, a high voltage substation (HVSS) and associated infrastructure and 
amenities. Detailed atmospheric dispersion modelling has been carried out to assess the impact of the proposed 
on-site generators. 
This report details the methodology and findings of the detailed dispersion modelling of emissions associated with 
the proposed on-site diesel generators. The existing air quality conditions are presented, and the likely air quality 
impacts from the proposed on-site diesel generators associated with the Proposed Development are considered. 
The effects are assessed in the context of relevant national, regional and local air quality l egislation, polices and 
guidance.  
The DC is a mission critical facility and therefore requires back up power generation. The design indicates that the 
two DC buildings will require a total of 64 No. 2.8 MW generators and two 800 kW generator. One 1.12 MW 
generator will also be required for the Tape Storage building. These generators are to be diesel fuelled. While in 
use diesel generators produce a range of pollutants including nitrogen oxide (NO x), carbon monoxide (CO), 
particulate matter (PM), sulphur dioxide (SO2), unburned hydrocarbons (HC) and volatile organic compounds 
(VOCs). There is therefore potential for adverse impacts on nearby sensitive receptors associated with the 
pollutants emitting from the flues of the diesel generators. Detailed dispersion modelling of the diesel generators is 
therefore required.  
Machinery used during demolition, earthworks and construction can generate new sources of emissions, as well as 
traffic movements to/from the site and the works themselves. When assessing the effect of dust emissions 
generated during construction works, receptors include those nearest to the construction boundary of the site in 
each direction. These receptors have the potential to experience effects of greater magnitude due to emissi ons of 
dust generated by the works, when compared with more distant receptors. The nearest high sensitivity receptors 
are approximately 1 km from the site. There are however workplace receptors within 50 m (the industrial sites in the 
immediate surrounding area) which would be classified as medium sensitivity. As there are sensitive human 
receptors within 350 m of the site, a construction dust risk assessment has been conducted. Emissions of 
construction dust have been assessed using the qualitative approach outlined in the IAQM guidance. It was 
concluded that in the absence of any adequate mitigation, there is a High risk from the demolition, a Medium risk 
from earthworks, construction, and trackout dust-generating activities associated with the Proposed Development. 
It is assumed that dust management measures to control the generation and spread of dust would be outlined in a 
Construction Environmental Management Plan (CEMP), to be implemented during the construction phase. With 
appropriate mitigation in place, it is considered that the residual effects on dust soiling and human health from the 
construction phase activities would be reduced and not significant. 
The following assessment scenarios were undertaken to determine the potential impact under the ant icipated 
operational conditions of the standby generators. 
▪ Scenario 1: A justified worst-case scenario of all generators in operation (100% load) under a loss of mains 
power situation.  
▪ Scenario 2: Realistic operations e.g. regular generator testing where generators are tested  in planned 
maintenance hours. 
The results for both scenarios show that the predicted cumulative concentrations for CO, SO2, benzene and PAHs 
averaged to 1 hour are significantly below the relevant threshold criteria.  
The results show that the predicted cumulative concentrations for PM10 and PM2.5 averaged to 24 hours exceed the 
relevant threshold criteria (50 µg/m3 and 25 µg/m3, respectively). The cumulative concentrations include the 
maximum 24-hour PM10 and PM2.5 concentrations recorded at Prospect monitoring site in 2017. At 61.1 µg/m3 and 
30.1 µg/m3, respectively, these measured background concentrations prior to addition of incremental contributions

all 67 generators be required to be operational at one time, an event never before experienced on the electricity 
network would occur, and the probability of that event would be significantly smaller than 0.001.  In addition, the 
SAIDI target includes all connections to the Endeavour energy grid, including remote areas of NSW. Based on the 
location of the Site in Wetherill Park and the on-site 122 kV-32 kV substation, the realistic prediction of entire site 
downtime is far less than this. 
The findings of the assessment have demonstrated that operation of the generators associated with the required

1.0 Introduction 
Cundall has been commissioned to prepare an air quality impact assessment Report for the proposed Client (The 
Proponent’s) datacentre located at 3 Davis Road, Wetherill Park, NSW. The proposed development comprises two 
three storey datacentre (DC) buildings, a two storey Tape Storage building, a high voltage substation (HVSS) and 
associated infrastructure and amenities. 
This report details the methodology and findings of the detailed dispersion modelling of emission s associated with 
the proposed on-site diesel generators. The existing air quality conditions are presented and the likely air quality 
impacts from the proposed on-site diesel generators associated with the Proposed Development are considered. 
The effects are assessed in the context of relevant national, regional and local air quality legislation, polices and 
guidance.  
1.1 Purpose of this Assessment  
This air quality impact assessment outlines an assessment of operational air quality impacts of the Client (The 
Proponent’s) Proposed Development located at 3 Davis Road, Wetherill Park, NSW in response to the industry -
specific Secretary’s Environmental Assessment Requirements (SEARs). 
The report outlines the relevant air quality legislation and policy guidance and presents the assessment 
methodology. The existing air quality conditions are presented and the likely air quality impacts from the operation 
of the Proposed Development are considered. The effects are assessed in the context of relevant air quality 
legislation, polices and guidance. Mitigation measures are proposed, as required, to reduce the effect of the 
Proposed Development to negligible, as far as reasonably practicable. The scope of the assessment is detailed 
further in section 5.0. 
1.2 SEARs relevant to this Assessment 
In accordance with the requirements of New South Wales (NSW) Government, all development applications (DAs) 
for State Significant Development (SSD) have to go through a comprehensive assessment process3. The proposed 
development is classified as State Significant Development (SSD) under the provisions of the State Environmental 
Planning Policy (Planning Systems) 20214 and is therefore subject to the industry-specific Secretary’s 
Environmental Assessment Requirements (SEARs). 
Section 11 of the Data Storage Centres SEARs indicates the assessment requirements for Air Quality, which is 
shown in Table 1-11. The scope of this assessment is also listed addressing the requirements specified by SEARs.

1.3 Key assumptions 
The air quality impact assessment is based on the following key assumptions:  
▪ Project schematic design drawings dated 20 September 2023 
▪ Technical specification relating to the generators, as provided by the manufacturers. 
▪ Additional assumptions relating to key parameters for the generators as provided by the design team, such 
as location and height of generator flues and assumed flue diameters.  
▪ Anticipated operational hours of the generators based on proposed testing and maintenance schedule, 
including detail on generator test load, and how many generator would be tested at a given time.  
▪ There will be 101 car parking spaces, which is a net reduction compared to the existing on-site parking 
provision of 382 spaces.  
▪ The nearest high sensitivity receptors are approximately 1 km from the site  . There are however workplace 
receptors within 50 m (the industrial sites in the immediate surrounding area) which would be clas sified as 
medium sensitive. 
▪ There are also no ecologically sensitive sites within 50m of the site boundary or the within 50m of the route 
used by construction vehicles, up to 250m from the site entrance. The nearest ecological site is Lower 
Prospect Canal Reserve, located approximately 1km north-east from the site boundary. 
▪ A construction dust impact assessment has been undertaken in accordance with Institute of Air Quality 
Management (IAQM) guidance. It was concluded that without appropriate mitigation measures, High risks 
are posed by demolition and Medium risks for earthworks, construction and trackout activities. However, 
with appropriate mitigation measures implemented, it was concluded that the construction effects of the 
Proposed Development would be not significant. It is assumed that appropriate dust management 
measures to control the generation and spread of dust would be outlined in a Construction Environmental 
Management Plan (CEMP), to be implemented during the construction phase.

2.1 The Site 
The subject site is located within the Fairfield Local Government Area (LGA) and is zoned E4 General Industrial, pursuant 
to the Fairfield Local Environmental Plan 2013 (FLEP2013). A data centre is permissible with consent in the E4 General 
Industrial zone under State Environmental Planning Policy ( Transport and Infrastructure) 2021 (Transport and 
Infrastructure SEPP). The subject site is identified as 3 Davis Road, Wetherill Park, legally described as Lot 1 DP864615. 
Existing attributes of the subject site are noted as follows: 
▪ The subject site exhibits an area of 105,400m2 and has a frontage of 360m to Davis Road.  
▪ In its existing state, the subject site comprises the Reservoir Distribution Centre which is used for distribution 
purposes. The Reservoir Distribution Centre comprises two units which are leased by separate tenants. The 
existing development has 44,599m2 of GFA and provides 382 car parking spaces.  
▪ A number of existing mature trees are located along the site’s boundaries and two bus stops are located along 
the site’s frontage to Davis Road which is serviced by route 812 running between Blacktown and Fairfield via the 
Prospect Highway. 
▪ A stormwater drainage canal and associated 22.86m wide drainage easement (DP 249417) runs along the 
western boundary of the subject site.  
▪ An existing transmission tower is located within the site’s setback to Davis Road, refer to Figure 2-3. A 30.48m 
wide easement for electricity transmission purposes (J259850) is located along the sites frontage and TransGrid 
has entered into a restrictive covenant agreement for a further 15 meters to the side of this easement. 
▪ The immediate site context exhibits an industrial character, being an established industrial precinct identified as 
Smithfield-Wetherill Park Industrial Estate. The surrounding area p redominantly comprises warehousing and 
waste transfer facilities.

2.4 Identification of Potential Emissions Sources 
2.4.1 Construction Phase 
The site currently consists of a large warehouse which will need to be demolished to enable redevelopment. 
The development proposals are comprised of the following activities: 
▪ Demolition activities (existing warehouse buildings) 
▪ Construction of two three-storey DC buildings, Tape Storage building and associated car parking and 
infrastructure.  
Machinery used during demolition, earthworks and construction can generate new sources of emissions, as well as 
traffic movements to/from the site and the works themselves.  
When assessing the effect of dust emissions generated during construction works, receptors include those nearest 
to the construction boundary of the site in each direction. These receptors have the potential to experience effects 
of greater magnitude due to emissions of dust generated by the works, when compared with more distant 
receptors. 
Without appropriate mitigation controls in place, there is the potential for adverse effects to occur during  the 
construction of the Proposed Development. The implementation of best practice mitigation controls can ensure any 
potential adverse effects would be not significant. 
2.4.2 Operational Phase 
2.4.2.1 Road Traffic Emissions 
The Proposed Development has the potential to impact existing air quality as a result of pollutants from road traffic 
exhaust emissions, such as NO2, PM10 and PM2.5, associated with traffic travelling to and from the development during 
the operation phase. 
Sustainable Transport Surveys Pty Ltd have produced a draft Green Travel Plan8. The Green Travel Plan reports that 
car parking is proposed to be provided across the site and will include the site adjacent to each data centre office area 
(along the north-south internal road between the two data centres) and a larger parking area adjacent to the sallyport. A 
total of 101 parking spaces are proposed. Compared to the existing on-site parking provision of 382 parking spaces, 
there will be a net reduction in car parking spaces.  
2.4.2.2 Generator Emissions 
Any on-site combustion plant has the potential to impact air quality receptors both on and off-site.  
The DC is a mission critical facility and therefore requires back up power generation. The design indicates that the two 
DCs buildings will require a total of 64 No. 2.8 MW generators and two 800 kW generator. One 1.12 MW generator will 
also be required for the Tape Storage building.  
These generators are to be diesel fuelled. While in use diesel generators produce a range of pollutants including 
nitrogen oxide (NOx), carbon monoxide (CO), particulate matter (PM), sulphur dioxide (SO2), unburned hydrocarbons 
(HC) and volatile organic compounds (VOCs). There is therefore potential for adverse impacts on nearby sensitive 
receptors associated with the pollutants emitting from the flues of the diesel generators. Detailed dispersion modelling of 
the diesel generators is therefore required.

2.4.3.2 Generator Process Conditions  
Technical specifications have been provided for the type of units that are proposed to be installed at the Proposed 
Development. These included the Cummins C3500 D5e 3MW engine, the Caterpillar Cat 3516E engine and the MTU 
20V4000G94F engine. Emissions calculations have been carried out based on the parameters listed in the technical 
specification for each of the generators. At 100% load, emission rates for the Cummins C3500 D5e are predicted to be 
higher than the other proposed generators. At 10% load, emission rates for the MTU 20V4000G94F are predicted to be 
higher than the other proposed generators. Process conditions have therefore been determined based on provision of 
the Cummins C3500 D5e at 100% load and MTU 20V4000G94F at 10% load as worst case, based on the load 
requirement during each scenario as outlined in section 2.4.3.4.  
For the 800 kW and 1.12 MW generators, process conditions were determined based on use of the MTU 979 kW (MTU 
16V2000G76F) and MTU 1,575 kW (MTU 12V4000G74F), respectively, in the absence of any other manufacturer 
specifications. 
Emissions from the diesel generators would include NOx/NO2, PM10 and PM2.5, CO, SO2, hydrocarbons (HC). Due to the 
limited availability of emissions data and the fact that virtually 100% of particulates emitted from diesel combustion are 
<1 µm in diameter, it is anticipated that all particulate matter (PM10 and PM2.5) would to be present as PM2.5. To enable 
comparison against relevant background concentrations and impact assessment criteria, emissions for both PM10 and 
PM2.5 have been assumed to be equivalent. Emission rates for unburned hydrocarbons (HC) have been calculated 
based on availability of emission data from the technical specifications. HC are expected to include a range of 
hydrocarbons, including benzene and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), for which impact assessment criteria 
are listed in Table 3-2 of this report.  
For the main generators (3 MW), emission rates for NOx, PM10, PM2.5, CO, SO2, HC have been calculated for two 
different loads (100% and 10% or 25%) based on the process conditions listed in the engine technical specification. The 
parameters included in the dispersion model are provided in Table 2-22. For the 3 MW units, worst case values have 
been modelled, which correspond to the Cummins C3500 D5e for 100% load and the MTU 20V4000G94F for 10% load. 
The 25% load for Cummins C3500 and 100% for MTU 20V4000G94F scenarios have therefore not been modelled, but 
have been provided, in italics, for information purposes.

2.4.3.3 Emergency Generator Operation 
In the unlikely event of a mains power outage, the standby generators would switch on to provide power to each of the 
data centre buildings. It is assumed that all generators would be operating at 100% load in this scenario.  
Endeavour Energy network includes the area of the proposed development. Although it is not possible to determine 
when and for how long emergency generator use would be, the Endeavour Energy Distribution Annual Planning Report9 
provides an average unplanned actual system average interruption duration index (SAIDI) of power outage incident of 48 
minutes (total cumulative events) of unplanned outages per year per customer. This is based on the SAIDI actual 
minutes outages for the period 2020/21 of 48.2 minutes, compared to the SAIDI Target of 60.1 minutes. It is expected 
that any loss of mains power will be quickly resolved, within approximately 48 minutes. It is therefore anticipated that the 
emergency operation of the generators would only be for a short duration. 
The SAIDI target includes all connections to the Endeavour energy grid, including remote areas of NSW. Based on the 
location of the Site in Wetherill Park and the on-site 122 kV - 32 kV substation, the realistic prediction of entire site 
downtime is far less than this. 
2.4.3.4 Generator Routine Testing and Maintenance 
The routine backup power generator maintenance operations for the two DCs are understood to include: 
▪ One generator and one load bank operating at 100% as part of testing routine 
▪ Up to six (6) backup power generators running with no load bank  
Excluding emergency usage, cumulative operational time for all on-site generators may be up to 156.25 hours a year, to 
include hours required for maintenance and testing. It is understood that each generator would be subject to the 
following testing/maintenance regime: 
▪ Fortnightly confidence test: This test would be carried for a total of 55 minutes for the entire site (30 mins for DC01, 
50 mins for DC02, 5 mins for the Tape Storage Building), per fortnight at no load, with a maximum of 6 generators 
running simultaneously. Each group of generators will run for 5 minutes (a maximum of 6 simultaneously in DC01, a 
maximum of 3 simultaneously in DC02), followed by the next group of generators for 5 minutes and so on until all 
groups have been run. As worst case it has been assumed that these test runs will take place consecutively within 
the same hour. Due to limited availability of emissions data for generators operating at no load, a load of 10% has 
been assumed. 
▪ Annual PPM: Each generator would run at full load for 60 minutes every year, with an additional 90 minutes every 3 
years equating to an extra 30 minutes per year, for full load test. Generators would be run individually.  
▪ Circuit breaker maintenance: Each generator would run for 15 minutes every 3 years at 80% load. Due to limited 
availability of emissions data, a load of 100% has been assumed, as worst case. Each generator would run 
individually. 
It is proposed that generator testing will be undertaken during day-time or night-time hours due to the critical nature of 
the facility, to allow the operator operational flexibility. The modelling covers both day-time and night-time since the 
model was not limited to a particular time of the day, and background and meteorological data covers day, evening and 
night-time hours. Full details of the proposed testing/maintenance regime are given in Table 2-33.

4.1 Background Air Quality  
To assess the significance of any new development proposal (in terms of air quality), it is necessary to identify and 
understand the baseline air quality conditions in and around the study area. This provides a reference against 
which any potential changes in air quality can be assessed. Since air quality is predicted to change in the future 
(mainly because of changes to vehicle emissions), the baseline situation is extrapolated forward to the opening 
year. The future baseline scenario is the predicted baseline for the opening year. 
To identify the existing air quality conditions, a review of publicly available information has been undertaken, 
including review of the National Pollution Inventory, the latest monitoring data, and background concentration 
maps. This section presents the results of the review.

4.2 Local Meteorology 
Local meteorological conditions can affect the dispersion of pollutants in the local area. Hourly sequential 
meteorological data is therefore required as an input to the model. Data from Sydney International airport and 
Sydney Bankstown airport meteorological stations from 2017 to 2022 have been obtained for this assessment 
using the NOAA ISD. Sydney Bankstown airport is located 14.5 km south east of the Site and Sydney International 
airport is located approximately 28 km south east of the Site. Sydney International airport (site code:947670-99999) 
and Sydney Bankstown airport (code:947650-99999) are considered to be representative meteorological stations 
for the Site given data availability. Both the location of the Site and Sydney Bankstown airport have no significant 
terrain influence. The meteorological site at Sydney International airport is located close to the coast and therefore 
the wind speed is higher than the average for the NSW region due to over exposure. Therefore, Sydney Bankstown 
airport has been selected for temperature, wind speed and wind direction. This decision is further supported by the 
meteorological station’s proximity to the Site.  
As Sydney International airport is a 1st order meteorological station, cloud cover is recorded. Cloud cover is a 
requirement for AERMOD to run and interpolate other meteorological variables. Due to the absence of cloud cover 
data for Bankstown, cloud cover has been extracted from Sydney International airport and used alongside the 
meteorological variables from Sydney Bankstown airport. Due to the terrain and similar meteorological conditions 
across the region, cloud is assumed to be consistent within the study area. Horsley equestrian centre is a 
meteorological station (site code: 947600-99999) 7 km west of the Site. However, this meteorological station does 
not include cloud cover. In addition, the wind speed data for Horsley equestrian centre is much lower than expected 
for the region and is most probably a result of incorrect site exposure. The variables covered by all three 
meteorological stations come within an acceptable tolerance to validate the use of Sydney Bankstown Airport as 
the data source for the Site.  
As a result of the meteorological data used in the dispersion modelling, a level 2 impact assessment can be 
conducted.  
2017 has been used as the year of site-representative meteorological data. This data has been correlated against 
the following five consecutive years and is summarised below. Overall, all years display similar meteorological 
conditions, and so 2017 has been selected as this correlates with the year of background pollutant data.

5.0 Assessment Methodology  
5.1 Overall Approach  
The overall approach of this air quality impact assessment comprises the following:  
▪ A review of existing conditions at and within the vicinity of the site  
▪ An assessment of the potential to change in air quality conditions as a result of the construction and 
operation of the Proposed Development; and  
▪ The formulation of appropriate mitigation measures, where required, to mitigate any adverse impacts on air 
quality as a result of the construction and/or operation of the Proposed Development.

domain of 5km x 5km area with 50m grid spacing. This method ensures that potential impacts are assessed across the 
entire study area. The receptor grid has been modelled at a height of 1.5m to represent the breathing zone of the 
average adult. The assessment area is shown in Figure 5-11. Modelled grid results were evaluated to establish the 
maximum concentration expected in each receptor group location. It was established that the discrete receptors

5.4.2 Dispersion Model 
Detailed dispersion modelling of nitrogen oxides (NOx), particulate matter (PM10 and PM2.5), carbon monoxide 
(CO), sulphur dioxide (SO2) and hydrocarbons (HC) emissions from operational of the proposed on-site generators 
was used to undertake assessment of exposure at the sensitive receptors in the study area. This has been carried 
out using the latest version of AERMOD. AERMOD is an internationally recognised new generation dispersion 
model which has been accepted in Australia for use in a variety of regulatory applications 11. AERMOD uses 
advanced algorithms to describe the boundary layer structure, turbulence and stability. Modelling with AERMOD 
enables the incorporation of complex terrain and elevated sources, as is required for this assessment.   
5.4.2.1 Meteorological Data 
The NSW Approved Methods Section 4 requires one-year of site-specific meteorological data or site-representative 
meteorological data, to be used for atmospheric dispersion modelling. In accordance with Approved Methods, an 
analysis of site representative meteorological data was undertaken to determine the most representative year for typical 
meteorological conditions. Data from 2017 to 2022 was compared.  Overall, all years display similar meteorological 
conditions.  
Model runs were set up using different a year of meteorological data from 2017 to 2022 to enable a sensitivity test of the 
impact of the predicted results at the discrete receptors when changing this variable. The predicted results at the discrete 
receptors were fairly comparable, but overall using 2017 meteorological data resulted in the highest results. 
Given the similarity of meteorological conditions across the assessed years, the comparable but slightly higher predicted 
concentrations and a comparison of background air quality data across the five years, data for 2017 have been selected 
for inclusion in the dispersion modelling assessment, as this best represents the general trend across the past five years. 
The meteorological data used in this assessment is therefore for the period of 1st January 2017 – 31st December 2017, 
and  coincides with the selected background year of 2017 (refer to Section 4.1).  
5.4.2.2 Terrain  
Terrain information was obtained from NSW Elevation data (2 m resolution) for use within the dispersion model. The site 
around the Proposed Development has terrain that differs in elevation. The gradient of the terrain does not vary 
significantly within the development site and is typically at an elevation of 38m. Raised areas of land, with elevations up 
to a maximum of 118m lie within 500m to the west, north-west, east and north-east. The area with the most significant 
variation in terrain lies to the north-west. The topography of the area is shown in Figure 5-22. 
Surface roughness is a component of surface texture. Air travelling over the surface is affected by the surface 
roughness, rough surfaces would result in higher roughness values than smoother surfaces. Typical surface roughness 
values range from 1.5m (for cities, forests and industrial areas) to 0.0001m (for water or sandy deserts). The future 
setting of the Proposed Development has been considered in the modelling by setting the surface roughness length to 
1.0m. This is the value recommended by the model developers for ’cities and woodland’. A lower surface roughness of 
0.5m has been selected for the Sydney International Airport meteorological station, which is described in the model as 
representative of ‘parkland and open suburbia. Sensitivity testing has been carried out to assess the impact of adjusting 
the surface roughness. Surface roughness of 0.3m, 0.5m and 1.0m were included. The values that have been selected 
produced the highest results at the discrete receptors and their use therefore presents a worst-case approach.  
The Monin-Obukhov length is used to describe the effects of buoyancy on turbulence kinetic energy, particular in the 
lowest atmospheric boundary layer. This relates to the urban heat island effect, and its effects on turbulence due to 
surface topology and the effects from heated and shaded building surfaces. Monin-Obukhov values typically range from 
2m to 10m in rural settings but can be higher in urban area where buildings and traffic results in more heat generation. In 
this assessment, the minimum Monin-Obukhov Length Scale for the Proposed Development and was set to 10 m (the 
recommended model setting for small towns). A value of 10m was also used for the land around Sydney International

5.4.2.3 Buildings 
Buildings can have a significant effect on the dispersion of pollutants from flues, such as the proposed on-site generator 
flues. The presence of tall buildings close to a flue can cause the plume to be entrained in the cavity zone downwind of 
the building. This could result in higher ground concentrations near the flue than would be expected in the absence of 
buildings and can affect the dispersion of pollutants in the atmosphere. The presence of the buildings may induce better 
pollutant mixing and dispersion with ambient air, thereby resulting in lower concentrations further downwind. Based on 
Approved Methods guidance: 
“A point source is wake-affected if stack height is less than or equal to 2.5 times the height of buildings located within a 
distance of 5L (where L is the lesser of the height or width of the building) from each release point.” 
The assessment of the generators has therefore considered the buildings in the vicinity of the proposed flues for the 
generators. The Approved Methods states that the location and dimensions of buildings located within a distance of 5L 
from each release point for buildings with a height greater than 0.4 times the stack height should be included within the 
dispersion model.

5.4.2.5 Atmospheric Chemistry 
NO2 is associated with effects on human health and therefore the air quality standards for the protection of human health 
are based on NO2 rather than total NOx or NO. The model predicts NOx concentrations which comprise nitric oxide (NO) 
and nitrogen dioxide (NO2). NOx is emitted from combustion processes primarily as NO with a small percentage (usually 
<5%) of NO2. The emitted NO reacts with oxidants in the air (mainly ozone) to form secondary NO2. Factors affecting the 
rate of this oxidation occurs include the concentration of oxidants in the air, wind speed and temperature.  
A NOx:NO2 conversion has been applied to the modelled NOx concentrations, in order to determine the impact of the 
NOx emissions on ambient concentrations of NO2. The Approved Methods impact assessment criteria have been set 
for NO2, as this is the pollutant most harmful to human health. An appropriate conversion is therefore required to 
calculate NO2 from modelled NOx concentrations. In accordance with Section 8.1.2 of the Approved Methods, a 
photochemical conversion for the hourly average from NOx to NO2 was determined in line with the US EPA’s 
Ozone Limiting Method (OLM). This assumes an instantaneous and irreversible reaction and can be applied on an 
hourly basis.  
Predicted NOx concentrations have been processed using Equation 1 to determine annual mean nitrogen dioxide (NO2) 
concentrations for comparison with the annual mean NO2 objectives.

MIN   = the minimum of the two quantities within the braces

7.0 Discussion and Conclusions 
7.1 Construction 
The primary aim of the dust risk assessment is to identify the appropriate site-specific mitigation measures that will 
be adopted to ensure there will be no significant effect on local amenity and public health.  
Emissions of construction dust have been assessed using the qualitative approach outlined in the IAQM guidance . 
It was concluded that in the absence of any adequate mitigation, there is a High risk from the demolition, a Medium 
risk from earthworks, construction, and trackout dust-generating activities associated with the Proposed 
Development. 
Full details of mitigation measures are presented in Appendix D and have been selected based on the findings of 
the construction dust assessment (Section 6.1).  
The Client will commit to the implementation of the best practice mitigation measures identifie d above during the 
construction phase of the development. It is anticipated that the generation of dust and harmful pollutants 
emissions from construction site activities will be reduced with the correct implementation of these measures.  
7.2 Operation 
The following assessment scenarios were undertaken to determine the potential impact under the anticipated 
operational conditions of the standby generators. 
▪ Scenario 1: A justified worst-case scenario of all generators in operation (100% load) under a loss of mains 
power situation (refer to section 2.3.3.3). 
▪ Scenario 2: Realistic operations e.g. regular generator testing where generators are tested maintenance hours 
(refer to section 2.3.3.4). 
7.2.1 Scenario 1 
The exposure of sensitive receptors in the vicinity of the Proposed Development associated with scenario 1 has 
been assessed, with the following results: 
▪ The results show that the predicted cumulative concentrations for NO2 averaged to 1 hour exceed the relevant 
threshold criteria (164 µg/m3) for the majority of receptor groups. The cumulative concentrations include the 
maximum 1-hour NO2 concentration recorded at monitoring site Prospect in 2017 which at 112.8 µg/m3 is 
approximately two thirds of the threshold criterion. The maximum incremental concentration of 1,158.34 µg/m3, 
is predicted at industrial receptor group I3.  
▪ The Level 2 assessment identified the number of potential exceedances of the hourly NO2 objective to be zero, 
or negligible when expressed as a percentage of the total year, for the majority of receptors. All but the industrial 
receptor groups had predicted exceedances in less than 1% of the year. Higher numbers of hourly exceedances 
were determined for the industrial receptors, with a maximum of 8,458 hourly exceedances at receptor group I3, 
representing the majority of the year (8,760 hours).  
▪ The results show that the predicted cumulative concentrations for PM10 averaged to 24 hours exceed the 
relevant threshold criteria (50 µg/m3). The cumulative concentrations include the maximum 24-hour PM10 
concentration recorded at monitoring site Prospect in 2017 which at 61.1 µg/m3 exceeds the impact assessment 
criterion. The only incremental concentration above the impact criteria, is industrial receptor group I3 with 
105.81 µg/m3 predicted at this location. 
▪ The Level 2 assessment for PM10 showed that there are not expected to be any additional exceedances of the 
daily PM10 criteria resulting from incremental contributions. 
▪ The results show that the predicted cumulative concentrations for PM2.5 averaged to 24 hours exceed the 
relevant threshold criteria (25 µg/m3). The cumulative concentrations include the maximum 24-hour PM2.5

▪ The Level 2 assessment for PM2.5 showed that, excluding the industrial receptors, there are no additional

In the unlikely event of a mains power outage, it is expected that any loss of mains power will be quickly resolved, within 
approximately 48 minutes as per the SAIDI average. It is therefore anticipated that the emergency operation of the 
generators would only be for a short duration. Although it is not possible to determine when and for how long emergency 
generator use would be, the Endeavour Energy Distribution Annual Planning Report40 provides an average unplanned 
actual system average interruption duration index (SAIDI) of power outage incident of 48 minutes (total cumulative 
events) of unplanned outages per year per customer. This is based on the SAIDI actual minutes outages for the period 
2020/21 of 48.2 minutes, compared to the SAIDI Target of 60.1 minutes. Based on the target outage of 60.1 minutes, 
power interruptions could be expected for just 0.001% of the year. Should all 67 generators be required to be operational 
at one time, an event never before experienced on the electricity network would occur, and the probability of that event 
would be significantly smaller than 0.001. When combined with the likely percentage of the year for which exceedances 
of the impact assessment criteria are anticipated to occur, the probability of exceedances is anticipated to be unlikely. 
The SAIDI target includes all connections to the Endeavour energy grid, including remote areas of NSW. Based on the 
location of the Site in Wetherill Park and the on-site 122kV-32kV substation, the realistic prediction of entire site 
downtime is far less than this.

Mitigation for all sites: Communications High Risk 
1. Develop and implement a stakeholder communications plan that includes community engagement before work 
commences on site. H 
2. Display the name and contact details of person(s) accountable for air quality and dust issues on the site 
boundary. This may be the environment manager/engineer or the site manager. H 
3. Display the head or regional office contact information. H 
4. Develop and implement a Dust Management Plan (DMP), which may include measures to control other 
emissions, approved by the Local Authority. The level of detail will depend on the risk, and should include as a 
minimum the highly recommended measures in this document. The desirable measures should be included as 
appropriate for the site. In London additional measures may be required to ensure compliance with the Mayor of 
London’s guidance. The DMP may include monitoring of dust deposition, dust flux, realtime PM10 continuous 
monitoring and/or visual inspections. 
H 
Site Management 
5. Record all dust and air quality complaints, identify cause(s), take appropriate measures to reduce emissions in a 
timely manner, and record the measures taken. H 
6. Make the complaints log available to the local authority when asked. H 
7. Record any exceptional incidents that cause dust and/or air emissions, either on- or off-site, and the action 
taken to resolve the situation in the log book. H 
8. Hold regular liaison meetings with other high risk construction sites within 500 m of the site boundary, to ensure 
plans are co-ordinated and dust and particulate matter emissions are minimised. It is important to understand the 
interactions of the off-site transport/deliveries which might be using the same strategic road network routes. 
H 
Monitoring 
9. Undertake daily on-site and off-site inspection, where receptors (including roads) are nearby, to monitor dust, 
record inspection results, and make the log available to the local authority when asked. This should include regular 
dust soiling checks of surfaces such as street furniture, cars and window sills within 100 m of site boundary, with 
cleaning to be provided if necessary. 
H 
10. Carry out regular site inspections to monitor compliance with the DMP, record inspection results, and make an 
inspection log available to the local authority when asked. H 
11. Increase the frequency of site inspections by the person accountable for air quality and dust issues on site 
when activities with a high potential to produce dust are being carried out and during prolonged dry or windy 
conditions.
