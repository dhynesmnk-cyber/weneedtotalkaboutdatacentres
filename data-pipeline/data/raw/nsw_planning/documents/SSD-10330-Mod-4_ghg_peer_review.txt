Independent review for a data centre in accordance with the NSW EPA’s Guide for Large Emitters 
 
Zephyr Environmental Pty Ltd (Zephyr) has been engaged by Canberra Data Centres (CDC) to perform an 
independent review of the Greenhouse Gas (GHG) Assessment Report prepared for the proposed modification 
to the Eastern Creek Data Centre (MOD 4), located in Eastern Creek, NSW (Revision 05, SLR Consulting, 16 
July 2026; “the GHG Assessment Report”). 
Zephyr understands that the GHG Assessment Report was prepared as the proposed Eastern Creek 
modification consisting of additional back-up diesel generators is anticipated to exceed the thresholds for large 
emitter classification; namely that the Scope 1 & 2 emissions exceed 25,000 tCO2-e per year, per the NSW 
Guide for Large Emitters (NSW EPA, January 2025). 
The GHG Assessment Report has been prepared by SLR Consulting Australia on behalf of CDC to accompany 
a State Significant Development Application (SSDA) for the Eastern Creek modification.  
This letter serves as the formal submission of the independent expert reviewer’s report, conducted in 
accordance with Section 4.7 of the NSW Guide for Large Emitters. 
1. Independent expert reviewer 
In accordance with Section 4.7 of the NSW Guide for Large Emitters the GHG Assessment Report and 
Mitigation Plan are subject to an independent expert review from an independent and suitably qualified expert 
reviewer.

2. Scope of review 
For projects with Scope 1 and 2 emissions exceeding 100,000 tCO 2-e per year at any time over the operational 
life of the project, mitigation assessments must be verified by an independent expert review.  
If offsets are proposed for a project, the independent expert review must consider whether suitable high -
integrity offsets are likely to be available at the time when it is proposed to acquire offsets.  
Thus, in accordance with Section 4.7 of the NSW Guide for Large Emitters, the independent expert review 
focuses upon: 
 The mitigation assessment 
 The offsets strategy. 
3. Emissions overview 
The proposed Eastern Creek modification is expected to emit over 25,000 tonnes CO2 -e of Scope 1 and 2 
emissions in initial financial years of the project’s operations. Therefore, this project meets the criteria of a large 
emitter and is required by the NSW Guide for Large Emitters to prepare an emissions mitigation strategy.  
The modification to the data centre (under State Significant Development 10330) is expected to involve the 
addition of 24 new diesel generators and 32 new chillers. These construction of this equipment, and associated 
activities, is known as “MOD 4” or “the project”. 
The GHG Assessment Report has examined the project regarding its assessment boundary, emissions 
scenarios and sources, emissions mitigation measures, project assumptions, and reporting obligations and 
goals. The report has examined the whole lifecycle of the project being from 2026 to 2076. Scenarios explored 
in the report include the Business as Usual (BAU), modified business, and project only scenarios to compare 
emissions from Eastern Creek Data Centre without the modification, emissions from previously approved 
activities at Eastern Creek plus the modification, and emissions from the modification alone, respectively.  
As a result of activities carried out under the project, associated emissions would originate from the following 
sources:  
 Combustion of diesel for backup generators (Scope 1)  
 Refrigerant fugitive emissions (Scope 1)  
 Consumption of electricity from the grid (Scope 2) 
 As noted in the GHG Assessment Report, electricity emissions include those from customer IT and 
facility/non-IT consumption. Customer IT electricity falls under Scope 3 emissions using the GHG 
Protocol Corporate Standard. However, for the purposes of the GHG Assessment Report, IT electricity 
has been included under Scope 2 emissions in reference to the EPA’s definition of indirect emissions 
from electricity use.  
 Production and installation of equipment (Scope 3)  
 Minor services associated with the modification (Scope 3)  
 Manufacture and delivery of generators (Scope 3) 
The GHG analysis provided mainly relates to the ‘modified business’ scenario, which is the aggregate of the 
BAU and the project only components. This, in turn is evaluated against an ‘operational throughput’ emissions 
estimate (assumes an estimated average annualised PUE of 1.3) and a ‘maximum capacity’ emissions 
estimate (assuming full load utilisation at 100%).

4. Findings and recommendations 
4.1 Mitigation assessment 
The mitigation strategy by CDC employs measures for Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions for the project. The 
mitigation measures follow an “Energy efficiency strategy hierarchy” set out by CDC (Figure 1). This hierarchy 
begins with reducing energy demand and leaves offsets only for residual emissions, which is consistent with 
the NSW GHG Emissions Mitigation Hierarchy (Figure 2). Avoidance and substitution strategies are not directly 
referenced in the hierarchy figure by CDC; however, associated strategies are disc ussed elsewhere in the 
report (Table 14 and Section 4.4.2).

As a significant avoidance measure, CDC plans to purchase electricity backed by renewable sources. As noted 
above, the GHG Assessment Report includes electricity consumed by the data centre tenant in the emissions 
estimation; therefore, it is our current understanding that Scope 2 emissions by the tenant are also considered 
within the mitigation plan.  
This strategy involves purchasing and surrendering large-scale generation certificates (LGCs) while they are 
available, and Renewable Electricity Guarantee of Origin certificates (REGOs) from 2031 onwards. By 
purchasing LGCs (and subsequently REGOs), CDC expects to achieve a zero-emission factor for electricity 
used by the data centre. There is also reliance on the predicted grid decarbonisation sited in the GHG 
Assessment Report. Assuming renewable energy trends continue as expected, CDC anticipates that t he GHG 
intensity of the grid will continue to decline which will consequently reduce the (unabated) Scope 2 emissions 
associated with the project.  
It is expected that renewable electricity Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) and/or other market-based 
renewable procurement opportunities will be readily available to support both non-IT and IT operational use 
throughout the lifespan of the project. The Renewable Energy Target and LGCs provide an option for 
renewable-backed electricity contracts; however, LGCs will no longer be eligible beyond 2030. The Renewable 
Electricity Guarantee of Origin (REGO) Scheme provides an example of Australian renewable certificates 
(REGOs) that will be eligible beyond 2030 (Clean Energy Regulator, 2026). This is consistent with CDC’s plan 
to purchase REGOs from 2031 onward. It is noted that CDC matched 100% of its electricity used in data 
centres with renewable electricity during financial year 2026.  
As a renewable electricity option, GreenPower products are accredited by the National GreenPower 
Accreditation Program. The GreenPower logo is an independent guarantee that the product comes from 
genuine, government approved renewable energy sources (wind, solar, hydro and landfill gas generation) 
(GreenPower Renewable Energy, 2024). It should be noted that GreenPower intends to adapt to the REGO 
Scheme, should the final legislation and regulations be appropriate for the GreenPower objectives 
(GreenPower, 2023). Based on the continuation of government-approved renewable energy schemes through 
REGOs, and the availability of GreenPower, CDC should therefore have access to renewable electricity for the 
lifespan of the project.  
Annual projections for static Scope 2 emissions do not consider any NSW grid decarbonisation, which will likely 
over-state the Scope 2 GHG emissions in future years. This is supported by the statement (SLR, 2026) “Static 
scope 2 emissions have been calculated based on a constant national grid emission factor for NSW assuming 
no grid greening”. Alternatively, Scope 2 emissions incorporating projected grid decarbonisation have been 
explored and quantified in Table 27 and Table 28 for the modified business sce nario.   
Another aspect of the mitigation strategy is built into the design of Eastern Creek Data Centre. The GHG 
Assessment Report notes that the Eastern Creek Data Centre is designed to have an average Power Usage 
Effectiveness (PUE) of 1.3, which is stated to be 19% lower than the market average PUE of 1.61. Proposed 
generators are also HVO-compatible (Hydrotreated Vegetable Oil-compatible), strengthening the potential of 
renewable diesels implemented for future use. A detailed monitoring system for Eastern Creek Data Centre 
equipment (electricity meters and hydraulic plant) has been set up to help identify excessive energy use and 
points of potential failure to increase energy efficiency. Additionally, regular mainten ance and emission control 
technologies will be adopted for generator efficiency.  
Some other design initiatives that would reduce energy demand for the modification project include  a closed-
loop cooling system, high efficiency water fixtures (low flow), rainwater harvest and reuse, and efficient lighting 
appliances and equipment. These features have already been implemented and are currently operating at the 
data centre site, contributing to ongoing mitigation that also supports MOD 4. CDC also commits to choosing 
suppliers that share its sustainability values, and where possible, focus on using low carbon equipment. It is 
expected that CDC will seek to use reduced carbon alternatives for steel and concrete needed during 
construction for the proposed modification.

The mitigation strategy for the Eastern Creek modification proposal is assessed for feasibility and effectiveness 
in Table 14 of the GHG Assessment Report. Evaluating the feasibility of mitigation measures, helps to prevent 
the suggestion of unachievable actions to meet the requirements of the Guide. For example, alternatives to 
diesel fuel are noted as a mitigation measure, with the consideration that these are not yet readily available. 
Uncertainties are also explored in sections 4.4.1 of the GHG Assessment Report, which discusses the 
possibilities of improved technology, but also financial or regulatory barriers that may develop.  
CDC also has wider sustainability strategies to meet external obligations such as net zero carbon (for Scope 1 
and 2) by 2030, compliance with the Corporations Act 2001 (mandatory climate -related disclosures) and the 
Australian Sustainability Reporting Standard (AASB S2). These wider measures contribute to the emissions 
mitigation strategies set out for the proposed modification project.  
As a last resort, CDC has specified an offset strategy to address residual emissions. Quantifying emissions will 
rely upon accurate reporting under the NGER Scheme. The offset strategy for the Eastern Creek modification is 
further discussed in Section 4.2 of this report. 
4.2 Offset strategy 
The GHG Assessment Reports notes that CDC plans to purchase Australian Carbon Credit Units (ACCUs) 
from 2030 onwards to offset Scope 1 emissions. Associated offsets should mitigate residual emissions that are 
not avoided, reduced, or substituted through CDC’s mitigation hierarchy. Scope 1 emissions from diesel 
combustion and refrigerant leaks have been assessed for avoidance, reduction, and substitution. At the time 
the GHG Assessment Report was provided, there were not feasible diesel fuel or refrigerant s ubstitution 
options for the Eastern Creek modification.  
Australian Carbon Credit Units (ACCUs) are referenced in the NSW Guide for Large Emitters (2025) as an 
example of appropriate offsets and are anticipated to be the default offset product applicable to the Eastern 
Creek modification. NSW-based ACCU projects will be prioritised for offsetting Scope 1 emissions resulting 
from the project. The GHG Assessment Report states that CDC offset 100% of its Scope 1 emissions from 
FY26 using ACCUs.  
The Clean Energy Regulator (CER) provides detailed data on ACCU issuance, cancellations, and project 
registrations. Their Quarterly Carbon Market Reports provide a comprehensive analysis of market trends, 
including information on both offsets supply and demand. 
Evaluation of these data indicates that ACCU issuance has grown steadily since the scheme's inception in 
2011. In recent years, total annual ACCU issuances were recorded as being between 18 -19 million. 
An evaluation of the ACCU market supports that changes in government policy, particularly the reforms to the 
Safeguard Mechanism, have a significant impact on demand and, consequently, supply (via new project 
registration and ACCU generation). 
Both the CER and market analysts (e.g. the Carbon Market Institute) project continued growth in ACCU 
generation. Forecasts suggest issuance could rise to 31 million in 2033, driven by a growing demand for offsets 
from the Safeguard Mechanism and voluntary markets.  
Assuming the Proponent adheres to its commitment to source renewable electricity, this leaves approximately 
1,027 ACCUs needed per annum to offset Scope 1 emissions by the modification project.  
Given the quantum of offsets required, and total anticipated annual ACCU issuance into the future, it is 
considered that there will be sufficient availability high-integrity offsets in any one year of operations. It is also 
noted that ACCUs may be surrendered from multiple vintages (years of issuance). 
In relying on ACCUS, CDC’s offset strategy for the Eastern Creek modification is considered satisfactory.
