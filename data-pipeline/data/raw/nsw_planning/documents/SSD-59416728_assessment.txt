Preface 
This report details the Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure’s  (the Department’s) 
assessment of the State significant development (SSD) application for the Davis Road Da ta Centre 
(the development). 
The Department’s assessment considers all documents submitted by the Applicant, including the 
Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) and Submissions Report, submissions received from the public 
and Fairfield City Council, advice from State government authorities, and all legislation and planning 
instruments relevant to the site and the development. 
The report includes: 
• a description of the development and the surrounding environment 
• an assessment of the development against government policy and statutory requirements, 
including mandatory considerations 
• an explanation of why the development is SSD and who the consent authority is 
• consideration of matters raised by the community and other stakeholders 
• an assessment of the likely environmental, social and economic impacts of the development and 
recommendations for managing any impacts during construction and operation 
• an evaluation which weighs up the likely impacts and benefits of the development, having regard 
to the proposed mitigation  measures, community views  and government advice  and provides a 
view on whether the impacts are, on balance, acceptable 
• a recommendation to the decision- maker, along with the reasons for the recommendation, to 
assist them in making an informed decision about whether development consent for the 
development should be granted and any conditions that should be imposed.

Executive Summary  
Introduction  
Cundall Johnston and Partners Pty Ltd (the Applicant) proposes to construct and operate a data 
centre at 3 Davis Road, Wetherill Park in the Fairfield local government area (LGA).  
The development has a capital investment value of $1,597,338,256 and is expected to generate up to 
556 construction jobs and 72 operational jobs. 
Site Context  
The site is located approximately 28 kilometres (km) west of the Sydney CBD, 9 km south-west of the 
Parramatta CBD and 9.6 km north-west of Liverpool and covers approximately 10.54 hectares (ha) of 
E4 General Industrial  zoned land under the Fairfield Local Environmental Plan (LEP) 2013. The site 
currently features an existing local distribution centre known as the Reservoir Distribution Centre, a 
30.48 metre (m) wide electricity transmission easement located within the front setback of the site  
and a 22.86 m drainage canal easement running north-south along the western boundary of the site.  
Statutory Context  
The development is classified as State significant development (SSD) under Part 4 of the 
Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979  ( E P & A  A c t )  b e c a u s e  i t  i n v o l v e s  c o n s t r u c t i o n  a n d  
operation of a data centre that meets the criteria in Clause 25 of Schedule 1 in State Environmental 
Planning Policy (Planning Systems) 2021 (Planning Systems SEPP). Consequently, the Minister for 
Planning and Public Spaces is the  consent authority for the development under section 4.5(1) of the 
EP&A Act. 
Engagement  
The Department exhibited the development application and accompanying Environmental Impact 
Statement (EIS) for the development from 19 March 2024 until 15 April 2024. During the exhibition 
period, the Department received no submissions from the public, a submission from Fairfield City 
Council, advice from six (6)  government authorities , one state -owned corporation and one utility 
provider.  
Council raise d comments relating to traffic, asset management, stormwater management, 
remediation, biodiversity and landscaping, waste management and contributions. Concerns raised in 
the government advice related to flooding and biodiversity. The Department requested the Applicant 
address the matters raised in submissions and government authority advice in a Submissions Report. 
The Department also raised issues regarding noise and air quality impacts, the design, bulk and scale 
of the development, site access, biodiversity, ESD and dangerous goods storage.

accordance with the EPA’s Noise Policy for Industry (NPfI).  
The NVIA predicted the noise levels of two operating scenarios being a standard operating scenario

with the NPfI. Further, the Department considers the mitigatio n measures identified in the acoustic 
report to be adequate in reducing the potential noise emissions of the development’s operations.  
The Department has recommended conditions of consent limiting noise emissions of the development 
at receiver locations and requiring the Applicant to prepare and submit a noise verification report. 
With these conditions in place, the Department’s assessment concludes the development would not

Air Quality 
Pollutant emissions from the operation of emergency back -up generators during testing or critical 
power failure scenarios may impact on sensitive receivers if not carefully controlled. The Applicant

provided an air quality impact assessment (AQIA) that predicted the pollutant emissions of the 
development under both a standard operating scenario and a worst -case critical power failure 
scenario.  
The Applicant provided an AQIA prepared in accordance with the EPA’s Approved Methods which 
modelled the predicted emission concentrations from key pollutants such as nitrogen dioxide (NO 2) 
and particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10), for both operating scenarios.  
The AQIA predicted cumulative exceedances of the NO2 pollutant criterion (164 micrograms per cubic 
metre (µg/m3)) over a 1-hour averaging period at all but two residential receiver catchment areas (R4 
and R6), all but one commercial receiver (C3) and all nearby industrial receivers.  The AQIA also 
predicted multiple exceedances of the PM 2.5 and PM10 criterion over the 24 -hour averaging period 
which were  attributed to existing elevated background levels in the vicinity of the site. All other 
pollutants assessed against the worst- case operating scenario demonstrated compliance with the 
relevant pollutant criteria. 
The Applicant provided an updated AQIA in support of the Submissions Report which included a Level 
2 assessment to evaluate the occurrence of hourly exceedances of NO2 at each receiver location. The 
AQIA predicted that a maximum of one hour of exceedance is likely to occur at each industrial receiver 
location, equating to a percentage likelihood of less than 1% throughout the year.  Furthermore, the 
development is predicted to comply with the annual averaging NO 2 criterion at all receiver locations 
under both the 10% and 100% load testing. 
The predicted emission concentrations during the worst-case scenario were predicted to significantly 
exceed the relevant impact assessment criteria however, the AQIA justified that any critical power 
failure event is deemed to be highly unlikely and would have minimal duration.  
The Department’s assessment considered the Applicant has sufficiently demonstrated that proposed 
standard operations of the development will have an acceptable incremental and cumulative impact 
on air quality, and that the likelihood of a critical power outage event is highly unlikely.  
The Department recommended conditions of consent requiring the preparation and implementation 
of an Air Quality Management Plan which will include procedures for annual emissions testing and 
reporting, the reporting of emergency back-up generators operation subsequent to any power outage 
event and a power outage notification protocol. The Department’s assessment concluded the 
development would not result in unacceptable air quality impacts on surrounding receivers subject to 
the implementation of the recommended conditions and management measures.

Conclusion  
The Department’s assessment concluded the impacts of the development can be mitigated and/or 
managed to ensure an acceptable level of environmental performance, subject to the recommended 
conditions of consent. 
Subject to the implementation of the proposed mitigation measures and the recommended conditions 
of consent: 
• the development is not expected to have adverse noise or air quality impacts on the locality and 
sensitive receivers in relation to the construction and operation of the development  
• impacts on visual amenity are considered to be acceptable given the industrial nature of the site.  
Consequently, the Department considers the development is in the public interest and is 
recommended for approval, subject to conditions.

1.5 Project Sector Description   
Data centres are places which are used by organisations to store their electronic computer 
applications and data. This could include personal data (such as a Facebook account or Instagram 
photos), corporate data (such as a business’s payroll system) or sensitive government data (such as 
tax and Medicare records).  
In recent years, there has been an increased need for new large and small-scale date centres across 
the State. As new technologies  (such as artificial intelligence)  and electronic services and systems 
become commonplace, a significant number of new data centres will be required to store the volume 
of electronic data accessed and created by Australians.  
The Applicant is a global multi-disciplinary consulting engineer and design firm with experience in a

2.3 Process Description  
The primary purpose of the development is for the collection, storage, processing and distribution of 
electronic data in associated IT hardware by cloud and content providers or government entities (the 
tenant/s).  
The data centre operator will fit out the development’s data halls with its own computer systems, 
server systems and networking equipment. This IT hardware would be stored in racks designed to 
maximise the efficiency of the space. The data centre operator will then lease storage space within 
these systems to its tenant/s.  
The development would be cooled used a system of AHUs located on each floor of the data centre. 
The cooling system has been designed to maintain a constant temperature within the data halls 
throughout the year. The ancillary office space would primarily be used by on-site technicians, who 
would provide IT support to external users and undertake regular maintenance as necessary.  
Data centres also require sophisticated energy back-up systems to ensure the data centre operator’s 
customers are always able to access their data. To this end, the development includes a system of 
lithium-ion batteries and diesel back -up generators designed to minimise downtime during a power 
outage event.

predicted to occur at Receiver I3 , comprising an incremental contribution of 96.29 µg/m3 
(approximately 59% of the hourly criterion ). The AQIA additionally noted that this exceedance was 
significantly influenced by  elevated background levels of NO 2 in the vicinity of the site, being 
approximately 69% of the hourly criterion.  
Given the predicted incremental exceedances, t he updated AQIA also u n d e r t o o k  a  L e v e l  2  
assessment to evaluate the occurrence of hourly exceedances of NO 2 at each receiver location s 
based on the predicted incremental concentrations of the 10% load testing and hourly background 
concentrations throughout the year. The Level 2 assessment predicted that a maximum of one hour 
of exceedance is likely to occur at each industrial receiver location , equating to a percentage 
likelihood of less than 1% throughout the year. Furthermore, the development is predicted to comply 
with the annual averaging NO2 criterion at all receiver locations under both the 10% and 100% load 
testing.  
Exceedances have also been identified at all receiver locations for PM2.5 and PM10 under the 24-hour 
averaging periods for both the 10% and 100% load testing. However, predicted incremental 
concentrations have been demonstrated to be minor , and the Applicant highlighted that such  
exceedances are the result of existing elevated background levels already exceeding the relevant 
criterion. Therefore, the development has been considered to have negligible contribution to PM2.5 and 
PM10 pollutant concentrations.  
No exceedances of the assessment criterion for all other relevant pollutants have been predicted at 
all assessed receiver locations. While the  updated AQIA has identified the potential for some 
exceedances of the relevant criteria for NO 2, PM2.5 and PM10 emissions during the 10% load test, the 
Applicant stressed that the modelling was extremely conservative and based on the assumption that 
[[page 38]]
 
  Davis Road Data Centre (SSD-59416728) Assessment Report | 29 
all such tests would be undertaken consecutively within the same hour. In reality, each test would 
only run for 5 minutes and would be spread out over a fortnightly period. The updated AQIA 
subsequently concluded the operation of the development (including generator maintenance testing) 
would not significantly impact on air quality at nearby sensitive receivers.  
The Department has reviewed the updated AQIA and notes cumulative exceedances of the NO2 1-hour 
averaging criterion during the 10% load test have been conservatively predicted at surrounding 
industrial receiver locations. The Department understands that elevated background levels of NO 2 
pollutant concentrations are a significant contributing factor to the predicted cumulative 
exceedances. The Department also understands the Applicant has undertaken an assessment of a 
highly conservative operational scenario and that typical operations would comprise of 5-minute tests 
being appropriately separated to reduce the occurrence of exceedances throughout the year. The 
Department has also considered that exceedances to the PM2.5 and PM10 pollutant concentrations are 
resultant of existing elevated background levels and is satisfied the incremental concentrations 
associated with  the development would make a minor contribution to the overall pollutant 
concentrations.  
The Department considers the development could potentially present significant air quality impacts 
under the conservative ‘worst case ’ operating scenario defined as Scenario 1. However, the 
Department accepts that the likelihood of a critical power outage event requiring the simultaneous 
operation of all 67 emergency back-up generators for a period greater than the 1 hour averaging 
period assessed, would be highly unlikely given the reliability of the surrounding electricity network.  
Nonetheless, the Department considers reducing the number of generators simultaneously tested 
under a 10% load will further reduce the likelihood of the NO2 criteria being exceeded  and has 
subsequently recommended a condition of consent restricting the number of generators tested 
simultaneously at any one time.  
The Department has also recommended conditions of consent requiring the Applicant to prepare and 
implement a site-specific Air Quality Management Plan (AQMP), which will include procedures for 
monitoring pollutants during a power outage event, a Power Outage Notification Protocol, emissions 
control measures and a program to verify the performance of the development against the criteria 
established under the Approved Methods.  
In addition, the Applicant would also be required to undertake annual emissions testing of the back-up 
generator system, and to submit a report to the Planning Secretary should the back-up generators be 
used during a  power outage event . The Department considers that annual emissions testing will 
ensure that maintenance and monitoring of the emergency back -up generators performance are 
adequate and capable of managing pollutant emissions to an acceptable level. Furthermore, reporting 
[[page 39]]
 
  Davis Road Data Centre (SSD-59416728) Assessment Report | 30 
how often the back-up generators are used during a power outage event will provide an opportunity 
to identify whether further mitigation measures are required to be implemented to reduce air quality 
emissions.  
6.2.3  Conclusion  
The Department’s assessment concludes the air emissions associated with the construction of the 
development would be minimal and can be appropriately managed through the preparation and 
implementation of a CEMP in conjunction with all reasonable steps to minimise dust generation.  
In addition, the Department’s assessment concludes the Applicant has sufficiently demonstrated  
through a conservative air quality analysis  that the day -to-day operation  of the development 
(including regular generator testing) will have an acceptable incremental contribution to cumulative 
pollutant concentration levels. The Department considers these emissions can be appropriately 
managed and mitigated through the restrictions placed upon  the testing regime and through the 
implementation of a site-specific AQMP.  
Furthermore, the Department accepts the likelihood of a critical power outage event occurring to be 
highly unlikely and notes that any  associated air quality  impacts can be appropriately managed 
through the recommended conditions of consent . This would include annual  testing of emissions 
performance, pollutant monitoring and the continual implementation of management and mitigation 
measures to further reduce pollutant emissions throughout the life of the development.  
Therefore, the Department is satisfied that , subject to the implementation of the recommended 
conditions and the Applicant’s proposed management and mitigation measures, the construction and 
operation of the development would not result in unacceptable air quality impacts on surrounding 
sensitive receivers.
