Eastern Creek Data Centre Expansion (SSD-21342738) | Assessment Report v 
Executive Summary 
Introduction  
This report details the Department of Planning and Environment’s (the Department ) assessment of a 
State Significant Development (SSD) application for the Eastern Creek  Data Centre Expansion 
Project (SSD- 21342738). NineZero DC Sub TC I Pty Ltd trading as DCI Data Centers (the Applicant) 
is proposing to expand the existing Eastern Creek Data Centre located at 90 Peter Brock Drive,  
Eastern Creek in the Blacktown local government area (LGA). 
The existing data centre was originally constructed and operated by Woolworths Limited (Woolworths) 
in accordance with Project Approval MP 08_ 0108, which was  granted by the then Minister for 
Planning on 15 December 2008, under the former Part 3A of the Environmental Planning and 
Assessment Act 1979 (EP&A Act). The Applicant acquired the site in 2019 and subsequently obtained 
development consent through Blacktown Council for an eight-storey extension of the data centre. 
However, the Applicant has since identified an alternative design for the data centre expansion, which 
is the subject of this development application.  
Site Context 
The site has an area of approximately 4.1 hectares (ha) and is located approximately 30 kilometres 
(km) west of the Sydney central business district (CBD) and 4.4 kilometres (km) south- west of the 
Blacktown CBD. The site is zoned General Industrial  (IN1) under the State Environmental Planning 
Policy (Industry and Employment)  2021 and adjoins Prospect Nature Reserve to the South. The 
Applicant proposes  to expand the development site to encompass four lots: Lot 34 DP  1131779 
(original site), Lots 32 and 33 DP 1140168, and Lot 25 DP 1122038. 
The nearest residential receivers are located approximately 1.16 km to the north-west of the site in 
the residential area of Prospect, within the Blacktown LGA. 
Current Proposal 
The SSD application seeks development consent to construct and operate an expansion to t he 
existing data centre , comprising additional data halls, associated office space, supporting 
infrastructure and landscaping. As part of the proposed development (the development), the Applicant 
has acquired additional landholdings immediately to the west of the existing data centre  site and is 
proposing to expand the data centre facility onto these sites. The Applicant has also acquired an 
additional landholding immediately to the southeast of the data centre site and is proposing to develop 
a zone substation on this site to service the data centre. The Applicant also intends to  surrender the 
existing Council consent relating to the eight-storey data centre expansion and operate under this one 
consent once granted. 
Statutory Context 
The development is classified as State significant development (SSD) pursuant to Part 4 of the EP&A 
Act because it involves the construction and operation of a data centre with a total power 
consumption of more than 10 megawatts, which meets the criteria in clause 25 of Schedule 1 in State 
Environmental Planning Policy (Planning Systems) 2021 as in force at the time. Consequently, the 
Minister for Planning and Public Spaces  is the consent authority for the proposed development under 
section 4.5(1) of the EP&A Act. 
Engagement

The Department exhibited the EIS for the development from Tuesday 11 October  2022 until Monday 7 
November 2022. During the exhibition period, the Department received three public submissions (one 
individual and two businesses/ utility providers) of which one submission objected to the development. 
The Department also received advice from seven government a uthorities, including Blacktown City 
Council (Council).   
Key issues raised in public submissions related to overshadowing, building height, construction traffic, 
hazards and electrical supply.  Concerns raised in the government advice related to cumulative 
impacts, stormwater, landscaping and bushfire risk.  The Department subsequently requested the 
Applicant address the matters raised in submissions and government advice in a Response to 
Submissions (RTS) report.  
The Applicant submitted an RTS in February 2023. The RTS was supported by a number of revised 
technical assessments and plans, including a revised air quality assessment , changes to landscaping 
plans and responses to noise matters raised. The RTS was subsequently reviewed by key agencies 
including Council, Environment and Heritage Group of the Depart ment and the Environment 
Protection Authority . Additional information was also submitted in May and June 2023 to address  
outstanding air quality issues and bushfire risk issues raised by the Department and EHG. 
Assessment 
The Department’s assessment of the application has fully considered all relevant matters under 
section 4.15 of the EP&A Act, the objects of the EP&A Act and the principles of ecologically 
sustainable development. The Department has identified the key issues for assessment are air quality 
and noise impacts b ut has also assessed all other relevant matters, including hazards and risk, 
stormwater and bushfire risk.  
The Department has reviewed the findings of  the revised air quality impact assessment, which has 
considered the potential impacts of the development on both residential and nearby industrial 
receivers. The assessment found that for typical day-to-day operations and back-up generator testing 
scenarios, the development would not exceed the air quality criteria  at the nearest residential 
receivers. However, the development is predicted to exceed the maximum NO 2 concentrations during 
testing of backup generators  at two industrial receiver s. Notwithstanding, the Department is satisfied 
the likelihood of these exceedances occurring is extremely low given the overall duration of the 
proposed testing regime (which would only occur for a few hours  a year) and the low likelihood of a 
power outage event occurring. The Department has recommended conditions requiring the Applicant 
to prepare and implement a Power Outage Notification Protocol and to ensure the installation and 
operation of the back-up generators and/or the associated enclosures are in line with best practice 
The Department reviewed the revised noise impact assessment report and is satisfied that 
construction and operational noise impacts associated with the development can be adequately 
managed by the Applicant. The development is predicted to comply with the operational noise criteria 
at all surrounding sensitive receivers for the worst -case scenario, subject to implementation of 
management and mitigation measures such as ensuring back-up testing only occurs  during daylight 
hours to prevent any night -time disturbances. The Department has recommended several conditions 
including noise limits  and noise verification testing following commencement of operation of the 
development to ensure actual noise levels ar e consistent with the assumptions adopted in the 
acoustic report and the development is  carried out in accordance  with the Applicant’s management 
and mitigation measures.

Summary 
The Department’s assessment concluded the impacts of the development can be mitigated and/or 
managed to e nsure an acceptable level of environmental performance, subject to the recommended 
conditions of consent. In summary, the development would: 
• provide up to 150 construction jobs and 76 additional operational jobs and represents a direct

• help satisfy market demand through the provision of additional, flexible data storage capacity 
within Western Sydney 
• be consistent with the objectives of the relevant strategic planning framework, including the 
Greater Sydney Region Plan –  A Metropolis of Three Cities , the Central City District Plan  and 
Council’s Local Strategic Planning Statement. 
Consequently, the Department considers the development is in the public interest and is 
recommended for approval, subject to conditions.

1 Introduction 
1.1 The Department’s assessment 
This report details the Department of Planning and Environment’s (the Department ’s) assessment of 
the State significant development (SSD) application for the Eastern Creek Data Centre Expansion 
(SSD-21342738). The proposed development (the development) involves an expansion to an existing 
data centre located at 90 Peter Brock Drive, Eastern Creek  in the Blacktown local government area 
(LGA) (see Figure 1).  
The Department’s assessment considers all documentation submitted by NineZero DC Sub TC I Pty 
Ltd (trading as DCI Data Center ) (the Applicant), including the Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) 
and Response to Submissions (R TS), submissions received from the public and advice from 
government agencies. The Department’s assessment also considers the legislation and planning 
instruments relevant to the site and the development. 
This report describes the proposed development, surrounding environment, relevant strategic and 
statutory planning provisions and the issues raised in submissions. The report evaluates the key 
issues associated with the development and provides recommendations for managing any impacts 
during construction and operation. 
1.2 Development background 
The Applicant is a data centre provider specialising in data storage, data connectivity, and business 
continuity solutions acros s the Asia Pacific. In Australia, the Applicant  currently operates the Eastern 
Creek Data Centre (existing data centre) at 90 Peter Brock Drive, Eastern Creek in the Blacktown 
LGA (see Figure 1).

The existing data centre was originally owned by Woolworths Limited (Woolworths) having obtained 
Project Approval in 2008 (MP 08_0108) to construct and operate the original data centre comprising a 
data storage building with three data storage halls,  an emergency power generation building,  chiller 
plant building and ancillary plant and facilities.  
In 2019, the Applicant acquired the existing data centre and subsequently obtained development 
consent from Black town City Council (Council) for an 8- storey expansion to the existing data centre. 
However, this expansion did not proceed, and the Applicant has considered an alternative expansion 
design for the existing data centre, which is the subject of this DA.   
Due to the emergence of new technologies such as driverless cars, faster mobile networks and 
artificial intelligence, there has been increase in the need for new large and small -scale data centres 
across greater Sydney and NSW to store this data. As such, the Applicant is proposing to expand its 
existing data centre (also referred to as SYD03) to accommodate the growing demand for data 
storage.   
The development involves the construction and operation of a new data building adjacent to the 
existing development comprising data storage halls, associated office space, a detached emergency

landscaping. The development would involve the storage of electronic data in associated information 
technology (IT) hardware, and would operate 24 hours a day, seven days a week.  To facilitate 
development, the applicant has acquired three additional lots adjacent to the existing data centre.

2.1 Physical layout and design 
The physical layout and design of the development is shown in Figure 4 to Figure 8 below. The 
proposed expansion presents as a new five-storey building to the west of the existi ng data centre, 
adjoining Raceway Place. The development also includes a new  four-storey office building between 
the existing and proposed data centre buildings. The two buildings would be connected via a  link 
bridge on Level 2.  
The ground and upper -ground floors  of the data centre expansion would primarily contain general 
plant areas and the development’s diesel back -up generators, while levels 1 to 3 would contain data 
halls and plant areas for ancillary equipment. The bulk of the development’s cooling system would be 
located on the roof of  the proposed expansion, along with some mechanical power plant and four lift 
motor rooms.  
The development’s façade would be constructed from off -form concrete and pre -finished cladding 
panels in off -white and grey to provide visual interest. Anodised met al screens would be used to 
create a clear separation between the development’s lower and upper floors, and the external back-
up generator exhaust flues would help to provide further articulation along the building’s Raceway 
Place façade. Similar materials would also be used across the adjoining office building and the new 
zone substation. The colours, materials and finishes proposed to be used across the development  
have been designed to be durable, high quality and low maintenance.  
Most heavy vehicles and all light vehicles accessing the development would enter and exit via a 
two-way driveway off Peter Brock Drive, located in the north- eastern corner of the site (originally 
approved under 08_0108) . A new, minor access driveway for heavy vehicles would also be provided 
off the Raceway Place cul -de-sac, and would directly service the development’s southern loading 
dock. Access to both driveways would be controlled via security intercoms and sliding gates.  
Light vehicles accessing the existing data centre would continue to use the existing car park 
(43 spaces) located in the eastern section of the site. The development would also provide 57 new 
parking spaces across the site, including:  
• 5 spaces to the south-east of the existing car park  
• 23 spaces to the east of the new ancillary office building  
• 29 spaces to the south of the new ancillary office building.   
2.2 Process description 
The primary purpose of the development is for the collection, storage, processing and distribution of 
electronic data in associated IT hardware by cloud and content providers or government entities (the 
tenant/s).  
The Applicant intends to lease space with in the expansion’s data halls to its tenant/s, who will 
undertake the fit -out of this space with their own computer systems, server systems and networking 
equipment. This IT hardware would be stored in racks designed to maximise the efficiency of the 
space.  
The development would be cooled using a system of chillers and air processing units located on the 
roof of the data centre expansion. The cooling system has been designed to maintain a constant 
temperature within the data halls throughout the year. The ancillary office space would be primarily 
[[page 16]]

used by on- site technicians, who would provide IT support to external users and undertake reg ular 
maintenance as necessary.  
Data centres also require sophisticated energy back -up systems to ensure the data centre operator’s 
customers are always able to access their data. To this end, the development includes a system of 
lithium-ion batteries and diesel back -up generators designed to minimise downtime during a power 
outage event. The Applicant may also be directed to operate the back -up generator system by the 
Australian Energy Market Operator (AEMO) to help prevent major blackouts from occurring across the 
state’s electricity network.  
The site would be serviced by a new zone substation, located to the south- east of the existing data 
centre building. This substation would comprise a number of transformers, switchgear, circuit 
breakers and associated plant and equipment to step the incoming 132 kV electricity supply down to 
33 kV, which would then service the overall site. The substation would be connected to Endeavour 
Energy’s adjacent 132 kV high voltage electricity transmission line via two new underground feeder 
cables (being undertaken via a separate approval process under Part 5 of the EP&A Act).  
In the event of a full power outage (all site feeders are taken offline), lithium- ion batteries located in 
IPB units would provide an uninterrupted power supply until the back -up generator system reaches 
full load. Once they have reached full load, 27 diesel back-up generators would be used to provide 
continuous power to the development until power has been restored by Endeavour Energy .  
To ensure the development can function during a power outage event, the Applicant would also 
undertake regular testing of each back -up generator. During such tests, an artificial load would be 
applied to the generators using an electrical switching device. The test scenarios would be as follows:  
• Annual Testing – Up to 6 generators operating at a time on load for up to 6 hours  
• Monthly Testing – Up to 2 generators operating at a time on load for up to 1 hour each.   
All testing would occur between the hours of 7:00 am to 6:00 pm, Monday to Saturday.

2.4 Applicant’s need and justification for the development 
The Applicant has justified the need for the development by highlighting the growing demand for 
secure data storage and cloud-based infrastructure at a regional, state and national level.  
The ongoing impact of the COVID -19 pandemic has radically changed t he way Australians live and 
work and has driven a significant increase in demand for data storage services within the Sydney 
region. The Applicant has highlighted that through the provision of additional data storage capacity 
within western Sydney, the development would help to facilitate the generation of additional advanced 
technology jobs and flow-on economic opportunities for the city’s IT sector.  
The proposed expansion would allow the Applicant to further develop the site as a world class data 
centre, one that is capable of servicing its clients’ needs in an efficient manner while also contributing 
to the amenity and serviceability of the wider Raceway Precinct.  
In addition, the Applicant has advised that the development would:  
• be consistent with the site’s industrial zoning and the strategic direction of the Raceway Precinct  
• generate additional employment opportunities in western Sydney , comprising approximately 
150 construction jobs and up to 76 additional knowledge-intensive operational jobs  
• provide a considered, sustainable design that is sympathetic to the surrounding area.

The proposal is State significant development (SSD) pursuant to section 4.36 of  the EP&A Act

consent on land in an IN1 zone.

A BDAR waiver request was submitted to the Department, on the basis that the likelihood of remnant 
native vegetation communities occurring at the site was extremely low and no threatened ecological 
communities had been previously mapped on-site.  
Section 7.9(2) of the Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016 (BC Act) requires all applications for SSD to 
be accompanied by a BDAR unless the Planning Agency Head and the Environment Agency Head 
determine that the proposed development is not like ly to have any significant impact on biodiversity 
values. 
The Director, Greater Sydney (of the Environment and Heritage Group)  and the Director , Industry 
Assessments (of the Planning Group) , as nominee of the Planning Secretary, subsequently 
determined the development is not likely to have any significant impact on biodiversity values. A 
BDAR waiver under section 7.9(2) of the BC Act was granted for the development on 6 July 2021.

6.1 Air quality  
The construction and operation of the development has the potential to result in air quality impacts to 
surrounding sensitive receivers.  
The primary emissions from the development would include:  
• particulate matter and dust emissions generated during construction works  
• potential exhaust emissions associated with the testing and operation of the development’s 
back-up generator system.  
The EIS included an assessment of potential air quality impacts associated with the construction of 
the development. The E IS was also accompanied by  an operational air quality impact  assessment 
which was prepared in accordance with the Approved Methods for the Modelling and Assessment of 
Air Pollutants in New South Wales (Approved Methods) (EPA, 2017). During the assessment process, 
the Department sought further information from the Applicant regarding the application of the 2022 
version of the Approved Methods, consideration of industrial land uses as sensitive receivers and the 
need to assess  a worst -case ‘generator testing’  scenario. The Applicant subsequently met with the 
Department and prepared three air quality advice letters (the addendum air quality advice) to address 
these outstanding concerns.  
Both the original air quality assessment and the addendum air quality advice provided an assessment 
of potential air quality impacts at representative sensitive receivers surrounding the site (see 
Figure 10 and Figure 11), including a mix of residential and industrial receivers.  
Construction  
During construction, dust and particulate emissions may be generated during demolition, earthworks 
and erection of both the data hall and ancillary office buildings. Construction of the development 
would be carried out concurrently with the operation of the existing data centre and is expected to 
take up to 24 months to complete.  
Council did not raise any concerns in relation to construction air quality impacts.  
The Applicant’s EIS concluded potential dust and particulate emission impacts during construct ion 
would be low, subject to the implementation of standard mitigation measures as part of a Construction 
Environmental Management Plan (CEMP) for the development. These measures would include:  
• minimising the area of disturbance as far as practicable during works  
• minimising drop heights for materials being worked on the site  
• keeping exposed surfaces moist at all times  
• rehabilitating/revegetating disturbed surfaces as soon as practicable

• ensuring that trucks are covered and do not track sediment onto public roads.  
The Department has reviewed the EIS and is satisfied air emissions associated with the construction 
of the development would be minimal and can be appropriately managed through the preparation and 
implementation of a CEMP.  
The Department has subsequently recommended conditions requiring the Applicant prepare and

• Scenario 1 – all 27 back -up generators operating at 50% load, with the existing data centre

• Scenario 2 – 23 back-up generators operating at 75% load and 3 back -up generators operating at

The addendum air quality advice found the development would comply with the 1-hour impact

Conclusion  
The Department’s assessment concludes the air emissions associated with the construction of the 
development would be minimal and will be appropriately managed through the preparation of a CEMP 
and the implementation of all reasonable steps to minimise dust generation.  
With regard to operational impacts, the Department’s assessment concludes  the development would 
not result in adverse air quality impacts at nearby residential receivers and that the likelihood of the 
development exceeding the relevant NO2 criterion at nearby industrial receivers is extremely low.  
The Department has subsequently recommended a number of conditions which would provide a 
robust monitoring and reporting framework for the testing and operation of the back -up generator

6.2 Noise 
The construction and 24- hour operation of the development has the potential to emit noise and 
vibration, which could impact on the acoustic amenity of the surrounding area.  
The development would involve the construction of a two- storey data centre b uilding with ancillary 
office space, a staff parking area and associated plant over a period of approximately 24  months. 
Working hours would align with the recommended standard construction hours identified in the EPA’s 
Interim Construction Noise Guideline (ICNG) (DECC, 2009), and would occur at the following times:  
• Monday to Friday – Between 7:00am and 6:00pm  
• Saturday – Between 8:00am and 1:00pm  
• Sundays and public holidays – No works.   
The development’s main operational noise sources include the rooftop chillers, the outdoor air 
processing units and the back-up diesel generators.  
The EIS included an acoustic report prepared by RWDI Australia, which considered both construction 
and operational noise impacts. During consultation on the EIS, no comments were received from 
[[page 37]]

Council and the public in relation to construction and operational noise. However, the Department 
requested the Applicant provide further information to justify the suitability of the chosen operational 
noise model, particularly given the large separation distance (approximately 1.3 km) between the site 
and the nearest residential receivers to the north-east.  
The Applicant subsequently provided an updated version of the acoustic report to address this.  
Construction  
The Applicant’s acoustic report noted that construction of the development would occur across three 
separate phases, for a period of 24 months:  
• Stage 1 – Earthworks and demolition  
• Stage 2 – Concrete works  
• Stage 3 – Site construction.  
Major plant and equipment to be used during the construction works would include excavators, dozers, 
concrete pumps, piling/auger equipment, cranes, trucks and hand tools.   
The acoustic report found the earthworks and demolition works  would be the noisiest construction 
phase, with equi pment sound power levels of up to 117 dB(A). Based on the Applicant’s modelling, 
these works would comply with the LAeq, 15min ‘standard construction hours’ construction noise 
management level of 70 dB(A) at all nearby industrial receivers (i.e. predicted noise levels at these 
receivers would be 0-2 dB(A) below the criteria of 70 dB(A)). In addition, given the nearest residential 
receivers are located approximately 1.3 km to the north- east of the site, construction works are likely 
to be inaudible at the residential locations (predicted levels of <20 dB(A)).  
To help minimise construction noise impacts for nearby industrial receptors, the Applicant has 
identified several possible management and mitigation measures in the revised acoustic report. Such 
measures would include the preparation and implementation of a Construction Noise and Vibration 
Management Plan (CNVMP) , ongoing consultation with the surrounding community ( including public 
display of contact details for the construction contractor) and implementation of a complaints 
management system.  
With regard to construction risk associated with vibration intensive works, the Applicant noted 
compliance with vibration li mits is likely to be achieved by maintaining safe working distances to 
adjoining properties.  
The Department has considered the information submitted by the Applicant and is satisfied 
construction noise and vibration impacts are acceptable and would be fur ther minimised  via the 
management and mitigation measures proposed by the Applicant.  
To ensure construction noise is appropriately managed in accordance with these  commitments, the 
Department has recommended conditions requiring the preparation and implementation of a CNVMP, 
and to ensure construction works are undertaken in accordance with the standard hours outlined in

7 Evaluation 
The Department’s assessment of the application has fully considered all relevant matters under 
section 4.15 of the EP&A Act, the objects of the EP&A Act and the principles of ESD .  
The Department has considered the development on its merits, taking into consideration strategic 
plans that guide development in the area, the EPIs that apply to the development, advice received 
from the relevant public authorities, including Council, and submissions from the public.  
The development involves the construction of an expansion to an existing data centre, which will help 
to satisfy market demand through the provision of additional, flexible data storage capacity within 
Western Sydney. In the coming years, this additional capacity will be crucial to ensure the ongoing 
data storage needs of greater Sydney and NSW are addressed. 
In addition, the development will provide up to 150 construction jobs and 76  additional operational 
jobs and represents a direct investment of more than $330 million in the Blacktown LGA.  
The Department acknowledges the concerns raised by the local community regarding potential 
overshadowing impacts, traffic and diesel storage . The Department has sought to address any issues 
raised, including those raised in public submissions, through consultation with both the government 
authorities and the Applicant. The Department’s assessment found that the development would not 
result in any overshadowing or traffic impacts, and the proposed volumes  of diesel would not exceed 
the screening thresholds in the Applying SEPP 33 Guideline.  
The Department’s assessment co nsidered air quality and noise to be the key matters for 
consideration. 
The Department has carefully considered the revised  air quality impact assessment and is satisfied 
the Applicant has provided a conservative assessment of the development’s operational air quality 
impacts. While the development is predicted to exceed  the maximum N O2 concentrations during 
testing of backup generators at two industrial receivers, the Department is satisfied the likelihood of 
these exceedances occurring is extremely low given the overall duration of the proposed testing 
regime and the low likelihood of a power outage event occurring.  The Department has recommended 
conditions requiring the Applicant to prepare and implement a Power Outage Notification Protocol and 
to ensure the installation and operation of the back -up generators and/or the associated enclosures 
are in line with best practice 
With regard to noise impacts, the Department is satisfied that  construction and operational noise 
impacts associated with the development can be adequately managed subject to the implementation 
of conditions concerning noise limits , hours of construction and operation and man agement and 
mitigation measures. The development is predicted to comply with the operational noise criteria at all 
surrounding sensitive receivers for the worst-case scenario, subject to implementation of 
management and mitigation measures such as ensuring back up testing only occur s during daylight 
hours. The Department has recommended noise limits  for the residential receivers as well as 
requiring the Applicant to undertake noise verification testing following commencement of operation of 
the development.

Planning Systems SEPP 
The Planning Systems SEPP identifies certain classes of development as SSD. The proposal is State 
significant development pursuant to section 4.36 of Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 
1979 (EP&A Act) because it  involves the construction and operation of an expansion to an existing 
data centre with a total power consumption of more than 10 MW  which meets the criteria in Clause 25 
of Schedule 1 in the Planning Systems  SEPP. The development has a total power consumption of 
around 32 MW, which meets the threshold for SSD under the Planning Systems SEPP.  
State Environmental Planning Policy (Transport and Infrastructure) 20 21 (Transport and 
Infrastructure SEPP) 
Chapter 2 of t he Transport and Infrastructure SEPP aims to facilitate the effective de livery of 
infrastructure across the State by improving regulatory certainty and efficiency, identifying matters to 
be considered in the assessment of  development adjacent to certain types of infrastructure 
development, and providing for consultation with relevant public authorities about certain types of 
development during the assessment process. 
TfNSW and Council did not raise any concerns regarding potential traffic impacts associated with the 
development.  The assessment found that o perational traffic wo uld be low. Further, no road or 
driveway upgrades are needed to facilitate the development. The Department has recommended 
conditions of consent including implementation of a construction traffic management plan  
State Environmental Planning Policy (Resilience and Hazards)  2021 (Resilience and Hazards 
SEPP) 
Chapter 3 of the Resilience and Hazards SEPP  aims to identify developments with the potential for 
significant off -site impacts, in terms of risk  and/or offence. A development is defined as potentially 
hazardous and/or potentially offensive if, without  mitigating measures in place, the development 
would have significant risk and/or adverse impact on off-site receptors. 
Chapter 4 of the Resilience and Hazards SEPP  aims to provide a State- wide approach to the 
remediation of contaminated land. In particular,  it aims to promote the remediation of contamin ated 
land to reduce the risk of harm to human health and the environment by specifying: 
• under what circumstances consent is required 
• the relevant considerations for consent to carry out remediation work 
• the remediation works undertaken meet certain standards and notification requirements.

The Applicant is seeking development consent for the construction and operation of an expansion to 
an existing data centre. While the development would involve the on-site storage of up to 685,000 L of 
diesel fuel at any one time, the Department notes diesel is not classified as a dangerous good under 
the Dangerous Goods Code. The Department has recommended conditions to manage the storage 
and handling of diesel fuel at the site, and to ensure any dangerous goods stored at the site do not 
exceed the screening threshold quantities listed in the Hazardous and Offensive Development 
Application Guidelines – Applying SEPP 33 (DoP, 2011).  
 
Additionally, the development would store up to 122,000 kg of lithium- ion batteries. The Department 
has recommended a condition requiring a FSS be prepared to manage the special hazards 
associated with those back -up power systems which contain large quantities of lithium- ion batteries. 
Consequently, the Department is satisfied the development would not be considered a potentially 
hazardous or potentially offensive industry. 
 
The EIS also included a contamination assessment , which found the site is considered to have low 
risk for potential contamination . The Department has reviewed the EIS and is satisfied the site would  
be suitable for its intended use as a data centre.  
 
The Department’s assessment concludes the development is consistent with the aims of the 
Resilience and Hazards SEPP and would not be considered a potentially hazardous industry under 
clause 3 of this SEPP.
