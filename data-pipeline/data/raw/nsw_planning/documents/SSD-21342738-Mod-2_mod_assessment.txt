Preface 
This assessment report provides a record of the Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure’s 
(the Department’s) assessment and evaluation of NineZero DC Sub TC I Pty Limited’s application to 
modify the State significant development (SSD) consent for the Eastern Creek Data Centre Expansion, 
located in the Blacktown local government area. The report includes: 
• a description of the proposed modification 
• an assessment of the modification against government policy and statutory requirements, 
including mandatory considerations 
• a demonstration of how matters raised by stakeholders have been considered 
• an assessment of the likely environmental, social and economic impacts of the modification 
• an evaluation which weighs up the likely impacts and benefits of the modification, having regard 
to the proposed mitigation measures, and provides a view on whether the impacts are, on 
balance, acceptable 
• a recommendation to the decision -maker, along with the reasons for the recommendation, to 
assist them in making an informed decision about whether the approval should be modified and 
any conditions that should be imposed.

1 Introduction 
The modification application seeks to modify the development consent for the Eastern Creek Data 
Centre Expansion (SSD-21342738-Mod-2) to increase the development’s overall power consumption 
from 32 megawatts (MW) to 63 MW and make corresponding changes to the data centre’s supporting 
plant and equipment, substation and ancillary office space.  
The application was lodged on 23 January 2026 by NineZero DC Sub TC I Pty Limited (the Applicant), 
pursuant to section 4.55(1A) of the Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979 (EP&A Act). 
A more detailed overview of the proposed modification is provided in Section 2 of this report. 
1.1 Background 
The Applicant is undertaking an expansion to an existing data centre located at 90 Peter Brock Drive, 
Eastern Creek in the Blacktown local government area (LGA) (see Figure 1).  
At the time of writing this report, the Applicant has largely completed on-site demolition works and is 
preparing to commence construction of the approved development.

1.2 Subject Site 
The site encompasses an area of approximately 4.1 hectares (ha) of General Industrial (IN1) land zoned 
under the State Environmental Planning Policy (Industry a nd Employment) 2021. The site is legally 
described as Lot 34 in DP 1131779, Lots 32 and 33 in DP 1140168 and Lot 25 in DP 1122038.

Motorway (see Figure 3).

2 Proposed Modification 
2.1 Modification Overview 
The proposed modification is described in full in the modification report included in Appendix A , 
summarised in Table 2 and is illustrated in Figure 4 to Figure 7 below.  
The modification seeks to improve operational efficiency through an increase in the power 
consumption of the development from 32 MW to 63 MW. This change would be achieved through the 
use of greater rack densities within the approved SYD02 building and, consequently, would not 
require significant changes to the design of this building. However, the increase in power demand 
would necessitate associated upgrades to the development's cooling and back-up power systems, as 
well as an increase in the size of the approved on-site substation.  
Additionally, the approved 4-storey office building (between the SYD01 and SYD02 buildings) is to be 
removed, with a corresponding reduction in operational staff numbers.  
The modification originally proposed the installation of a  retaining wall  adjacent to the on-site 
substation, with soil nails and rock anchors encroaching into the Prospect Nature Reserve. Due to the 
concerns raised by the National Parks and Wildlife Service (NPWS) regarding this method of 
construction (see Section 4 below), the Applicant revised the design of the retaining wall to remove 
the need for the encroachment (see Figure 4).

2.2 Applicant’s Justification for the Proposed Modification 
The Applicant advised that since the approval of the SSD application, it has progressed the detailed 
design of the data centre and explored opportunities for further optimisation. This process has 
identified an opportunity to increase the overall power capacity of the data centre while maintaining 
the existing approved footprint for the SYD02 building and minimal changes to the overall site layout.  
The Applicant has highlighted t he proposed modification would help facilitate further data centre 
capacity in western Sydney at an existing, operational facility, while ensuring any potential 
environmental impacts associated with the changes are minimal or improved when compared to the 
approved development.

3.3 Biodiversity Development Assessment Report  
Section 7.17(2) of the Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016 (BC Act) requires all SSD modifications to be 
accompanied by a Biodiversity Development Assessment Report (BDAR) unless the authority or 
person determining the application is satisfied that the modification will not increase the impact on 
biodiversity values (as identified in the BC Act and in the Biodiversity Conservation Regulation 2017).  
The Department is satisfied that the modification will not increase the impact on biodiversity values 
as the proposed changes are largely confirmed to exi sting, disturbed areas previously assessed for 
biodiversity values under the original approval.  
The modification will not require the removal of any additional trees beyond what was considered 
under the original assessment and, consequently, a BDAR is not required to accompany the 
modification application.
