Preface 
This assessment report provides a record of the Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure’s 
(the Department) assessment and evaluation of an application to modify the State significant 
development consent for the Lane Cove West Data Centre  that is located in the Lane Cove  local 
government area. The modification was lodged by Greenbox Architecture Pty Ltd (the Applicant). The 
Department’s assessment report includes: 
• a description of the proposed modification 
• an assessment of the modification against gove rnment policy and statutory requirements, 
including mandatory considerations 
• a demonstration of how matters raised by the community and other stakeholders  have been 
considered 
• an assessment of the likely environmental, social and economic impacts of the modification 
• an evaluation which weighs up the likely impacts and benefits o f the modification, having regard 
to the proposed mitigation  measures, community views and government/expert advice, and 
provides a view on whether the impacts are, on balance, acceptable 
• a recommendation to the decision -maker, along with the reasons for t he recommendation, to 
assist them in making an informed decision about whether the consent should be modified and 
any conditions that should be imposed.

1 Introdu ction 
Greenbox Architecture Pty Ltd (the Applicant)  is constructing a data centre at 1 Sirius Road, Lane 
Cove West in the Lane Cove local government area (LGA) under the State significant development 
consent, SSD -9741, as modified . The modification applica tion seeks  to modify the Lane Cove Data 
Centre to facilitate external, internal and operational design amendments in response to detailed 
design and end user requirements.  
The application was lodged on 14 February 2023 by the Applicant pursuant to section 4.55(2) of the 
Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979 (EP&A Act). 
A more detailed overview of the proposed modification is provided in Section 2. 
1.1 Development Location  
The site is located in the Lane Cove West Business Park, approximately 8 kilometres (km) north-west 
of the Sydney central business district (CBD), 13 km east of the Parramatta CBD and 3.5 km west of 
Chatswood. The site is legally described as Lot 1 DP 1271404 (1 Sirius Road, Lane Cove West) in the 
Lane Cove LGA. The site has an area of approximately 4.2 hectares (ha) (see Figure 1).  
Two patches of the Plant Community Type (PCT) ‘Smooth-barked Apple - Red Bloodwood open forest’ 
are situated in the north-eastern and southern sections of the site. 
The site is surrounded by industrial land uses and natural features (see Figure 2 ), including:  
• industrial and commercial buildings, such as Harley -Davidson Australia and New Zealand 
(1A Sirius Road) and the Lane Cove Tunnel Motorway Control Centre (5 Sirius Road) to the east 
• Lane Cove River, a large area of native vegetation and an Ausgrid -operated transmission line to 
the south and west 
• Stringybark Creek and the former ‘Cumberland Paper Mill’ site (now occupied by S. C. Johnson & 
Son) to the north. 
The nearest sensitive receivers are in ‘Arise Lane Cove’, a residential apartment tower located 
approximately 175 metres (m) to the north of the site (see  Figure 1). Farther af ield also  include 
R2 zoned low density residential uses in East Ryde and near Magdala Park.

2.2 Applicant’s justification for the proposed modification  
The design amendments are driven by the detailed design process and end user requirements, and 
the Applicant’s justification for the modification is summarised as follows: 
• the additional plant gantrie s are required to accommodate the additional electric switch rooms 
and equipment 
• the modified gantry design serves to minimise services reticulation to the data halls to allow for a 
higher level of redundancy, for which redundancy is also supplemented by t he addition of five 
back-up generators 
• the screening of gantries is required to ensure airflow between critical equipment is suf ficiently 
managed 
• the reduction in car parking is required to accommodate additional water tanks in support of future 
operations 
• the reduction in landscaping and additional tree removal are driven by the modification to asset 
protection zones, fence line a nd retaining wall as well as the need for an increase to hardstand 
areas for staging works and ongoing equipment maintenance. 
With these proposed modifications, the Applicant is seeking amendments only to the development 
layout plans in Appendix 1 and mitigation measures in Appendix 3 of the conditions of consent.

The proposed modification, which features an increased number of mechanical plant, elevated gantry

application included an operational noise assessment report which evaluated the predicted

chillers are operated at a fan speed load of no more than 35% and standby generators are tested one

During consultation on the modification application, a member of the public raised concerns regarding 
the validity of the Applicant’s operational noise modelling as constant mechanical plant noise can be 
heard at d istances around 2 km away from the site. Furthermore, the submitter noted that constant 
mechanical plant noise is particularly audible under the presence of north-easterly wind and that this 
noise appears to be the only source of nuisance after 8:00pm when road traffic noise becomes lower.  
To ensure issues raised by the public  are adequately addressed , the Department requested the 
Applicant provide an updated assessment that is un derpinned by  a calibrated and validated 
operational noise model  as per Section 7 of the N PfI given the first phase of the development has

commenced operation. That is, where a development is partly operational , the noise model needs to 
be informed by on -site measurement of noise -generating plant and calibrated with noise 
measurements at sensitive receivers to give accurate noise level prediction. The Department also 
requested additional information from the Applicant to clarify predicted operational noise impacts 
under noise -enhancing weather conditions at the potentially most -affected residential locations 
surrounding the site, including other apartments within the Arise Lane Cove  building and the 
residential area west of Pittwater Road in East Ryde.  
In response, the Applicant provided an updated operational noise assessment that was supplemented 
by noise measurements and peer reviewed by an independent acoustic consultant . The Applicant’s 
updated operational noise assessment found  that measured receiver no ise levels from the current 
operations do not exhibit annoying noise characteristics, and that the risk for strong low frequency 
content or tonality to be present at sensitive receiver locations is negligible. However, measured 
operational noise from the f irst phase of the development (i.e. 25% operational) was found to be  5 
dB(A) higher than previously predicted operational noise from all four phases of the development at 
35% assumed fan speed load . The discrepancy between  measured (i.e . 25% operational) a nd 
predicted (i.e. 100% operational) noise levels was attributed to chillers being operated at a higher fan 
speed load of 60% rather than the assumed load of 35%. 
The Applicant advised a higher chiller load utilisation than previously assumed is required to achieve 
customer demand. As a result, this would  increase predicted operational noise for the proposed 
modification at all receivers surrounding the site and exceed the night-time noise limit of 43 dB(A) at 
Arise Lane Cove by 11 dB(A).  
In an effort to achieve compliance with operational noise limits, t he Applicant’s first iteration of 
mitigation design during the assessment process featured rooftop boundary noise barriers 
(approximately 7.8 m above the equipment deck), installation of cowl covering at the discharge of all 
chiller units (to direct some noise away from sensitive receivers) and adoption of quieter chillers for 
the remaining phases . In 2024, the Applicant settled on its final iteration of mitigation desig n which 
comprises the following (see Figure 17 for a summary of rooftop mechanical plant mitigation plan): 
• internal and external acoustic panel treatment to existing chillers to reduce casing radiated sound 
• 1.2 m high localised screening above the discharge fan on top of selected chillers 
• quiet ‘low noise’ chillers for the remaining phases. 
The Applicant’s updated assessment for the proposed modification predi cted ‘mitigated’ operational 
noise would achieve compliance with noise limits  at existing compliance locations R1, C1 and P1 , 
including the nearest residential receiver (i.e. Arise Lane Cove ). However, it is predicted that 
operational noise at residential receivers farther afield would increase beyond levels predicted under 
SSD-9741-Mod-2, by up to 3 dB during the evening period and 1 dB during the night-time period at 14 
[[page 23]]

Jeanette Street, East Ryde. Further, the updated assessment found noise impact could extend beyond 
14 Jeanette Street, at other residences west of Pittwater Road, at levels above existing background 
noise in the suburban area.  Despite the predicted increase in noise, t he Applicant considers this  
operational noise impact to be acceptable as operational noise is no more than 5 dB(A) above existing 
background noise levels. 
To address any unforeseen situations (e.g. mitigation measures are less effective than predicted and 
noise-enhancement from local weather conditions is higher than anticipated), the Applicant referred

three months of the commencement of phase 3 of the development and identified several 
contingency measures that could be adopted should exceedance of noise limits be recorded. 
Contingency measures may include the installation of additional or more extensive localised barriers 
to screen rooftop plan as well as further optimisation of chiller operations.

The Department has carefully considered the updated operational noise assessment and issues raised 
in the public submission and is satisfied the Applicant has now adopted a set of realistic worst-case 
noise emission inputs to inform noise mitigation design for the proposed modification.  
The Department acknowledges the Applicant’s effort at investigating the feasibility of various noise 
mitigation options and proposed significant engineering controls to  ensure operational noise can be 
reduced to achieve compliance with the relevant noise limits . With the proposed noise  mitigation 
measures controls in place, the Department considers operational noise from the modified data centre 

[[page 24]]

would be adequately mitigated at surrounding residential areas, subject to performance verification 
and ensuring ongoing compliance with noise limits.  
Given the varying combination  of proposed noise mitigation measures for each of the remaining 
phases as shown in Figure 17, the Department has recommended Condition B15 be updated to require 
additional noise verification steps, that is, within three months upon the commencement of operation 
of Phase 2, Phase 3 and Phase 4.  A stage -by-stage process would enable the effectiveness of 
proposed engineering controls to be verified progressively and enhanced (if required) prior t o the 
development becoming fully  operational. Further, the Department has recommended the updated 
Condition B15 include additional requirements to evaluate any uncertainties around the presence of  
annoying noise characteristics and the ability for the development to achieve compliance at all times 
under noise-enhancing weather conditions.  
To address noise complaints received in East Ryde, the Department has also recommended Condition 
B14 be updated to include other residences farther afield, towards the La ne Cove National Park, and 
that noise limits at additional monitoring locations be set based on achievable ‘mitigated’ noise levels 
predicted by the Applicant. This would provide a benchmark for t he Applicant to verify the 
performance of the development in East Ryde while limiting the increase in noise beyond the original 
approval for the Lane Cove Data Centre.  
The Department’s assessment concludes that noise impacts during operation of the development can 
be appropriately managed through the implementatio n of existing and modified conditions of 
consent.

6 Evaluation 
The Department has reviewed the Modification Report, RtS and additional information, and all advice 
from government authorities and councils, taking into consideration the relevant matters under the 
EP&A Act. 
The Applicant is proposing to modify the consent to amend the  external, internal  and operation al 
elements of the approved data centre to address detailed design and end user requirements.  
The Departme nt exhibited the modification application and received one public submission which 
objected to the proposed modification on the grounds of operationa l noise  concerns. Government 
agencies and local councils did not raised concerns with regard to noise, however, provided comments 
on other aspects of the proposed modification such as landscaping and biodiversity.  
The Department’s assessment considered op erational noise impacts associated with the proposed 
modification to be the key matter for consideration.  
The 24 -hour operation of the development has the potential to adversely impact on the acoustic 
amenity of surrounding sensitive land uses. The propos ed modification, which features an increased 
number of mechanical plant, elevated gantry structures and hig her chiller operating load, requires a 
considered approach towards noise control to ensure operational noise impacts would not be 
exacerbated beyond the envelope approved originally. The Applicant has refined its  proposed noise 
mitigation measures over the course of the assessment, which  resulted in the commitment to use 
quieter equipment and implement engineering controls such as acoustic panel treatm ent and 
localised noise barriers to minimise operational noise.  
The Department is satisfied the proposed modification has been designed to respond sensitively to 
surrounding residential areas. The Department has recommended conditions to limit operational noise 
in the East Ryde suburban area and ensure performance verification is undertaken by the Applicant 
progressively prior to the development becoming fully operational. 
The Department’s assessment has considered the relevant matters and objects of the E P&A Act and 
considers the proposed modification is appropriate on the basis that it would: 
• improve operational efficiency and reduce operational risk to the subject data centre 
• continue to satisfy market demand through the provision of additional, flexible data storage 
capacity within the Eastern Economic Corridor 
• not significantly increase the environmental impacts of the development beyond those 
assessed under the original development application. 
The Department is satisfied  the modification application should be approved , subject to the 
recommended modifying conditions of consent.
