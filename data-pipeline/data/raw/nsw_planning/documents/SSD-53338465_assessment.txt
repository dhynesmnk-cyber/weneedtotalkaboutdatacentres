Preface 
This report details the Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure’s (the Department) 
assessment of the State significant development  (SSD) application for an expansion of an existing 
data centre located at 8 Grand Avenue, Rosehill, lodged by Equinix Hyperscale 2 (SY10) Pty Limited.  
The Department’s assessment considers all documents submitted by the Applicant, including the 
Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) and Submissions Report, submissions received from the public 
and City of Parramatta Council  (Council), advice from government agencies, and all legislation and 
planning instruments relevant to the site and the development. 
The report includes: 
• a description of the development and the surrounding environment 
• an assessment of the development against government policy and statutory requirements, 
including mandatory considerations 
• an explanation of why the development is SSD and who the consent authority is 
• consideration of matters raised by the community and other stakeholders 
• an assessment of the likely environmental, social and economic impacts of the development 
and recommendations for managing any impacts during construction and operation 
• an evaluation which weighs up the likely impacts and benefits of the development, having 
regard to the proposed mitigation measures, community views and government advice, and 
provides a view on whether the impacts are, on balance, acceptable 
• a recommendation to the decision -maker, along with the reasons for the recommendation, to 
assist them in making an informed decision about whether development consent for the 
development should be granted and any conditions that should be imposed.

Executive Summary 
Introduction 
Equinix Hyperscale 2 (SY10) Pty Limited (the Applicant) proposes to construct and operate a n 
expansion to an existing data centre at 8 Grand Avenue, Rosehill in the Parramatta local government 
area (LGA).  
The development has a capital investment value of $ 293 million and is expected to generate up to 
250 construction jobs and 36 operational jobs. 
The Applicant is a multinational company specialising in data centres and internet connectivity. It has 
identified the need for additional electronic data storage in Greater Sydney and proposes to expand 
its existing data centre on the site by the construction and operation of a second building. 
The Applicant has gained consent for the initial data centre building, supporting infrastructure works 
and a concept for a second data centre building. This application proposes to carry out work for the 
second data centre building and final stage of the concept development. 
Site Context 
The site is located on the Camellia Peninsula in Rosehill , 17 kilometres (km) west  of Sydney Central 
Business District and covers approximately 4.27 hectares (ha) of E5 Heavy Industrial zoned land under 
the Paramatta Local Environment Plan 2023.  
The Camellia Peninsula supports a range of industrial uses and is situated between the Parramatta 
River to the north, Duck River to the east and south and James Ruse Drive along the precinct’s western 
boundary. 
The nearest residential receivers are located approximately 1.1 km to the west of the site, along James 
Ruse Drive. 
Current Proposal 
The application seeks consent for the expansion of an existing data centre facility  including the 
construction and operation of a 31.2 megawatt (MW) data centre building, adding an additional 14,856 
square metres of gross floor area. The proposal also includes provision of 19 backup generators, two 
fuel tanks, batteries, cooling systems, four water tanks and other supporting infrastructure.  
Statutory Context  
The development is classified as State significant development (SSD) under Part 4 of the 
Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979 (EP&A Act) because it involves construction and 
operation of a data centre that meets the criteria in Clause 25  of Schedule 1 in State Environmental 
Planning Policy (Planning Systems) 2021 (Planning Systems SEPP) , being a data centre with greater 
than 1 0 MW capacity . Consequently, the Minister for Planning and Public Spaces is the consent 
authority for the development under section 4.5(1) of the EP&A Act.

Engagement  
The Department exhibited the development application (DA) and accompanying EIS  for the 
development from 27 October 2023  until 23 November 2023 . During the exhibition period, the 
Department received a submission each from Council and a utility provider,  and advice from five  
government and state-owned agencies. No public submissions were received. 
Key issues raised by Council and agencies included flooding impacts, water usage, landscaping and 
emergency response.  The Department requested the Applicant address the matters raised in 
submission and government agency advice in a Submissions Report. 
The Applicant submitted a Submissions Report in July 2024 which included revised assessments of 
flooding and additional information on water usage  and emergency responses to address the issues 
raised.  
Between the submission of the report and December 2024, the Applicant provided further information 
to address concerns raised by the Department, Council and government agencies, including  a Flood 
Impact Risk Assessment 
Assessment  
The Department’s assessment of the application has fully considered all relevant matters under 
section 4.15 of the EP&A Act, the objects of the EP&A Act and the principles of ecologically 
sustainable development. The Department has identified the key issue s for assessment are noise, air 
quality and flooding. 
Noise 
Noise from the operation of the data centre, including air handling units, transformers and emergency 
back-up generators, may impact on surrounding sensitive receivers if not carefully controlled through 
design and mitigation measures. The Applicant provided a noise and vibration impact assessment that 
predicted noise levels from the proposed development, comparing against the Project Noise Trigger 
Levels derived from background noise monitoring in accordance with the Environment Protection 
Authority (EPA’s) Noise Policy for Industry (NPfI). 
The noise assessment  predicted the noise levels of two operating scenarios being a standard 
operating scenario and a worst-case critical power failure scenario. It demonstrated compliance with 
the criteria during standard operations at all but two receivers (one residential and one industrial) , 
which were categorised as a negligible impact  (one decibel exceedance).  During emergency 
operations, the  development would see exceedances  at several residential receivers,  however the 
Applicant noted that failure event is deemed to be highly unlikely and would have minimal duration.  
The assessment also concluded that vibration from construction activities would be unlikely to impact 
on surrounding developments. 
The Department has considered the Applicant’s assessment and  notes some minor  exceedances of 
the NPfI occur during noise enhancing weather, however this is a result of conservative background 
noise monitoring and is still considered to be a negligible impact by NPfI . The Department also 
acknowledges the exceedances during emergency operation, however, notes this highly unlikely and 
would have minimal duration.

As such, the Department is satisfied the development’s noise impact to be negligible and acceptable. 
Further, the Department considers the mitigation measures identified in the acoustic report to be 
adequate in reducing the potential noise emissions of the development’s operations. 
The Department has recommended conditions of consent limiting noise emissions of the development 
at receiver locations and requiring the Applicant to prepare and submit a noise verification report. 
With these conditions in place, the Department’s assessmen t concludes the development would not 
have adverse impacts on sensitive receivers. 
Air Quality 
Pollutant emissions from the operation of emergency back- up generators during testing or critical 
power failure scenarios may impact on sensitive receivers if not carefully controlled. The Applicant 
provided an air quality impact assessment that predicted the pollutant emissions of the development 
(as well as the existing data centre) under both a standard operating scenario and a worst-case critical 
power failure scenario. 
The assessment predicted the pollutant emission concentrations to be below the impact assessment 
criteria of the EPA’s Approved Methods for the Modelling and Assessment of Air Pollutants in NSW 
during standard operating scenarios . It predicted emission concentrations during the worst -case 
scenario to significantly exceed the relevant impact assessment criteria however, the air quality 
assessment justified that any critical power failure event is deemed to be highly unlikely and would 
have minimal duration. 
The Department’s assessment considered the Applicant has sufficiently demonstrated that proposed 
standard operations of the development will have an acceptable incremental and cumulative impact 
on air quality, and that the likelihood of a critical power outage event is highly unlikely. 
The Department recommended conditions of consent requiring annual emissions testing and 
reporting, the reporting of emergency back-up generators operation subsequent to any power outage 
event and the preparation and implementation of an Air Quality Management Plan. The Department’s 
assessment concluded the development would not result in unacceptable air quality impacts on 
surrounding receivers subject to the implementation of the recommended conditions and 
management measures. 
Flooding  
The subject site is located within an area prone to flooding. The Department consulted closely with 
the Applicant, Council and Biodiversity Conservation and Science g roup of the NSW Department of 
Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water to ensure a robust flood impact risk assessment 
was undertaken. The final development sees all floor levels located above the 1% Annual Exceedance 
Probability flood event level. The Applicant’s final flood assessment also demonstrated the proposal 
would no t have any  unacceptable impacts on surrounding sites during this flood event and has 
considered emergency flood response procedures.  
The Department’s assessment concludes the design of the development will not increase the risk of 
flooding off-site. Conditions of consent are recommended to ensure the detailed design of the 
proposal does not increase flood levels on adjoining properties above an allowable amount and

construction and operation of the development. 
Conclusion 
The Department’s assessment conclude s the impacts of the development can be mitigated and/or 
managed to ensure an acceptable level of environmental performance, subject to the recommended 
conditions of consent. 
Overall, the Department’s assessment has concluded the development would provide additional data 
storage for Greater Sydney in an existing data centre campus and an increase in knowledge- based 
local jobs (an additional 250 construction jobs and 36 operational jobs), in line with the strategic intent 
for the area. 
The development is not expected to have adverse noise or air quality impacts on the locality and 
sensitive receivers , subject to the implementation of the proposed mitigation measures and the 
recommended conditions of consent.  In relation and the flood impacts can be suitably managed 
through an emergency response plan 
Consequently, the Department considers the development is in the public interest and is 
recommended for approval, subject to conditions.

1 Introduction 
1.1 Development Background 
Equinix Hyperscale 2 (Sy10) Pty Ltd (the Applicant) is seeking development consent for the 
construction and 24/7 operation of a data centre with an operational capacity of 31.2 megawatts (MW) 
located at 8 Gra nd Avenue, Rosehill in the Parramatta local government area (LGA) . A detailed 
description of the development is provided in Section 2. 
Due to the emergence of new technologies such as driverless cars, faster mobile networks and 
artificial intelligence, there has been increase in the need for new large and small-scale data centres 
across greater Sydney and NSW to store this data. As such, the Applicant is proposing to expand its 
existing data centre (referred to as SY9) to accommodate the growing demand for data storage, with 
the expansion referred to as SY10. 
The Applicant is a multinational company specialising in internet connectivity and provision of data 
centres on five continents.  The Applicant specialises in colocation centres, allowing retail and 
corporate customers to lease space within its centres to store data servers.  
The Applicant gained approval for a staged development consent on the site comprising of the Stage 
1 works, which included the construction and operation of part of the site as a data centre  as well as 
infrastructure works. It also included  the concept for an expansion to the data centre for its second 
and ultimate stage. The Applicant is now seeking detailed consent for the second stage of the project 
to expand the data centre campus, completing the project. 
1.2 Site Description 
The site encompasses an area of approximately 4.27 hectares (ha) of E5 Heavy Industrial land zoned 
under the  Parramatta Local Environment Plan 2023 at 8 Grand Avenue, Rosehill  and is  legally 
described as Lot 10 DP 1275083.  As mentioned, it is currently occupied by the existing SY9 data 
centre, with the portion relevant to this application, cleared of any structures and vegetation. 
The site is located approximately 17 kilometres (km) west of the Sydney Central Business District 
(CBD) and 2 km east of the Parramatta CBD (see Figure 1). 
1.3 Surrounding Land Uses 
The site is located on the Camellia Peninsula in Rosehi ll (see Figure 2), which was established as an 
industrial precinct in the 1950s. The precinct  supports a range of industrial uses and is situated 
between the Parramatta River to the north, Duck River to the east and south and James Ruse Drive 
along the precinct’s western boundary. 
Industrial uses are located to the north, east and south of the site. The Minster for Planning and Public 
Space’s (the Minister) delegate recently approved a warehouse facility adjoining to the north of the 
site (6 Grand Avenue).  A Transport for NSW’s (TfNSW) light rail depot is located immediately to the 
west of the site, with Rosehill Racecourse being located further west.  The nearest residential 
receivers are located approximately 1.1 km to the west of the site, along James Ruse Drive.

2.1 Physical Layout and Design 
The physical layout and design of the development is shown in Figure 4 to Figure 6. The data centre 
presents as a three-storey structure , surrounded by internal access roads and landscaped areas, 
existing data centre building SY9, and existing approved site infrastructure.  
Each data centre floor comprises of four data halls (12 total) surrounded by mechanical plant rooms , 
supporting the operation of the development. Office spaces are provided on the northern elevation of 
each floor. Back-up generators are located in stacks located on the western side of the building, with 
a loading dock located on the north -eastern corner of the building.  Mechanical plant in the form of

part of the SY9 consent.  However, the development sees an additional perimeter road around the 
western and southern sides of the building, connecting to the site’s existing access road network.

2.2 Process Description 
The primary purpose of the development is for the collection, storage, processing and distribution of 
electronic data in associated IT hardware by cloud and content providers. 
The data centre operator will fit out the development’s data halls with its own computer systems, 
server systems and networking equipment. This IT hardware would be stored in racks designed to 
maximise the efficiency of the space. The data centre operator will then lease storage space within 
these systems to its tenant/s. 
The development would be cooled using a cooling water loop. During cooler months, heat would be 
expelled from this loop through the cooling towers , using low ambient air temperatures. During 
warmer times of the year, this system would be supplemented with trim chillers to cool the water loop. 
The cooling system has been designed to maintain a constant temperature within the data halls 
throughout the year. The ancillary office spa ce would be primarily used by on -site technicians, who 
would provide IT support to external users and undertake regular maintenance as necessary. 
Data centres also require sophisticated energy back-up systems to ensure the data centre operator’s 
customers are always able to access their data. To this end, the development includes a system of 
lithium-ion batteries and diesel back -up generators designed to minimise downtime during a power 
outage event. 
The site is to be serviced by two 132 kilovolt (kV) feeder lines to be connected to the future switching 
yard near the entrance of the site, being fed through four feeders to the on-site substation to the east 
of the development, subject to a separate development consent  (DA/821/2022). In the event of a full 
power outage, lithium -ion batteries located in the data halls would provide an uninterrupted power 
supply until the back -up generator system reaches load. Once they have reached full load, diesel 
back-up generators would be used to provide continuous power to the development until power has 
been restored by Endeavour Energy. 
To ensure the development can function during a power outage event, the Applicant would also 
undertake regular testing of each back-up generator. During certain tests, an artificial load would be 
applied to the generators, with tests of differing durations occurring throughout the year. It is noted 
only one generator would be tested at any one time in the development, with one tested in SY9 at the 
same time. The test scenarios proposed are detailed in Table 3 below. The generators are proposed 
to be tested during standard day time operations, seven days a week.

4.2 Other Approvals and Authorisations 
Should development consent by granted, other approvals may be required  in order to carry out the 
development. Section 4.42 of the EP&A Act lists a number of approvals that cannot be refused if

6.1 Air Quality 
The construction and operation of the development has the potential to result in air quality impacts 
to surrounding sensitive receivers. The development is anticipated to generate the following 
emissions: 
• particulate matter and dust emissions generated during demolition and construction works 
• exhaust emissions associated with the testing and operation of the development’s back- up 
generator system.  
The Applicant provided an Air Quality Impact Assessment which was prepared in accordance with the 
EPA Approved Methods 2022 and considered existing operations on the site from the SY9 component. 
Following requests from the Department  relating to consideration of nearby childcare centres , the 
Applicant updated its Air Quality Impact Assessment , which f orms the basis of the following 
assessment. 
6.1.1 Construction Impacts  
Construction of the development would be carried out across a period of  15 months. The air quality 
assessment found that the sensitivity of the surrounding area to dust and particulate emissions 
impacts during construction would be low, further noting the closest residential receivers to be more 
than one kilometre to the west of the site . The air quality assessment concluded that subject to the 
implementation of standard construction mitigation measures as part of a Construction 
Environmental Management Plan (CEMP) such as dust suppression, water spraying and covering of 
trucks, the development would have negligible construction air quality impacts.  
Neither Council, nor the EPA raised any concerns over impacts of construction on air quality. 
The Department has reviewed the air quality assessment and is satisfied air emissions associated with 
the construction of the development, including demolition, would be minimal subject to the 
preparation and implementation of a CEMP containing standard construction mitigation measures. 
The Department has recommended conditions of consent requiring the Applicant to prepare and 
implement a CEMP for the duration of construction and to take all reasonable steps to minimise the 
generation of dust. 
6.1.2 Operation Impacts 
The air quality assessment identified key pollutants associated with the operation of the development 
to be nitrogen dioxide (NO2) and particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10). Other pollutants assessed include 
carbon monoxide (CO), sulfur dioxide (SO 2), Benzene and Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbon s (PAHs).

6.2 Noise 
The construction and 24 -hour operation of the development has the potential to emit noise and 
vibration, which could impact on the acoustic amenity of the surrounding area.  
The development would involve the construction of a two -storey data centre building with ancillary 
office space across a 24-month period. Working hours are proposed between 7 am and 6 pm Monday 
to Friday, between 8 am and 1 pm on Saturday and no works on Sundays or public holidays in line with 
the standard construction hours in the EPA’s Interim Construction Noise Guideline (ICNG).  
The development’s main operational noise sources include the cooling towers,  condenser units, and 
emergency back-up generators. Other sources of operational noise include vehicle movements to and 
from the site and manoeuvring around the site. 
The EIS included a Noise and Vibration Impact Assessment ( the acoustic report ) which considered 
both construction and operational noise impacts and considered cumulative impacts from the existing 
SY9 data centre and future adjoining substations . During the assessment process, the Applicant 
updated its acoustic report to respond to issues raised by the Department, including consideration of 
nearby childcare centres and assessment of feasible and reasonable  noise mitigation measures , 
which forms the basis of the below assessment. 
6.2.1 Construction 
The acoustic report  noted the primary construction activities of the development include site 
establishment and excavation, foundations, pavement and road works, building construction,  and 
plant installation and connection. Major plant and equipment to be used during the construction of 
the development would include trucks, excavators, jack hammer, grader, concrete pumps and power 
hand tools.  
The acoustic report modelled the predicted noise generation for each construction stage and 
compared it against the standard construction hours Noise Management Levels (NMLs) for sensitive 
receivers applied in accordance with the ICNG. The assessment found that the proposal would not be 
considered ‘Highly Noice Affected’  at impacted residential receivers , however, would be considered 
‘Noise Affected’ during site establishment and pavement work stages of the construction program, 
as it exceeded NMLs by between one and 15 decibels. It also found that surrounding industrial  sites 
would likely exceed NMLs at the boundary. 
In line with the ICNG, the assessment recommended a Construction Noise Management Plan (CNMP) 
be implemented that included  a suite of mitigation measures, including choosing low -noise 
equipment, acoustic screening, the placing of machinery away from sensitive receivers and 
implementing consultation and notification programs. 
The EPA and Council raised no concerns regarding construction noise impacts in their advice during 
the exhibition period.

6.3 Flooding 
During a 1% annual exceedance probability (AEP) event and probable maximum flood ( PMF) event, 
parts of Grand Avenue, including the intersection with James Ruse Drive, will be inundated with flood 
waters. This presents a safety issue with respect to safe evacuation of employees and visitors from 
the site via the Grand Avenue site access.  
BCS raised concerns with the robustness of the Applicant’s flood risk assessment and proposed flood 
evacuation strategy which included a ‘shelter -in-place’ approach and requested additional 
information regarding off-site flood impacts as a result of the development.  Council also raised 
concerns over potential off-site impacts. 
The Department facilitated consultation between the Applicant and BCS, resulting in several revisions 
to its Flood Impact Risk Assessment (FIRA). The final version of the FIRA was prepared in accordance 
with the Floodplain Development Manual (2023) (the Manual) and the associated flood risk 
management guidance – Flood impact and risk assessment (2023). 
The assessment determined the PMF to be Reduced Level (RL) 7.75 m Australian height datum (AHD) 
and 1% AEP level to be RL 4.08 m AHD. The development sees a finished floor level of RL 7.57 m AHD, 
well above the 1% AEP, however 0.18 m below the PMF event. 
The updated FIRA also found the proposal would not increase off-site impacts when the stormwater 
network is operational, however may have some minor off -site (30 mm – 40 mm) i ncrease during a 
100% blocked pipe scenario.  
The FIRA noted that the site could be inundated during PMF events for up to two hours, however noted 
that James Ruse Drive can be isolated for up to 12 hours, which can flood in 20% AEP flood events. As 
such, the FIRA recommended a shelter-in-place strategy be implemented during flood events. 
BCS reviewed the final FIRA and acknowledged the above off -site impacts to be extremely 
conservative, noting that the stormwater system is very unlikely to be 100% blocked. It recommended 
that final design of the proposal ensures that off -site flooding is not increased by more than 10 mm  
and requested the Department consider the proposal be considered critical infrastructure, requiring 
floor levels to meet the PMF levels stated above. It also recommended the Department consider 
warning times, isolation and necessity for the centre to operate 24 hours a day in its emergency flood 
response. 
Council reviewed the FIRA and acknowledged that the propos al was the second stage of a concept 
consent. However, it raised concerns over fill and its impact on off -site impacts and recommended 
the latest Parramatta River flood study be used. 
The Department’s Consideration 
While the Department acknowledges BCS’ advice on raising the floor level of the development to 
meet the PMF level, data centres are not considered ‘critical infrastructure’ under the Parramatta 
Development Control Plan 2023  and as such the 1% AEP is considered to be the relevant flood 
planning level, which the development will meet. 
[[page 35]]
 
  Grand Avenue Data Centre Expansion, Rosehill (SSD-53338465) Assessment Report | 26 
The Department notes Council’s concerns over fill relates  to the potential for off-site impacts which 
only occur during full blockage events.  The proposal forms the second stage of a staged concept 
consent, which set building footprints and levels in the concept consent, including the requirement to 
introduce fill. The proposal remains consistent with these levels  and as such, the Department 
considers the fill to be appropriate. Further, the Applicant has demonstrated that the off-site impacts 
would only occur when 100% blockage occurs  under an extremely conservative scenario. To reduce 
the risk of this, the Department has recommended a condition requiring the Applicant to prepare and 
implement a maintenance schedule for the stormwater management system. The  Department has 
also recommended a condition requiring the Applicant to ensure that the detailed design of the 
project not result in off-site flooding impacts of more than 10 mm. 
The Department is satisfied the final revised FIRA submitted in November 2024 provides a robust 
assessment of flood risk and flood impacts on and off the development site. The Applicant’s 
assessment has demonstrated the development remains flood free up to and including the 1% AEP 
flood event, with the upper levels of the building remaining flood free up to a PMF event. 
While flood evacuation is the preferred option, the Department notes th is is unavailable due to 
flooding on James Ruse Drive  and as such shelter -in-place is a more suitable option for the 
development. The Precinct Plan acknowledges that flood-free access to the Camellia Peninsula is an 
issue and recommends further routes be designed. As such, flood-free access will likely be available 
in the future in most flood events, and will reduce the isolation caused by James Ruse Drive flooding. 
To suitably manage an emergency response in the event of a flood event, the Department has 
recommended conditions requiring the preparation of a Flood Emergency Response Plan (FERP ) for 
both construction and operation of the development. The FERPs must address the provisions of the 
Manual and associated guidance in the Department’s Flood risk management toolkit (2023) . The 
FERPs must include details of flood levels, warning times, assembly points and evacuation routes, 
evacuation and refuge protocols and awareness training for both employees and contractors. It is also 
to include triggers for review of the FERP following a flood event, change in available flood warnings 
or evacuation routes or at least every five years. 
Conditions are recommended requiring floor levels to be no lower than the 1% AEP event plus 500 
mm freeboard and any structures below this level to be constructed from flood compatible building 
components and to withstand flood velocities. 
Overall, the Department is satisfied the development can be constructed to ensure it will not unduly 
increase the risk of flooding on or off the site and that with the recommended conditions of consent, 
there will be appropriate response protocols in place should a flood event occur. The Department’s 
assessment concludes any flood risk associated with the development can be suitably managed.

7 Evaluation 
The Department’s assessment of the application has fully considered all relevant matters under 
section 4.15 of the EP&A Act, the objects of the EP&A Act and the principles of ESD.  
The Department has considered the development on its merits, taking into consideration strategic 
plans that guide development in the area, the EPIs that apply to the development, advice received 
from the relevant public authorities, including Council, and submissions from the public. 
The development involves the construction and operation of a data centre with an operational 
capacity of 31.2 MW which will help to satisfy market demand for data storage infrastructure and 
capacity within Greater Sydney. 
No State government agencies, Council or the community have objected to the proposal and the 
Department has sought to address any issues raised through consultation with both the government 
agencies and the Applicant. 
The Department’s assessment has found there would be some amenity impacts during operation of  
the data centre expansion, such as noise, air quality and flooding impacts. The Department has worked 
closely with Council and BCS to improve flooding mitigation measures  and ensure a robust flood 
assessment has been undertaken. The Department has also negotiated with the Applicant to improve 
the development’s façade treatment when viewed from the Rosehill Gardens. Residual impacts can 
be adequately mitigated and managed subject to the implementation of the Applicant’s mitigation 
and management measures and the recommended conditions of consent. 
• the preparation of noise verification reporting to ensure the development meets the prescribed 
noise limits 
• requirement to undertake annual emissions testing and reporting including emissions 
reporting of emergency back-up generator operations subsequent to any power outage event 
• the preparation and implementation of a FERP for construction and operation to ensure safety 
of site occupants during flood events 
• the implementation of a landscape management plan incorporating the planting of local native 
species to improve the biodiversity values, including habitat for native fauna, on the site. 
Overall, the Department’s assessment has concluded the development would: 
• provide additional data storage capacity within Greater Sydney within an existing data centre 
site in an industrial area 
• promote a better environment through the provision of landscaping and planting of 24  trees, 
increasing vegetation over the existing site conditions 
• provide for an increase in productivity and employment within the Greater Parramatta to 
Olympic Peninsula Economic Corridor by investing $293 million and provide up to 250 full-time 
equivalent construction jobs and 36 operational jobs 
• be consistent with NSW Government policies including, the Greater Sydney Region Plan and

State Environmental Planning Policy (Transport and Infrastructure) 2021 (T&I SEPP) 
Chapter 2 of the T&I SEPP aims to facilitate the effective delivery of infrastructure across the State 
by improving regulatory certainty and efficiency, identifying matters to be considered in the 
assessment of development adjacent to certain types of infr astructure development, and providing 
for consultation with relevant public authorities about certain types of development during the 
assessment process. 
The Department consulted with TfNSW and Council as part of its assessment of the application. 
TfNSW’s comments are detailed in Section 5.  
TfNSW and Council did not raise any concerns regarding potential traffic impacts associated with the 
development. The assessment found that operational traffic would be low and generally only 
comprise of vehicle movements from operational staff and infrequent  delivery or servicing vehicles. 
Further, no road or driveway upgrades are needed to facilitate the development. The Department has 
recommended conditions of consent including implementation of a CTMP. 
Resilience and Hazards SEPP 
Chapter 3 of the Resilience and Hazards SEPP aims to identify developments with the potential for 
significant off-site impacts, in terms of risk and/or offence.  
The Applicant is seeking development consent for the construction and operation of a data centre. 
While the development would involve the on -site storage of up to 574 .1 kL of diesel fuel at any one 
time, the Department notes diesel is not classified as a dangerous good under the Dangerous Goods 
Code. Additionally, the development would store up to 121.4 tonnes of packaged lithium-ion batteries. 
However, the Department notes the significant storage and handling of these materials present 
special hazards which are to be addressed in the design stage of the development.  
Therefore, the Department has recommended conditions of consent requiring the Applicant to 
prepare a Fire Safety Study in consultation with Fire and Rescue NSW, Emergency Response Plan, 
ESIP and a restriction on screening thresholds of dangerous goods stored or transported to and from 
the development. 
Chapter 4 of the Resilience and Hazards SEPP aims to provide a State -wide approach to the 
remediation of contaminated land.  
Site contamination was previously assessed and appropriate conditions applied under the SY9  
consent with no further consideration required under the development.
