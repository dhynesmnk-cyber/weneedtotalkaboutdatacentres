Executive Summary 
This report details the Department of Planning and Environment’s (the Department) assessment of a State 
significant development application (SSD 8662) for the construction and operation of a warehouse and 
distribution centre. Dicker Data Limited (the Applican t) proposes to construct and operate the centre and 
associated office at 238-258 Captain Cook Drive, Kurnell in the Sutherland local government area (LGA).  
The site is located on the Kurnell Peninsula, approximately 20 kilometres (km) south of the Sydney CBD and covers 
17.2 hectares (ha) of industrial zoned land. The nearest sensitive receiver is a residential dwelling located in the 
township of Kurnell, approximately 800 metres (m) north of the site. The site is located in an established industrial 
precinct with surrounding land uses including the Kurne ll Oil Refinery, the Sydney De salination Plant, 4WD parks 
and factory and warehouse uses. Quibray Bay Aquatic Reserve and Towra Point Nature Reserve (a Ramsar Wetland) 
are located approximately 250 m and 500 m to the north of the site respectively.   
Current Proposal 
The Applicant is an Australian owned and operated ASX listed distributor of computer hardware, software and 
related products. The Applicant is seeking to relocate its existing warehouse located on the adjoining site at 230 
Captain Cook Drive. The new warehouse and associated office would replace the existing warehouse and allow 
for the company to grow its workforce and expand oper ations. This application s eeks development consent for 
construction of a new warehouse and distribution centre, associated offices, amenities, hardstand areas including 
car parking, service infrastructure, landscaping and subdivision. The proposed warehouse would be constructed 
over two stages, with approximately half of the warehouse and office area being constructed in each stage.   
The site currently contains remnant structures from previo us industrial uses associated with the manufacturing of 
healthcare products and pharmaceuticals and an area of partially cleared land. The perimeter of the site comprises 
native vegetation, some of which is identified as comprising Endangered Ecological Communities.   
The proposed development (the development) has a capital investment value of $77.2 million and is expected to 
generate 350 construction jobs and 257 operational jobs , which are additional to the 291 current employees at 
the existing Dicker Data site. Therefore, the operation of the proposed development would provide a total of 548 
jobs in the Kurnell area.  
Statutory Context 
The development is classified as State signif icant development (SSD) under Part 4 of the Environmental Planning 
and Assessment Act 1979  (EP&A Act), because it involves construction and operation of a warehouse and 
distribution centre which meets the criteria in Clause 12  of Schedule 1 in the State Environmental Planning Policy 
(State and Regional Development) 2011 (SRD SEPP). Co nsequently, the Minister for Planning is the consent 
authority for the proposed development under section 4.5(1) of the EP&A Act. 
Engagement 
The Department exhibited the EIS for the development from  Thursday 7 June 2018 until Thursday 5 July 2018. A 
total of 13 submissions were received, none of which were objections. Of these, 12 submissions were from public 
authorities, including Sutherland Sh ire Council (Council). One submission was from the adjoining landowner to 
the south-west, Besmaw Pty Ltd (Besmaw). Besmaw has oper ated a quarry (extracting sand) on the adjoining site 
since the 1960’s and is currently rehab ilitating the land for the potential deve lopment of the site for a range of 
permitted uses, including hotel, recreational and business uses.

Key concerns raised related to pote ntial traffic impacts, st ormwater and flooding, biodiversity impacts and 
contamination. The Applicant submitted a Response to  Submissions (RTS) in September 2018 to address and 
clarify matters raised in the submissions. Following this, the Applicant sought minor amendments to the proposed 
hours of operation. In November 2018, a further submissi on from Besmaw was received which raised additional 
concerns regarding the management of stormwater on the subject site. As a result, further consultation on this 
matter was required by the Department with Council and the Office of Environment and Heritage (OEH). 
Assessment 
The Department’s assessment of the application has fully considered all relevant matters under section 4.15 of the 
EP&A Act, the objects of the EP&A Act and the princi ples of ecologically sustainable development. The 
Department identified the key issues for assessment are contamination and remediation, stormwater, flooding and 
groundwater, traffic and access. 
Contamination and Remediation 
The EIS identified the presence of potential soil and gr oundwater contamination from historic uses of the site. 
Preliminary investigations of the site by the Applicant identified that, although the soils and groundwater of the site 
are not significantly contaminated, asbestos is a potent ial contaminant of concern. The Applicant proposes to 
remediate the soils of the site by collecting any fragme nts of asbestos at the soil surface and excavating and 
disposing of this material off site at an appropriately licensed facility.  
Further investigations of the site ar e required by the Applicant beneath existing site infrastructure. Once the 
infrastructure is cleared from the site, the Applicant would complete these investigations and incorporate any 
findings into an updated remediation strategy. The Environment Protection Authority (EPA) raised no concerns 
regarding the remediation strategy. As recommended by the EPA, the site is to be remediated in accordance with 
the updated remediation strategy, once this has been endorsed by an accredited Site Auditor. A Site Auditor has 
also provided interim advice concluding that the site  is capable of being made suitable for the proposed 
development, with remediation, and the implementati on of a Long Term Environmental Management Plan 
(LTEMP). 
The Department is satisfied that, with remediation, the site can be made suitable for the proposed commercial and 
industrial use and, with the implemen tation of a LTEMP, any residual asbe stos remaining on site appropriately 
managed.  
Stormwater, Flooding and Groundwater 
The Applicant proposes on site detention and stormwat er treatment to minimise impacts to upstream and 
downstream catchments from stormwater run-off. Th e Department is satisfied the proposed stormwater 
management measures comply with Council’s polluti on reduction targets and would not result in any 
unreasonable impacts on Council’s infr astructure. As the site is located on  highly permeable, sandy soils the 
development would have negligible impacts to exis ting stormwater flows. Both Council and the OEH 
considered the Applicant’s flood modelling and recommendations satisfactory. 
As the development would not change the volume of wa ter that would infiltrate into the groundwater, the 
Department also is satisfied any potential groundwa ter impacts can be appropriately managed through the 
implementation of a groundwater management and monitoring plan.  
Traffic and Access 
The Applicant has predicted the development would increase tra ffic travelling along Captain Cook Drive by          
7%. The Department is satisfied that nearby intersections would continue to  operate at a good level of service

construct two bus stops along Captain Cook Drive. The Department has incorporated this as a condition. 
The Applicant also proposes acceleration lanes along Ca ptain Cook Drive to facilitate access to the site. The 
Department is satisfied the proposed roadworks and si te access arrangements would maintain the safety of 
Captain Cook Drive.   
Summary 
The Department’s assessment concludes the potential im pacts of the development can be suitably mitigated 
and/or managed to ensure an acceptable level of en vironmental performance, subject to the recommended 
conditions of consent. Overall, the Department considers the development would: 
• optimise the use of an appropriately zoned industrial site for industrial and commercial uses 
• provide a $77.2 million investment, generate 350 construction jobs and an additional 257 operational jobs 
in the Sutherland LGA 
• be consistent with the objects of the EP&A Act, the strategic intent of the South District Plan and the 
Sutherland Local Environmental Plan 2015.  
Consequently, the Department considers the development is in the public interest and is recommended for

1.  Introduction 
 
1.1 The Department’s Assessment 
This report details the Department of Planning and Environment’s (the Departme nt) assessment of the State 
significant development (SSD 8662) for the Dicker Da ta warehouse and distribution centre. The proposed 
development (the development) involves the constructi on and operation of a warehouse for the storage and 
distribution of computer related hardware and software at Captain Cook Drive, Kurnell in the Sutherland Local 
Government Area (LGA). 
The Department’s assessment considers all documentation submitted by Dicker Data Limited (the Applicant), 
including the Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) and a Response to Submissions (RTS) report, as well as 
submissions received from government agencies and the public. The Department’s assessment also considers the 
legislation and planning instruments relevant to the site and the development. 
This report describes the development, surrounding en vironment, relevant strate gic and statutory planning 
provisions and the issues raised in submissions. The report evaluates the key issues associated with the 
development and provides recommendations for managing any impacts during construction and operation. The 
Department’s assessment of the development has concluded that the development is in the public interest and 
should be approved, subject to conditions. 
1.2 Development Background  
The Applicant is seeking development consent to cons truct and operate a warehouse, associated offices, 
amenities, hardstand areas including car parking, service infrastructure, landscaping and subdivision.  
Dicker Data Limited is an Australian owned and operated  wholesale distributor of computer hardware, software 
and related products in Australia and New Zealand. The company was founded in 1978 and is based in Kurnell at 
230 Captain Cook Drive, adjoining the eastern boundary of the subject site . The company stores and distributes 
wholesale computer related hardware and software which is bought from vendors such as HP, Cisco, Toshiba, 
Lenovo and Microsoft. The Applicant then repackages and redistributes the computer products to resellers to suit 
clients or customer’s needs. 
The Applicant is seeking to relocate its existing warehous e and head office from the adjoining site where 291 full 
time staff are currently employed to  allow for the company to grow it s workforce and operations. The new 
warehouse and associated office would replace the existing warehouse.  
The site is located approximately 7 kilo metres (km) south of Sydney Kingsford Smith Airport, 20 km south of the 
Sydney CBD, 7.5 km north-east of Cronulla, and 1.5 km south-west of the township of Kurnell (see Figure 1).

1.3 Site Description 
The site comprises two lots (Lot 1 in DP 225973 and Lot 2 in DP 1088703) of industrial zoned land which fronts 
Captain Cook Drive, Kurnell (see Figure 2). The site is currently vacant with remnant structures mostly associated 
with previous manufacturing industri al uses, including rubble, metal and concrete slabs, paving, stockpiles, 
parking areas and driveways. The central portion of the site, where these remnant structures are located, features 
scattered patches of native and exotic vegetation. The si te has a slight fall towards the south western boundary 
where a broad strip of native vegetation is located. Th is vegetation and a smaller area located along the north-
eastern boundary, comprise three endangered ecological communities being Bangalay Sand Forest, Swamp Oak 
Floodplain Forest, and Swamp Sclerophyll Forest. A small man-made pond is also located in the southern portion 
of the site (see Figure 2).  
The central portion of the site was formerly used by Abbott Australasia Pty Ltd to manufacture healthcare products 
and pharmaceuticals, including penicillin and codeine-based products. Since the operations ceased in 2003, the 
site has remained largely vacant as buildings and other substantial structures have been demolished. The soil and 
groundwater of the site is potentially contaminated fr om previous industrial and manufacturing uses, with 
contaminants of concern including asbestos, heavy metals and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons. 
Stormwater flows on the site are currently limited to surface runoff from impervious areas during rainfall events. As 
the site features sandy soils with high hydraulic conduc tivity, storm events up to the 1% Annual Exceedance 
Probability (AEP) permeate into the ground without significant ponding or overland flow.  
The site does not contain any items of heritage significan ce and it is not located within a designated heritage 
conservation area.

• transforming an underutilised site and providing a design that is sympathetic to the context of the site.

3. Strategic Context 
 
Greater Sydney Region Plan, 2018 
The vision of the Greater Sydney Region Plan 2018, A Metropolis of Three Cities falls within the integrated planning 
framework for Sydney (see Figure 9) and seeks to meet the needs of a growing and changing population by 
transforming Greater Sydney into a metropolis of three ci ties – the Western Parkland City, the Central River City 
and the Eastern Harbour City. It brings new thinking to land use and transport patterns to boost Greater Sydney’s 
liveability, productivity and sustainability by spreading the benefits of growth.  
 
Figure 9 | Integrated Planning for Greater Sydney 
Objective 23 outlines that industrial and urban services land (which includes industrial zoned land) is to be 
planned, retained and managed. Strategy 23.1 also recognises the need for certain office uses to be co-located 
with industrial uses to provide job opportunities closer to residents. By retaining existing and providing additional 
jobs closer to where people live and providing industri al development on industrial zoned land, the proposed 
development will support the objectives and strategies of the Greater Sydney Region Plan.

social and environmental matters to ac hieve the 40-year vision for Greater Sy dney. It is a guide for implementing 
the Greater Sydney Region Plan at a district level and is a bridge between regional and local planning.  
The proposed development would assist in meeting Actions 39 and 40 of the SDP as it would retain and manage 
industrial and urban services land in the Kurnell area which is recognised to have capacity for industrial and urban 
service uses (see Figure 10). 
The Department has considered the strategic context of the site and is satisfied the proposed development is

4. Statutory Context 
 
4.1 State Significant Development 
The proposal is SSD pursuant to section 4.36 of the Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979 (EP&A Act) 
because it has a CIV in excess of $50 million ($77 million) and is development for the purpose of a warehouse and 
distribution centre which meets the criteria in Clause 12 of Schedule 1 of State Environmental Planning Policy (State 
and Regional Development) 2011.  
4.2 Permissibility  
The site is zoned IN1 – General Industrial under the Sutherland Local Environmental Plan (SLEP) 2015. The 
development is characterised as a warehouse and distribution centre, with associated and ancillary office facilities. 
Warehouse and distribution centres located in the IN1 zone are permissible with consent.  
On this basis, the Minister for Planning or a delegate may determine the carrying out of the development. 
4.3 Consent Authority  
The Minister is the consent authority for the development under section 4.5 of the EP&A Act. On 11 October 2017, 
the Minister delegated the functions to determine SSD applications to the Executive Director, Key Sites and 
Industry Assessments where: 
• the relevant Council has not made an objection 
• there are less than 25 public submissions in the nature of objections 
• a political disclosure statement has not been made.

6.2 Stormwater, Groundwater and Flooding Impacts 
Stormwater 
The increase in impervious areas and disturbance to the terrain on the site from the development has the potential 
to increase stormwater flows. More stormwater runoff has the potential to impact on upstream and downstream 
catchments both in quantity and quality of flows, if not controlled by a suitable stormwater management system. 
The EIS includes a Stormwater Management Plan (SMP) prepared in consultation with Council and in accordance 
with Sutherland Shire Council’s De velopment Control Plan 2015 (DCP 2015) and Landcom’s ‘Managing Urban 
Stormwater – Soils and Construction (2004)’. The SMP inco rporates on-site detention to ensure that flows are 
maintained at pre-development levels and do not place extra pressure on Council’s existing drainage infrastructure 
or on surrounding waterways. The effectiveness of th e proposed stormwater treatment measures was assessed 
using the Model for Urban Stormwater Improvement Conceptualisation (MUSIC) and DRAINS model and 
compared against Council’s pollutant reduction targets.  
The proposed stormwater treatment measures include: 
• a pit and pipe network to collect all minor stormwater runoff  
• filter cartridges (Stormwater360) within the on-site stormwater detention (with capacity for 4,500 m³) 
• gross pollutant traps (SW 360 Enviropods) in all stormwater pits 
• a landscape buffer area and vegetated or grass-lined swale  
• a bio-retention basin.  
These stormwater treatment measures would be installed to reduce pollutant loads in stormwater runoff, such as 
total suspended solids, phosphorus and nitrogen. The de velopment also includes a 350 kL rainwater tank for 
harvesting roof water for reuse on site, to offset potable water use. 
Council queried the Applicant’s calculations of the pre-development discharge rates to be used and requested 
revised stormwater plans. The SMP proposed to utilise a Council stormwater pit adjacent to Captain Cook Drive, 
on the western side of the site. Council queried whether st ormwater currently discharges to this point, as there 
is no infrastructure in this location. Council also recommended the SMP be amended to meet the pollution 
reduction targets in the Botany Bay and Catchment Water Quality Improvement Plan (Botany Bay WQIP).

Flooding 
A Flood Study Report accompanied the EIS. The Flood Study Report found that, assuming the site had infiltration 
rates indicative of clay soil types (of 5.4 mm/hr), the proposed development had the potential to lead to adverse 
changes in existing stormwater flows and ponding. The Flood Study Report found that the development would 
result in up to 40 mm of ponding predicted on offsite pr operties to the south and east of the site during a 1% 
Annual Exceedance Probability (AEP) st orm event. To mitigate the potentia l impacts, the Flood Study Report 
recommended the adoption of a flood management strategy and a site emergency response flood plan.  
These predicted offsite flood impacts were unacceptable to Council. Council also questioned the accuracy of the 
infiltration rates given the site is located on highly permeable sandy soils with rapid infiltration rates, not clay soils. 
Council considered the Applicant’s flood modelling and th e assumed infiltration rate may have overestimated 
flood levels and the amount of ponding on the site  and surrounds. Council recommended revisions to the 
development to account for the impacts of sea level rise and higher flood planning levels. Besmaw also expressed 
concern that the proposed development would result in  stormwater and flooding impacts on its property and 
adjoining properties in general. 
In response to these submissions, the Applicant raised the design level of the parking area and provided an 
updated Flood Study Report. Import antly, the updated Food Study Report increased the infiltration rates to            
385 mm/hr to reflect the high permeability of the soils on the site, as confirmed by ge otechnical testing. Due to 
the highly permeable nature of the soils, and the site’s proximity to receiving waters, the Applicant advises most 
rainfall would infiltrate rapidly, without significant po nding or overland flow. The updated Flood Study Report 
concluded that increases in sea levels and tidal floodin g would not cause flooding of the development and, with 
the implementation of the revised SMP, there would be ne gligible changes to the 1% AEP flood levels (less than 
10 mm offsite and up to 300 mm on site).  
The proposed floor levels of the development would al so be above the Probable Maximum Flood (PMF) level. 
However, due to the low-lying nature of the catchment and the fact that most buildings would become flood 
islands in a PMF event, the report recommends the implem entation of a site emergenc y flood plan which details 
potential training and mitigation meas ures for a PMF event. Council raised no further concerns in relation to 
flooding. 
As noted in Section 5.7, following the submission of the Applicant’ s RTS, Besmaw raised further concerns in 
relation to the accuracy of the Applicant’s flood modelling and the potential flooding impacts on Captain Cook 
Drive. Besmaw engaged The Calibre Group to review the Applicant’s Flood Study Report and SMP. Besmaw is 
concerned the Applicant’s flood study catchment area did not include the full extent of Besmaw’s site and that

State Environmental Planning Policy No. 19 – Bushland in Urban Areas (SEPP 19) 
SEPP 19 generally aims to protect and preserve bushland in certain urban areas, including in the Sutherland LGA 
due to its value to the community as part of natural heritage, its aestheti c value and its value as recreational, 
educational and scientific resource.  
Under SEPP 19, a person must not disturb bushland zoned land or land adjoining land zoned for public open space 
purposes without consent. The site and land directly to the south, east and west is zoned IN1 General Industrial 
and land directly to the north is in the E1 National Pa rks and Nature Reserves zone under the Sutherland Local 
Environmental Plan 2015. Therefore, the site and adjoinin g sites are not located on bushland zoned land or land 
reserved for public open space purposes.  
Notwithstanding, the site contains areas of remnant nati ve vegetation or bushland. A detailed assessment of the 
potential biodiversity and ecological  impacts and proposed measures to minimise impacts on bushland is 
provided in Section 6 of this report. The proposed development is consistent with the aim of SEPP 19 of 
protecting and preserving bushland under SEPP 19.  
State Environmental Planning Policy No. 33 – Hazardous and Offensive Development (SEPP 33) 
SEPP 33 aims to identify proposed developments with the potential for significant off-site impacts, in terms of risk 
and/or offence. A development is de fined as potentially hazardous and/or  potentially offensive if, without 
mitigating measures in place, the development would have  a significant risk and/or adverse impact on off-site 
receptors.  
The Applicant seeks approval for the establishment of a warehouse and distribution centre and an associated 
office. The EIS did not identify any potentially hazardous or potentially offensive development under Clause 3 of 
SEPP 33.  
Therefore, SEPP 33 does not apply to the proposed development and the Department has not recommended any 
hazard related conditions, other than standard requirements  for the Applicant to prepare a Fire Safety Study and 
an Emergency Plan and to ensure the Applicant complies with  all relevant requirements in relation to the storage 
of chemicals, fuels or oils used on site (see Appendix E).

satisfied the proposal will meet the aims of the Vegetation in Non-Rural Areas SEPP.
