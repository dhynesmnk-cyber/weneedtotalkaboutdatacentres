Preface 
This assessment report provides a record of the Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure’s 
(the Department) assessment and evaluation of Macquarie Data Centres Pty Ltd  application to modify 
the State significant development (SSD)  consent for the Talavera Road Data Centre Campus 
Expansion, located in the City of Ryde local government area. The report includes: 
• a description of the proposed modification 
• an assessment of the modification against government policy and statutory requirements, 
including mandatory considerations 
• a demonstration of how matters raised by stakeholders have been considered 
• an assessment of the likely environmental, social and economic impacts of the modification 
• an evaluation which weighs up the likely impacts and benefits of the modification, having regard 
to the proposed mitigation measures and government advice, and provides a view on whether 
the impacts are, on balance, acceptable 
• a recommendation to the decision -maker, along with the reasons for the recommendation, to 
assist them in making an informed decision about whether the approval should be modified and 
any conditions that should be imposed.

1 Introduction 
The modification application seeks to modify the Talavera Road Data Centre Campus Expansion (SSD-
24299707) to allow for internal and external amendments to the design and layout  of the 
development, and an increase in capacity from 38 megawatts (MW) to 47 MW including associated 
increases in plant and equipment. 
The application was lodged on 15 August 2024 by Macquarie Data Centres Pty Ltd pursuant to section 
4.55(1A) of the Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979 (EP&A Act). 
A more detailed overview of the proposed modification is provided in Section 2. 
1.1 Background 
The Applicant is constructing an additional seven-stories to an existing data storage building at 17-
23 Talavera Road, Macquarie Park in the City of Ryde local government area (LGA) (see Figure 1 and 
Figure 2) as part of the approved Talavera Road Data Centre Campus Expansion (SSD-24299707). 
The Applicant is an Australian -based data centre operator, specialising in the delivery of secure, 
sovereign data storage services to Fortune 500 companies, large multi -national organisations and 
federal government agencies. It currently operates five faci lities across Canberra and Greater 
Sydney. 
The Applicant proposes to modify the development to increase the operational capacity , plant and 
equipment and configuration of the development as a result of opportunities identified during 
detailed design, to increase functionality, capacity, and spatial efficiency of the expansion. 
1.2 Subject Site 
The site comprises approximately 2 hectares (ha) of business-zoned land located at 17-23 Talavera 
Road, Macquarie Park in the City of Ryde LGA (the site). The site is legally described as Lot 527 DP 
752035 and Part Lot 3 DP 1043041. The site is located 12 kilometres (km) north west of the Sydney 
central business district (CBD) and approximately 12 km north east of the Parramatta CBD (see 
Figure 1).

The site is located in the Macquarie Park business precinct and is surrounded by a range of land uses,  
including:

Excelsia College, located to the south west.

Fontenoy Road, Macquarie Park (see Figure 2). The main residential areas within Macquarie Park are 
located approximately 310 m to the north-east and 500 m to the west of the site, respectively.

1.3 Approval History 
On 20 September 2019, development consent was granted by the NSW Land and Environment Court 
(by way of a Section 34 agreement) for alterations and additions to the existing data centre at the site 
(LDA2018/0322). The consent permits:  
• removal of 32 trees and site preparation works  
• construction and operation of Stage 1 of the project, comprising:  
o a data centre building extension with an overall height of 45 m and a gross floor area 
(GFA) of 6,731 square metres (m2)  
o provision of 101 car parking spaces  
• construction and operation of Stage 2 of the project, comprising: 
o a further building extension with an overall height of 45 m and a GFA of 6,731 m 2 
(overall GFA of 13,462 m2)  
o removal of 30 car parking spaces (71 spaces remaining).  
• associated infrastructure and landscaping 
Construction of Stage 1 of the project (as approved under LDA2018/0322) commenced in December 
2019 and was completed in March 2022 while construction of Stage 2 has not commenced.

Commission (the Commission)  for the development of the Talavera Road Data Centre Campus

efficiency by altering the approved layout.  The applicant identified that the proposed layout  would 
allow for a more usable and efficient floor plan, maximising available space and providing increased 
data storage capacity to meet updated demands of the data centres and artificial intelligence (AI) 
capability.

3.3 Biodiversity Development Assessment Report  
Section 7.17(2) of the Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016 (BC Act) requires all SSD modifications to be 
accompanied by a Biodiversity Development Assessment Report (BDAR) unless the authority or 
person determining the application is satisfied that the modification will not increase the impact on 
biodiversity values (as identified in the BC Act and in the Biodiversity Conservation Regulation 2017). 
As the proposed modifications do not change the footprint of works or propose any additional clearing 
to be undertaken, the Department is satisfied that the modification will not increase the impact on 
biodiversity values and consequently a BDAR is not required to accompany the modification 
application.

Findings and conclusions Recommended conditions 
• The AQIA predicted small additional exceedances of PM10 and PM2.5, 
and NO2 impacts at sensitive receivers when modelled against 
scenario 2, assuming emergency operation of all generators for one 
hour of every day of the year. However, the report notes that a more 
realistic scenario of emergency operation events lasting for one hour, 
once per year, exceedances are only likely once every 92 years for 
PM10, once every 183 years for PM2.5, and once every 2,190 years for 
NO2 at the nearest sensitive receiver, and less at further receivers.   
• Based on these findings the AQIA found the actual likelihood of an 
exceedance of the air quality criteria at nearby sensitive receptors 
due to the emergency operation of the development to be negligible. 
As a result, no mitigation measures were deemed necessary.  
• The Department notes existing conditions of consent require the 
Applicant to prepare and implement a Power Outage Notification 
Protocol to inform surrounding receivers in the event of a power 
failure event.

air quality impacts. The Department is satisfied existing conditions
