Executive Summary  
Introduction 
This report details the Department of Planning and Environment’s (the Department ) assessment of a 
State significant development (SSD) application for the Kemps Creek Data Centre.  ARUP Pty Ltd (the 
Applicant) proposes the construction and operation of a data centre located at 706-769 Mamre Road, 
Kemps Creek in the Penrith local government area (LGA).  
The development site is located in the Mamre Road Precinct (MRP) of the Western Sydney Employment 
Area (WSEA). The WSEA covers multiple LGAs and is progressively being development for 
employment generating industrial purposes, with warehousing and distribution centres a predominant 
use. The WSEA was expanded in 2020 to include the MRP, given the gr owing demand for industrial 
land and the area’s proximity to the new Western Sydney International (Nancy Bird Walton) Airport.  
Proposed development 
The SSD application seeks development consent to construct and operate a data centre comprising of 
two, two-storey data centre buildings including 60 data halls, an electrical switchyard, diesel fuel storage 
tanks, 62 emergency backup generators, 120 car parking spaces; and internal access roads, driveways 
and landscaping.  
The development would operate 24 hours a day, seven days a week. The primary purpose of the 
development is for the collection, storage, processing and distribution of electronic dat a by cloud and 
content providers or government entities.  
The development would be cooled using a system of evaporative cooling, air handling units (AHUs), 
chillers and cooling towers located on each floor of the data centre. The cooling system has been 
designed to maintain a constant temperature within the data halls throughout the year. Data centres 
also require sophisticated energy back-up systems to ensure the data centre operator’s customers are 
always able to access their data. To this end, the development includes a system of lithium-ion batteries 
and diesel back-up generators designed to minimise downtime during a power outage event.  
Site Context 
The site forms part of a new 91- hecatre industrial estate, known as The Yards, that is currently under 
construction and was approved under a separate State significant development application (SSD-9522) 
in December 2020. The Yards has a frontage to Mamre Road along the eastern boundary and adjoins 
Wianamatta-South Creek to the west. The site to be developed is Lot 2 within the subdivision approved 
under SSD-9522 on the southern most lot. Within the estate, the site will be adjacent to other industrial 
or warehouse land uses.  
The site is located approximately 40 kilometres (km) west of the Sydney CBD, 22 km west of Parramatta 
and 12 km south-east of Penrith within the Mamre Road precinct. The site has an area of approximately 
14.43 hectares (ha) and is zoned IN1 General Industrial under the State Environmental Planning Policy 
(Western Sydney Employment Area) . The nearest residential receivers to the site are located 
approximately 700 metres (m) to the west in Luddenham. There are also existing residences in the

MRP, which have been rezoned for industrial land uses. A group of schools and aged care facilities are 
also located within the MRP, approximately 1 km to 1.4 km to the east and north-east of the site.  
Current Proposal 
The development has a capital investment value of $1.29 billion and is expected to generate 300 
construction jobs and 79 operational jobs.   
Statutory Context 
The development is classified as State significant development (SSD) under Part 4 of the Environmental 
Planning and Assessment Act 1979 (EP&A Act) because it involves construction and operation of a 
data storage premise with a total power consumption of more than 10 MW, which meets the criteria in 
Clause 25 of Schedule 1 in State Environmental Planning Policy ( Planning Systems) 2021 (Planning 
Systems SEPP). Consequently, the Minister for Planning and Public Spaces is the consent authori ty 
for the proposed development under section 4.5(1) of the EP&A Act.  
Engagement 
The Department of Planning and Environment (the Department) exhibited the EIS for the development 
from Friday 19 November 2021 until Thursday 16 December 2021. During the exhibition period, the 
Department received no submissions from the public or special interest groups. The Department 
received advice from 12 government authorities, including Penrith City Council (Council).  
Key issues raised in the government advice related to design and visual impacts, development 
contributions, public road infrastructure, waterway health and bushfire risk. The Department requested 
the Applicant address the matters raised in submission s and government author ity advice  in a 
Response to Submissions (RTS). 
The Applicant submitted an RTS on 11 October 2022. The RTS was supported by revised technical 
assessments, updated architectural and landscaping plans, and a connection to country report.  
On 4 April 2022, Council’s Mamre Road Precinct Development Contributions Plan 2022 came into effect. 
The development site is not identified as being affected by the plan and therefore a Voluntary Planning 
Agreement was required to be entered into by the Applicant with Council to satisfy clause 66(2)(b) of 
the Environmental Planning and Assessment Regulation 2021 (EP&A Regulation)  before a 
determination could be made.  The Applicant engaged with Council in negotiating a VPA which was 
subsequently executed for the development on 7 June 2023.  
Assessment 
The Department’s assessment of the application has fully considered all relevant matters under section 
4.15 of the EP&A Act, the objects of the EP&A Act and the principles of ecologically sustainable 
development. The Department has identified the key issues for ass essment as visual impacts, design 
quality and noise. The Department has also assessed other relevant matters, including air quality, 
hazards and risks, bushfire and traffic impacts.  
Visual Impacts 
The development comprises two data centre buildings oriented east to west with lengths over 400 m 
and heights of 21 m. The bulk and scale of the development presents potential impacts on the visual

amenity of the direct locality including the sites interface with the RE1 zoned land adjacent to the 
development to the south. The Applicant prepared a landscaping and visual impact assessment (LVIA) 
to support the development application.  
The LVIA and architectural plans were updated to respond to advice provided by Council and the NSW 
Government Architect (NSW GA). T he updated visual and design documentation was also supported 
by a Connection to Country report which involved engagement with local Aboriginal parties and 
stakeholders. The Applicant incorporated outcomes from the Connection to Country engagement into

also considered the proposed development would b e generally consistent with the size and scale of 
surrounding approved and under construction industrial and warehouse development in the MRP. 
Council raised concern that the development may present visual impacts on residential receivers to the 
west of the development and the Department has subsequently recommended a condition of consent 
requiring additional façade and landscaping treatment to the western façade of the development  in 
consultation with Council.  
The Department’s assessment concludes the development would have an acceptable impact on visual 
amenity subject to the recommended conditions of consent and would be generally consistent with the 
scale and appearance of other industrial and warehouse development within the MRP.  
Noise Impacts 
Noise f rom the operation of the data centre, including air handling units (AHU), transformers and 
emergency back-up generators, may impact on residences in Luddenham if not carefully controlled 
through design and mitigation measures . The Applicant provided a nois e and vibration impact 
assessment (NVIA) that predicted noise levels from the proposed development, comparing against the 
noise limits of The Yards development consent (SSD -9522) and Project Noise Trigger Levels (PNTL) 
derived from background noise monitoring.  
The NVIA predicted the development would comply with the noise limits in The Yards consent and the 
PNTLs at the closest residential receivers in Luddenham and the rural residences in the MRP, with 
mitigation measures in place. However, the Department required the Applicant to assess the predicted 
noise levels against a more stringent amenity criteria based on noise contribution per area as a result 
of the significant increase in development within the MRP and intensification of noise sources.  
The Applicant undertook further investigation of additional noise mitigation and management measures 
to be incorporated to comply with the more stringent amenity criteria which was demonstrated in a 
revised NVIA. The revised NVIA concluded the development would be generally compliant with the 
stringent amenity criteria and have an acceptable impact on acoustic amenity.  
The Department’s assessment considere d the development would comply with the relevant noise 
criteria including The Yards and is consistent with th e objectives of the MRP. The Departm ent has 
recommended conditions of consent requiring the Applicant to undertake a design noise verification 
and validation reporting to ensure the development is compliant with the prescribed noise limits. A 
condition has also been included requiring the Applicant to prepare and implement an operation noise

noise. With these conditions in place, the Department’s assessment concludes the development would 
not have adverse impacts on sensitive receivers.  
Conclusion 
The Department’s assessment concluded that the impacts of the development can be mitigated and/or 
managed to ensure an acceptable level of environmental performance, subject to th e recommended 
conditions of consent.  
The development is not expected to have adverse visual amenity or operational noise impacts on 
existing and future receptors in the location, subject to the implementation of the proposed mitigation 
measures and the recommended conditions of consent. The development would involve investment of 
approximately $1.29 billion in the Penrith LGA and create 79 operational jobs.  
Consequently, the Department considers the development is in the public interest and should be 
approved, subject to conditions.

1 Introduction 
1.1 The Department’s Assessment 
This report details the Department of Planning and Environment’s (the Department) assessment of the 
State significant development (SSD -10101987) for the Kemps Creek  Data Centre. The proposed 
development (the development) involves the construction and operation of two, two-storey data centre 
buildings including 60 data halls, an electrical switchyard, diesel fuel storage tanks, 62 emergency 
backup generators, 120 car parking spaces; and internal access roads, driveways, and landscaping. 
Arup Pty Ltd (the Applicant) is a large-scale project design and delivery firm which has been approached 
by a data centre operator (the end user) who is seeking to expand its operations within Western Sydney. 
The end user currently operates multiple data centre assets within the Sydney Metropolitan area. The 
proposed development has been identified by the end user with the asset numbers SYD05, 06 & 07.  
The Department’s assessment considers all documentation submitted by the Applicant, including the 
Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) and R esponse to Submissions (R TS), submissions received 
from the public and advice from government agencies. The Department’s assessment also considers 
the legislation and planning instruments relevant to the site and the development. 
This report des cribes the development, surrounding environment, relevant strategic and statutory 
planning provisions and the issues raised in submissions. The report evaluates the key issues 
associated with the development and provides recommendations for managing any im pacts during 
construction and operation.  
1.2 Development Background 
The Applicant is seeking development consent to construct and operate a data centre comprising of 
two, two-storey data centre buildings including 60 data halls, an electrical switchyard, diesel fuel storage 
tanks, 62 emergency backup generators, 120 car parking spaces; and internal access roads, driveways 
and landscaping. The development would invol ve the storage of electronic data in associated 
information technology (IT) hardware, and would operate 24 hours a day, seven days a week.  
Data centers are places which are used by organisati ons and businesses to store their electronic 
computer applications and data. This could include personal data (such as social media data), corporate 
data (such as a business’s payroll system) or sensitive government data (such as tax and Medicare 
records).  
In recent years, there has been an increased need fo r new large and small-scale data centres across 
the State. As new technologies become commonplace, a significant number of new data centres will 
be required to store the amount of electronic data accessed and created by Australians.  
Arup Pty Ltd is a global advisory, planning, engineering, and architectural firm that specializes in the 
design and delivery of large-scale projects across the world such as data centre projects. Arup Pty Ltd 
have employees working across 140 countries around the world including Sydney, to deliver projects 
with its main business headquarters established in London, United Kingdom.  
The Applicant has been approached by a data centre operator (the end user) who is seeking to expand 
its operations within the Sydney region and in particular, Western Sydney. The development is to be 
facilitated by the Applicant and operated by the end user.

2.2 Physical Layout and Design 
The physical layout and design of the development is shown in Fig ures 4  to 6. The data centre 
comprises two parallel two- storey buildings , approximately 400 m long, oriented east to west , and 
separated by an internal service road. The development consists of three data centre components 
referred to as SYD05, SYD06 and S YD07 as identified in Figure 5. The development also includes a 
high voltage electrical switchyard at the east of the site, site access and security gate to the north-west, 
and landscaping in the south-west of the site.  
The ground floor of the data centre buildings comprises data halls and mechanical service areas to 
support the operation of the data centre. Associated plant such as back-up diesel generators are located 
externally alongside the data centre buildings. Three office and administration spaces are located on 
the ground floor levels of the development. The second storey comprise s data halls identical to the

2.3 Process description 
The primary purpose of the development is for the collection, storage, processing and distribution of 
electronic data in associated IT hardware by cloud and content providers or government entities.  
The data centre will be co nstructed on behalf of the future end user who will undertake a detailed fit-
out of the data halls including computer systems, server systems and networking equipment. This IT 
hardware would be stored in racking systems designed to maximise space efficiency.  
The development would be cooled using a direct evaporative cooling system which introduces outside 
air directly into the data hall when outside air temperatures are below 10 degrees Celsius ( oC). 
Evaporative cooling would be used when a ir temperatures exceed 30 oC. The development will 

[[page 19]]
 
Kemps Creek Data Centre (SSD-1010198SSD/SSI XXXX7) | Assessment Report 10 
additionally utilise air-cooled chillers and water-cooled chillers to ensure data halls maintain a consistent 
low temperature throughout the year.  
Data centres also require sophisticated back -up energy syst ems to ensure IT hardware remains 
operational and data access is uninterrupted. To this end, the development includes a system of lithium-
ion batteries and diesel back -up generators designed to minimise downtime during a power outage 
event.  
The site would be serviced by an Endeavour Energy feed cable connection to the proposed high voltage 
electrical switchyard. In the event of a full power outage (Endeavour Energy electrical conn ection is 
offline), lithium-ion batteries located within the data halls would provide an immediate and uninterrupted 
power supply until the back-up generator system reaches full load.  
Once full load has been achieved, 63 diesel back -up generators would be used to provide continuous 
power to the development until power has been res tored by Endeavour Energy. The Applicant 
anticipates the likelihood of a power outage event to be 0.05% per year.  
To ensure the development remains ready and capable to function during a power outage event, the 
Applicant will undertake regular load testing of each back -up generator. During each test, an artificial 
load would be applied to generators using an electrical switching device. The test scenarios would be 
as follows: 
• Unloaded Maintenance Testing – Maximum test length of 10 minutes, bi-monthly 
• Loaded Maintenance Testing (70%) – Maximum test length of 30 minutes, quarterly 
• Maximum Load Testing (100%) – Maximum test length of 1 hour, annually.  
No more than 2 generators would be tested at one time during unloaded maintenance testing and only 
1 generator during loaded and maximum load testing, and all testing would occur during daytime hours.

Applicant’s Assessment 
The NVIA included a quantitative assessment of construction noise in accordance with the EPA’s NSW

Stage 2: Pavement and road works – 3 months 
Stage 3: Building construction – 10 months 
Stage 4: Plant installation and connection – 2 months

and equipment are operating concurrently over a fifteen- minute period. The assessment predicted 
exceedance at residential receivers during Stages 1 to 3 of construction with the highest predict noise

The NVIA noted a Construction Noise and Vibration Management Plan (CNVMP) would be prepared 
prior to constructio n to specify plant and scheduling of construction activities and subsequent noise 
control and mitigation measures. Nonetheless, the NVIA provided preliminary mitigation measures 
including noise shielding of stationary construction equipment and the schedul ing of high noise 
generating activities.  
Department’s consideration 
Noise during construction has been conservatively assessed against a worst-case scenario which has 
predicted exceedances at receivers during construction stages. The Department notes the 
exceedances are not considered to be ‘highly affected’ and that the Applicant has provided preliminary 
mitigation measures to reduce impacts on the noise amenity of receivers. Furthermore, the Department 
understands the most noise generating periods of con struction are relatively short -term and can be 
mitigated through the implementation of respite periods where necessary.  
The Department h as recommended conditions requiring a Construction Noise Management Plan be 
prepared to demonstrate measures to be unde rtaken to achieve the noise management levels of the 
EPA’s NSW Interim Construction Noise Guidelines. The Department also recommends  conditions 
requiring the Applicant  to coordinate construction works with other neighbouring developments to 
ensure cumulative noise impacts are minimised, and to consult with residences through construction 
and develop management strategies for minimising the impacts of high noise generating works.  
Operational Impacts 
Applicant’s Assessment 
The Applicant provided a noise and vibration impact assessment (NVIA) which was updated in the RTS 
to address matters raised by the Department. The NVIA predicted noise levels from both a standard 
operating scenario of the data centre and a worst-case emergency operating scenario where all back-
up generators are operating concurrently during a critical power failure event.  
The NVIA also considered traffic noise impacts which noted the development would generate a 
maximum of 10 service vehicle (19m B-doubles) and 119 light vehicle trips per day. This is lower than 
the amount for the site  assessed for The Yards. The NVIA concluded the development  is unlikely to 
result in an increase in road traffic noise, and the traffic volumes of the development accounted for in 
the assessments of road upgrade projects across the MRP.  
The predicted noise levels of the two modelled operating scenarios were compared against the noise 
limits in The Yards development consent. The NVIA additionally considered the schools and retirement 
village located 1 km to the north-east, passive recreation areas immediately west and south of the 
development and receivers to the north.  
The proposed data centre would operate 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. The primary noise sources 
include back-up generator testing,  transformers, cooling plant such as air handling units (AHU) and 
vehicle movements on-site. The NVIA notes back-up generator testing is to only occur during daylight 
hours for intervals of between 10 minutes up to 1 hour, with a maximum of two generators tested at any 
one time. The NVIA also noted that a conservative assessment of plant and equipment noise sources

to vary based on internal and external temperatures.  
In addition, the largest vehicles to access the site,  being 19 m articulated vehicles (AV) for deliveries 
and maintenance, will also only occur during daytime hours of operation.  
The NVIA predicted the development would comply with the noise limits in The Yards consent at all 
residential receivers assessed. The NVIA predicted noise levels of 28 to 31 dB(A) at the two residential 
receivers assessed in Luddenham. The NVIA also demonstrated the development will comply with the 
relevant criteria for the schools, retirement village, passive recreation and industrial receivers.

The NVIA calculated a project amenity noise level of 33 dB(A)  during the night-time period based on 
the alternative approach, which the proposed development was predicted to comply with.  
The noise mitigation measures incorporated for operational plant acoustic treatment include enclosures, 
noise attenuators, noise barriers and acoustic absorptive material lining of plant rooms. Additional 
mitigation measures have been proposed for the building envelope including insulated and acoustic 
treated materials, and acoustic louvres for data hall exhaust outlets.

The Department has considered the development on its merits, taking into consideration strategic plans

from relevant government agencies, Council and utility providers.  
The development involves the construction and operation of a data centre with an operational capacity 
of 190 MW which will help to satisfy market demand for data storage infrastructure and capacity within 
western Sydney. The development will ensure the ongoing data storage needs of greater Sydney and 
the State are addressed.  
In addition, the development will provide up to 300  construction jobs and 79  operational jobs, and 
represents a direct investment of more than $1.29 billion in the Penrith LGA. The proposal is consistent 
with the key objectives of the Regional Plan and the WSEA, as it will help to facilitate technology industry 
development within the industrial-zoned land of the MRP.  
The Applicant has undertaken thorough and considered con sultation with relevant Aboriginal 
community members and stakeholders to achieve the objectives of connection to country framework 
and has subsequently incorporated outcomes of the consultation into the design of the development 
and landscaping.  
The Department’s assessment identified the proposal could potentially have visual amenity impacts on 
the locality and also potentially generate amenity impacts during operations, primarily around noise and 
air. The Department has considered the information provided in the revised NIA, AQIA, LVIA and 
supporting documentation and is satisfied impacts associated with the development can be adequately 
managed subject to conditions  of consent and the implementation of recommended mitigation and 
management measures.  
Furthermore, the Department has recommended a r ange of stringent conditions to minimise these 
impacts, including: 
• the preparation of a design noise verification and nois e validation reporting to ensure the 
development meets the prescribed noise limits; and 
• implementation of a NDMP during power outage events, which will include control measures to 
minimise the duration of each event and respond to any observed exceedances of the relevant NO2 
criterion.  
• the preparation of a design report  in consultation with Council,  for the implementation of further 
façade treatment options to further reduce the visual impact on sensitive visual receivers. 
 
The Department has also recommended a range of detailed conditions to address any residual hazards, 
traffic and visual impacts associated with the construction and op eration of the development. These 
conditions were informed by the recommendations of the relevant government agencies.
