Executive Summary 
HDI SYD1 Property Holdings Limited (the Applicant) proposes the expansion of an existing data centre 
facility at 390 -422 Harris Street and 273 Pyrmont Street, Ultimo in the City of Sydney  local 
government area (LGA).  
The development has a n estimated development cost (EDC)  of $ 605.6 million and is expected to 
generate up to 80 construction jobs and retention of 45 operational jobs. 
The project seeks to respond to growing demand for secure, high-performance digital infrastructure 
in the Sydney metropolitan area by improving the capacity and efficiency of an existing data centre 
facility. The site is located approximately 1.2 kilometres (km) southwest of the Sydney CBD and covers 
1.1 hectares (ha) of land within the Darling Harbour precinct under the State Environmental Planning 
Policy (Precincts – Eastern Harbour City) 2021 (Eastern Harbour City SEPP) and currently comprising 
the existing DigiCo SYD1 data centre facility.  
Surrounding land uses include a mix of low density, medium density and high density residential within 
the Ultimo and Pyrmont area, entertainment and commercial development within the Darling Harbour 
precinct and recreational development. The nearest residential receivers are located adjacent the 
development on Harris Street and Quarry Street.  
The development involves the expansion o f the existing data centre facility  and reconfiguration of 
existing internal floor space to plant and equipment space including an increase in building height of 
12 metres (m) to a maximum of 54.425 metres (m) and an overall reduction of site GFA by 3,600 square 
metres (m2) to a total of 41,190 m 2. The facility would have an increased power consumption to 120 
MW ( 88  MW  IT Lo ad)  and would be supported by coolers, pumps, back -up diesel generators, bulk 
diesel storage and lithium-ion battery storage. 
The Department exhibited the development application and accompanying Environmental Impact 
Statement (EIS) for the development from 2 May 2025 until 29 May 2025 and received 25 submissions 
from the public (three  special interest groups, one business organisation and 21 individuals), a 
submission from City of Sydney Council (Council) and advice from six government authorities, one 
State-owned corporation and one utility provider. Around 22 public submissions objected to the 
proposal and 3 public submissions provided comments only.  
Key issues raised by the public related to overshadowing, visual impacts, noise impacts, air quality/ 
pollution, traffic and parking, sustainability, construction impacts, fire safety and community 
consultation. The Environment Protection Authority (EPA) provided a number of comments regarding 
noise and air quality modelling and Council provided comments relating to solar access  and 
overshadowing, street tree protection and landscaping.

The Department’s assessment focused on three key issues being building design and visual impacts, 
noise impacts and air quality impacts.  
The Department considered the design of the expansion was consistent with the existing facility and 
building form of the broader Darling Harbour Precinct . The Department also considered that the 
expansion had been appropriately setback from the existing building line to reduce the potential for 
view loss and overshadowing of surrounding sensitive receivers. The Applicant’s view loss and s olar 
access reports have suitably demonstrated the impacts of the development on surrounding receivers 
would be minimal compared to the existing development.  
The Department is satisfied the construction noise impacts can be mitigated through adaption of 
standard construction hours and the implementation of a site -specific Construction Noise and 
Vibration Management Plan. The development’s operational noise modelling confirmed compliance 
with the established project noise trigger levels at all receivers du ring normal operations, subject to 
measures such as limiting generator testing, acoustic treatment of external plant and equipment, and 
maintaining sound power levels as modelled.  
Air quality impacts during construction would be negligible, subject to the implementation of 
standard dust suppression measures under a Construction Environmental Management Plan required 
by conditions of consent . Operational emissions from the proposed back -up generators were also 
assessed, during both standard testing and emergency outage scenarios. No exceedances of relevant 
criteria were predicted during the testing scenario, and while conservative modelling indicated 
potential exceedances of particula te matter (PM 2.5) and nitrogen dioxide (NO 2) during a prolonged 
emergency event, the likelihood of such an event is extremely low. Conditions of consent will require 
the Applicant to implement an Air Quality Management Plan to emissions from generator use and a 
neighbour notification protocol to help manage any residual impacts during a power outage event. 
The Department considers the development is consistent with the Greater Sydney Region Plan,  
Eastern City District Plan (District Plan) and the Pyrmont Peninsula Place Strategy (Place Strategy) , 
which aim to increase productivity and local jobs within the City of Sydney LGA . The development 
would deliver significant economic benefits, including $ 605.6 million in capital investment and 
employment opportunities, while increasing critical digital infrastructure and efficiencies of  an 
existing data centre facility.  
The Department concludes the development would not result in significant environment or amenity 
impacts, subject to recommended conditions, and is in the public interest. Accordingly, the 
Department considers the development is approvable, subject to conditions.

1 Introduction 
1.1 Development Background 
HDI SYD1 Property Holdings Limited (DigiCo) (the Applicant) is seeking development consent for the 
expansion of an existing data centre development (the development). A detailed description of the 
development is provided in Section  2. 
Data centres are secure facilities used by organisations to store, process and manage electronic data 
and digital applications using specialised information technology (IT) infrastructure. This may include 
personal data (such as social media content or cloud photo libraries), corporate data (such as financial 
systems or customer records), or sensitive government data (such as health and taxation information). 
Increasingly, data centres also support high -performance computing tasks, including artificial 
intelligence (AI), machine learning and real-time analytics. 
In recent years, demand for both large-scale and edge data centres have grown significantly across 
New South Wales. This growth is being driven by the rapid adoption of digital technologies, including 
AI, autonomous systems, cloud computing and next -generation mobile networks. As these 
technologies become more embedded in everyday life and business operations, additional data centre 
capacity is required to support the increasing volume, speed and complexity of data generated and 
accessed by Australians. 
The Applicant is an asset management group which specialises in real estate, private equity, energy 
transition and digital infrastructure. The Applicant has recently purchased the DigiCo digital 
infrastructure company including the existing SYD1 data centre at Ultimo. The Applicant is seeking to 
expand the existing facility’s operational capacity.  
1.2 Site Description 
The site comprises 1.1 hectares (ha) of land within the Darling Harbour precinct  located at 390-422 
Harris Street and 273 Pyrmont Street, Ultimo within the City of Sydney Local Government Area (LGA) 
(the site) and is legally described as Lot 1 DP 109652 and Lot 1 DP 1189030 (see Figure 1).  
The site is accessible for pedestrians via Harris Street and vehicle access is provided to the site via 
Quarry Street and Pyrmont Street. The site is situated next  to the Western Distributor and the City 
West Link and has direct connection to George Street/ A22 (Parrama tta Road and Hume Highway) , 
providing connectivity to the regional road network. Public transport services, including high -
frequency bus routes, operate along Harris Street an d Broadway, while Central station is located 
approximately 1.2 kilo metres (km) to the east of the site providing local and regional train services  
(see Figure 2).

2.2 Physical Layout and Design 
Physical layout and design of the development is shown in Figure 5 to Figure 8 below. The data centre 
expansion presents as a vertical extension to the existing data centre buildings comprising of two 
new levels, mezzanine levels and roof structures. The extension is proposed to be constructed of a 
steel framed system to reduce structural mass  on the existing structure  and include a raised 
webforge floor system with steel posts and beams to house additional plant and equipment such as 
generators, cooling towers and chillers.  
The built form extension would result in a maximum building height of 54.425 m being an increase of 
12 m  from the existing building height. The proposed expansion will also entail the conversion of 
approximately 3,600 m2 of existing floor space of the buildings into additional electrical plant rooms 
and mechanical equipment space.  
The extension works will be constructed of pre- finished metallic wall cladding panels and louvres. 
The materials and finishes are proposed to be consistent with the existing architectural design and 
features of the SYD1E component of the development.  
The architectural design of the development has been informed by advice received by the 
Government Architect NSW (GANSW) through the State Design Review Panel (SDRP) process to 
achieve design excellence requirements.

2.3 Process Description 
The primary purpose of the development is for the collection, storage, processing and distribution of 
electronic data in associated IT hardware by cloud, content providers, digital services providers or 
government entities (the tenant/s).  
The data centre operator will fit out the development’s data halls with its own computer systems, 
server systems and networking equipment. This IT hardware would be stored in r acks designed to 
maximise the efficiency of the space. The data centre operator will then lease storage space within 
these systems to its tenant/s.  
The development’s data halls would be cooled using water-cooled chillers and cooling tower system 
which involves heat rejection through evaporation. The cooling system has been designed to maintain 
a constant temperature within the data halls while balancing energy and water demands for cooling. 
The ancillary office space would primarily be used by on-site technicians, would provide IT support to 
external users and undertake regular maintenance as necessary.  
T h e  s i t e  i s  c u r r e n t l y  s e r v i c e d  b y  s i x  3 3  k i l o v olt (kV) feeder cables from the existing Ausgrid HV 
substation at Pyrmont. No additional electrical infrastructure is required to service the proposed 
expansion of the site. However, re-routing of feeder cables to bypass Union Street is proposed to be 
undertaken separately to reduce the potential for network congestion.  
Data centres also require sophisticated back-up power systems to ensure the data centre operator’s 
customers are always able to access their data and ensure digital systems such as online banking and 
public transport systems always remain functional. To this end, the development includes a system 
of lithium-ion batteries and diesel back-up generators designed to minimise downtime during a power 
outage event.  
To ensure the development can function during a power outage event, the Applicant would also 
undertake regular testing of each back-up generator. During certain tests, an artificial load would be 
applied to the generators using and electrical switching dev ice. The typical routine maintenance

2.4 Applicant’s Justification for the Development 
The Applicant has justified the need for the development by identifying the increasing demand for 
secure, high-capacity data storage and processing infrastructure within close proximity to the Sydney 
CBD. The proposed expansion of the existing data centre facility would contribute to meeting this 
demand in a strategic location without the need for additional land and building footprint within the 
Sydney CBD urban context. The proposed expansion would also facilitate improvements to energy 
and performance efficiencies of the existing data centre operations  through the upgrading of plant 
and equipment and floor space reconfiguration.  
In addition, the Applicant has advised the development would: 
• maintain the existing use of the development and preserve its iconic built-form 
• support the retention and growth of digital infrastructure within the Sydney metropolitan area 
• generate additional employment opportunities in Sydney, comprising approximately 80 full-time 
equivalent construction jobs and retention of 45 operational jobs.

6.2 Noise 
The construction and 24 -hour operation of the development has the potential to emit noise, which 
could impact on the acoustic amenity of the surrounding area.  
The development would involve the vertical expansion of an existing data centre by 12 m, including 
internal reconfiguration of existing building floor area  over a 21-month period. Working hours are 
proposed between 7:00am and 6:00pm Monday to Friday, between 8:00am and 1:00pm on Saturday 
and no works on Sundays or public holidays, in line with the standard construction hours in the EPA’s 
Interim Construction Noise Guideline (ICNG) (DECC, 2009).

the risk to impact on amenity at surrounding receivers to be low, noting the nature of the development 
as a vertical expansion with no soil disturbance or groundwork  activities are to be undertaken . The 
AQIA considered that construction air quality impacts can be appropriately managed through 
standard construction mitigation and management measures, and via the implementation of a 
Construction Environmental Management Plan (CEMP).  
The Department has reviewed the AQIA and is satisfied air emissions associated with the construction 
of the development can be suitably managed through the preparation and implementation of a CEMP 
containing standard construction mitigation measures including dust screens, covering of loads and 
plant and equipment maintenance and selection.  
The Department has recommended conditions of consent requiring the Applicant to prepare and 
implement a CEMP for the duration of construction and to take all reasonable steps to minimise the 
generation of dust at the site.

6.3.3 Conclusion  
The Department’s assessment concludes the air emissions associated with the construction of the 
development would be minimal and can be appropriately managed through the preparation and 
implementation of a CEMP in conjunction with all reasonable steps to minimise dust generation.  
In addition, the Department’s assessment concludes the Applicant has sufficiently demonstrated 
through a conservative air quality analysis that the day -to-day operation of the development 
(including regular generator testing) will be compliant with the rel evant criteria at all receiver 
locations and therefore have adequately addressed concerns raised in public submission rega rding 
diesel generator pollution and existing site performance. The Department considers these emissions 
can be appropriately managed and mitigated through the implementation of a site-specific AQMP. 
Furthermore, the Department accepts the likelihood of a critical power outage event occurring to be 
highly unlikely and notes that any associated air quality impacts can be appropriately managed 
through the recommended conditions of consent. This would inc lude annual testing of emissions 
performance, pollutant monitoring and the continual implementation of management and mitigation 
measures to further reduce pollutant emissions throughout the life of the development.  
Therefore, the Department is satisfied that, subject to the implementation of the recommended 
conditions and the Applicant’s proposed management and mitigation measures, the construction and 
operation of the development would not result in unacceptable air quality impacts on surrounding 
sensitive receivers.

7 Evaluation 
The Department’s assessment of the application has fully considered all relevant matters under 
section 4.15 of the EP&A Act, the objects of the EP&A Act and the principles of ESD.  
The Department has considered the development on its merits, taking into consideration strategic 
plans that guide development in the area, the EPIs that apply to the development, advice received 
from the relevant public authorities, including Council, and submissions from the public. 
The development involves the expansion of an existing data centre  with an increase in operational 
capacity to 88 MW of IT load which will help to satisfy market demand for data storage infrastructure 
and capacity within close proximity to the Sydney CBD. 
During the exhibition period, several members of the community including community groups, 
objected to the development. The Department required the Applicant to prepare a Submissions 
Report to address issues raised by the public, Council and government agencies. The Department has 
sought to address these issues raised through consultation with both the government authorities, 
Council and the Applicant. Although some minor issues were raised by government authorities or 
Council in their advice, these matters  have been resolved through the provision of additional 
information by the Applicant during the assessment process.  
The Department’s assessment has found there would be some amenity impacts during operation of  
the data centre, such as noise and air quality and impacts to visual and solar amenity from the vertical 
expansion of the facility. The Department has considered the information provided in the revised NIA, 
AQIA, VIA and supporting documentation and is satisfied the Applicant has designed the development 
to minimise these impacts. Residual impacts can be adequately mitigated and managed subject to the 
implementation of the Applicant’s mitigation and management measures and the recommended 
conditions of consent.  
In particular, the Department’s recommended conditions include: 
• the preparation of noise verification reporting to ensure the development meets the prescribed 
noise limits 
• the preparation and implementation of an Air Quality Management Plan including annual 
emissions testing and reporting, and a power outage notification protocol 
• the preparation and implementation of a FERP for construction and operation to ensure safety 
of site occupants during flood events 
Overall, the Department’s assessment has concluded the development would: 
• provide additional data storage capacity within Greater Sydney within an existing data centre
