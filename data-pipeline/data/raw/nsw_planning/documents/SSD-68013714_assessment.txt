Preface 
This report details the Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure’s  (the Department) 
assessment of the State significant development (SSD) application for the 43-61 Turner Road Data 
Centre (the development). 
The Department’s assessment considers all documents submitted by Arup Pty Ltd  (the Applicant), 
including the Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) and Submissions Report , submissions received 
from the public and Camden Council, advice from government a uthorities,  a n d  a l l  l e g i s l a t i o n  a n d  
planning instruments relevant to the site and the development. 
The report includes: 
• a description of the development and the surrounding environment 
• an assessment of the development against government policy and statutory requirements, 
including mandatory considerations  
• an explanation of why the development is SSD and who the consent authority is 
• consideration of matters raised by the community and other stakeholders 
• an assessment of the likely environmental, social and economic impacts of the development 
and recommendations for managing any impacts during construction and operation 
• an evaluation which weighs up the likely impacts and benefits of the development, having 
regard to the proposed mitigation measures, offsets, community views and government advice, 
and provides a view on whether the impacts are, on balance, acceptable 
• a recommendation to the decision-maker, along with the reasons for the recommendation, to 
assist them in making an informed decision about whether development consent for the 
development should be granted and any conditions that should be imposed.

Executive Summary 
Arup Pty Ltd (the Applicant) proposes to construct and operat e a data centre  with associated 
infrastructure, substation and ancillary office space at 43-61 Turner Road, Gregory Hills within the 
Camden local government area (LGA). The development has an estimated cost of development (EDC) 
of $800 million and is expected to generate up to  100 full-time equivalent construction jobs and 50 
operational jobs. 
The project seeks to respond to growing demand for secure, high-performance digital infrastructure 
in the Sydney metropolitan area. The site is located approximately 45 kilometres (km) southwest of 
the Sydney CBD and covers approximately 9.74 hectares (ha) of IN1 General Industrial  zoned land 
under the State Environmental Planning Policy (Western Parkland City) 2021 (WPC SEPP)  and is 
currently vacant.  
Surrounding land uses  include warehouse and distribution facilities, light industries and other data 
centre development within the Turner Road precinct . The nearest residential receivers are located 
approximately 150 metres (m) to the east of the site, in the Gregory Hills residential area.  
The development involves a 24 -hour data centre  containing 12 data halls over two -storeys, a gross 
floor area of 14,941 m 2, and a maximum building height of 23 m. The facility would have a total of 
power consumption of 61.7 megawatts (MW) (53 MW IT Load)  and would be supported by coolers, 
pumps, back-up diesel generators, bulk diesel storage and lithium-ion battery storage.  
The Department exhibited the development application and accompanying Environmental Impact 
Statement (EIS)  for the development  from 5 March 2 0 2 5  u n t i l  1  A p r i l  2025 and received four 
submissions from the public (individuals), a submission from Camden Council and advice from eight 
government authorities and State-owned corporations, and advice from one utility provider.  
Key issues raised by the public relate d to the location of the development to residential receivers , 
noise, air quality, traffic , visual impacts  and fire safety. Council provided a number of comments 
relating to building height, civil engineering and stormwater drainage, flooding, building certification, 
traffic, landscaping, biodiversity and environmental health.  
The Department’s assessment focused on three key issues being noise, air quality  and, design and 
visual impacts. The Department is satisfied the construction noise impacts can be mitigated through 
standard construction hours and the implementation of a site -specific Construction Noise and 
Vibration Management Plan. The development’s operational noise modelling of confirmed compliance 
with the established project noise trigger levels at all receivers during normal operations, subject to 
measures such as limiting generator testing, acoustic treatment of external plant and equipment, and 
maintaining sound power levels as modelled.

Air quality impacts during construction would be negligible, subject to the implementation of 
standard dust suppression measures under the development’s Construction Environmental

were predicted during the testing scenario, and while conservative modelling indicated potential 
exceedances of particulate matter (PM2.5) and nitrogen dioxide (NO2) during a prolonged emergency 
event, the likelihood of such an event is extremely low. Conditions of consent will require the

The size and scale of the  development would not be out of place with the existing character of the

r e s i d e n t i a l  a r e a s  t o  t h e  s o u t h e a s t  o f  t h e  s i t e  as vegetation screening from the existing riparian

The Department considers the development is consistent with the Greater Sydney Region Plan , the

The Department concludes the development would not result in significant environment or amenity

Department considers the development is approvable, subject to conditions.

1.5 Project Sector Description  
Data centres are secure facilities used by organisations to store, process and manage electronic data 
and digital applications using specialised information technology (IT) infrastructure. This may include 
personal data (such as social media content or cloud photo libraries), corporate data (such as financial 
systems or customer records), or sensitive government data (such as health and taxation information). 
Increasingly, data centres also support high -performance computing tasks, including artificial 
intelligence (AI), machine learning and real-time analytics. 
In recent years, demand for both large -scale and edge data centres has grown significantly across 
New South Wales. This growth is being driven by the rapid adoption of digital technologies, including 
AI, autonomous systems, cloud computing and next -generation mobile networks. As these 
technologies become more embedded in everyday life and business operations, additional data centre 
capacity is required to support the increasing volume, speed and complexity of data generated and 
accessed by Australians.

2.3 Process Description 
The primary purpose of the development is for the collection, storage, processing and distribution of 
electronic data in associated IT hardware by cloud, content providers, digital services providers or 
government entities (the tenant/s).  
The data centre operator will fit out the development’s data halls with its own computer systems, 
server systems and networking equipment. This IT hardware would be stored in r acks designed to 
maximise the efficiency of the space. The data centre operator will then lease storage space within 
these systems to its tenant/s.  
The development’s data halls would be cooled using a hybrid system of fresh air free-cooling systems 
during cooler weather and evaporative cooling during hotter weather . The cooling system has been 
designed to maintain a constant temperature within the data halls while minimising water demands 
for cooling. The ancillary office space would primarily be used by on-site technicians. 
Data centres also require sophisticated back-up power systems to ensure the data centre operator’s 
customers are always able to access their data and ensure digital systems such as online banking and 
public transport systems always remain functional. To th is end, the development includes a system 
of lithium-ion batteries and diesel back-up generators designed to minimise downtime during a power 
outage event.  
The site is proposed to be supplied power through two separate 132 kilovault (kV) supply routes from

4.6 Biodiversity Development Assessment Report  
Section 7.9(2) of the Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016  (BC Act) requires al l SSD applications to be 
accompanied by a Biodiversity Development Assessment Report (BDAR) unless the Planning Agency 
Head and the Environment Agency Head determine that the development is not likely to have any 
significant impact on biodiversity values  ( a s  i d e n t i f i e d  i n  t h e  B C  A c t  a n d  i n  t h e  Biodiversity 
Conservation Regulation 2017). 
However, section 7.6 of the BC Act separately notes that a BDAR or BDAR waiver is not required for 
an SSD application if the development is proposed to be carried out on ‘biodiversity certified land’ as 
described in Part 8 of the Act.  
The site is located within the Oran Park and Turner Road Precinct Plan of the WSC SEPP,  under the 
now repealed State Environmental Planning Policy (Sydney Region Growth Centres) 2006  in which the 
site had previously been provided with biodiversity certification  under Part 7 of S che dule  7 of th e  
Threatened Species Conservation Act 1995 . On 6 June 2025, the Minister for Environment signed an 
order extending the biodiversity certification of the g rowth centres under the repealed State 
Environmental Planning Policy (Sydney Region Growth Centres) 2006 until 30 June 2026.  
Given the above (and in accordance with section 7.6 of the BC Act), the Department is satisfied the 
development is proposed to be carried out on ‘biodiversity certified land’ as described in Part 8 of the 
BC Act and, consequently, a BDAR is not required for the subject SSD application.

The construction and 24 -hour operation of the development has the potential to emit noise , which 
could impact on the acoustic amenity of the surrounding area.  
The development would involve the construction of a two -storey data centre building with ancillary

7:00am and 6:00pm Monday to Friday, between 8:00am and 1:00pm on Saturday and no works on

Construction Noise Guideline (ICNG) (DECC, 2009).  
The development’s main operational noise sources would include air handling units, rooftop fans, 
air-cooled condensers, discharge condensers and emergency back -up generators. Other sources of 
operational noise include vehicle movements to and from the site and manoeuvring around the site.
