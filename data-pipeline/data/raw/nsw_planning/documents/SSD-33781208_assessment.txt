Executive Summary 
This report details the Department of Planning and Environment’s (the Department’s) assessment of a 
State significant development (SSD) application for the Station Road Data Centre Expansion 
(SSD-33781208). Lehr Consultants International (Australia) Pty Ltd  (the Applicant) proposes the 
construction and operation of an expansion to an existing data centre located at 57 Station Road, Seven 
Hills in the Blacktown local government area (LGA).  
The Applicant is an engineering and design consult ancy which specialises in providing sustainable, 
tailor-made design solutions to its clients. It acts on behalf of a multinational data centre operator who 
would construct and manage the expansion.  
The site has an area of 2.57 hectares and is located 6.8 kilometres (km) north-west of the Parramatta 
central business district (CBD) and approximately 3.8 km east of the Blacktown CBD . The rear of the 
site adjoins Blacktown Creek, while an Endeavour Energy transmission line and easement crosses over 
the south-eastern corner of the site.  
The nearest residential receivers are located approximately 45 metres (m) to the south-east of the site, 
along Edna Avenue in the residential area of Toongabbie. The site also adjoins McCoy Park, which is 
located in the Parramatta LGA.  
In early 2022, the Applicant obtained approval from Blacktown City Council (Council) for the 
construction and operation of a 1.2-megawatt (MW) data centre on the southern  section of the site, 
adjoining Station Road. The subject SSD application would be located in the northern section of the 
site, and would rely on the finished site levels, stormwater management system and site access 
arrangements previously approved by Council.  
Proposed development  
The SSD application seeks development consent to construct and operate an expansion to the 
Council-approved data centre, comprising additional data halls, associated office space, supporting 
infrastructure, new handstand areas (including a perimeter access road and carparking) and 
landscaping.  
The development would be located towards the rear of the site and would operate 24 hours a day, 
seven days a week. The primary purpose of the development is for the collection, storage, p rocessing 
and distribution of electronic data by cloud and content providers or government entities.  
The development would be cooled using a system of air handling units (AHUs) located on each floor of 
the data centre. The cooling system has been designed  to maintain a constant temperature within the 
data halls throughout the year.  
Data centres also require sophisticated energy back -up systems to ensure the data centre operator’s 
customers are always able to access their data. To this end, the development includes a system of 
lithium-ion batteries and diesel back -up generators design ed to minimise downtime during a power 
outage event. The Applicant may also be directed to operate the back -up generator system by the 
Australian Energy Market Operator (AEMO) to help prevent major blackouts from occurring across the 
state’s electricity network.

The development would see the site’s overall power consumption increase from 1.2 MW to 20.4 MW, 
while the number of diesel back-up generators on-site would increase from 3 to 12.  
Justification and need  
The development would help the data centre operator respond to the ongoing demand for cloud-based 
data storage space within western Sydney.  
The data centre operator requires a number of sites throughout Sydney that are well -connected to 
existing utility infrastructure, including pow er and optic fibre cabling. The development would help the 
data centre operator to expand its presence within the city, while also supporting the ongoing demand 
for internet usage and data storage across the state.  
Statutory context  
The development is classified as SSD pursuant to section 4.36 of the Environmental Planning and 
Assessment Act 1979 (EP&A Act) because it involves the construction and operation of a data centre 
with a total power consumption of more than 10 MW, which meets the criteria in clause 25 of Schedule 
1 in State Environmental Planning Policy (Planning Systems) 2021. Consequently, the Minister for 
Planning is the consent authority for the SSD application.  
Engagement  
The Department exhibited the SSD application and the Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) for the 
development from 11 July 2022 until 8 August 2022 . During the exhibition period, the Department 
received two submissions from the public, two submissions from utility providers, two submissions from 
local councils and advice from four government authorities. While Council originally objected to the 
SSD application, it formally withdrew its objection to the development on 6 October 2022.   
Key issues raised by the local councils and in government advice related to compliance with Council’s 
development control plan, cumulative noise and air quality impacts and the suitability of the proposed 
landscaping scheme.  The public submissions raised concerns in relation to the potential impacts 
associated with the back -up generator system (noise, air quality, etc) and increased traffic in the 
surrounding area.  
The Department subsequently requested the Applicant address the matters raised in submissions and 
government advice in a Response to Submissions (RTS) report. The Applicant submitted an RTS report 
on 19 September 2022 . The RTS was supported by a number of revised technical assessment s and 
plans, including an updated acoustic report, air quality assessment and landscape plans.  
Assessment  
The Department’s assessment of the SSD application has fully considered all relevant matters under 
section 4.15 of the EP&A Act, the objects of the EP&A Act and the principles of ecologically sustainable 
development.  
The key environmental issues associated with the development related to noise and vibration and air 
quality impacts.   
The Department has considered the information provided in the revised acoustic report and is satisfied 
construction noise and vibration impacts associated with the development can be adequately managed, 
subject to the implementation of standard construction hours and the noise management and mitigation 
measures proposed by the Applicant. While construction noise exceedances have been predicted at

several nearby residential receivers, the Department is satisfied the associated works would be short-
term and are unlikely to exceed the 75 dB(A) ‘highly noise affected’ management level established in 
the Interim Construction Noise Guideline.  
In addition, the Department is satisfied the noise management and mitigation mea sures proposed by 
the Applicant would help to minimise the impact of construction noise on nearby sensitive receivers 
during all construction phases. The Department has recommended the Applicant be required to prepare 
and implement a Construction Noise and  Vibration Management Plan for the duration of the 
construction works, which would set out how and when the noise management and mitigation measures 
would be implemented.  
As noise associated with the back -up generator system was a key concern raised by me mbers of the 
public and Council, the Department focused its attention upon ensuring the submitted noise modelling 
has provided a comprehensive, quantitative assessment of all possible noise impacts in accordance 
with the Noise Policy for Industry . To achieve compliance with the development’s noise mitigation 
trigger levels, the Applicant has put forward a number of operational management and mitigation 
measures, including ensuring the back-up generators are only tested one at a time during daylight hours 
and are provided with attenuators on the air intakes and mufflers/attenuators on the exhaust ducts. The 
Department has incorporated these measures into the recommended conditions of consent. In addition, 
the Applicant would be required to submit an operation al noise verification report to help ensure the 
development complies with the established noise limits.  
Finally, the Department has carefully considered the revised air quality assessment in the context of 
the issues raised by the public in relation to the diesel back-up generators, and is subsequently satisfied 
the Applicant has provided a conservative assessment of the development’s operational air quality 
impacts. While exceedances of the hourly nitrogen dioxide (NO2) criterion were predicted in the revised 
assessment, the Department is satisfied the likelihood of such exceedances occurring during typical, 
day-to-day operations would be extremely low and can  be effectively managed via the mitigation 
measures which have been proposed by the Applicant.  
Subject to the implementation of these conditions and the commitments made by the Applicant, the 
Department is satisfied the aforementioned environmental impact s can be adequately managed and 
would not result in adverse impacts upon the amenity of the surrounding area.  
The Department’s assessment concludes the potential impacts of the development can be mitigated 
and/or managed to ensure an acceptable level of e nvironmental performance, subject to the 
recommended conditions of consent. In summary, the development would:  
• provide up to 250 construction jobs and 32 additional knowledge-intensive operational jobs within 
the Seven Hills industrial precinct  
• help satisfy market demand through the provision of additional, flexible data storage capacity within 
western Sydney  
• be consistent with the objectives of the relevant strategic planning framework, including the Greater 
Sydney Region Plan – A Metropolis of Three Cities , the Central City District Plan  and Council’s 
Local Strategic Planning Statement.  
Consequently, the Department considers the development is in the public interest and should be 
approved, subject to conditions.

1 Introduction 
1.1 The Department’s assessment 
This report details the Department of Planning and Environment’s (the Department ’s) assessment of 
the State significant development (SSD) application for the Station Road Data Centre Expansion  
(SSD-33781208). The proposed development (the development)  involves the con struction and 
operation of an expansion to a data centre located at 57 Station Road, Seven Hills  in the Blacktown 
local government area (LGA) (see Figure 1). 
The Department’s assessment considers all documentation submitted by Lehr Consultants 
International (Australia) Pty Ltd (the Applicant), including the Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) , 
Response to Submissions (RTS) and additional information, and the advice and submissions received 
from government agencies, stakeholders and the public. The Department’s assessment also considers 
the legislation and planning instruments relevant to the site and the development. 
This report describes the development, surrounding environment, relevant strategic and statutory 
planning provisions and the issues raised in submissions and government advice. The report evaluates 
the key issues associated with t he development and provides recommendations for managing any 
impacts during construction and operation.  The Department’s assessment of the Station Road Data 
Centre Expansion has concluded the development is in the public interest and should be approved, 
subject to conditions.   
1.2 Development background 
The Applicant is seeking development consent to expan d an existing data centre  which will include a 
two-storey data storage building, associated office space, supporting infrastructure, new hardstand 
areas (inc luding a new perimeter access road and car parking) and landscaping. The development 
would involve the storage of electronic data in associated information technology (IT) hardware  and 
would operate 24 hours a day, seven days a week.  
Data centres are places which are used by organisations to store their electronic computer applications 
and data. This could include personal data (such as a Facebook account or Instagram photos), 
corporate data (such as a business’s payroll system) or sensitive government dat a (such as tax and 
Medicare records).  
In recent years, there has been an increased need for new large and small -scale data centres across 
the State. As new technologies (such as driverless cars, faster mobile data networks and artificial 
intelligence) become commonplace, a significant number of new data centres will be required to store 
the volume of electronic data accessed and created by Australians.  
The Applicant is an engineering and design consultancy which specialises in providing sustainable, 
tailor-made design solutions to its clients. It acts on behalf of a multinational data centre operator which 
is seeking to expand its operations within the Sydney region. If approved, the development would be 
constructed and managed by the data centre operator.

1.3 Site description 
The site comprises approximately 2.57 hectares (ha) of industrial zoned land located at 57 Station Road, 
Seven Hills in the Blacktown LGA. The site is legally described as Lot B Deposited Plan (DP) 404669  
and is located 6.8 kilometres (km) north -west of the Parramatta central business district (CBD) and 
approximately 3.8 km east of the Blacktown CBD (see Figure 1).  
The site has been largely cleared to facilitate the construction of a small -scale data centre in the 
southern section of the  site, adjacent to Station Road. This development was approved by Blacktown 
City Council (Council) in early 2022 (see Section 1.5 below).  
The site slopes down approximately 8 metres (m) from Station Road towards the north -eastern 
boundary, directing surface water flows into Blacktown Creek and the associated riparian area. A single 
tree is located in the north -eastern corner of the site. An Endeavour Energy transmission line and 
easement crosses over the south-eastern corner of the site.  
The site does not contain any items of heritage significance and is not located within a designated 
heritage conservation area.

The site is located on the edge of the Seven Hills industrial precinct and is surrounded by a range of 
land uses, including:  
• Blacktown Creek and industrial land uses, located to the north-east and north-west  
• McCoy Park, neighbourhood shops and residential receivers, located to the south-east  
• the Main Western railway line and residential receivers, located to the south-west.

2.2 Physical layout and design 
The physical layout and design of the development is shown in Figure 3 to Figure 6. The data centre 
presents as a two-storey warehouse building  towards the rear of the site , surrounded by an access 
road and landscaped areas.  
Both floors of the data centre comprise data halls and mechanical plant areas which support the 
operation of the development. A two -storey office space is provided at the front of the bu ilding, facing 
the Council-approved data centre. External plant areas are also located to the north and west of the 
data centre building and would contain diesel back-up generators and water tanks.  
The development’s façade would be constructed from vertically modulated panels in white and grey, 
with some copper -coloured ‘highlight’ panels used to provide visual interest. Colours, materials and 
finishes proposed to be used in the development’s façade are designed to be durable, high quality and 
low maintenance. A decorative perforated metal screen and louvres would also be installed on the 
eastern and western façades of the data centre building, to help minimise noise and screen internal 
and external plant from surrounding sensitive receivers (see Figure 5).  
All heavy and light vehicles accessing the site would enter and exit via a two -way driveway off Station 
Road, located in the south-western corner of the site. This entrance was approved under DA-21-01058 
and access would be controlled via a security gatehouse and associated boom gates. A separate 
emergency access driveway is also located in the south-eastern corner of the site.  
Light vehicles would primarily park in the parking area located between the development and the 
Council-approved data centre. Heavy vehicles would use the perimeter access road to access the main 
loading dock on the western side of the data centre, whi le diesel fuel would be delivered directly to a 
bunker located adjacent to the generators on the north side of the building.  
2.3 Process description 
The primary purpose of the development is for the collection, storage, processing and distribution of 
electronic data in associated IT hardware by cloud and content providers or government entities  
(the tenant/s).  
The data centre operator will fit out the development’s data halls with its own computer systems, server 
systems and networking equipment. This IT hard ware would be stored in racks designed to maximise 
the efficiency of the space. The data centre operator will then lease storage space within these systems 
to its tenant/s.  
The development would be cooled using a system of AHUs located on each floor of the data centre. 
The cooling system has been designed to maintain a constant temperature within the data halls 
throughout the year. The ancillary office space would be primarily used by on-site technicians, who 
would provide IT support to external users and undertake regular maintenance as necessary.  
Data centres also require sophisticated energy back -up systems to ensure the data centre operator’s 
customers are always able to access their data. To this end, the development includes a system of 
lithium-ion batteries and diesel back -up generators designed to minimise downtime during a power 
outage event. The Applicant may also be directed to operat e the back -up generator system b y the 
Australian Energy Market Operator (AEMO) to help prevent major blackouts from occurring across the 
state’s electricity network.   
[[page 17]]

The site would be serviced by two underground feeder cables (33 kV each) from Endeavour Energy’s 
Seven Hills Zone substa tion. In the event of a full power outage (both feeders are taken offline), 
lithium-ion batteries located in the data halls would provide an uninterrupted power supply until the 
back-up generator system reaches full load.  
Once they have reached full load,  9 diesel back-up generators would be used to provide continuous 
power to the development until power has been restored by Endeavour Energy . The Applicant 
anticipates that, on average, the back-up generators would be used to generate power for up to 6 hours 
each year (based on the State of the energy market 2021 report produced by the Australian Energy 
Regulator).  
To ensure the development can function during a power outage event, the Applicant would also 
undertake regular testing of each back -up generator. During certain tests, an artificial load would be 
applied to the generators using an electrical switching device. The test scenarios would be as follows:  
• Unloaded Maintenance Testing – 10 minutes each month 
• Loaded Maintenance Testing (assumed to be the generators operating at 70% of full power) – 
35 minutes each quarter  
• Maximum Load Testing (assumed to be the ge nerators operating at 100% of full power)  – 
65 minutes each year.  
The generators would only be tested one at a time , and all testing would occur between the hours of 
1:00 pm to 4:00 pm on weekdays.

The proposal is State significant development (SSD) pursuant to section 4.36 of  the Environmental 
Planning and Assessment Act 1979 (EP&A Act) because it involves the construction and operation of 
a data centre with a total power consumption of more than 10 MW, which meets the criteria in clause 25 
of Schedule 1 in State Environmental Planning Policy (Planning Systems) 2021.  
4.2 Permissibility  
The site is zoned IN1 – General Industrial under the Blacktown Local Environmental Plan 2015. Under 
clause 2.31 of State Environmental Planning Policy (Transport and Infrastructure) 2021, development 
for the purpose of a data storage premises may be carried out by any person with consent on land in 
an IN1 zone.  
4.3 Consent authority 
The Minister for Planning (the Minister) is the consent authority for the development under section 4.5 
of the EP&A Act. On 9 March 2022, the Minister delegated the functions to determine SSD applications 
to the Director, Industry Assessments where:  
• the application has not been made by a person  who has disclosed a reportable political donation 
under section 10.4 of the EP&A Act  
• there are less than 15 unique public submissions in the nature of objections and  
• the relevant local council has not made a submission by way of objection under the manda tory 
requirements for community participation listed under Schedule 1 of the EP&A Act.  
None of the public submissions received objected to the development. While Council originally objected 
to the SSD application, it formally withdrew its objection  to the development  on 6 October 2022. In 
addition, no reportable political donations were made by the Applicant in the last two years.

6 Assessment 
The Department has considered the EIS, the issues raised in the submissions and government advice,

Department considers the key assessment issues to be noise and air quality.  
A number of other issues have also been considered. These issues are considered to be relatively

6.1 Noise 
The construction and 24-hour operation of the development has the potential to emit noise and vibration, 
which could impact on the acoustic amenity of the surrounding area.  
The development would involve the construction of a two-storey data centre building with ancillary office 
space, a staff parking area and associated plant over a period of  approximately 21 months. Working 
hours are proposed between 7:00a m and 6:00pm Monday to Friday , between 8:00am and 5:00pm on 
Saturdays and no works on Sundays or public holidays . While the weekday construction hours would 
be consistent with the recommended standard construction  hours in the EPA’s Interim Construction 
Noise Guideline  (ICNG) (DECC, 2009) , the proposed Saturday hours would exceed the standard 
construction hours by 4 hours (from 1:00pm to 5:00pm). No works are proposed outside of these hours, 
on Sundays or public holidays.  
The development’s main operational noise sources include the AHUs and the back-up diesel generators. 
Other sources of operational noise include vehicle movements to  and from the site and manoeuvring 
around the main data centre building.  
The EIS included an acoustic report prepared by Pulse White Noise Acoustics, which considered both 
construction and operational noise impacts. During consultation on the EIS, no comments were made 
regarding construction noise and vibration by Council. However, several concerns were raised by 
Council and the public regarding operational noise impacts, particularly in relation to cumulative impacts 
associated with the Council-approved data centre and the use of the back-up generators during a power 
outage event.  The Department also requested the  Applicant provide further information to justify 
construction works being undertaken outside the recommended standard construction hours outlined 
in the ICNG.  
The Applicant subsequently provided an updated version of the acoustic report to address these 
concerns. 
6.1.1 Construction 
The Applicant’s acoustic report noted that construction of the development would occur across three 
separate phases:  
• Phase 1 – General excavation works 
• Phase 2 – Concreting works  
• Phase 3 – Building construction.  
Major plant equipment to be used during the construction works would inc lude excavators, dozers, 
trucks, hand tools and a truck-mounted crane.

6.2.2 Operation 
Key pollutants associated with the operation of the development would be nitrogen oxide (NO), nitrogen 
dioxide (NO2), carbon monoxide (CO) and particulate matter (PM2.5). These potential exhaust emissions 
are associated with the combustion of diesel fuel in the back-up generators. 
The revised air quality assessment  considered the development’s potential air quality impacts in the 
context of three realistic operating scenarios:  
• Routine maintenance 1 – One back-up generator being tested at 75% load for 35 minutes   
• Routine maintenance 2 – One back-up generator being tested at 100% load for 65 minutes  
• Realistic ‘worst case’ scenario  – All back-up generators operating concurrently during a power 
outage event (e.g. during severe bushfire incidents, severe weather events and /or planned works 
by electricity distributor).  
The test modelled under the ‘routine maintenance 1’ scenario would be undertaken for each back -up 
generator on a quarterly basis, while the test modelled under the ‘routine maintenance 2’ scenario would 
be undertaken for each generator on an annual basis.  
The assessment found no exceedances were predicted for CO at surrounding sensitive receivers under 
all scenarios and that incremental PM2.5 impacts are considered negligible given the presence of  
elevated background concentrations of pollutants in western Sydney.  
With regard to  NOX emissions (oxides of nitrogen  from combustion consists largely of NO and partly 
NO2), the Applicant’s assessment reported that exceedances were predicted at surrounding sensitive 
receivers, for which the zone of influence would progressively increase with the increase in generator 
load and utilisation. First, the predicted incremental con centration for the quarterly testing scenario 
(75% load for 35 minutes) exceeded the hourly NO 2 criterion at the nearest commercial properties 
directly north of the site. Second, the predicted exceedances for the annual testing scenario (100% load 
for 65 minutes) extended the impact zone farther into the surrounding commercial area and included 
residential properties on the other side of Station Road and those next to McCoy Park. Third, the 
predicted exceedances under the realistic ‘worst-case’ scenario (all generators operating at capacity ) 
would extend beyond the study area presented in the revised air quality assessment.  
The Applicant noted that while exceedances have been predicted based on the highest possible 
pollutant concentration, the assessment provided is extremely conservative and assumes the modelled 
back-up generators would operate continuously across every hour of the day. In reality, the testing 
regimes modelled under the two ‘routine maintenance’ scenarios would occur for a maximum of 
5.25 hours and 9.75 hours  each year , respectively.  In addition, the Applicant anticipates that, on 
average, the development would be subject to power outages for a maximum of 6 hours each year .  
In its submission on the EIS, Council requested the development’s cumulative impact assessment be 
updated to include the previously approved data centre. Several concerns were also raised by members 
of the public regarding the potential human health impacts associated with the back-up generators, and 
the Department subsequently requested the Applicant explore whether additional measures could be 
implemented to further reduce the likelihood of exceedances occurring.  
In response to the concerns raised regarding cumulative impacts, the Applicant indicated the total NOX 
emissions are expected to increase in the power outage scenario by approximately 20% with the 
inclusion of the three additional generators from the already approved data centre. However, the 
Applicant asserted the increase in NOX emissions would not change the  outcome of the assessment 
[[page 40]]
 
Station Road Data Centre Expansion (SSD-33781208) | Assessment Report 31 
as the likelihood of power outage event occurring is extremely low. In addition, the Applicant noted the 
site’s emergency response plan would require all practical measures be implemented to reduce the 
duration of any power outages, and monitoring of NO 2 would be carried out at sensitive receivers 
downwind of the site to inform the implementation of further air quality management measures.   
In response to the concerns raised regarding potential exceedances of the NO 2 criteria during typical 
day-to-day operations , the Applicant undertook further analysis of hourly background NO 2 
concentrations from 2017 to 2021. This analysis found background levels are lower during the summer 
months, and are at their lowest between the hours of 1pm to 4pm each day. Accordingly, the Applicant 
committed to undertaking the annual 65-minute maintenance test between 1pm and 4pm in the summer 
months and the quarterly 35‐minute maintenance tests between 1pm and 4pm on weekdays.   
The Department has carefully considered the revised air quality assessment in the context of the issues 
raised during the exhibition period, and is satisfied the Applicant has provided a conservative 
assessment of the air pollution emissions associated with the operation of the development . Despite 
the revi sed air quality assessment predicting exceedances of the maximum NO 2 concentrations at 
nearby sensitive receivers, the Department accepts that the predicted likelihood of such exceedance s 
occurring is extremely small  given the overall duration of the propo sed testing regime and the low 
likelihood of a power outage event occurring. 
The Department considers the air pollution emissions during scheduled maintenance can be 
appropriately managed by requiring the Applicant to avoid maintenance testing during periods of high 
background concentration levels. Further, the Department considers worst-case air pollution emissions 
during a power outage event  can be appropriately managed through the preparation and 
implementation of a Nitrogen Dioxide Monitoring Procedure (NDMP), including the requirement to detail 
control measures that have been implemented during each power outage and any additional corrective 
measures needed to address unforeseen exceedances.  
The Department has recommended conditions requiring the Applicant prepare and implement a n 
NDMP, take all reasonable steps to minimise the generation of air emissions and undertake 
maintenance testing between 1pm and 4pm on weekdays, with the annual test to occur only during the 
summer months. In addition, conditions have also been recommended  requiring the Applicant ensure 
the design, installation and operation of the back -up generators and/or the asso ciated enclosures are 
in line with best practice and do not preclude the ability for additional air pollution emission controls to 
be retrofitted if required.

7 Evaluation 
The Department’s assessment of the SSD application has fully considered all relevant matters under 
section 4.15 of the EP&A Act, the objects of the EP&A Act and the principles of ecologically sustainable 
development.  
The Department has considered the development on its merits, taking into consideration strategic plans 
that guide development in the area, the EPIs that apply to the development, the advice received from 
the relevant government authorities, including Council, and the submissions received from the public.  
The development involves the construction of a n expansion to an existing data centre , which will help 
to satisfy  market demand through the provision of additional, flexible data storage capacity within 
western Sydney. In the coming years, this additional capacity will be crucial to ensure the ongoing data 
storage needs of greater Sydney and the State are addressed.  
In addition, the development will provide up to 250 construction jobs and 32 additional 
knowledge-intensive operational jobs and represents a direct investment of more than $ 167 million in 
the Blacktown LGA. The proposal is consistent with the key objectives of the Region Plan and the 
CCDP, as it will help to retain and improve the efficiency of industrial-zoned land within close proximity 
to the Seven Hills Town Centre.  
The Department acknowledges the concerns raised by the local community regarding the potential 
impacts associated with the back -up generator system (noise, air quality, etc) and increased traffic in 
the surrounding area . Similar issues were also raised by government authorities in relation to the 
development’s noise, air quality and water-related impacts. The Department has sought to address any 
issues raised, including those raised in publ ic submissions, through consultation with both the 
government authorities and the Applicant.  
The Department’s assessment considered noise and vibration and air quality to be the key matters for 
consideration.  
The Department has considered the information provided in the revised acoustic report and is satisfied 
construction noise and vibration impacts associated with the development can be adequately managed, 
subject to the implementation of standard construction hours and the noise management and mitigation 
measures proposed by the Applicant. While exceedances have been predicted at several nearby 
residential receivers, the Department is satisfied the associated works would be short -term and are 
unlikely to exceed the 75 dB(A) ‘highly noise affected’ management level established in the ICNG.  
In addition, the Department is satisfied the noise management and mitigation measures proposed by 
the Applicant would help to minimise the impact of construction noise on nearby sensitive receivers 
during all construction phases. The Department has recommended the Applicant be required to prepare 
and implement a CNVMP for the duration of the construction works, which would set out how and when 
the noise management and mitigation measures would be implemented.  
As noise associated with the back -up generator system was a key concern raised by members of the 
public and Council, the Department has focused its attention upon ensuring the submitted modelling 
has provided a comprehensive, quantitative assessment of all  possible noise impacts in accordance 
with the Noise Policy for Industry. To ensure compliance with the development’s noise mitigation trigger 
levels, the Applicant has put forward a number of operational management and mitigation measures, 
[[page 51]]
 
Station Road Data Centre Expansion (SSD-33781208) | Assessment Report 42 
including ensuring the back-up generators are only tested one at a time during daylight hours and are 
provided with attenuators on the air intakes and mufflers/attenuators on the exhaust ducts. The 
Department has incorporated these measures into the recommended conditions of consent. In addition, 
the Applicant would be required to submit an operational noise verification report to help ensure the 
development complies with the established noise limits.  
Finally, the Department has carefully considered the revised air quali ty assessment in the context of 
the issues raised by the public in relation to the diesel back-up generators, and is subsequently satisfied 
the Applicant has provided a conservative assessment of the development’s operational air quality 
impacts. While exceedances of the hourly NO2 criterion were predicted in the revised assessment, the 
Department is satisfied the likelihood of such exceedances occurring during typical, day -to-day 
operations would be extremely low and can be effectively managed via the mitigation measures which 
have been proposed by the Applicant. These measures have been incorporated into the recommended 
conditions of consent and include:  
• placing further restrictions on the generator testing regime to ensure individual tests do not occur 
during periods of elevated background NOX levels 
• the implementation of a NDMP during power outage events , which will include control measures 
to minimise the duration of each event and respond to any observed exceedances of the relevant 
NO2 criterion.  
The Department has also recommended a range of detailed conditions to address any residual hazards, 
traffic and visual impacts associated with the construction and operation of the development . These 
conditions were informed by the recommendations of the r elevant government authorities and have 
been reviewed by the Applicant.  
Overall, the development is consistent with the objectives of the relevant NSW Government policies 
and would help support the the ongoing data storage needs of  greater Sydney. On bala nce, the 
Department considers the development is in the public interest and the SSD application could be 
approved, subject to conditions (see Appendix E).

State Environmental Planning Policy (Planning Systems) 2021 (Planning Systems SEPP) 
The Planning Systems SEPP identifies certain classes of development as SSD. The construction and 
operation of storage premises used for the storage of data and related information technology hardware 
with a total power consumption of more than 10 MW , meets the criteria of clause 25 of Schedule 1 of 
the Planning Systems SEPP  and is consequently classified as SSD. The development satisfies the 
criteria in clause 25 of Schedule 1 as it would involve the construction of an expansion to an existing 
data centre with a total power consumption of 19.2 MW. 
State Environmental Planning Polic y ( Transport and Infrastructure) 20 21 (Transport and 
Infrastructure SEPP) 
Chapter 2 of the Transport and Infrastructure SEPP aims to facilitate the effective delivery of 
infrastructure across the State by improving regulatory certainty and efficiency, iden tifying matters to 
be considered in the assessment of development adjacent to certain types of infrastructure 
development, and providing for consultation with relevant public authorities about certain types of 
development during the assessment process. 
Under Chapter 2, section 2.31 of the SEPP , development for the purpose of a data storage premises 
may be carried out by any person with consent on land within certain business and industrial zones. As 
the site is located on land zoned IN1 – General Industrial  under the Blacktown LEP 2015 , the 
development is permissible with consent. 
Under Chapter 2, section 2.122 of the SEPP , certain development types are identified as ‘traffic-
generating development’, and subsequently require referral to Transport for NSW (TfNSW). These 
development types are detailed in Schedule 3 of the Transport and Infrastructure SEPP.  
The Department notes the development does not require formal referral to TfNSW under Schedule 3 of 
the SEPP, as it would not generate more than 200 vehicles per hour. Accordingly, the Department has 
consulted with Council as the relevant roads authority and, where applicable, has included suitable 
transport-related conditions in the recommended conditions of consent. 
State Environmental Planning Policy ( Resilience and Hazards ) 2021 (Resilience and Hazards 
SEPP)
