Preface 
This assessment report provides a record of the Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure’s 
(the Department) assessment and evaluation of AW Edwards Pty Ltd application to modify the State 
significant development (SSD) consent for the 51 Huntingwood Drive Data Centre , located in the 
Blacktown local government area. The report includes: 
• a description of the proposed modification 
• an assessment of the modification against government policy and statutory requirements, 
including mandatory considerations 
• a demonstration of how matters raised by stakeholders have been considered 
• an assessment of the likely environmental, social and economic impacts of the modification 
• an evaluation which weighs up the likely impacts and benefits of the modification, having regard 
to the proposed mitigation  measures, offsets, government advice, and provides a view on 
whether the impacts are, on balance, acceptable 
• a recommendation to the decision -maker, along with the reasons for the recommendation, to 
assist them in making an informed decision about whether the consent should be modified and 
any conditions that should be imposed.

1 Introduction 
The modification application seeks to amend the consent for the 51 Huntingwood Drive Data Centre 
(SSD-41589232) to allow the consolidation of data centre shells and IT load, a reduction in gross floor 
area (GFA), and the relocation of mechanical plant equipment to ground level. 
The application was lodged on 12 June 2026 by AW Edwards Pty Ltd pursuant to section 4.55(1A) of 
the Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979 (EP&A Act). 
A more detailed overview of the proposed modification is provided in Section 2 of this report. 
1.1 Background 
The approved development comprises the construction and operation of a data centre with a total 
capacity of 400 megawatts (MW) at 51 Huntingwood Drive, Huntingwood, within the Blacktown Local 
Government Area (see Figure 1 and Figure 2). The development includes backup power generation 
and cooling systems, diesel fuel and lithium-ion battery storage, associated infrastructure, demolition 
works, and bulk earthworks  
1.2 Subject Site 
The site is legally described as Lot 1 DP 857249 and is located at 51 Huntingwood Drive, Huntingwood.  
The site is situated within the Huntingwood industrial precinct, between the Great Western Highway 
to the north and the M4 Motorway adjoining directly to the south. The site  is located approximately 
32 kilometres (km) west of the Sydney central business district (CBD), 12 km west of Parramatta and 
4 km south of Blacktown.

1.3 Approval History 
On 15 September 2023, development consent was granted by Director, Industry Assessments for the 
development of the 51 Huntingwood Drive Data Centre . The development consent  permits the 
following works: 
• Construction and operation of a 400 MW data centre comprising of a seven-storey data centre 
building with 10 data shells and ancillary office space 
• Associated plant and equipment including emergency back-up generators, cooling 
infrastructure, diesel storage, lithium-ion battery storage and ancillary infrastructure 
• Two on-site electrical substations 
• Internal access roads, circulation areas and car parking 
• Demolition of existing structures and bulk earthworks  
• landscaping 
The development consent has not been previously modified. A modification application (SSD -
41589232-Mod-1) was lodged , however, the application was subsequently withdrawn and was not 
determined.

2.2 Applicant’s Justification for the Proposed Modification 
The Applicant advised that since the approval of the SSD application, it has progressed the detailed 
design of the data centre and explored opportunities for further optimisation. This process has 
identified an opportunity to increase the overall power capacity of the data centre while maintaining 
the existing approved footprint for the SYD02 building and minimal changes to the overall site layout.  
The Applicant has highlighted the proposed modification would help facilitate further data centre 
capacity in western Sydney at an existing, operational facility, while ensuring any potential 
environmental impacts associated with the changes are minimal or  improved when compared to the 
approved development.

5.1 Operational Noise Impacts 
The modification application involves the relocation of mechanical plant and equipment, including 
Hybrid Dry Coolers (HDCs) , ground-level chiller farms and gantries along the southern, eastern and 
western boundaries of the site. The relocation of this plant has the potential to result in additional 
noise impacts on surrounding receivers. 
The Modification Report included a n A coustic Memorandum , which considered the potential 
operational noise impacts of the modified development on surrounding receiver locations and against 
the noise limits imposed under existing conditions of consent.  
The Acoustic Memorandum provided a quantitative assessment of  the operational noise impacts of 
the modified development , including noise associated with  the 144 HDC’s and 316 back -up diesel 
generators. The Acoustic Memorandum modelled the development’s operations under ‘worst -case’ 
noise emission scenario during the day, evening and night periods which considered the operation of 
all HDC’s and three back-up generators (day-time period only).  
The Acoustic Memorandum found that the proposal would maintain compliance with the existing 
noise limits under the consent at all receiver locations during all assessment periods. However, it 
predicted increases in noise levels of up to 15 dB at surroundin g industrial receiver locations. The 
Acoustic Memorandum also noted that the predicted noise impacts at residential receiver locations 
would remain unchanged. 
The EPA noted the predicted increase in noise levels at surrounding industrial receivers and raised 
concerns that the noise assessment had not considered emergency operating scenarios or other 
generator operating scenarios. 
[[page 18]]
 
  Modification 2 of 51 Huntingwood Drive Data Centre (SSD-41589232-Mod-2) Assessment Report | 13 
In response to the EPA's concerns, the Applicant provided additional information confirming that the 
modification does not propose any changes to the operational scenarios for the backup diesel 
generators, including emergency operating scenarios  of back -up diesel  including emergency 
operating scenarios,  and therefore no additional acoustic modelling of emergency operating 
scenarios is warranted.  
The Department acknowledges that the modification is predicted to increase noise levels at 
surrounding industrial receivers. However, the development is predicted to remain compliant with the 
noise limits specified in the existing consent across all assessment periods. To manage the increased 
noise impacts, the Department considers that the implementation of an Operational Noise 
Management Plan (ONMP), including periodic reviews of the development's operations to identify and 
implement further noise mitigat ion measures where practicable and reasonable, would provide an 
appropriate mechanism for the ongoing management and reduction of operational noise impacts. 
Therefore, the Department concludes that, while the modification would result in increased noise 
levels at some industrial receivers, the development would remain compliant with the noise limits 
prescribed under the conditions of consent. The Department re commends imposing a condition of 
consent requiring the preparation and implementation of an Operational Noise Management Plan 
(ONMP) to appropriately manage and minimise operational noise impacts from the development.

6 Evaluation 
The Department’s assessment has considered the relevant matters and objects of the EP&A Act and 
considers the proposed modification is appropriate on the basis that: 
• the proposed modification will result in minimal environmental impacts beyond the approved 
facility 
• the modification would facilitate a reduction in bulk and scale through the reconfiguration of 
the site layout, while largely maintaining the approved development footprint 
• the proposed modification would improve the operational efficiency of the development ’s 
operations 
• the modification would not result in unacceptable impacts to surrounding receivers.  
The Department has recommended conditions to manage residual environmental impacts, including 
requirements for air quality and noise. The revised conditions would ensure the modification continues 
to operate within an acceptable environmental performance framework.  
Overall, the Department considers the impacts of the modification would be minor and acceptable 
and concludes it can be approved, subject to the recommended, modified conditions.
