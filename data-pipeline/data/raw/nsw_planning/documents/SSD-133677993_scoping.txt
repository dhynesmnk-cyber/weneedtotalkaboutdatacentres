PART A  PRELIMINARY 
1.1 INTRODUCTION 
This Scoping Report has been prepared by Willowtree Planning Pty Ltd (Willowtree Planning) on behalf 
of CDC Data Centres Pty Ltd (the Proponent), in relation to the proposed construction and operation of 
a data centre, an ancillary electricity substation and other associated works (the Proposal) at 859-901 
Mamre Road, Kemps Creek (the Site). 
The purpose of this Scoping Report is to request Project-Specific Secretary’s Environmental Assessment 
Requirements (SEARs) from the NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure (the 
Department) for the preparation of an Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) for a State Significant 
Development Application (SSDA). 
Data centres are an emerging necessity in the digital world and are vital for online services, banking, 
emergency systems, transport, and business. Data centres are projected to rapidly expand in Australia 
from 1,350 megawatts (MW) in 2024 to 3,100 MW by 2030, resulting in an investment of $26 billion into 
Australia’s economy. The Proposal is considered a primary contribution with a significant investment 
into this the digital industry and Australian economy. 
The Site is located in the Mamre Road Precinct (MRP) (refer to Figure 1), which forms part of the Western 
Sydney Employment Area (WSEA) and was rezoned by the NSW Government in June 2020. The 
rezoning facilitated 850 hectares of industrial land for future investment and is anticipated to provide 
17,000 ongoing jobs when fully developed. This presents an opportunity for data centres to expand into 
a centralised industrial precinct which is positioned for exponential growth. The Western Sydney 
Aerotropolis Precinct (inclusive of the MRP) will have over $25 billion in investment by the NSW and 
Commonwealth Governments in airport and critical transport infrastructure and over $14.4 billion in 
investment for planned developments. 
The Proposal is anticipated to have a total power consumption of approximately 144 MW, which satisfies 
the definition of State Significant Development (SSD) pursuant to Schedule 1, Clause 25 of State 
Environmental Planning Policy (Planning Systems) 2021 (Planning Systems SEPP), being development 
for the purpose of a data centre that has a total power consumption of more than 15 MW. 
This Scoping Report has been prepared in accordance with the NSW Department of Planning, Housing 
and Infrastructure (DPHI) SSD Guidelines – Preparing a Scoping Report (the Guidelines) dated October 
2022, in accordance with Section 173 of the Environmental Planning and Assessment Regulation 2021 
(EP&A Regulation). This Scoping Report should be read in conjunction with the Scoping Summary Table 
(Appendix 1) and Architectural Plans (Appendix 2).

1.3 OVERVIEW OF THE SITE AND REGIONAL SETTING  
The Site is located on Mamre Road, Kemps Creek, and has an approximate combined area of 88.4ha 
across four (4) lots. The Site is within the Penrith Local Government Area (LGA) and forms part of the IN1 
General Industrial zoned land within the WSEA, pursuant to the State Environmental Planning Policy 
(Industry and Employment) 2021  (Industry and Employment SEPP). The WSEA is an industrial and 
logistics precinct spanning across several local government areas and serves as a major logistics and 
manufacturing hub, benefiting from its proximity to key transport infrastructure including the M4 and 
M7 motorways, and its central location adjacent to the emerging Western Sydney Aerotropolis (WSA) 
(refer to Figure 2). The NSW Government anticipates that in the next 20 years, the WSEA will facilitate 
200,000 long term jobs through harnessing the Western Sydney Airport. 
The Site is located approximately 6km north-east of the Western Sydney International Airport and 39km 
west of the Sydney Central Business District (CBD). The Site is accessed via several points along the 
Mamre Road frontage to the east. 
The Proposal will significantly benefit from development and infrastructure in the WSA and generate 
attractive employment opportunity given its location in context to the new airport and surrounding 
precincts.

1.4 PERMISSIBILITY 
The Proposal is primarily categorised as a data centre, falling under the land use definition of ‘high 
technology industries’ which is a type of ‘light industry’, and is permitted with consent in the IN1 General 
Industrial zone, pursuant to Chapter 2 of the Industry and Employment SEPP. Data centres are also 
permitted with consent in the IN1 General Industrial zone under the State Environmental Planning 
Policy (Transport and Infrastructure) 2021 (Transport and Infrastructure SEPP). 
In addition, the proposed ancillary electricity substation is defined as electricity generating works under 
the Standard Instrument LEP. Whilst electricity generating works are prohibited in the IN1 General 
Industrial zone, they are made permissible under Chapter 2, Division 4, Clause 2.36(1)(b) of the Transport 
and Infrastructure SEPP as the IN1 General Industrial zone is a prescribed non-residential zone.

1.6 OBJECTIVES OF THE DEVELOPMENT 
The vision of the Proposal is to develop the Site to accommodate the first stage of a data centre campus, 
including a 144 MW data centre and an ancillary electricity substation to provide power supply to the 
data centre campus, delivering significant investment in NSW’s digital economy. The Proposal is 
positioned as a crucial component of Greater Sydney’s digital infrastructure, with the rise of hyperscale 
data centres (50 MW and greater) reflective of the global surge in artificial intelligence (AI) and cloud 
computing, which is positioned to grow Australia’s economy by $45 billion to $115 billion by 2030. To 
achieve this growth, NSW must leverage large-scale processing and storage data centres to remain 
competitive within the Asia-Pacific (APAC) region. The Proposal is committed to driving this evolution, 
while ensuring environmental impacts and planning considerations are adequately addressed. The key 
objectives of the Proposal are outlined below: 
 A Competitive Western Sydney: Provide the first stage of a world-class data centre campus that 
is internationally competitive and reflective of Australia’s growth in the sector. The investment 
will help deliver significant infrastructure to the region, provide employment opportunities 
within the technology trades, including for information and communication technology (ICT) 
professionals, which are needed to support the growing demand for data centre capacity. 
 Efficiency: Data centres consolidate electricity demand and help businesses operate without 
needing to rely on their own on-site servers. Hyperscale data centres are found to be up to five 
(5) times more efficient than on-premises servers and smaller-scale data centres. The Proposal 
would deliver efficient data storage services by maximising capacity and minimising resources 
needed for day-to-day operation. 
 Sustainability: The Proposal would create and facilitate the aims for best-in-class power usage 
efficiency (PUE) and to optimise water usage efficiency (WUE), including exploring 
opportunities to utilise recycled water and for water capture and re-use. The Proposal also seeks 
to minimise impacts on existing receivers while the MRP transitions to an industrial area.

2.2 KEY FEATURES 
2.2.1 Surrounding Development 
Parts of the Site are located within the MRP, WSA and the WSEA. Adjoining the Site to the east is Mamre 
Road, which is identified as a classified road. The portion of Mamre Road adjoining the Site is subject to 
future road upgrades between Erskine Park Road and Kerrs Road which will involve upgrades to 
existing intersections, new road intersections, road widening, and new shared and pedestrian footpaths 
on either side of the road. 
On the opposite side of Mamre Road from the Site are three (3) separate allotments located within the 
IN1 General Industrial zone. These properties are also located within the WSEA. At present, these 
properties are currently being developed for the purpose of large industrial units along with new public 
roads. 
Adjoining the Site to the north is 845-857 Mamre Road, which currently contains two (2) dwellings at 
the front of the Site with various ancillary and agricultural outbuildings. The property is accessed from 
Mamre Road via three (3) access points. 
To the south of the Site is 917 Mamre Road, a property containing a single-storey detached dwelling 
with various sheds and ancillary structures. The property is accessed from Mamre Road via an access 
handle. 
West of the Site is C2 Environmental Conservation, C4 Environmental Living, ENT Enterprise, and ENZ 
Environment and Recreation zoning. 
2.2.2 Flooding 
The Site or part of the Site is identified as being located within a flood planning area. The Site contains 
a section of Kemps Creek which intersects from the north-west to the south-east through the middle 
portion of the Site.  
The developable IN1 General Industrial zoned portion of the Site in the east is generally outside of the 
mapped flood extent. It is noted that the current DA25/0034 under assessment would create flood-free 
building pads suitable for subsequent development. As detailed above, the Proponent reserves the 
option to incorporate enabling civil works within the subject SSDA should DA25/0034 not be 
determined prior to the lodgement of the EIS. 
2.2.3 Bushfire

Category 3. The developable IN1 General Industrial zoned portion of the Site in the east is generally 
outside of the mapped bushfire prone extent, with the exception of part of the north-western boundary.

in accordance with the NSW Noise Policy for Industry, along with any other additional assessment 
requirements specified in the SEARs for both the construction and operation periods. Best practice noise 
mitigation strategies would be implemented as a result.  
7.2.2 Air Quality and Odour Assessment 
An Air Quality Impact Assessment (AQIA) would be undertaken as part of the Proposal, identifying and 
modelling potential construction and operation impacts in accordance with EPA Policy. Mitigating 
impacts to receivers in the locality would be a priority, noting the MRP is undergoing a transition to an 
industrial precinct and the Proposal intends to utilise a methodology for air quality control that would be 
suitable for existing and future development in the locality. A Construction Environmental Management 
Plan (CEMP) would be prepared outlining all air quality mitigation measures to be utilised during the 
construction stage of the development. The CEMP would factor all sensitive receivers and adjoining 
development in the locality. 
The AQIA would address diesel generator testing and identify a methodology to achieve the 
relevant pollutant emission criteria for pollutants at nearby sensitive receiver locations. An Environmental 
Management Plan is to be prepared for the construction and operation stages to ensure air quality impacts 
are suitably managed. 
7.3 INTEGRATED WATER CYCLE MANAGEMENT AND FLOODING

following: 
 
 Water-related servicing infrastructure required to service the Site;

 Erosion and sediment control measures; 
 Sustainability initiatives that would minimise or reduce the demand for drinking water, including

 Compliance with the relevant Sydney Water requirements; and 
 Flooding affectations associated with the Site. 
 
The south-eastern boundary of the Site is reserved for the provision of stormwater infrastructure in the form

The Site or part of the Site is situated within a flood planning area and prone to local overland flooding, 
largely due to the characteristics of the existing topography which includes sloping towards the rear of the 
Site where a portion of Kemps Creek is located. The Proposal would be designed noting the estimated 
peak extents and levels of the 1% AEP and probable maximum flood (PMF) events through the Site under 
existing conditions.

PART H  CONCLUSION  
This Scoping Report has been prepared in accordance with the Guidelines to request Project-Specific 
SEARs for the proposed data centre, ancillary electricity substation and associated works as the first stage 
of a data centre campus at 859-901 Mamre Road, Kemps Creek. The Proposal is declared SSD under 
Schedule 1, Section 25 of the Planning Systems SEPP.  
 
The ultimate vision of the Proposal is to develop the Site into a data centre campus, delivering billions of 
dollars of investment in NSW’s digital economy and operate as a critical component of Greater Sydney’s 
surge in delivering digital infrastructure due to the increased demand in AI and cloud computing. Australia 
is in a unique position to become a front-runner in the digital infrastructure sector within the APAC region. 
For this to be successful, the development of large-scale processing and hyperscale data centres is critical. 
 
It must be noted that the currently under assessment DA25/0034 is considered to be a matter of efficient 
sequencing rather than a precondition. The SSD would be capable of standing on its own merits, and the 
Proponent reserves the option to incorporate enabling civil works within the SSDA should DA25/0034 not 
be determined prior to the lodgement of the EIS. 
 
The Proposal is projected to support approximately 510 construction jobs over an 18-24 month period as 
well as 280 ongoing direct and indirect operational jobs annually, and would contribute significantly to the 
delivery of infrastructure in the MRP, which is critical in establishing the region as a world-class industrial 
precinct. 
 
This Scoping Report has outlined the preliminary information regarding the Site and the Proposal, 
identified the relevant strategic and statutory context, summarised the approach to engagement with 
stakeholders, and identified the scale and nature of the impacts of the project. 
 
The information detailed in this Scoping Report is sufficient to enable the Secretary to issue Project-Specific 
SEARs for the preparation of an Environmental Impact Statement.
