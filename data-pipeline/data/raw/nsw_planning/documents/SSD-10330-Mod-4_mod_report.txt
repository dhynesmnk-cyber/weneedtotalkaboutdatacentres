Executive Summary 
This Modification Report has been prepared by Urbis Ltd (Urbis) in support of a section 4.55(1A) modification 
application, on behalf of Canberra Data Centre Pty Ltd (CDC) (the Applicant), to modify Development 
Consent SSD-10330, which relates to an expanded data centre at 17 Roberts Road, Eastern Creek (the site). 
The data centre expansion is currently under construction in accordance with SSD-10330, which was 
approved by the Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure (DPHI) on 14 July 2020. The SSDA was 
modified to amend the built form of the approved Building 5 and Building 6 under SSD-10330 Mod 1 (Mod 1), 
approved on 17 April 2024 and subsequently modified to update generator layout and testing regime under 
SSD-10330 Mod 2 (Mod 2), approved on 6 March 2026. 
In collaboration with the operational tenant at the site, the project team has been conducting ongoing 
investigation and analysis of the requirements to support the operations of the data centre. It has been 
identified that further updates to the back-up diesel generators (also referred to as generators throughout 
this Modification Report), as well as modifications to the provision of rooftop chillers, are required to support 
the intended operations of the data centre. Additionally, the proposed modification seeks to formalise 
existing carparking to support operations at the site.  
A separate modification application has been lodged to DPHI for the purposes of updating the electrical 
infrastructure which supports the operation of the data centre (identified as SSD-10330 Mod 3). As such, this 
modification application is anticipated to be identified as SSD-10330 Mod 4, or Mod 4. 
Proposed Modification  
The proposal seeks to modify SSD-10330 as follows: 
▪ Power and backup infrastructure  
– Modify the total number of back -up diesel generators approved under SSD -10330 from 72 
generators to 96 generators. The proposed generators include:  
• 18 generators located near existing EC1 generators; and  
• 6 generators located adjacent to the approved Mod 2 generator area.  
The increase in generators will ensure that downtime during any power outage event is 
appropriately minimised. The modification to the number of diesel generators is proposed to be 
facilitated by an update to the provision of diesel storage tanks.  
– Modify the back ‑up diesel generator testing regime to reflect the increased number of generators 
and site configuration. The updated regime adjusts the frequency and grouping of tests to maintain 
reliable operation.  
▪ Batteries and Switch rooms  
– Modification to the Level 3 floor plans for Building 5 and Building 6 to facilitate the fitout of battery 
storage and switchrooms.  
▪ Cooling Infrastructure  
– Installation of 32 additional chillers and associated infrastructure across the roof of Buildings 5 and 
6. 
– Modification to the acoustic mitigation measures plan  to facilitate additional acoustic screening  at 
the rooftop chiller deck . 
▪ Other Modifications  
– Modification to site plan to formalise 61 of the existing, temporary construction car parking spaces 
at the northern end of the site to provide  the use of permanent, operational carparking spaces.  
[[page 6]]
 
Modification Report | SSD-10330 MOD 4   Introduction 6 
 
 
– Modification to the north- western corner of the internal road to facilitate adequate turning paths.  
– Modification to remove a redundant waste storage room at the north- western corner of the site.  
It is noted that while this application seeks to modify SSD-10330 only, various operations and environmental 
thresholds are inclusive of elements approved under both SSD-10330 as well as other applications across the 
site. Notably, the proposed modification: 
▪ Results in a total of 111 generators across the site (inclusive of generators approved under other 
applications) ; 
▪ Results in a total of 154.75 hours per year  of testing for generators across the site; and  
▪ Results in a total of 3,609 tonnes of maximum diesel storage capacity across the site (noting, the 
proposal will maintain the approved mitigation measures and physical controls to ensure cumulative 
on-site diesel storage remains below 2,000 tonnes  at any one time. ) 
Justification of the Modified Project  
This Modification Report assesses the development as proposed with regard to relevant planning 
instruments and policies and outlines the mitigation measures to ensure the project does not result in 
unreasonable or adverse environmental effects.  
The key issues for all components of the project as proposed to be modified have been assessed in detail, 
with specialist reports underpinning the key findings and recommendations identified in the Assessment of 
Impacts in Section 6.  
Having considered all relevant matters, the development as modified has significant merit and should 
be approved subject to the implementation of the updated mitigation measures described in this report 
and supporting documents.

application, on behalf of Canberra Data Centre Pty Ltd (CDC) (the Applicant), to modify Development

1.2 Project Background 
1.2.1 Approved Development– Overall Power and Backup infrastructure 
1.2.1.1 Original Major Project – MP 10_0142 (Hewlett Packard Data Centre) 
The original data centre development at the site was approved on 27 September 2011 under Part 3A of the 
Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979 as Major Project MP 10_0142 (Hewlett‑Packard Data Storage 
Project) (Part 3A approval). The Part 3A approval (approved 27 September 2011) granted consent for the 
construction, fit‑out and operation of a data storage facility, including associated infrastructure required to 
support a high‑reliability data centre use. In particular, the approval included: 
▪ construction of a single ‑storey data centre building comprising two data halls, together with ancillary 
administration and technical support areas;  
▪ establishment of backup power infrastructure, including diesel generators housed within acoustic 
enclosures for emergency and testing purposes;  
▪ provision of on‑ site diesel fuel storage to support generator operation during power outage and testing 
events; 
▪ construction of a high‑ voltage electrical switching station and associated electrical infrastructure to 
ensure secure and redundant power supply to the data centre; and  
▪ associated site works, access, hardstand areas, landscaping and security infrastructure.  
[[page 8]]
 
Modification Report | SSD-10330 MOD 4   Introduction 8 
 
 
The Part 3A approval expressly recognised that the data centre would operate 24 hours per day, seven days 
per week, and that uninterrupted power supply was fundamental to its operation. Backup diesel generators 
and fuel storage were therefore assessed and approved as integral operational infrastructure, rather than 
incidental or temporary works. 
This Part 3A approval established the baseline data centre use and associated power and backup 
infrastructure at the site, which was subsequently expanded and built upon through the State Significant 
Development approval under SSD‑10330 and its subsequent modifications. 
1.2.1.2 State Significant Development Approval – SSD-10330  
On 14 July 2020, SSD-10330 was approved by DPHI for an expansion to an existing data centre at the site. The 
approval granted consent for: 
An expansion to an existing data centre, including associated office space, supporting infrastructure and 
services, car parking and landscaping. 
Specifically, SSD-10330 approved the following: 
▪ Construction and operation of three (3) additional data centre buildings, including a 3 -storey data 
centre building (‘ Building 4’ which has been constructed) and two other data centre buildings at the 
western end of the site (herein, referred to as ‘ Building 5’ and ‘Building 6’) which are currently under 
construction.  
▪ 48 diesel back -up generators, including 12 existing generators installed under CDC -19-01926. 
▪ Diesel fuel storage for up to 1,914,000 litres (L) across the whole site.  
▪ Provision of combined 101 parking spaces inclusive of:  
– 92 secure parking spaces, inclusive of 5 accessible parking spaces,  
– 7 short-term visitor parking spaces at the site entrance,  
– 2 security staff parking spaces.  
This approval expanded the development and operations of the data centre that was originally approved 
under the Part 3A approval, MP10_0142. 
The approval included batteries and backup diesel generators, which are required onsite to ensure 
uninterruptable power supply and for the data centre buildings to operate in the event of power failure. 
Notably, this development was approved based on the actual, usable amount of diesel that would be stored 
at the site to support development operations. 
1.2.1.3 SSD-10330 Modification 1 
SSD-10330 Mod 1 (Mod 1) was approved by DPHI on 17 April 2024 for the modification of the built form and 
design of Building 5 and Building 6 as approved under SSD-10330. The approved modifications include the 
following: 
▪ re-configuration of the internal layout of Buildings 5 and 6, to facilitate a total of thirty -two (32) 
dedicated battery rooms in each building;  
▪ increase to the height of Buildings 5 and 6 from three storeys to four storeys with a plant deck on the 
roof of the buildings (thereby an increase in the overall gross floor area);  
▪ modification to the rooftop plant configuration, including an increase in the number of 
chillers/condensers and a reduction in the number of packed air -conditioning ( PAC) units; and 
▪ removal of rooftop noise barrier structures.  
1.2.1.4 SSD-10330 Modification 2 
SSD-10330 Mod 2 (Mod 2) was approved by DPHI on 6 March 2026 for the modification for the provision of 
additional generators onsite and a revised testing regime which includes the following:

2 Strategic Context 
2.1 Project Justification - Strategic Context 
The proposal will support the operational needs of the future tenants for the approved data centre building 
halls and thus, support the delivery of usable data centres within the Blacktown LGA. The development, as 
proposed to be modified, will continue to deliver a key piece of technical infrastructure consistent with the 
strategic principles of the relevant strategies as: 
▪ The proposal will continue to support the provision of employment opportunities accessible to residents 
of the Central and Western Cities.  
▪ The proposal will continue to support the appropriate use of the site within the Western Sydney 
Employment Area and south of the Bungarribee, Huntingwood and Arndell Park industrial areas.  
As such, the development as proposed to be modified continues to align with the relevant strategic 
objectives of the Greater Sydney Region Plan: A Metropolis of Three Cities, Our Greater Sydney 2056: Central 
City District Plan and Future Transport Strategy 2056. The proposal supports the strategic role of the Western 
Sydney Employment Area by enabling the ongoing operation and adaptation of critical digital infrastructure. 
2.2 Key Features of Site and Surrounds 
2.2.1 Site Description 
The site is located at 17 Roberts Road, Eastern Creek and is within the Eastern Creek Business Park. The legal 
description of the site is Lot 2 in Deposited Plan 1159804. The site is 14.52ha in area and is bounded by the M4 
Western Motorway to the north and the West Link M7 Motorway to the east. The site is located in the Blacktown 
Local Government Area (LGA) and within the Western Sydney Employment Area (WSEA). 
The site is approximately 13 kilometres west of the Parramatta Central Business District (CBD) and six 
kilometres south of Blacktown CBD. It is within the southern part of the Blacktown LGA, approximately 1.3 
kilometres north of the Fairfield LGA boundary. 
Currently, the site accommodates an existing building (herein, referred to as ‘Building 1’) located at the 
western portion of the site. This existing building accommodates a data centre hall as well as existing 
office/admin area. In support of the Building 1, there are existing 15 supporting back-up generators. At the 
south-eastern corner of the site, there is an existing, high voltage plant. 
West of Building 1, there are two (2) constructed and operational data centre buildings. One 2-storey data 
centre building (herein, referred to as ‘Building 3’) features associated 12 back-up generators. The other, 3 
storey data centre building (‘Building 4’) was approved as part of SSD-10330. Further west are Buildings 5 
and 6 approved under SSD-10330 (as approved to be modified under Mod 1).

2.2.2 Surrounding Context 
The development site is surrounded by a variety of industrial land use activities and significant transport and 
utilities infrastructure. 
▪ North: The adjoining land to the north comprises large -scale warehouses, freight and logistics and light 
industrial activities with ancillary offices, all forms part of the Eastern Creek Business Park.  
▪ East: To the east is a parcel of vacant land and landscaped buffer between the light industry use land 
on the western side of M7 Highway. Western Sydney Park Land and SUEX Eastern Creek Resource 
Recovery Park is further to the east of the site, located on the eastern side of M7 Highway.

multiple high voltage transmission lines. Austral Bricks is located further south.  
▪ West: The land to the west of Roberts Road is Australian Personnel Solutions National Service Centre. 
Further to the west is Old Wallgrove Road and TransGrid Eastern Creek site, containing high voltage 
transmission lines and substations.  
The nearest residential accommodation is located approximately 900m to the southeast of the site, along 
Flavex Lane. Other nearby residential areas include Minchinbury to the north of the site beyond the M4 
Motorway (approximately 4km from the site), and Erskine Park to the west (approx. 2.5km from the site).

▪ MTU 20V4000 DS4000 (Penske) generators, with an individual electrical generation capacity of 
approximately 3.3  MW per unit; and  
▪ Where relevant, Cummins QSK ‑series standby generators (including QSK95 ‑G4 and QSK60 ‑G4 units) 
with individual capacities ranging from approximately 1.9  MW to 3.2  MW per unit, consistent with those 
already operating on the site.  
The proposed, additional generators will be supported by diesel fuel storage tanks. These tanks will be 
designed and constructed in accordance with the requirements of AS 1657:2018, AS 1692:2006 and AS 
1940:2017.  
The diesel storage tanks on site are in the form of self-bunded, double-skin belly tanks that are mounted to 
the base of the diesel generator engine skid and installed above ground. The diesel fuel storage amount 
varies between different generators on site. The total maximum possible capacity for diesel fuel storage will 
increase to 2,862 tonnes under SSD-10330 Mod 4.  
However, the tanks will also be fitted with a physical control that limits the maximum fill level of the tank to a 
value below its installed maximum capacity, such that the cumulative on‑site diesel storage remains below 
2,000 tonnes at any one time. This will be designed and installed consistent with the design and 
management measures conditioned under the approved SSD-10330 MOD 2. 
3.2.2 Updated Testing Regime 
The proposal seeks to establish a modified testing schedule, which will align with the updated number of 
generators. The proposed, updated testing regime seeks to undertake no-load and full-load tests on each of 
the 111 generators across the whole site. The proposed “no-load” test will function as a basic systems check 
for the generators without energising anything. The proposed “full-load” test will function as a simulation of a 
power failure event, as the generators are connected to a power sink and operated at full capacity.

4.1 Substantially the Same Development 
The proposed modification is considered to be substantially the same development as that originally 
approved under SSD-10330, consistent with section 4.55(1A) of the EP&A Act with consideration of the 
following: 
▪ The nature and purpose of the development remains unchanged and continues to comprise the 
operation of a data centre with supporting power, cooling and ancillary infrastructure approved under 
SSD‑10330. The proposed modifications are to works that are operational and ancillary in nature and 
are required to support the ongoing use of the approved data centre development under SSD‑ 10330. 
▪ The modification does not introduce any new land use. The additional generators, chillers, internal 
fit‑out works, formalisation of existing car parking and minor internal access refinements are contained 
within the approved site and development footprint.  
4.2 Minimal Environmental Impact 
Section 4.55(1A) of the EP&A Act requires that an application to modify a consent demonstrates that the 
proposed modification will result in minimal environmental impact. As detailed in Section 6 of this 
Modification Report, the proposal has been supported by technical assessments that consider the impacts of 
the modified development relative to the approved SSD-10330 development. 
The proposed modification is considered to result in minimal environmental impact for the purposes of 
section 4.55(1A) of the EP&A Act because: 
▪ Detailed technical assessments demonstrate that the environmental impacts of the development as 
modified are consistent with, and in some cases reduced from, those previously assessed and 
approved under SSD ‑10330 and its modifications. While cumulative operational and environmental 
impacts have been assessed having regard to infrastructure operating across the broader site, the 
proposed modification itself is limited to amendments to infrastructure approved under SSD- 10330. No 
new or unacceptable environmental impacts are identified as a result of the proposed modifications.  
▪ technical assessments confirm that impacts relating to noise, air quality, visual amenity, stormwater 
and dangerous goods can be appropriately managed and remain generally consistent with the 
approved development; and  
▪ the proposal will not result in any additional biodiversity, heritage or contamination impacts beyond 
those previously assessed and approved under SSD- 10330. 
Accordingly, the proposed modification is considered to satisfy the minimal environmental impact test under 
section 4.55(1A) of the EP&A Act. 
While a number of technical assessments have considered cumulative operational scenarios across the 
broader Eastern Creek Data Centre campus, this approach has been adopted to ensure a conservative and 
transparent assessment framework having regard to operational thresholds and environmental 
considerations relevant to the site as a whole. Notwithstanding this approach, the proposed modifications to 
SSD-10330 remain limited in scope and are confined to the specific works and operational changes described 
in this application.

The primary noise sources associated with the Eastern Creek Data Centre comprise operational noise from

▪ Scope 3 emissions, largely associated with the manufacture, delivery and installation of major 
equipment (notably generators and chillers), with smaller ongoing contributions from fuel supply, 
waste and supporting services.  
The GHG Assessment identifies that Scope 2 emissions associated with electricity consumption are the 
dominant source of unmitigated emissions over the operational life of the project. 
The cumulative operation of the broader Eastern Creek Data Centre campus, inclusive of existing operations, 
approved development and the MOD 4 infrastructure, would exceed the NGER facility reporting threshold of 
25,000 t CO₂-e per annum for combined Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions and is therefore classified as a large 
emitter for the purposes of the EPA Guide. 
However, the GHG Assessment concludes that: 
▪ emissions associated with diesel generator operation are relatively small compared to 
electricity ‑related emissions and are limited by strict operational controls on generator testing and use;  
▪ Scope 3 emissions are concentrated in the construction and installation phase, particularly associated 
with major plant and equipment, and reduce substantially during ongoing operations; and  
▪ the project’s contribution to NSW ‑wide greenhouse gas emissions remains very small, representing a 
fraction of one per cent of projected State emissions, even under conservative unmitigated scenarios  
(with the assessment estimating a peak contribution of approximately 0.16% of projected NSW 
emissions in 2040 prior to mitigation measures being applied).  
Additionally, greenhouse gas emissions associated with the modified development can be effectively 
managed through established and committed mitigation measures. The GHG Assessment notes that CDC 
has committed to achieving net zero carbon for Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions by 2030 through a 
combination of operational efficiency measures, renewable electricity procurement and carbon offsetting. 
CDC’s sustainability commitments and emissions reporting framework are publicly reported through its 
annual Sustainability Report prepared in accordance with the Australian Sustainability Reporting Standards. 
The Peer Review supports this conclusion, noting that the mitigation hierarchy and proposed offset approach 
are appropriate for the scale and nature of residual emissions associated with the modified project. 
6.7.3 Mitigation Measures 
As per the GHG Assessment recommendations, the mitigation measures approved under SSD‑10330 MOD 2 
can continue to support the proposed modification and maintain consistency with: 
▪ the NSW Government’s legislated target of net zero emissions by 2050 under the Climate Change (Net 
Zero Future) Act 2023; and  
▪ the principles of ecologically sustainable development  (ESD), including the minimisation of greenhouse 
gas emissions through design, operational efficiency and offsets.  
A continuation and scaling of the existing emissions management framework that has been established for 
the Eastern Creek Data Centre and previously assessed under MOD 2 can account for the increased number 
of generators and additional cooling infrastructure proposed. This includes continued procurement of 
renewable electricity certificates to match electricity consumption across the campus and the purchase of 
Australian Carbon Credit Units (ACCUs) to offset residual Scope 1 emissions associated with generator testing 
and refrigerant leakage. The Peer Review confirms that the availability of ACCUs is expected to be sufficient to 
meet the projected residual Scope 1 offset requirement identified for the modified project. As such, no new 
avoidance, substitution or offset measures are proposed specifically as a result of the additional generators 
and chillers under MOD 4.

6.9 Site Suitability 
A Site Suitability Assessment has been prepared by Douglas Partners (Appendix D) to assess contamination 
considerations associated with SSD-10330 MOD 4. The assessment included a review of the proposed 
modification plans, previous contamination and geotechnical investigations undertaken for the site, and 
previous desktop reviews prepared for SSD-10330 MOD 2. The assessment also reviewed the Preliminary Site 
Investigation prepared for the original SSD application and the earlier Site Suitability Assessment undertaken 
for the Eastern Creek campus. The assessment notes that the site forms part of a broader area previously 
subject to a Site Audit Statement and Site Audit Report prepared by Peter J Ramsay & Associates Pty Ltd in 
2011, which concluded the site was suitable for the approved industrial land use. The proposed modification 
works remain within the previously audited boundary. 
The assessment concludes that the site has a low risk of potential contamination and that site conditions 
have not significantly changed since preparation of the previous MOD 2 desktop assessment. The proposed 
modification works, including the installation of additional diesel generators, additional cooling infrastructure, 
internal fitout works, formalisation of parking and minor road modifications, are not considered to 
significantly alter previous conclusions regarding suitability of the site for the proposed development. In 
relation to the proposed generators, the assessment notes that where constructed appropriately with respect 
to relevant standards and codes of practice, designed, constructed and operated in accordance with the 
relevant Australian Standards, operational controls and applicable codes of practice, the risk of diesel leaks 
or spills would be minimised. 
While limited excavation may be required for elements of the proposed modification, works would occur 
within previously assessed areas of the site and any imported materials would be required to be legally 
importable and appropriately classified. An unexpected finds protocol can also be implemented during works 
to manage any residual risks during excavation. 
As such, the site is considered to have a low risk of potential contamination, the proposed modification is not 
considered to pose any additional contamination risk, and no further contamination investigation is 
warranted at this stage. The Douglas review concludes that the proposed modification works do not 
significantly alter previous conclusions regarding the suitability of the site for the approved development.
