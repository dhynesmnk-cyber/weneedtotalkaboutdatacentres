1 Introduction 
This report provides the NSW Department of Planning, Industry and Environment’s (the Department’s) 
assessment of an application to modify the State significant development (SSD) consent for  the Lane 
Cove West Data Centre (SSD-9741).  
The modification application seeks consent to replace the approved medium voltage (MV) back-up 
generators with smaller, low voltage (LV) generators, and to undertake minor alterations and additions 
to improve the operational efficiency of the development.  
The application was lodged on 5 February 2020 by Greenbox Architecture Pty Ltd ( the Applicant) 
pursuant to section 4.55(1A) of the Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979 (EP&A Act). 
1.1 Background 
The Applicant is constructing a data centre at 1 Sirius Road, Lane Cove West  in the Lane Cove local 
government area (see Figure 1).  
 
Figure 1 | Local context 
Data centres are places which are used by organisations to store their electronic computer applications 
and data. This could include your personal data (such as your Facebook account or Instagram), 
corporate data (such as a business’s payroll system) or sensitive government data (such as Medicare 
records).  

[[page 7]]
 
Lane Cove West Data Centre Modification 1 (SSD-9741-Mod-1) | Modification Assessment Report 2 
In recent years, there has been an increased need for new large and small -scale data centres across 
the state. As new technologies (such as driverless cars, fas ter mobile data networks and artificial 
intelligence) become commonplace, a significant number of large and small -scale data centres will be 
required to store the volume of electronic data accessed and created by Australians.  
1.2 Site Description 
The site is located in the Lane Cove West Business Park, approximately 8 kilometres (km) north-west 
of the Sydney central business district (CBD), 13 km east of the Parramatta CBD and 3.5 km west of 
Chatswood. The site is legally described as Lot 1 in Depo sited Plan (DP) 1151370 and has an area of 
approximately 3.9 hectares (ha).  
Two patches of the Plant Community Type (PCT) ‘Smooth-barked Apple - Red Bloodwood open forest’ 
are situated in the north-eastern and southern sections of the site. The north-western corner of the site 
was historically used for landfilling and is the subject of a current ‘Maintenance of Remediation Order’ 
(Notice No. 28027, dated 27/09/2005) issued by the Environment Protection Authority (the EPA). 
The site is surrounded by industrial land uses and natural features (see Figure 2), including:  
• industrial and commercial buildings, such as Harley -Davidson Australia and New Zealand 
(1A Sirius Road) and the Lane Cove Tunnel (LCT) Motorway Control Centre (5 Sirius Road)  to 
the east 
• Lane Cove River, a large area of native vegetation and an Ausgrid-operated transmission line to 
the south and west 
• Stringybark Creek and the former ‘Cumberland Paper Mill’ site (now occupied by S. C. Johnson 
& Son) to the north. 
The nearest sensitive receivers are in ‘Arise Lane Cove’, a residential apartment tower located 
approximately 175 metres (m) to the north of the site (see Figure 1).

1.3 Approval history 
On 15 November 2019, development consent was granted by the then Executive Director, Compliance, 
Industry and Key Sites for the Lane Cove West Data Centre (SSD -9741). The development consent 
permits the construction and 24 -hour operation of a data centre with associated offic e, supporting 
infrastructure and services, suspended driveway, car parking and landscaping (see Figure 3).  
At the time of writing this report, the Applicant has commenced bulk earthworks across the site to 
facilitate construction of the suspended driveway and the data centre building.

2 Proposed modification 
On 5 February 2020 , the Applicant lodged a modification application under section 4.55(1A) of the 
EP&A Act to modify development consent SSD-9741. The modification is described in full in the 
Statement of Environmental Effects (SEE) included in Appendix B and a comparison of the approved 
development against the proposed modification is illustrated in Figure 3 to Figure 6 below.  
The Applicant’s client intends to lease space within the development’s data halls to a single tenant, who 
will undertake the fit-out of this space with their own computer systems, server systems and networking 
equipment.  
The original SSD application included the installation of 80 medium voltage (MV) back-up generators 
to provide continuous power to the development during a power outage event. Following further 
discussions with the tenant  regarding their essential infrastructure requirements, the Applicant is 
seeking to replace the MV generators with smaller, low voltage (LV) generators, and to undertake minor 
alterations and additions to improve the operational efficiency of the development.  
Consequently, the Applicant proposes the following changes to the approved development as part of 
the modification:  
• an increase in the development’s gross floor area (GFA), from 32,429 m2 to 34,983 m2 
• relocation of 80 diesel fuel storage tanks from t he external plant platforms to 16 steel tanks 
located internally on Level 1, with a total storage capacity of 1,952 kilolitres (kL) 
• replacement of 80 MV generators with 116 LV generators , to be located on the external plant 
platforms 
• relocation of all switchgear and power train units to the external plant platforms 
• subsequent increases in the height and footprint of the external plant platforms to accommodate 
the additional LV generators and plant equipment, including: 
o North-eastern plant platform – From three (3) to six (6) levels 
o North-western plant platform – From three (3) to five (5) levels 
o South-western plant platform – From two (2) to four (4) levels 
• realignment of the western fire trail to accommodate changes to the external plant platforms 
• an increase in the number of trees removed from the site, from 123 to 127 trees 
• a reduction in the floor height of Level 1 , from Reduced Level (RL) 9.9 m to RL 8.4 m, and the 
subsequent construction of two additional data halls on this floor 
• installation of six water storage tanks and an associated pump room at the southern end of the 
main parking area 
• relocation of 18 parking spaces to the southern and western sections of the vehicle ramp 
• extension of one (1) goods lift to facilitate maintenance access to the roof  and the installation of 
an internal passenger lift adjoining the north-eastern façade of the development.

3 Strategic context 
3.1 Greater Sydney Region Plan 
The vision of the Greater Sydney Region Plan 2018, A Metropolis of Three Cities (the Region Plan) 
seeks to meet the needs of a growing and changing population by transforming Greater Sydney into a 
metropolis of three cities – the Western Parkland City, the Central River City and the Eastern Harbour 
City. It brings new thinking to land use and transport patterns to boost  Greater Sydney’s liveability, 
productivity and sustainability by spreading the benefits of growth. 
The proposed modification would continue to support the objectives of the Region Plan by: 
• providing additional, flexible data storage capacity within the Eastern Economic Corridor 
(see Objective 3). 
• providing new, knowledge -intensive jobs closer to where people live (see Objective 23 and 
Strategy 23.1). 
3.2 North District Plan 
The Greater Sydney Commission has released six district plans encompassing Greater Sydney, which 
will guide the delivery of the Region Plan. The subject site is located in the North District, which forms 
a large part of the Eastern Harbour City. The North District Plan (the NDP) is a 20-year plan to manage 
economic, social and  environmental matters in northern Sydney. It is a guide for implementing the 
Region Plan at a district level and is a bridge between regional and local planning. 
The proposed modification would continue to align with the actions of the NDP by:  
• supporting Sydney’s status as a global city and ensuring knowledge-intensive jobs are located 
close to areas of high population growth (see actions 36 to 39) 
• continuing to support the retention and improvement of industrial -zoned land within the Lane 
Cove West Precinct (see actions 46 to 49). 
The Department has considered the strategic context of the site and is satisfied the proposed 
modification is consistent with the intent of the relevant strategic planning documents.

4 Statutory context 
4.1 Scope of modifications 
The Department has reviewed the scope of the modification application and is satisfied the proposed 
modification would result in minimal environmental impacts, and relates to substantially the same 
development as the original development consent on the basis that: 
• the primary function and purpose of the approved development would not change as a result of 
the proposed modification 
• the modification is of a scale that warrants the use of section 4.55(1A) of the EP&A Act  
• any potential environmental impacts would be minimal and appropriately managed through the 
existing or modified conditions of consent. 
Therefore, the Department is satisfied the proposed modification is within the scope of section 4.55(1A) 
of the EP&A Act and does not constitute a ne w development application. Accordingly, the Department 
considers that the application should be assessed and determined under section 4.55(1A) of the 
EP&A Act rather than requiring a new development application to be lodged.  
4.2 Consent authority 
The Minister for Planning and Public Spaces (the Minister) is the consent authority for the application 
under section 4.5(a) of the EP&A Act.  
However, under the Minister’s delegation dated 9 March 2020, the Director, Industry Assessments, may 
determine the application under delegation as: 
• the application has not been referred to the Independent Planning Commission prior to the date 
of the delegation and 
• a political disclosure statement has not been made and 
• there are no public submissions in the nature of objections. 
4.3 Mandatory matters for consideration 
The Department undertook a comprehensive assessment of the development against the mandatory 
matters for consideration as part of its original assessment of SSD-9741. The D epartment considers 
this modification application does not  result in significant changes that would alter the mandatory 
matters for consideration under section 4.15 of the EP&A Act and  the conclusions made as part of the 
original assessment.

5 Engagement 
5.1 Department’s engagement 
Under the Environmental Planning and Assessment Regulation 2000, there is no requirement for 
section 4.55(1A) modifications to be publicly exhibited. In addition, the Department’s Community 
Participation Plan notes that the exhibition requirements for section 4.55(1A) modifications are 
discretionary, and based on the urgency, scale and nature of the proposal.  
Given the proposed modification would result in minimal environmental impacts and  relates to 
substantially the same development as the original development consent (see Section 4.1), the 
application was not publicly exhibited.  
However, the application was made publicly available on the Department’s website and was referred to 
Lane Cove Council (Council) and the relevant public authorities for comment. The Department received 
six submissions, none of which objected to the proposed modification.  
Council noted the design amendments proposed would increase the development’s GFA by 
approximately 2,554 m2. Accordingly, Council recommended Condition A21 of the consent be updated 
to reflect the associated increase in the development’s section 7.11 contribution.  
The EPA noted the total storage capacity of the development’s diesel storage tanks would not trigger 
the need for an Environment Protection Licence (EPL) under Schedule 1 of the Protection of the 
Environment Operations Act 1997  (the POEO Act) , but requested the Applicant provide further 
information to assist in determining whether the back-up generators would trigger Schedule 1 of the 
POEO Act as electricity generating works. The EPA also sought further information regarding potential 
noise and air quality impacts associated with the testing and use of the back-up generators. 
The Environment, Energy and Science Group  (EESG) requested the Applicant clarify the  total 
number of trees proposed to be removed from the site  and whether the size of the planted Asset 
Protection Zone (APZ) would be reduced as a result of the subject modification.  
Transport for NSW (TfNSW) noted the lowest level of the data centre (Level 1) would be set at an RL 
of 8.4 m, instead of 9.9 m as approved. TfNSW subsequently requested the Applicant confirm whether 
the modification would alter the RL of the foundation piles  and/or impact upon the LCT western 
ventilation shaft. 
Fire and Rescue NSW (FRNSW) advised it had no specific comments beyond those  it had provided 
on the original development application.  
SafeWork NSW advised it had no specific comments. 
The Rural Fire Service (RFS) did not provide a submission on the proposed modification.

5.2 Response to submissions 
On 17 March 2020, the Applicant provided a Response to Submissions (RTS) on the issues raised 
during notification of the proposed modification (see Appendix B ). The RTS wa s made publicly 
available on the Department’s website and provided to the EPA and the EESG to consider whether it 
adequately addressed the issues raised. 
The EPA subsequently advised the proposal would not require an EPL under the POEO Act and  
recommended the Applicant’s generator testing procedure be incorporated into the development 
consent. The EPA also recommended the Department ensure the Applicant’s background noise 
monitoring is representative of long-term background noise levels at surrounding sensitive receivers. 
The EESG noted that the RTS did not confirm whether the total number of trees removed from the site 
would be replaced at a ratio greater than 1:1, as has previously been committed to by the Applicant. 
Accordingly, the EESG  reiterated its comments on the original development appl ication, and 
recommended the Department ensure all trees removed from the site be replaced be replaced at a ratio 
greater than 1:1. 
5.3 Additional information 
On 18 March 2020, the Department requested the Applicant provide a response to TfNSW’s comments, 
as the public authority’s submission was received following the conclusion of the notification period. In 
addition, the Department also requested the Applicant provide: 
• updated land owner’s consent for the subject modification application 
• an updated list of management and mitigation measures which reference s the data centre’s 
updated bushfire protection measures. 
On 31 March 2020, the Applicant provided a response to the Department’s request, and confirmed the 
RL of the foundation piles for the north-eastern plant platform (the closest element to the LCT western 
ventilation shaft) would not change as a result of the subject modification (see Appendix B).
