1 Introduction 
1.1 Development Background 
Goodman Property Services (Aust) Pty Ltd  (the Applicant) is seeking development consent for the 
construction and operation of a data centre with an operational capacity of 100 megawatts (MW) (the 
development). A detailed description of the development is provided in Section  2. 
Data centres are secure facilities used by organisations to store, process and manage electronic data 
and digital applications using specialised information technology (IT) infrastructure. This may include 
personal data (such as social media content or cloud photo libraries), corporate data (such as financial 
systems or customer records), or sensitive government data (such as health and taxation information). 
Increasingly, data centres also support high -performance computing tasks, including artificial 
intelligence (AI), machine learning and real-time analytics. 
In recent years, demand for both large-scale and edge data centres have grown significantly across 
New South Wales. This growth is being driven by the rapid adoption of digital technologies, including 
AI, autonomous systems, cloud computing and next -generation mobile networks. As these 
technologies become more embedded in everyday life and business operations, additional data centre 
capacity is required to support the increasing volume, speed and complexity of data generated and 
accessed by Australians. 
The Applicant is a global industrial property group, which develops and manages property in sectors 
including logistics, warehousing and distribution, e -retail, automotive, professional services, 
pharmaceuticals, and technology. Given the growing demand for data connectivity and technology in 
Australia, the Applicant has identified an opportunity to redevelop an underutilised industrial site into 
a data centre.

2.3 Uses and Activities 
The primary function of the development is to facilitate the secure collection, storage, processing, 
and distribution of electronic data and digital applications. These services are delivered through high-
performance IT infrastructure operated by cloud service providers, enterprise clients, or government 
entities. 
The facility comprises nine data halls distributed across two levels, each to be fitted out by the 
prospective tenant(s)  with racking systems to accommodate computer servers, networking 
equipment, and associated hardware  (see Figure 3). Tenants lease storage capacity within these 
systems and remotely manage their digital assets and applications via secure network connections. 
The development’s data halls would be cooled using a mechanical evaporative cooling system which 
incorporates the use of cooling towers to manage temperatures within the data halls. Recycled water 
is proposed to be used by cooling towers to reduce the development’s dependence on potable water.  
Ancillary office space is provided on the ground floor level , for on -site technicians and operational 
staff. These spaces support routine maintenance, system monitoring, and IT support functions. 
Access to these areas is facilitated via secure entry points and internal circulation cores. 
The site is proposed to be supplied power through the Guildford West Transmission Station for Stage 
1 of the development. Stage 2 (total operations) of the development would be supplied power through 
a new 132 kilovolt (kV) underground transmission line undertaken by Endeavour Energy to connect to 
the proposed onsite 132 kV switching station and substation. Feeders are to be connected from the 
switching station to the switchrooms within the data centre to distribute the electrical load. 
To ensure uninterrupted operations during power outages, the facility incorporates a comprehensive 
backup power system. This includes  68 diesel generators and a network of  lithium-ion batteries  
configured to provide uninterruptible power supply (UPS). The batteries bridge the gap between grid 
failure and generator start -up, while the generators maintain full operational capacity until mains 
power is restored. 
Routine generator testing is undertaken to ensure system reliability. The Applicant proposes testing 
between 7:00 am and 6:00 pm Monday to Saturday, and 8:00 am to 6:00 pm on Sundays and public 
holidays. Two generators are pro pos ed to be  t ested at  any o ne  time  with no load during quarterly 
testing and 100% load for an annual test. Testing involves an artificial load applied to the generators 
u s i n g  u p  t o  t w o  p o r t a b l e  l o a d  b a n k s  a n d  undertaken in accordance with the site’s operational

The construction and 24 -hour operation of the development has the potential to emit noise, which

The development would involve the construction of a two -storey data centre building  with ancillary

Construction Noise Guideline (ICNG) (DECC, 2009).

7 Evaluation 
The Department’s assessment of the application has fully considered all relevant matters under 
section 4.15 of the EP&A Act, the objects of the EP&A Act and the principles of ESD.  
The Department has considered the development on its merits, taking into consideration strategic 
plans that guide development in the area, the EPIs that apply to the development, advice received 
from the relevant public authorities, including Council, and submissions from the public. 
The development involves the construction and operation of a data centre with an operational 
capacity of 100 MW which will help to satisfy market demand for data storage infrastructure and 
capacity within Greater Sydney.  
None of the State government authorities, Council or the community have objected to the proposal . 
Although some minor issues were raised by government authorities or Council in their advice, these 
matters have been resolved through the provision of additional information by the Applicant during 
the assessment process. 
The Department’s assessment has found there would be some amenity impacts during operation of  
the data centre, such as noise, air quality and visual impacts . The Department has considered the 
information provided in the revised NIA, AQIA, VIA and supporting documentation and is satisfied the 
Applicant has designed the development to minimise these impacts. Residual impacts can be 
adequately mitigated and managed subject to the implementation of the Applicant’s mitigation and 
management measures and the recommended conditions of consent.  
In particular, the Department’s recommended conditions include: 
• the preparation of noise verification reporting to ensure the development meets the prescribed 
noise limits 
• the preparation and implementation of an Air Quality Management Plan including annual 
emissions testing and reporting, and a power outage notification protocol 
• the preparation and implementation of a Sustainability Management Plan including annual 
reporting on environmental sustainability performance metrics and greenhouse gas emissions and 
procedures for continual improvements and reductions.  
Overall, the Department’s assessment has concluded the development would: 
• provide additional data storage capacity within Greater Sydney  within an established industrial 
area 
• provide for an increase in productivity and employment within the  Cumberland LGA by investing 
$1.1 billion and provide up to 467 full-time equivalent construction jobs and 60 operational jobs

be approved, subject to conditions.
