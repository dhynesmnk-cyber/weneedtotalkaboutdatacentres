2. SCOPE OF REVIEW 
For projects with Scope 1 and 2 emissions exceeding 100,000 tCO 2-e per year at any time over the 
operational life of the project, mitigation assessments must be verified by an independent expert review.  
If offsets are proposed for a project, the independent expert review must consider whether suitable 
high-integrity offsets are likely to be available at the time when it is proposed to acquire offsets. 
Thus, in accordance with Section 4.7 of the NSW Guide for Large Emitters, the independent expert 
review focuses upon: 
▪ The mitigation assessment 
▪ The offsets strategy.  
3. EMISSIONS OVERVIEW 
The proposed Eastern Creek modification is expected to emit over 25,000 tonnes CO 2-e of Scope 1 
and 2 emissions in initial financial years of the project’s operations. Therefore, this project meets the 
criteria of a large emitter and is required by the NSW Guide for Large Emitters to prepare an 
emissions mitigation strategy.  
The modification to the data centre (under State Significant Development 10330) is expected to 
involve the construction and relocation of 4 previously approved generators and an addition of 24 
diesel generators for emergency power. 
The GHG Assessment Report has examined the Eastern Creek modification regarding its 
assessment boundary, emissions scenarios and sources, emissions mitigation measures, project 
assumptions, and reporting obligations and goals. The report has examined the whole lifecycle of the 
project being from 2025 to 2075. Scenarios explored in the report include the Business as Usual 
(BAU), modified business, and project only scenarios to compare emissions from Eastern Creek Data 
Centre without the modification, emissions from previously approved activities at Eastern Creek plus 
the modification, and emissions from the modification alone, respectively.  
As a result of activities carried out under the Eastern Creek modification, associated emissions would 
originate from the following sources:  
 Combustion of diesel for backup generators (Scope 1)  
 Refrigerant fugitive emissions (Scope 1)  
 Consumption of electricity from the grid (Scope 2) 
o As noted in the GHG Assessment Report, electricity emissions include those from 
customer IT and facility/non-IT consumption. Customer IT electricity falls under Scope

emissions in reference to the EPA’s definition of indirect emissions from electricity 
use.  
 Production and installation of equipment (Scope 3)  
 Minor services associated with the modification (Scope 3)  
 Manufacture and delivery of generators (Scope 3) 
The majority of the analysis provided relates to the ‘modified business’ scenario, which is the 
aggregate of the BAU and the project only components. This, in turn is evaluated against an 
‘operational throughput’ emissions estimate (stated as ‘a realistic load utilisation which, based on 
industry experience is…likely to be 50-70%’) and a ‘maximum capacity’ emissions estimate.

4. FINDINGS AND RECOMMENDATIONS 
4.1 Mitigation assessment 
The mitigation strategy by CDC employs measures for Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions for the Eastern 
Creek modification. The mitigation measures follow an “Energy efficiency strategy hierarchy” set out 
by CDC (Figure 1). This hierarchy begins with reducing energy demand and leaves offsets only for 
residual emissions, which is consistent with the NSW GHG Emissions Mitigation Hierarchy (Figure 2). 
Avoidance and substitution strategies are not directly referenced in the hierarchy figure by CDC; 
however, associated strategies are discussed elsewhere in the report (Table 14 and Section 4.4.2). 
 
Figure 1: Energy Efficiency Strategy Hierarchy (Source: Roberts Road Data Centre GHG 
Assessment Report, Figure 4) 
 
Figure 2: NSW GHG Emissions Mitigation Hierarchy (Source: NSW Guide for Large 
Emitters, Figure 1) 
As a significant avoidance measure, CDC plans to purchase electricity generated using renewable 
sources. As noted above, the GHG Assessment Report includes electricity consumed by the data 
centre tenant in the emissions estimation; therefore, it is our current understanding that Scope 2 
emissions by the tenant are also considered within the mitigation plan.

This strategy involves purchasing and surrendering large-scale generation certificates (LGCs) while 
they are available, and Renewable Electricity Guarantee of Origin certificates (REGOs) from 2031 
onwards. By purchasing LGCs (and subsequently REGOs), CDC expects to achieve a zero-emission 
factor for electricity used by the data centre. There is also reliance on the predicted grid 
decarbonisation sited in the GHG Assessment Report. Assuming renewable energy trends continue 
as expected, CDC anticipates that the GHG intensity of the grid will continue to decline which will 
consequently reduce the (unabated) Scope 2 emissions associated with the Eastern Creek 
modification.   
Annual projections for static Scope 2 emissions do not consider any NSW grid decarbonisation, which 
will likely over-state the Scope 2 GHG emissions in future years. This is supported by the statement 
(SLR, 2025) “Static scope 2 emissions have been calculated based on a constant national grid 
emission factor for NSW assuming no grid greening”. Alternatively, Scope 2 emissions incorporating 
projected grid decarbonisation have been explored and quantified in Table 27 and Table 28 for the 
modified business scenario.   
Another aspect of the mitigation strategy is built into the design of Eastern Creek Data Centre. The 
GHG Assessment Report notes that the Eastern Creek Data Centre is designed to have an average 
Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) of 1.3, which is stated to be 19% lower than the market average 
PUE of 1.61. A detailed monitoring system for Eastern Creek Data Centre equipment (electricity 
meters and hydraulic plant) has been set up to help identify excessive energy use and points of 
potential failure to increase energy efficiency. Additionally, regular maintenance and emission control 
technologies will be adopted for generator efficiency. 
Some other design initiatives that would reduce energy demand for the modification project include a 
closed-loop cooling system, high efficiency water fixtures (low flow), rainwater harvest and reuse, and 
efficient lighting appliances and equipment. CDC also commits to choosing suppliers that share its 
sustainability values, and where possible, focus on using low carbon equipment. It is expected that 
CDC will seek to use reduced carbon alternatives for steel and concrete needed during construction 
for the proposed modification.  
The mitigation strategy for the Eastern Creek modification proposal is assessed for feasibility and 
effectiveness in Table 14 of the GHG Assessment Report. Evaluating the feasibility of mitigation 
measures, helps to prevent the suggestion of unachievable actions to meet the requirements of the 
Guide. For example, alternatives to diesel fuel are noted as a mitigation measure, with the 
consideration that these are not yet readily available. Uncertainties are also explored in sections 4.4.1 
and 5.4 of the GHG Assessment Report, which discusses the possibilities of improved technology, but 
also financial or regulatory barriers that may develop.  
CDC also has wider sustainability strategies to meet external obligations such as net zero carbon by 
2030, compliance with the Corporations Act 2001 (mandatory climate-related disclosures) and the 
Australian Sustainability Reporting Standard (AASB S2). These wider measures contribute to the 
emissions mitigation strategies set out for the proposed modification project.  
As a last resort, CDC has specified an offset strategy to address residual emissions. Quantifying 
emissions will rely upon accurate reporting under the NGER Scheme. The offset strategy for the 
Eastern Creek modification is further discussed in Section 4.2 of this report. 
The NSW EPA provided feedback on CDC’s initial mitigation strategy for the Eastern Creek 
modification. In their feedback, they recommended inclusion of specific details on the timing and 
feasibility of sourcing Australian Carbon Credit Units (ACCUs) and LGCs. Additionally, they requested 
amending the report to include a figure on Scope 1, Scope 2, and Scope 3 emissions. The GHG 
Assessment Report provided by SLR Consulting (Revision 04) has incorporated these suggestions in 
Section 7 Offset Strategy and Figure 1 of the report. Other points raised by the EPA have been 
addressed separately by CDC in a letter responding to these recommendations.

4.2 Offsets Strategy 
The GHG Assessment Reports notes that CDC plans to purchase ACCUs from 2030 onwards to 
offset Scope 1 emissions. Associated offsets should mitigate residual emissions that are not avoided, 
reduced, or substituted through CDC’s mitigation hierarchy. Scope 1 emissions from diesel 
combustion and refrigerant leaks have been assessed for avoidance, reduction, and substitution. At 
the time the GHG Assessment Report was provided, there were not feasible diesel fuel or refrigerant 
substitution options for the Eastern Creek modification.  
Australian Carbon Credit Units (ACCUs) are referenced in the NSW Guide for Large Emitters (2025) 
as an example of appropriate offsets and are anticipated to be the default offset product applicable to 
the Eastern Creek modification.  
The Clean Energy Regulator (CER) provides detailed data on ACCU issuance, cancellations, and 
project registrations. Their Quarterly Carbon Market Reports provide a comprehensive analysis of 
market trends, including information on both offsets supply and demand. 
Evaluation of these data indicates that ACCU issuance has grown steadily since the scheme's 
inception in 2011. In recent years, total annual ACCU issuances were recorded as being between 18-
19 million. 
An evaluation of the ACCU market supports that changes in government policy, particularly the 
reforms to the Safeguard Mechanism, have a significant impact on demand and, consequently, supply 
(via new project registration and ACCU generation). 
Both the CER and market analysts (e.g. the Carbon Market Institute) project continued growth in 
ACCU generation. Forecasts suggest issuance could rise to 31 million in 2033, driven by a growing 
demand for offsets from the Safeguard Mechanism and voluntary markets.  
Assuming that the Proponent adheres to its commitment to source renewable electricity, this leaves 
904 ACCUs needed per annum to offset Scope 1 emissions by the modification project.  
Given the quantum of offsets required, and total anticipated annual ACCU issuance into the future, it 
is considered that there will be sufficient availability high-integrity offsets in any one year of 
operations. It is also noted that ACCUs may be surrendered from multiple vintages (years of 
issuance). 
In relying on ACCUS, CDC’s offset strategy for the Eastern Creek modification is considered 
satisfactory.  
5. CONCLUSIONS 
This independent expert review has assessed the GHG Assessment Report provided by SLR 
Consultants for the proposed Eastern Creek Data Centre modification, in accordance with Section 4.7 
of the NSW Guide for Large Emitters, the independent expert review focuses upon the proposal’s 
mitigation assessment and offsets strategy.  
The review determined that the GHG Assessment Report is compliant with the NSW EPA’s Guide for 
Large Emitters.
