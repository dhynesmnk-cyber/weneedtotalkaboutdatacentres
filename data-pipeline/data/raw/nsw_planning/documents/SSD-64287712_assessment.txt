Executive Summary 
The Trustee for NineZero  DC Sub Trust I, trading as DCI Data Centers (the Applicant)  proposes to 
construct and operate a data centre with associated infrastructure and ancillary office space at 
2 Wolseley Place, Jerrabomberra, within the Queanbeyan -Palerang local government area . The 
development has an estimated capital investment value of $ 278 million and would generate up to 
125 construction jobs and 24 operational jobs.  
The project seeks to respond to growing demand for secure, high-performance digital infrastructure 
in close proximity to the Australian Capital Territory (ACT) . The site is approximately 4.5 kilometres 
(km) south-w e s t  o f  t h e  t o w n  o f  Q u e a n b e y a n  a n d  1 2  k m  s o u t h -east of Canberra’s central business 
district (CBD). The site comprises 4.05 hectares (ha) of B7 Business Park-zoned land within Stage 1 of 
the Poplars Innovation Precinct and is cleared of any structures or vegetation.  
Surrounding land uses include future Innovation Precinct land, the Poplars Grassland Reserve , an 
Essential Energy substation and other  commercial and industrial premises. The nearest residential 
receivers are located approximately 330 metres (m) to the east of the o f  t h e  s i t e ,  i n  t h e  S o u t h  
Jerrabomberra residential area.  
The development involves a 24 -hour data centre with five data halls, a gross floor area of 5,826 m² 
and a maximum building height of 7.6 m. The facility would have a total power consumption of 
25.4 megawatts (MW) and would be supported by an external plant yard containing adiabatic coolers, 
pumps, back-up diesel generators, bulk diesel storage and lithium-ion battery storage.  
The Department exhibited the State significant development (SSD)  application and accompanying 
Environmental Impact Statement  from 25 March 2025 until 22 April 2025  and received two 
submissions from the public (one from Canberra Airport and one from the local electricity provider), 
one submission from Queanbeyan -Palerang Regional Council (Council) and advice from six 
government agencies. While Council’s submission was received outside of the exhibition period, it 
raised concerns regarding the development’s proposed water consumption of up to 66,000 kilolitres 
(kL) per annum.  
In response to this concern, the Applicant made refinements to the proposed cooling system to help 
reduce the development’s annual water consumption to 5,921 kL (reduction of approximately 90%).  
None of the other agencies or submissions objected to the development and, where relevant, all 
parties provided advice on conditions of consent to address residual impacts.  
The Department’s assessment focused on two key issues, being noise and vibration and air quality . 
During construction, activities associated with bulk earthworks and rock excavation have the potential 
to result in exceedances of the Noise Management Levels at some nearby receivers under worst-case 
conditions. However, the Department is satisfied these  impacts can be mitigated through standard 
[[page 5]]

construction hours and the implementation of a site -specific Construction Noise and Vibration 
Management Plan. In addition, the development’s operational noise modelling confirmed compliance 
with the established project noise trigger levels at all receivers during normal operations, subject to 
measures such as limiting generator tests to weekdays, acoustic treatment of building elements, and 
maintaining sound power levels as modelled.  
Air quality impacts during construction would be negligible ,  s u b j e c t  t o  t h e  i m p l e m e n t a t i o n  o f  
standard dust suppression measures under the development’s  Construction Environmental 
Management Plan. Operational emissions from the proposed back-up generators were also assessed, 
during both standard testing and emergency outage scenarios. No exceedances of relevant criteria 
were predicted during the testing scenario , and while conservative modelling indicated potential 
exceedances of particulate matter (PM2.5) and nitrogen dioxide (NO2) during a prolonged emergency 
event, the likelihood of such an event is extremely low. Conditions of consent will require the 
Applicant to implement an Air Quality Management Plan and a neighbour notification protocol to help 
manage any residual impacts during a power outage event.  
The Department considers the development is consistent with the South East and Tablelands 
Regional Plan 2036, the South Jerrabomberra Regional Job Precinct Master Plan and Council’s Local 
Strategic Planning Statement, which all support the ongoing development of the South 
Jerrabomberra Regional Job Precinct as a key strategic centre within close proximity to the ACT. The 
development would deliver significant economic benefits, including $278 million in capital investment 
and employment opportunities, while providing critical digital infrastructure within an emerging 
industrial and commercial innovation precinct.   
The Department concludes the development would not result in significant environmental or amenity 
impacts, subject to recommended conditions, and is in the public interest. Accordingly, the 
Department considers the development is approvable, subject to conditions.

1 Introduction 
1.1 Development Background 
T h e  T r u s t e e  f o r  N i n e Z e r o  D C  S u b  T r u s t  I ,  t r a d i n g  a s  D C I  D a t a  C e n t e r s  (the Applicant) is seeking 
development consent for the construction and operation of data centre with associated infrastructure 
and ancillary office space (the development) in Jerrabomberra, adjacent to the State’s border with the 
Australian Capital Territory (ACT) (see Figure 1). A detailed description of the development is provided 
in Section 2 of this report.  
Data centres are secure facilities used by organisations to store, process and manage electronic data 
and digital applications using specialised information technology (IT) infrastructure. This may include 
personal data (such as social media content or cloud photo libraries), corporate data (such as financial 
systems or customer records), or sensitive government data (such as health and taxation information). 
Increasingly, data centres also support high -performance computing tasks, including artificial 
intelligence (AI), machine learning and real-time analytics. 
In recent years, demand for both large -scale and edge data centres has grown significantly across 
the State . This growth is being driven by the rapid adoption of digital technologies, including AI, 
autonomous systems, cloud computing and next-generation mobile networks. As these technologies 
become more embedded in everyday life and business operations, additio nal data centre capacity is 
required to support the increasing volume, speed and complexity of data generated and accessed by 
Australians. 
The Applicant is a data centre provider specialising in data storage, data connectivity, and business 
continuity solutions across the Asia Pacific. In Australia, the Applicant currently operates two data 
centre campuses – one in western Sydney and one in Adelaide.

The site comprises 4.05 hectares (ha) of B7 Business Park -zoned land located at 2 Wolseley Place, 
Jerrabomberra,  i n  t h e  Queanbeyan-Palerang local government area  (LGA).  T h e  s i t e  i s  l e g a l l y  
described as Lot 6 Deposited Plan (DP) 1288362 and is located approximately 4.5 kilometres (km)

The site is bound by Tompsitt Drive to the north, Environa Drive to the east and Wolseley Place to the

2.2 Physical layout and design 
The physical layout and design of the development is shown in Figure 3 to Figure 5 below. The data 
centre presents as a  single-storey structure fronting Tompsitt Drive, with external plant areas , car 
parking and an access road located between the building and Wolseley Place.    
The development  comprises five data halls surrounding by mechanical and access corridors. Two 
ancillary office spaces are provided at either end of the building, each serviced by its own parking 
area. The external plant area includes space for the development’s adiabatic coolers , lithium -ion 
batteries, back-up generators, and associated pumping and electrical equipment.   
The main building would primarily be clad in precast concrete panels, with sections of textured metal 
cladding in warm red and dark grey used around the office spaces and to help break up the Tompsitt 
Drive façade. Elsewhere, vertical louvre screens would be used to shield the external plant area.  
All vehicles accessing the site would enter and exit via two driveways off Wolseley Place. Typical 
day-to-day deliveries would be accommodated by a loading dock  located at the centre of the data 
centre building, which can handle heavy vehicles up to 8.8 m in length. The internal access road has 
also been designed to accommodate occasional heavy vehicles up to 12.5 m in length.  
2.3 Uses and activities 
The primary purpose of the development is for the collection, storage, processing and distribution of 
electronic data and digital applications in IT hardware by cloud and content providers or government 
entities (the tenant/s).  
The Applicant will lease space within the data halls to its tenant/s, who will then fit out the space with 
associated computer systems, server systems and networking equipment. This IT hardware would be 
stored in racks designed to maximise the efficiency of the space.  
[[page 14]]
 
  DCI Poplars Data Centre Project (SSD-64287712) Assessment Report | 7 
A constant temperature would be maintained throughout the data halls using a series of adiabatic 
coolers located within the development’s plant yard . The ancillary office space would be used by 
on-site technicians, who provide IT support to external users and carry out regular maintenance.   
Data centres also require sophisticated energy back -up systems to ensure the Applicant’s tenant/s 
are always able to access their data. To this end, the  development includes a system of lithium -ion 
batteries and diesel back-up generators to maintain operations during a power outage event.  
The site would be serviced by two 11 kilovolt (kV)  transmission feeders from the existing zone 
substation adjoining the site . In the event of a power outage (one/all feeders are taken offline), 
lithium-ion batteries would provide an uninterrupted power supply until the back-up generator system 
reaches load. Then, the back-up generators would provide continuous power to the development until 
mains power has been restored by Essential Energy.  
To ensure the development can function during a power outage event, the Applicant would also 
undertake regular testing of each back -up generator. During some of these tests, an artificial load 
would be applied to the generators using up to two portable load banks. Further information regarding 
the proposed testing regime is provided in Table 2 below.

4.2 Mandatory Matters for Consideration 
Section 4.15 of the EP&A Act sets out matters to be considered by a consent authority when 
determining a development application  (DA). The Department’s consideration of these matters is 
discussed in Appendix  C of this report. 
4.3 Public Exhibition and Notification  
In accordance with section 2.22 and Schedule 1 to the EP&A Act, the DA and any accompanying 
information of an SSD application are required to be publicly exhibited for at least 28 days. The 
application was on public exhibition from 25 March 2025 until 22 April 2025 (29 days). Details of the 
exhibition process and notifications are provided in Section 5 of this report. 
4.4 Objects of the EP&A Act  
In determining a DA, the consent authority  should consider whether the development is consistent 
with the relevant objects of the EP&A Act (section 1.3) , including the principles of ecologically 
sustainable development (ESD). The Department has fully considered these matters in Section 6 and 
Appendix  C of this report, and is subsequently satisfied that the development is  consistent with the 
objects of the EP&A Act and the principles of ESD. 
4.5 Biodiversity Development Assessment Report  
Section 7.9(2) of the BC Act requires all SSD applications to be accompanied by a Biodiversity 
Development Assessment Report (BDAR) unless the Planning Agency Head and the Environment 
Agency Head determine that the development is not likely to have any significant impact on 
biodiversity values (as identified in the BC Act and in the Biodiversity Conservation Regulation 2017). 
A BDAR waiver request was submitted to the Department on 14 November 2024 . The Environment 
Agency Head and the Acting Team Leader, Industry Assessments, as delegate of the Planning 
Secretary, determined that the development is not likely to have any significant impact on biodiversity 
values and a BDAR waiver was subsequently granted on 20 December 2024.

approval are required from the Australian Government if a development is likely to impact on a Matter 
of National Environmental Significance (MNES), as it is considered to be a ‘controlled action’.

methods for rock -breaking, use of a ‘shroud’ for the rock -breaker arm (if required) and general 
measures such as avoiding simultaneous use of noisiest plant and equipment, limiting idling of 
vehicles and plant, orientation of equipment away from receivers/site boundaries and implementation

6.2.2 Operational impacts 
The AQIA identified key pollutants associated with the operation of the development to be nitrogen 
dioxide (NO 2) and particulate matter (PM 2.5 and PM 10). Other pollutants assessed include carbon 
monoxide (CO), sulfur dioxide (SO 2) and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). These pollutants 
are associated with the combustion of diesel fuel by the back-up generators.  
The AQIA considered the development’s potential air quality impacts against two operating scenarios:  
• Scenario 1 (‘Standard testing’ operating scenario)  – One back-generator and one load bank 
operating at 100% load 
• Scenario 2 (‘Worst case’ emergency operating scenario) – All 16 back-up generators operating 
concurrently under 100% load during a critical power outage event. 
For the ‘standard testing’ scenario (Scenario 1), the AQIA found that no exceedances of the relevant 
criteria were predicted for PM10, NO 2,  C O ,  S O 2 and PAHs at nearby sensitive receivers. While one 
exceedance was predicted for PM2.5, the AQIA noted this was due to elevated background levels and 
not as a result of back-up generator testing at the site.  
For the ‘emergency’ scenario (Scenario 2), the AQIA found that no exceedances of the relevant criteria 
were predicted for PM10, CO, SO2 and PAHs at nearby sensitive receivers. However, it was noted that 
exceedances of the PM 2.5 criteria were p ossible at up to five adjoining industrial receivers, and 
exceedances of the NO2 criteria was possible at one residential receiver location (R_17) to the east of 
the site.  
The Applicant noted that while exceedances have been predicted during the ‘emergency’ scenario , 
the assessment provided is extremely conservative and assumes the back-up generator system would 
need to be operated continuously across every hour of the day. In reality, a 1-hour power outage event 
would need to occur once a year for approximately 91 years before an exceedance of the PM2.5 criteria 
is likely to occur, and approximately 94 years before a single exceedance of the NO2 criteria is likely 
to occur.  
The Department has carefully considered the AQIA  and is satisfied the Applicant has provided a 
conservative assessment of the air pollution emissions associated with the operation of the 
development. Despite the AQIA predicting possible exceedances of the PM2.5 criteria at up to five  
adjoining industrial receivers and the NO 2 criteria at one residential receiver location during the 
‘emergency’ scenario, the  Department accepts the predicted likelihood of such exceedances 
occurring is extremely small given the low likelihood of a catastrophic power outage event occurring.  
The Department considers the air pollution emissions during scheduled maintenance can be 
appropriately managed by requiring the Applicant to ensure the generators are only tested in

7 Evaluation 
The Department’s assessment of the application has fully considered all relevant matters under 
section 4.15 of the EP&A Act, the objects of the EP&A Act and the principles of ESD.  
The Department has considered the development on its merits, taking into consideration strategic 
plans that guide development in the area, the EPIs that apply to the development, advice received 
from the relevant public authorities, including Council, and submissions from the public.  
he development involves the construction and operation of a data centre with an operational capacity 
of 25.4 MW which will help to satisfy market demand for data storage infrastructure within the State 
and in close proximity to the ACT.  
The Department’s assessment has found there would be some amenity impacts during operation of 
the data centre, such as noise and vibration and air quality impacts. The Department has considered 
the information provided in by the Applicant in its EIS and Submissions Report and  is satisfied the 
Applicant has designed the development to minimise these impacts.  
The Department is satisfied any residual impacts can be adequately mitigated and managed, subject 
to the implementation of the Applicant’s mitigation and management measures and the 
recommended conditions of consent. The conditions include, but are not limited to:  
• the preparation of a noise verification report to ensure the development meets the prescribed 
noise limits  
• a requirement to maintain a log of all instances where the back-up generators are used or tested 
at the site 
• the preparation and implementation of an Air Quality Management Plan which includes a 
requirement for monitoring and the implementation of a neighbour notification protocol during 
power outage events.  
Overall, the Department’s assessment has concluded the development would: 
• provide additional data storage capacity within an emerging strategic jobs precinct, in close 
proximity to the ACT  
• provide for an increase in productivity and employment within the Queanbeyan-Palerang LGA via 
a capital investment of approximately $278 million and the provision of up to 125 construction 
jobs and 24 knowledge-intensive operational jobs  
• be consistent with NSW Government policies, including the South East and Tablelands Regional 
Plan 2036, by helping to increase productivity and job opportunities within Jerrabomberra.  
The Department considers that these benefits can be realised without any significant amenity o r 
environmental impacts and therefore, considers the development is in the public interest and should 
be approved, subject to conditions.
