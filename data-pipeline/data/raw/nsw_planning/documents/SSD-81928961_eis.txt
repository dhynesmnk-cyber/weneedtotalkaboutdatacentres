The proposal will form part of a state -of-the-art data centre campus operated by Green 
Square Data Centres (Green Square DC), equipped to cater for growing data storage needs 
within Australia and the emergence of artificial intelligence (AI). 
The EIS has been prepared to respond to Secretary’s Environmental Assessment

LOCATION AND SITE DESCRIPTION 
The proposal is located in Norwest, a relatively new suburb within The Hills Shire LGA, 
approximately 35km north -west of the Sydney CBD and 12km north of Parramatta CBD. 
Norwest functions as a strategic centre for the region, characterised by knowledge-intensive 
employment uses including health, education, science, technology, finance and advanced 
manufacturing. 
The subject site is legally described as Lot 2021 DP 831173, known as 3 Brookhollow Avenue, 
Norwest. The site is irregular in shape with a total area of approximately 51,867sqm. A portion 
of the site contains an existing 4MW data centre approved under D93/318 (21 June 1993), which

north are Norwest Boulevard, commercial offices, an early learning centre, the Castle Pines 
retirement community and Castle Hill Country Club Golf Course. To the eas t are Windsor 
Road, commercial offices, limited residential pockets and industrial development. To the 
south are Brookhollow Avenue, commercial development, a hotel and medium -density 
residential uses. To the west are commercial uses including Norwest Mark etown shopping 
centre, retail premises and the Norwest Metro Station. 
The site is well serviced by public transport, with access to bus services and the Sydney Metro. 
Norwest Metro Station provides connections to Tallawong and the Sydney CBD, while bus 
services along Norwest Boulevard connect to Parramatta, Seven Hills and Rouse Hill. The site

PROJECT SUMMARY

PROJECT ALTERNATIVES 
Under clause 192 the provisions of the EP&A Regulation 2021, and in accordance with the 
State Significant Development Application Guide prepared by the Department, there is a 
requirement to analyse any feasible alternatives for SSDAs. 
1. ‘Do Nothing’:  The ‘Do Nothing’ option would result in the site remaining 
underutilised and an opportunity missed to deliver an enhanced data storage 
offering at the subject site, which has a long legacy of being utilised for the purpose 
of a data centre. 
Taking into consideration the above, it is considered that the construction of a new 
data centre at the site provides for a better outcome when compared to a ‘do nothing’ 
scenario given the expansion to the existing data centre campus and the critical role 
data centres play in the proper functioning of the economy, which is becoming 
increasingly digitalised. 
2. Alternative Design Options:  A comprehensive assessment of alternative design 
options was undertaken throughout the master planning and detailed design phases 
of the project. These options tested variations in building footprint, massing, height, 
and site layout in response to environ mental constraints, landscape values, and 
planning controls across the site. 
Early design iterations investigated a wider building footprint and alternative site 
configurations. These approaches would have resulted in the removal of a substantial 
number of trees across the site, including extensive impacts within the established 
20-metre perimeter buffer, and a reduced capacity to retain existing vegetation and 
landscape character.

ALTERNATIVES CONSIDERED 
Despite being heavy consumers of energy, data centres contribute substantially to energy 
savings in the economy. In addition, large -scale data centres can achieve higher efficiencies 
over smaller-scale ones. 
A multi -criteria analysis was undertaken to determine the most viable backup power 
technology for the site. As detailed in Section 4.2 of the Backup Power Report, alternative 
technologies were considered but deemed unviable for the following reasons: 
• Lithium-ion Batteries: whilst used for short-term Uninterruptable Power Supply (UPS) 
(up to 5 minutes), using batteries for long-duration backup (24+ hours) is not spatially 
or commercially viable due to the substantial physical footprint required. 
• Vanadium redox flow batteries: No viable due to lack of commercial availability at the 
scape required to achieve energy autonomy on-site. 
• Natural Gas Fuel (Gas Turbines/Reciprocating Engines): discounted due to the lack of 
sufficient pipeline capacity in the area and the complex cooling and ventilation 
requirements. 

[[page 73]]

POLICY AND PLACE.

E - [email redacted] 
W- patchplanning.com.au 
• Hydrogen Fuel (Fuel Cells): currently not viable due to the lack of an established 
hydrogen supply chain, significant on -site storage risks, and spatial constraints for 
storage tanks. 
• HVO Renewable Diesel Fuel: Deemed to have moderate viability. The proposed diesel 
generators can utilise HVO renewable diesel in lieu of standard diesel, subject to local 
supply chain availability. 
Consequently, reciprocating diesel generators were selected as the only technology currently 
offering the necessary reliability, market readiness, and power density for a facility of this scale. 
Section 7.1.5 of Appendix 29 provide a detailed comparative analysis of all alternative solutions 
considered, and the reasoning behind the proposed selection. 
UNINTERRUPTABLE POWER SUPPLIES 
Uninterruptable power supplies (UPS) will be used to allow the facility to continue to function 
between any disruption to the supply of utility power and the backup generators coming 
online. The UPSs will use energy stored in batteries, and recharge the ba tteries once power 
supply (generator or utility) is restored.

6.2.3. BULK AND SCALE 
Data centres represent a specialised building typology that requires tailored consideration of 
bulk and scale. The proposed built form is driven by the operational requirements of a 
hyperscale data centre while appropriately responding to its location with in the Norwest 
Business Park. 
The design team has rigorously tested alternative built form configurations to ensure the 
development is as compact and sensitive to site characteristics as possible. Key design 
responses in minimising overall building scale include: 
• Significant reduction in building height from RL 143.550 to RL 130.000 (a 13.55-metre 
reduction); 
• Full compliance with DCP controls , including building setbacks, site coverage, and 
the retention of established landscaped buffers; 
• Articulation of building volumes  to break down façade massing and express 
functional elements; 
• Enhanced architectural treatment  of the eastern and western facades with 
expressed stair cores to reduce perceived height and bulk; and 
• A refined façade strategy , incorporating variation in materials, finishes, and louvre 
systems to support airflow and avoid blank or visually dominant walls. 
The proposed development comprises a four-storey data centre with rooftop plant, achieving 
a maximum height of RL 130 (41.5m). While this results in a 12.06% variation to the RL 116 height 
standard under THLEP 2019, the scale is appropriate to and aligned with the evolving 
commercial character of the Norwest Precinct.  
The variation is largely driven by the need to retain existing mature vegetation. Reducing the 
building height would have necessitated significant vegetation removal and encroachment 
into the front setback. Through an iterative design process, the proposal  has been 
progressively refined to balance built form efficiency with environmental and visual 
outcomes, while retaining the majority of perimeter planting across the site. 
The architectural language adopts an honest expression of its functional purpose through a 
palette of perforated metal screens, glass, pre-cast panels, and metal battens. This materiality, 
combined with façade modulation, reduces visual massing and enhances the presentation to 
the public domain.

6.7.4. NABERS ENERGY RATING 
The NABERS Energy rating system provides a nationally recognised framework for assessing 
the operational energy performance of data centre infrastructure, based on measured energy 
consumption and benchmarked using Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE). According  to the 
NABERS FY25 Annual Report for Data Centres (NABERS, 2025), the average NABERS 
Infrastructure Energy rating across certified data centres in FY25 was 4.0 stars, with an 
average PUE of 1.52. 
From 1 July 2025, all new data centre developments in Australia are required to achieve a 
minimum 5 -star NABERS Energy rating, reflecting the sector’s alignment with national 
decarbonisation objectives and the Australian Government’s Net Zero in Government  
Operations Strategy. A 5 -star Infrastructure Energy rating typically corresponds to a PUE of 
approximately 1.34 or lower, indicating high energy efficiency and reduced environmental 
impact. 
GreenSquareDC has commenced the certification pathway by signing a NABERS 
Commitment Agreement (Reference: CA0458) and is targeting a design PUE of 1.33, which, if 
achieved in operation, would support a 5.5 -star NABERS Infrastructure Energy rating. This 
commitment provides assurance that the project will minimise operational emissions, 
enhance energy resilience and reliability, and align with government s ustainability 
requirements and broader net-zero goals.

6.9. BACK-UP POWER SYSTEM 
A Backup Power Report has been prepared by Aurecon (Appendix 20) to detail the proposed 
emergency power infrastructure required to support the mission-critical nature of the facility. 
6.9.1.  SYSTEM OVERVIEW 
The development requires a backup power system capable of supporting a total IT load of 
96MW in the event of a utility power failure. The selected system comprises 77 reciprocating 
diesel generators housed within acoustic-rated enclosures located on the external gantries of 
Buildings B, C, and D. 
The configuration includes: 
• Data Hall and Mechanical Load: 75 x 2.5MW generators (25 per building). 
• Front of House (FOH): 2 x 1MW generators. 
The generators are designed to operate using either standard diesel or Hydro treated 
Vegetable Oil (HVO), a renewable diesel alternative that significantly reduces lifecycle carbon 
emissions. 
6.9.2.  ALTERNATIVES CONSIDERED 
A multi -criteria analysis was undertaken to determine the most viable backup power 
technology for the site. As detailed in Section 4.2 of the Backup Power Report, alternative 
technologies were considered but deemed unviable  as standalone backup power for the 
following reasons: 
• Lithium-ion Batteries: whilst used for short-term Uninterruptable Power Supply (UPS) 
(up to 5 minutes), using batteries for long-duration backup (24+ hours) is not spatially 
or commercially viable due to the substantial physical footprint required. 
• Vanadium redox flow batteries: No viable due to lack of commercial availability at the 
scape required to achieve energy autonomy on-site. 
• Natural Gas Fuel (Gas Turbines/Reciprocating Engines): discounted due to the lack of 
sufficient pipeline capacity in the area and the complex cooling and ventilation 
requirements. 
• Hydrogen Fuel ( Fuel Cells ): currently not viable due to the lack of an established 
hydrogen supply chain, significant on -site storage risks, and spatial constraints for 
storage tanks. 
• HVO Renewable Diesel Fuel: Deemed to have moderate viability. The proposed diesel 
generators can utilise HVO renewable diesel in lieu of standard diesel, subject to local 
supply chain availability. 
Consequently, reciprocating diesel generators were selected as the only technology currently 
offering the necessary reliability, market readiness, and power density for a facility of this scale. 
6.9.3.  FUEL STORAGE AND AUTONOMY 
Fuel will be stored in bulk tanks located within fire-rated tank chambers on the ground floor, 
feeding day tanks located within the generator enclosures. This configuration complies with 
AS 1940 and eliminates outdoor separation distance requirements to protected places and 
property lines. The system is sized to provide a minimum of 24 hours of continuous operation 
at full load as follows: 
• Building B: 543kL nominal storage.

6.9.4.   TESTING AND MAINTENANCE 
To ensure the reliability of the mission-critical infrastructure, the generators require a rigorous 
and periodic testing regime. The testing procedures are categori sed into three  (3) distinct 
types, each designed to verify specific system functions without relying on the public utility 
grid: 
• Full Load Tests (Annually): An endurance test where each generator is run at 100% 
capacity for a duration of 2 to 4 hours. This is achieved using on -site permanent load 
banks, which simulate the electrical demand of the data halls. This test ensures the 
system can sustain the facility's peak load during a prolonged outage. 
• Start Tests (Monthly): A brief run of approximately 10 to 15 minutes to verify the 
integrity of the start -up sequence, battery health, and initial synchroni sation. These 
tests are conducted without taking on any electrical load. 
• Transformer Maintenance: 0.2 exercises per year per generator (once every 5 years). 
Run time is 240 minutes (+15 minutes cooldown), testing 1 generator at a time 
(totaling 77 tests). 
TESTING STRATEGY AND SIMULTANEOUS OPERATIONS 
The facility utilises a ‘simultaneous testing’ strategy to manage the ‘time budget’ for the site. 
While there are 77 generators in Stage 2, testing them individually would result in excessive 
operational hours. Instead, the proponent intends to test at least two (2) generators 
concurrently whenever possible. 
By staggering these tests in pairs or small groups , the total cumulative running time for the 
site is reduced to approximately 161.5 hours per year. This efficiency ensures the project 
remains well below the 200 -hour limit specified under the Protection of the Environment 
Operations Act 1997 , maintaining its status as a non -scheduled activity regarding air 
emissions. 
To minimi se impacts on the surrounding Norwest community, the following constraints 
apply: 
• Rotational Schedule: Testing is conducted on a staggered, rotational basis. No more 
than two (2) generators across the entire campus (suiting the load bank arrangement 
of one (1) at SYD1 Stage 1 and two (2) at SYD1 Stage 2) will be tested simultaneously. 
• Time of Day: All routine testing is restricted to daytime hours to minimize noise and 
air quality impacts on sensitive receivers: 
o Monday to Saturday: 07:00 to 18:00 
o Sundays and Public Holidays: 08:00 to 18:00 
• Cumulative Duration: The total cumulative testing time for the facility is estimated at 
161.5 hours per year. This falls well within the 200 -hour limit specified under Clause 
17(1A) of the Protection of the Environment Operations Act 1997 , ensuring the 
proposal remains exempt from stricter NO₂ and NO emission concentration limits. 
• Acoustic Treatment:  All generators are housed within individual acoustic canopy 
enclosures to mitigate noise pollution.

The main potential source of air quality impacts is the testing and operation of the 87 diesel

Zealand Standards. Low-sulphur diesel will be used for the standby generators. In addition to 
tank containment, spill control measures will be provided at tank fill points, pumps, and 
filtration equipment where required.  
The Protection of the Environment Operations (Clean Air) Regulation 2022  exempts 
emergency standby plant from NO 2 (Nitrogen Oxide) and NO (Nitric Oxide) concentrations

6.12. GROUND AND WATER CONDITIONS 
A Geotechnical Investigation (Appendix 27) and Groundwater Impact Assessment (Appendix 
23) has been prepared by Douglas Partners  to characterise the subsurface conditions and 
assess the geotechnical risks associated with the proposed development. The assessment is 
based on data from 20 boreholes (6 augered, 14 cored), groundwater monitoring, and 
laboratory testing. 
6.12.1.  EXISTING GEOTECHNICAL ENVIRONMENT 
The site is underlain by Ashfield Shale, consistent with the regional geology of the Penrith 
1:100,000 Geological Sheet. The investigation identified four distinct geotechnical units: 
• Unit 1 – Fill: Pavement materials and uncontrolled fill comprising silty clay with gravel 
inclusions. Depth varies significantly across the site, ranging from 0.0m to 4.1m, 
reflecting historical earthworks to level the existing building platform. 
• Unit 2 – Residual Soil: Stiff to hard clays of medium to high plasticity. Thickness ranges 
from 0.6m to 6.4m. 
• Unit 3 – Siltstone (Very Low to Low Strength): Highly weathered and fractured 
siltstone with clay seams. 
• Unit 4 – Siltstone (Medium Strength or Stronger): Slightly weathered to fresh siltstone 
with some sandstone laminations. This unit provides a competent founding stratum 
for heavy structural loads. 
Laboratory testing confirms that the site soils are non -saline and do not require a specific 
Salinity Management Plan. The soils are assessed as non -aggressive to steel and mildly 
aggressive to concrete, which will inform the structural concrete mix design  in accordance 
with AS 2159-2009.

proposed basement levels for the new data centre buildings and the generator facility.  In 
particular:

basement Finished Floor Level (FFL) of RL 86.8m.

6.16.1.  SITE HISTORY AND BACKGROUND 
The site has been used primarily as a data centre since its development in the 1990s. Prior to 
this, the land was owned by the North Sydney Brick & Tile Company but was not developed 
for brickworks. 
A key area of environmental concern is a historical diesel spill identified in August 2019, 
involving the loss of approximately 4,000L of diesel from a pipe servicing the above -ground 
storage tanks (ASTs). Extensive remediation works have been undertaken b etween 2019 and 
2023, including: 
• Recovery of approximately 1,897L of free-phase diesel; 
• Multi-phase vacuum extraction (MPVE) events recovering an estimated additional 
1,885L; and 
• Excavation and off -site disposal of impacted soils and replacement of stormwater 
infrastructure. 
Whilst remediation has removed approximately 95% of the released hydrocarbon, residual 
Light Non-Aqueous Phase Liquid (LNAPL) remains present in groundwater beneath Diesel 
Generator Room 2 (DG2). 
6.16.2. ASSESSMENT METHODOLOGY 
JBS&G undertook a comprehensive intrusive investigation in April and May 2024 to address 
data gaps regarding the broader site condition and the residual diesel impacts. The scope of 
works included: 
• Soil Investigation: Advancement of 10 boreholes (BH01–BH10) across the broader site 
to assess general soil conditions and potential contamination outside the known spill 
area. 
• Groundwater Investigation:  Installation of four (4) new monitoring wells (JMW01 –
JMW04) and gauging/sampling of five (5) existing wells to assess groundwater quality 
and the extent of the dissolved phase plume. 
• Soil Vapour Investigation: Installation of 18 sub -slab soil vapour probes (SV01 –SV18) 
within the existing data centre building to assess potential vapour intrusion risks to

6.21.1. ELECTRICAL INFRASTRUCTURE 
The proposed data centre is a high-demand facility with a total estimated maximum demand 
of 120 MVA (including 96 MW of IT load). Given the scale, the existing local electrical network 
is insufficient to support the proposal directly. 
Servicing Strategy: 
• Diverse Points of Entry: five (5) new points of entry will be established – three (3) from 
Norwest Boulevard and two (2) from Brookhollow Avenue. 
• Carrier Neutrality: the site will connect to major carriers including NBN, Optus, Telstra, 
TPG, and Vocus, who have existing fibre pathways in the surrounding streets. 
• Internal Ring: a secure “customer ring”  of 20 x 100mm conduits will encircle the 
campus, connecting the Points of Entry to Meet-Me-Rooms (MMRs) in each building. 
6.21.2. WATER SERVICES 
The facility requires water for both domestic use and mechanical cooling (industrial water). 
Potable Water: 
• Connection: a new 150mm connection will be made to the existing Sydney Water 
main in Brookhollow Avenue. 
• Domestic Demand: estimated peak daily consumption is low (approximately 800 
L/day) due to the low staff occupancy. 
Industrial and Fire Water: 
• Cooling Demand: the mechanical cooling system requires significant water make-up. 
Peak industrial water demand is estimated at 31.54 L/s. 
• Storage: A stage 2 centralised water storage tank room will equip 8 x 600kL water 
storage tanks with a combined effective capacity of 4,800kL, designed to supply 24 
hours storage of 3,600kL for all stage 2 buildings, plus an additional 1,200kL reserved 
as redundancy to meet the mechanical cooling demand of the buildings B, C and D. 
This redundant capacity allow for the tanks to be services and maintained without 
the need to introduce a higher demand flow rate from Sydney water.  
• Fire Water: two  (2) x 245 kL fire water storage tanks will be installed to support the 
sprinkler and hydrant systems, with no direct connection to the town’s main. 
6.21.3. SANITARY DRAINAGE 
The site will connect to the existing 150mm gravity sewer main located near the southern 
boundary. Discharge volumes are minimal  and can be accommodated by the existing 
network. 
6.21.4. GAS 
No natural gas connection is required. The facility is fully electrified, with backup power 
provided by diesel generators . No works will impact the existing Jemena high -pressure gas 
mains located in the surrounding road reserves. 
6.22. BUSH FIRE RISK 
The site is not mapped nor is it in vicinity of bushfire prone land. No further consideration is 
required.

8. CONCLUSION 
In summary, the proposed data centre at 3 Brookhollow Avenue, Norwest represents a well -
considered, strategically aligned and future-focused development that warrants approval by 
the Department.  
The proposal is consistent with relevant State and local strategic planning frameworks and 
satisfies the applicable provisions of the THLEP 2019. Where variation is proposed it is 
reasonably justified through the provision of a 4.6 variation statement.  
A comprehensive environmental assessment has confirmed that the development can be 
delivered with minimal adverse impacts. Key matters  including acoustics, air quality, 
overshadowing, visual amenity and traffic have been thoroughly examined and appropriately 
mitigated through robust design measures and adaptive operational controls. These 
responses ensure that the proposal will integrate appropriately with surrounding land uses 
and maintain acceptable amenity outcomes for sensitive receivers. 
The proposal enhances the public domain through substantial landscaping and perimeter 
tree planting, improving the streetscape and contributing positively to the broader Norwest 
urban environment. The landscape design coupled with built form considerations ensures 
that the proposal is contextually appropriate and aligned with both the existing and desired 
future character of the Norwest Strategic Centre, reinforcing the precinct’s role as a key 
employment and innovation hub. 
The project will generate approximately 555 full-time equivalent construction jobs and 20 
ongoing operational jobs, strengthening the local economy and supporting long -term 
employment growth within a designated strategic employment precinct.  More importantly 
however, not only will direct employment result from the project, indirect employment and 
economic benefits will result from the proposal, as it will significantly advance AI capabilities 
of NSW. 
The proposal contributes meaningfully to NSW broader strategic priorities, including 
supporting digital infrastructure resilience, advancing the State’s transition to a more 
knowledge-based and technology -enabled economy, and enabling continued growth in 
data-dependent industries such as health, finance, logistics and advanced manufacturing. As 
demand for secure and efficient digital infrastructure continues to escalate, this development 
provides essential capacity that underpins economic competitiveness, s ervice reliability and 
future innovation across the State. 
Collectively, for the reasons outlined above and throughout this EIS, there is considered to be 
compelling justification for approval. The proposed data centre represents a high -quality, 
strategically significant and environmentally responsible investment that will deliver 
enduring benefits to the Norwest Strategic Centre, the regional economy and t he State of 
New South Wales.
