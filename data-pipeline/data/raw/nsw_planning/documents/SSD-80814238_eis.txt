SUMMARY 
Overview 
This Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) has been prepared by Urbis Ltd on behalf of Stockland (the 
applicant) to accompany a State Significant Development Application (SSDA) for the construction and 
operation of a data centre, referred to as the Road 1 Data Centre. The application is submitted to the NSW 
Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure (DPHI) and relates to the site at 1–5 Khartoum Road, 
Macquarie Park. 
Specifically, the SSDA seeks consent for site preparation works, the construction and operation of a 10 -
storey data centre, associated landscaping, utilities and services, and the delivery of sections of Road 1 and 
Road 23 within the subject site. 
The area subject of the SSDA is 8,096sqm (legally described as part of Lot 2 Deposited Plan 1043041) and 
is contained within the broader ‘MPark’ Stockland landholding (refer Figure 1).  
The proposed data centre has a total power consumption of 34.3 megawatts and, as such, is classified as a 
State Significant Development (SSD) under Schedule 1, Clause 25(1) of the State Environmental Planning 
Policy (Planning Systems) 2021. This report has been prepared to address the Secretary’s Environmental 
Assessment Requirements (SEARs) issued for the project (SSD-80814238).  
The intended outcomes of the project are to:  
▪ Deliver a state-of-the-art data centre to meet growing demand for secure, high-capacity digital 
infrastructure in NSW. 
▪ Promote design excellence through a high-quality architectural and public domain outcome, shaped by 
feedback from the State Design Review Panel (SDRP). 
▪ Continue to embed Aboriginal cultural values through meaningful engagement with the MPark First 
Nations Working Group, consistent with the Connecting with Country framework.  
▪ Incorporate sustainable design measures to minimise energy use, emissions, and operational impacts 
including targeting a low Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE). 
▪ Enhance site connectivity and support the broader Macquarie Park urban renewal through the delivery of 
new public roads, landscaping and essential infrastructure within the site.  
▪ Support Macquarie Park’s evolution as a leading innovation and technology precinct within the Eastern 
Economic Corridor. 
▪ Ensure an efficient and compatible use of underutilised serviced land, aligned with local, district and 
regional strategic planning objectives. 
▪ Facilitate job creation, with approximately 250 construction jobs and 50 ongoing operational roles.

The Project  
The proposal seeks consent for a data centre and ancillary office space to address the growing demand for 
digital infrastructure. The site is strategically located within the Macquarie Park Precinct, a key component of 
the Eastern Economic Corridor and identified as a strategic centre under the Greater Sydney Reg ion Plan 
and North District Plan. The proposal supports the precinct’s continued evolution as a specialised 
employment and innovation hub. 
The development leverages the site’s proximity to existing digital infrastructure, including two adjacent data 
centres, and major transport links, including Macquarie University and Macquarie Park Metro Stations (within 
800m) and the regional road network via the M2 Motorway. The site also benefits from access to high -
frequency bus services along Waterloo Road, Khartoum Road and Talavera Road. 
The site is zoned E3 Productivity Support under the Ryde Local Environmental Plan 2014 (RLEP 2014) 
which permits data centres and aims to accommodate employment-generating uses that support regional 
productivity. The proposal is consistent with the zone objectives and will optimise the use of underutilised 
urban land. 
A range of alternatives were considered, including a ‘do nothing’ scenario, which was discounted as it would 
fail to achieve the project’s objectives and result in the continued underutilisation of the site. The proposed 
scheme was identified as the most appropriate outcome following a detailed constraints and opportunities 
analysis and consultation with key stakeholders. 
The proposal has undergone design refinement through the SDRP process, including two meetings. 
Feedback has been incorporated where feasible to ensure the development responds appropriately to its 
urban context and delivers high-quality built form, public domain, and environmental outcomes. 
Environmental impacts including traffic, noise, air quality, visual amenity, or biodiversity have been assessed 
in detail and concluded to be acceptable when applying the specified mitigation measures.

Matter Response 
Connecting with 
Country  
The proposal has been shaped by a culturally responsive design process that meaningfully 
engages with Country as a living system of knowledge, culture and ecology. Stockland has 
worked closely with the MPark First Nations Working Group since 2020, to embed Aboriginal 
cultural values into the planning and design of the site. This process was guided by four core 
principles: Sensing, Imagining, Shaping and Caring. It has resulted in a landscape and public 
domain strategy that supports cultural connection, ecological restoration and long-term 
stewardship. Design responses include native and culturally significant planting, water-sensitive 
design, open and inclusive public interfaces, and opportunities for future interpretive storytelling. 
These elements reflect a commitment to creating a culturally safe and respectful place 
grounded in Country. 
Design Excellence / 
Better Placed 
The proposal demonstrates design excellence in accordance with Clause 6.13 of the RLEP 
2014, having been reviewed and refined through two meetings with the NSW Government 
Architect’s Office NSW Government Architect’s Office (GANSW) as part of the SDRP process.

increased deep soil zones and canopy cover, and stronger integration of Connecting with 
Country principles. The SDRP has confirmed that the project is capable of achieving design 
excellence, with final recommendations regarding landscape quality, durable materials, and 
best-practice ESD initiatives incorporated into the final design.

infrastructure, rainwater harvesting, and passive environmental controls. 
▪ Enhances public domain outcomes through active frontages, new landscaping, improved

▪ Provides inclusive, high-quality public interfaces informed by engagement with the MPark 
First Nations Working Group.

The proposal addresses the principles of ecologically sustainable development (ESD) in 
accordance with the requirements at section 193 of the EPA Regulation. Overall, the proposal 
will not have any unacceptable impacts on the natural environment. The ESD Report 
(Appendix CC) identifies sustainability measures including energy savings, energy efficiency 
and waste minimisation. 
The project 
minimises impacts 
on the built 
environment 
The proposal has been assessed in relation to the following key built environment impacts: 
 
▪ Built form: The proposed data centre is 10 storeys (72.8 m) high, well below the 130 m 
maximum height limit under the RLEP 2014. The building form has been carefully modelled 
to reduce bulk through façade articulation, with future residential towers along Waterloo 
Road expected to screen much of the development from key viewpoints. Setbacks comply 
with the Macquarie Park Design Guide on three boundaries, with a 6.5 m setback to the 
southeast to retain existing mature vegetation. The ground plane has been designed to 
positively contribute to the public domain and local amenity, with a legible and generous 
site entry, maximised active frontages to Road 1 and Road 23, landscaped street 
frontages, and recessed security fencing. The proposal results in minimal overshadowing, 
improves streetscape amenity, incorporates CPTED measures, and demonstrates design 
excellence consistent with precinct planning objectives. 
 
▪ Visual impact: The proposal is visually compatible with the desired future character and 
land uses within the Macquarie Park Design Guide. The visual impacts range from nil to 
medium-low. The proposal does not block views to any heritage items or areas of unique 
scenic quality. The proposal can therefore be supported on visual impact grounds as 
highlighted in Section 6.2.  
 
▪ Trees and Landscaping: The proposal will remove 19 trees (8 high retention value, 11 
medium) but retain 27 trees protected during construction. An extensive landscape strategy 
will deliver 36 new trees, deep soil planting (929 m²) and an increase in canopy cover from 
10.9% to 27.7% at maturity. Landscaping will enhance biodiversity, urban cooling, and 
public domain amenity as demonstrated in Section 6.4. 
 
▪ Traffic: Operational traffic generation is predicted to low (8 -10 vehicle movements/hour in 
peak periods), well below that of the former warehouse use. Access is via Roads 1 and 23,

3.4. CONNECTING WITH COUNTRY 
The project has been guided by a culturally responsive design process that recognises Country as a living 
system of knowledge, ecology, culture and story. The design team has sought to embed Aboriginal values, 
narratives and relationships into the physical and experiential qualities of the site ensuring that Country is not 
only acknowledged but actively engaged and respected through design. 
Since 2020, Stockland has engaged regularly and meaningfully with the MPark First Nations Working Group. 
This engagement forms part of a broader precinct-wide commitment to embed Aboriginal cultural values into 
planning and design outcomes. For this project, engagement included a Walk on Country in October 2023, 
followed by further consultation in April 2024, where Traditional Custodians shared insights relating to the 
cultural significance of the landscape, ecological memory, and principles for creating cu lturally safe and 
respectful places. 
This process was structured around four core principles - Sensing, Imagining, Shaping and Caring - which 
describe how Country has been listened to, interpreted and honoured through the project’s evolution: 
▪ Sensing: Engagement began with attentiveness to the land, waters and skies, understanding the cultural 
and ecological foundations of the site through direct experience. Custodians shared stories of the 
broader Wallumatta landscape, including sacred ochre grounds, riverbanks, fig roots and native 
ecologies. 
▪ Imagining: Through deep listening, the design team sought to understand the cultural lore, stories and 
knowledge systems embedded in the site. This informed early design thinking and shaped spatial 
planning, planting palettes and site expression. 
▪ Shaping: Rather than imposing form, the design allowed cultural values and community insights to guide 
outcomes. Responses include the use of native species, soft landscape edges, interpretive elements, 
and pathways that encourage movement and connection with Country. 
▪ Caring: The proposal includes strategies that support long-term cultural and ecological health such as 
deep soil planting, urban heat mitigation, water-sensitive design and open, welcoming public interfaces. 
These reflect a commitment to stewardship, intergenerational responsibility, and the continuing role of 
Country in shaping place.

The RDCP 2014 does not prescribe a specific car parking rate for data centres. However, it sets a maximum

Benchmarking against a comparable facility (NEXTDC Tullamarine) indicates a peak demand of 10  - 12 
spaces based on the technical data floor area. The proposal includes 19 on-site parking spaces, which is 
considered sufficient to meet forecast demand while remaining well below the DCP maximum.  
Car parking will be designed to comply with Australian Standard AS2890.1-2004, ensuring appropriate 
dimensions, access, and functionality.

The proposed development includes a dedicated on-site loading dock comprising two bays capable of

6.6.3. Cumulative Impact Assessment  
The potential for cumulative noise and vibration impacts has been considered in NVIA for the proposed 
development. While the assessment primarily focuses on the project in isolation, it acknowledges the 
broader Macquarie Park context and the presence of other existing and future developments, including 
nearby data centres. 
Cumulative construction noise impacts may occur if multiple projects in the vicinity undertake high -noise 
works concurrently. At the time of assessment, no overlapping construction programs were confirmed. 
However, any future concurrent activities can be effectively managed through inter-contractor coordination 
and the implementation of best practice management measures as outlined in a CNVMP. These measures 
will include stakeholder notification, activity scheduling, and mitigation strategies to minimise cu mulative 
impacts on shared sensitive receivers. 
The NVIA confirms that the proposed data centre will comply with all relevant noise criteria under normal 
operation and during routine generator testing. The assessment was undertaken independently and without 
reliance on any external shielding or mitigation from nearby developments. While the NPfI does not require 
assessment of cumulative operational noise, the proposed development will not prevent surrounding or 
future developments from achieving compliance independently. 
Operational vibration impacts are not anticipated due to the nature of the development. Similarly, cumulative 
vibration impacts are considered unlikely given the type of equipment involved and the separation from 
vibration-sensitive receivers. 
Overall, the proposed development will not contribute to significant cumulative noise or vibration impacts, 
and future developments in the area will not be constrained in their ability to demonstrate compliance with 
applicable criteria. 
6.6.4. Mitigation Measures 
Construction Noise and Vibration Impacts  
A range of standard and project-specific measures will be implemented to minimise noise and vibration 
impacts during construction. These will be detailed in a CNVMP, to be prepared by the appointed contractor 
prior to commencement of works. Key mitigation measures include: 
▪ Selection of modern, low-noise plant and equipment where practicable; 
▪ Use of noise barriers, enclosures or shielding for high-noise activities as required; 
▪ Staging of works and appropriate scheduling to avoid concurrent high-noise activities near sensitive 
receivers; 
▪ Restriction of high-impact works to standard construction hours, consistent with the NSW Interim 
Construction Noise Guideline (ICNG); 
▪ Implementation of respite periods or alternative work methods where predicted levels exceed highly 
noise-affected thresholds; 
▪ Regular communication with potentially affected stakeholders, including advance notice of high -noise 
activities; 
▪ Monitoring of noise and vibration levels as required to verify compliance and inform adaptive 
management; 
▪ Identification of underground services prior to use of vibration-intensive equipment, and adjustment of 
working methods as necessary. 
These measures are consistent with industry best practice and the guidance provided in the ICNG and the 
Assessing Vibration: a Technical Guideline. The CNVMP will also include procedures for complaints 
handling and coordination with other contractors should overlapping construction occur.  
Operational Noise and Vibration Impacts  
Noise and vibration mitigation has been embedded in the design of the facility to ensure compliance with the 
NPfI and relevant vibration guidelines. Measures incorporated into the development include:
