Preface 
This report details the Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure’s (the Department) 
assessment of the State significant development (SSD) application for the Apollo Place Data Centre 
(the development). 
The Department’s assessment considers all documents submitted by EMKC Cubed Management Pty 
Ltd (the Applicant), including the Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) and Submissions Report, 
submissions received from the public and Lane Cove Council, advice from government authorities, and 
all legislation and planning instruments relevant to the site and the development. 
The report includes: 
 a description of the development and the surrounding environment 
 an assessment of the development against government policy and statutory requirements, 
including mandatory considerations  
 an explanation of why the development is SSD and who the consent authority is 
 consideration of matters raised by the community and other stakeholders 
 an assessment of the likely environmental, social and economic impacts of the development 
and recommendations for managing any impacts during construction and operation 
 an evaluation which weighs up the likely impacts and benefits of the development, having 
regard to the proposed mitigation measures, offsets, community views and government advice, 
and provides a view on whether the impacts are, on balance, acceptable 
 a recommendation to the decision-maker, along with the reasons for the recommendation, to 
assist them in making an informed decision about whether development consent for the 
development should be granted and any conditions that should be imposed.

Executive Summary 
EMKC Cubed Management Pty Ltd (the Applicant) proposes to construct and operate a data centre 
with associated infrastructure and ancillary office space at 1 Apollo Place and part of 1 Sirius Road, 
Lane Cove West, within the Lane Cove local government area (LGA). The development has an 
estimated capital investment value of $288.4 million and would generate up to 380 construction jobs 
and 44 operational jobs. 
The project responds to growing demand for secure, high-performance digital infrastructure in 
Sydney and would be co-located with the approved Lane Cove West Data Centre (SSD-9741). The site 
is approximately 10 kilometres (km) north-west of the Sydney CBD and 12 km east of the Parramatta 
CBD, covering 0.53 hectares (ha) of E4 – General Industrial land under the Lane Cove Local 
Environmental Plan 2009. It is currently occupied by a two-storey commercial kitchen and a four-
storey office building, both to be demolished under separate consent.  
Surrounding land uses include the existing Lane Cove West Data Centre, the Lane Cove Tunnel 
ventilation facility, and other commercial and industrial premises. The nearest residential receivers 
are located approximately 290 metres (m) north (Arise by Meriton), with additional residences 520 m 
north-west near Magdala Park and 560 m south-west in East Ryde. 
The proposed development involves a 24-hour data centre with four data halls, a gross floor area of 
9,328 m², and a maximum building height of 32.68 m. The facility would have a total power 
consumption of 45 megawatts (MW) and rely on the existing Lane Cove West Data Centre for lithium-
ion battery storage, bulk diesel storage, firewater tanks, and industrial water supply. 
The Department exhibited the Development Applicant and accompanying Environmental Impact 
Statement between 17 October and 13 November 2024 and received three public submissions (two 
objections and one comment), a submission from Lane Cove Council, and advice from eight 
government authorities, a State-owned corporation, and a utility provider. Key issues raised included 
construction and operational noise, traffic management, bulk and scale, and building height. Council 
raised concerns about view impacts, setbacks, tree removal, and planning contributions. None of the 
agencies, Council or utility providers objected to the project and, where relevant, provided advice on 
conditions of consent to address residual impacts. 
The Department’s assessment focused on three key issues, being variation to the building height 
standard, operational noise and construction noise. The proposed building height of 32.68 m 
considerably exceeds the 18 m control under the Lane Cove LEP by 81.5%. Notwithstanding, the 
Department considers the variation justified due to the site’s industrial context and topography, and 
that it would not undermine the integrity of the height control. Council did not raise concerns with the 
proposed height. Operational noise impacts would be managed through measures including on-unit 
absorptive screens, enclosed rooftop plant, and acoustically treated ventilation and generator 
[[page 5]]
 
  Apollo Place Data Centre (SSD-67407231) Assessment Report | iii 
enclosures, ensuring emissions remain at least 10 dB below those of the existing data centre. 
Construction noise impacts would be managed through conditions limiting works to standard hours, 
with exceptions only for inaudible or safety-critical works, supported by a Construction Noise and 
Vibration Management Plan. 
The Department considers the development is consistent with the Greater Sydney Region Plan, the 
North District Plan, and the Lane Cove Local Strategic Planning Statement, which support the growth 
of digital infrastructure and targeted industry sectors. The project would deliver significant economic 
benefits, including $288.4 million in capital investment and employment opportunities, while 
providing critical digital infrastructure within an established industrial area. 
The Department concludes the development would not result in significant environmental or amenity 
impacts, subject to recommended conditions, and is in the public interest. Accordingly, the 
Department considers the project approvable, subject to conditions.

1 Introduction 
1.1 Development Background 
EMKC Cubed Management Pty Ltd (the Applicant) is seeking development consent for the 
construction and operation of a data centre with associated infrastructure and ancillary office space 
(the development). The site is located at 1 Apollo Place and part of 1 Sirius Road, Lane Cove West. A 
detailed description of the proposed development is provided in Section 2 . 
Data centres are secure facilities used by organisations to store, process and manage electronic data 
and digital applications using specialised information technology (IT) infrastructure. This may include 
personal data (such as social media content or cloud photo libraries), corporate data (such as financial 
systems or customer records), or sensitive government data (such as health and taxation information). 
Increasingly, data centres also support high-performance computing tasks, including artificial 
intelligence (AI), machine learning and real-time analytics. 
In recent years, demand for both large-scale and edge data centres have grown significantly across 
New South Wales. This growth is being driven by the rapid adoption of digital technologies, including 
AI, autonomous systems, cloud computing and next-generation mobile networks. As these 
technologies become more embedded in everyday life and business operations, additional data centre 
capacity is required to support the increasing volume, speed and complexity of data generated and 
accessed by Australians.  
The Applicant is a property development and asset management firm which has been approached by 
a data centre operator (the end user) who is seeking to expand its operations within the Lane Cove 
West Business Park. The end user currently operates multiple data centre assets within Sydney, 
including the approved Lane Cove West Data Centre that is co-located with the proposed 
development.  
1.2 Site Description 
The site comprises approximately 0.53 hectares (ha) of industrial-zoned land located at 1 Apollo Place 
and part of 1 Sirius Road, Lane Cove West, within the Lane Cove Local Government Area (LGA) (the 
site). The site is legally described as SP 80721 and part of Lot 1 in Deposited Plan (DP) 1271404, and 
forms part of a larger consolidated landholding of approximately 4.48 hectares. It is situated 
approximately 10 kilometres (km) north-west of the Sydney central business district (CBD) and 12 km 
east of the Parramatta CBD (see Figure 1 ). 
The site is accessible via Apollo Place and Sirius Road, with access to the M2 Motorway and Epping 
Road, providing connectivity to the regional road network. Public transport services, including high-
frequency bus routes, operate along nearby arterial roads, linking the site to Macquarie Park, North 
Ryde, and the Sydney CBD.

Vegetation on the site is limited, comprising a small number of planted native and exotic trees located

services, vehicular access, and a pedestrian link bridge that is not yet constructed. 
The electrical infrastructure is managed by Ausgrid, while drainage assets are under the authority of 
Lane Cove Council.

2.2 Physical Layout and Design 
The physical layout and design of the Apollo Place Data Centre (APDC) is illustrated in Figure 5  to 
Figure 7 .  
The structure is rectilinear and set back approximately 8 metres from Apollo Place, allowing for a 
landscaped frontage. The building is excavated into the sloping site, with lower ground and basement 
levels recessed below street level. The maximum building height is 32.68 metres above existing 
ground level, including rooftop plant and ventilation infrastructure. 
Each floor contains a single data hall, with associated technical spaces, switch rooms, and plant 
rooms. Floor-to-floor height is 6 metres. Mechanical plant is located on the northern and western 
gantries and on the roof. The rooftop includes cooling towers, air handling units, and a raised mesh 
platform. Acoustic screening and horizontal roof baffles are installed around the rooftop perimeter. 
The building exterior comprises precast concrete panels in grey and green tones, vertical baffles, 
galvanised mesh screens, and aluminium louvres as can be seen in Figure 8  to Figure 10 . The north, 
east and west facing façades feature vertical banding to visually recess the building into the 
surrounding bushland. The south facing façade include horizontal banding to introduce visual breaks, 
as viewed from Apolo Place.  
Shared infrastructure and servicing have been integrated across the consolidated site to ensure 
operational efficiency and minimise duplication. The proposed APDC relies on the existing LCWDC for 
lithium-ion battery storage, bulk diesel storage, firewater tanks, and industrial water supply. A new 
link bridge connects the two buildings at Level 1, enabling secure pedestrian movement and 
facilitating the transfer of services such as fuel lines and data cabling. Fire services infrastructure 
has been upgraded and consolidated in the southern corner of the site to serve both facilities. 
Furthermore, secured personnel and vehicle access points, car parking, bicycle storage, and end-of-
trip facilities are shared across the campus. These shared arrangements are supported by a 
concurrent modification application to SSD-9741 to ensure consistency across both consents. 
2.3 Uses and Activities 
The primary purpose of the development is for the collection, storage, processing and distribution of 
electronic data and digital applications in associated IT hardware by cloud and content providers or 
government entities (the tenant/s). 
The data centre operator will fit out the development’s four data halls with racking systems, which 
will house computer servers, networking equipment and associated infrastructure. The operator will 
lease storage capacity within these systems to its tenant/s, who will remotely manage their digital 
assets and applications. 
[[page 22]]
 
  Apollo Place Data Centre (SSD-67407231) Assessment Report | 20 
A stable internal environment will be maintained within the data halls using a series of rooftop cooling 
towers and hybrid dry coolers. These systems are designed to operate under a range of ambient 
conditions and are supported by an industrial water supply reticulated from the adjoining LCWDC.  
Ancillary office space is provided for on-site technicians who perform routine maintenance, monitor 
system performance, and provide IT support to external users. These spaces are located on the lower 
ground and ground floors and are accessible via the main entry and internal circulation cores. 
To ensure continuity of operations during a power outage, the development includes a system of 
lithium-ion batteries and diesel back-up generators. The lithium-ion batteries provide an 
uninterruptible power supply to bridge the gap between a power loss and the start-up of the 
generator system. Once operational, the diesel generators supply continuous power to the facility 
until mains power is restored. 
To ensure system reliability, each generator will be tested regularly. Testing is conducted individually 
and includes the application of artificial load using an electrical switching device. Testing is scheduled 
to avoid cumulative noise impacts and is managed in accordance with the site’s operational protocols.  
Generator tests would be undertaken during standard daytime hours (7:00 am to 6:00 pm, Monday to 
Friday).  
2.4 Applicant’s Justification for the Development 
The Applicant has justified the need for the development by identifying the increasing demand for 
secure, high-capacity data storage and processing infrastructure across Greater Sydney. The 
proposed data centre would contribute to meeting this demand by expanding the operator’s network 
within a strategically located industrial precinct. The site was selected due to its proximity to existing 
utility infrastructure, including power supply, industrial water connections, and telecommunications 
networks. Its integration with the adjoining Lane Cove West Data Centre allows for shared servicing 
and operational efficiencies across a consolidated campus. 
The Applicant has advised that the development would: 
 be consistent with the site’s E4 General Industrial zoning under the Lane Cove Local 
Environmental Plan 2009 
 support the retention and productive use of industrial land within the Lane Cove West Business 
Park 
 align with strategic planning objectives outlined in the Greater Sydney Region Plan and North 
District Plan 
 generate economic activity through construction and ongoing operations, with an estimated 380 
construction jobs and 44 operational jobs.

6.1 Variation to the Building Height Standard 
The Applicant seeks a variation to clause 4.3 of the Lane Cove Local Environmental Plan 2009 (LEP), 
which prescribes a maximum building height of 18m at the site. The proposed development would 
have a maximum building height of 32.68m (inclusive of rooftop plant), representing an exceedance 
of up to 81.5%. The variation request has been submitted in accordance with clause 4.6 of the LEP 
and the requirements of clause 35B of the Environmental Planning and Assessment Regulation 2021 
(EP&A Regulation). 
Under clause 4.6(3) of the LEP, development consent must not be granted unless the consent 
authority is satisfied that the Applicant has demonstrated: 
(a) compliance with the development standard is unreasonable or unnecessary in the 
circumstances, and 
(b) there are sufficient environmental planning grounds to justify the contravention. 
In response to subpoint (a), the Applicant contends that the objectives of clause 4.3 (Height of 
buildings) are achieved notwithstanding the non-compliance, as it considers the development 
responds appropriately to the site's topography and does not result in unreasonable solar, visual, or 
privacy impacts on neighbouring properties. The Applicant also argues the existing building height 
standard has been effectively abandoned in the locality, citing the adjoining LCWDC which exceeded 
the height control by 12.3m (a 68% exceedance).

grounds to justify the variation, primarily associated with:

measuring the building height from the basement level of the existing building rather than

6.1.2 Conclusion 
The Department’s assessment finds that the proposed height variation is contextually appropriate 
and avoids setting an undesirable precedent, particularly given the site’s unique topography, existing

The Department has considered the development on its merits, taking into consideration strategic

None of the State government authorities, Council or the community have objected to the proposal

government authorities and the Applicant. 
The Department’s assessment identified the key issues with the proposal as relating to bulk and scale, 
and noise impacts during both construction and operation.  
During the assessment process, the Department requested that the Applicant explore design 
modifications to improve urban design outcomes and mitigate operational noise impacts. The 
Department is satisfied that these matters have been addressed through changes to the façade 
design, incorporation of additional landscaping embellishments along Apollo Place, and 
implementation of enhanced operational noise control measures, including sound-absorptive lining 
and improved generator enclosure design. 
In relation to construction noise, the Department concluded that the proposed out-of-hours 
construction works could not be supported due to the elevated nature of predicted noise impacts and 
uncertainty regarding the effectiveness of the proposed mitigation measures. Instead, the 
Department has recommended that construction be limited to standard hours, with the option for the 
Applicant to seek the Planning Secretary’s approval for specific out-of-hours works on a case-by-
case basis—provided the appointed construction contractor can demonstrate that noise can be 
effectively managed. Additionally, the Department has recommended the Applicant prepare a 
Construction Noise and Vibration Management Plan for the duration of construction works. With these 
conditions in place, the Department is satisfied that construction noise can be adequately managed. 
The Department has also recommended conditions relating to manage other issues such as 
construction vibration, air quality, contamination, landscaping, biodiversity, hazards and risk, 
unexpected finds during construction, and waste management. The Department is satisfied the 
impacts of the development can be appropriately managed through implementation of the 
recommended conditions of consent.
